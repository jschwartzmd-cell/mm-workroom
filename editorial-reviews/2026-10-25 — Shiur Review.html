1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

A Sunday-morning shiur on the Shabbos שמונה עשרה, opened with a dedication and the second yahrzeit of the shul's late Rav (Tuesday; a bris for the rebbetzin's grandchild falls the same day). The weekday Amidah has nineteen berachos; Shabbos has seven (תפלת שבע). Why? The Gemara in Berachos 21a (one who began a weekday berachah on Shabbos finishes it, because he is still a בר חיובא and the Sages were merely לא מטריח מפני כבוד שבת) opens a Bavli / Yerushalmi machloket: some Rishonim (the Meiri named) say כבוד שבת means getting the people out in time for the meal; the Yerushalmi and the Midrash Tanchuma say a person may not demand his needs on Shabbos, since it brings צער and desecrates the spirit of the day. Objections from the room (Mi Shebeirach, Rachem, Rosh Chodesh, Tehillim, Eliyahu) are deferred or answered with a ברכה / תפילה distinction. Seven: the Name seven times in Psalm 92, and, the Rav's favourite, the Yaavetz: each Shabbos Amidah is another Sheva Berachos. The three different middle berachos (אתה קדשת, ישמח משה, אתה אחד) are the Tur's three Shabbasos and, as a second model, a wedding (kiddushin, simchas chasan v'kallah, yichud, with Musaf as the meal). Rashi never said ישמח משה; Rabbeinu Tam restored it; its meaning is either Moshe's joy over the Shabbos he chose for Pharaoh's slaves or the joy of Matan Torah on Shabbos morning (Shabbos 86b), with a guest's proof from הלילה הולך אחר היום. With a few minutes left, the Rav turns to the Kedushah: Masechet Soferim says Eretz Yisrael omitted weekday Kedushah; Pirkoi ben Baboi (Cairo Genizah) blames the Eretz Yisrael שמד and sent emissaries; the Chasam Sofer fought the Pressburg Reformers who wanted אז בקול רעש גדול removed; and the Eretz Tzvi suggests that אדיר וחזק is a typesetter's expansion of א' ו' ח', really אופנים וחיות.

2.Overall Fidelity — What the Write-Up Does Well

  • The Sofer.ai text is followed over the Plaud wherever they differ. The Plaud summary's "ticket/chicken Tuesday" (for [“תיקון Tuesday”]), its "Rabbi Isaac" instructor line, its "Instructor: Rabbi Isaac" header, and its closing assignment about coordinating "sponsorship details" are all correctly not carried over. The dedication wording ([“לעילוי נשמת מורינו ורבינו ר' יהושע בן מנחם מנדל הלוי”]) follows the Sofer.
  • The architecture is faithful and proportioned. The nine sections follow the live order: dedication; the 18/19/7 question; the Gemara's "Where am I?" and the case of the weekday berachah; the Bavli and Yerushalmi readings with the objections and the Tanchuma; why seven; why each Shabbos tefillah differs (the Tur, the wedding); Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam with the Moshe / Pharaoh midrash and the guest; the Kedushah with Soferim, Pirkoi and the Genizah; the Chasam Sofer and the Eretz Tzvi. The one reordering is in VII, where the Rav's closing remark (Eretz Yisrael did not say ישמח משה, Rabbeinu Tam brought it back) is moved up beside Rashi and Rabbeinu Tam; it is a sensible move.
  • The Rav's stance and hedges largely survive. "I don't know if the Yerushalmi would say that if you're in the middle of a berachah ... maybe he would tell you to stop" (IV); "I find it hard to believe that Moshe Rabbeinu didn't know ... but could be" (VII); "I don't know" for אדיר וחזק's origin (IX); "I'm not saying you should change it" (IX). The Yiddish and the jokes are kept at the right level, as are the Rav's humble admissions ("It has happened to me almost under a chuppah").
  • The corrections to the Rav's citations are reported without being put in his mouth. Tanchuma Vayera (not Vaeira); Rav Abba and the Yerushalmi; the Tur's text; the Genizah discoverers; Pirkoi's date. All appear in the notes, and the Sefaria log (fn1 through fn21) matches the HTML note for note.
  • Sofer-over-Plaud choices checked and confirmed. For the date, the sponsor, the Midrash Tanchuma, the Rokeach-style misattributions (none this week) and the Kedushah passages the Sofer reading is the better one in every case compared (see Section 4).
  • The header date is right. 25 October 2026 is 14 Cheshvan 5787 (7 Cheshvan was 18 October).

3.Content Flags — What Is Wrong, Mis-Attributed, Omitted, or Added in the Final Draft

Everything below was tested against the Sofer text, and the speaker labels in the Plaud transcript were used to settle who is speaking. None of these has been applied; they are the recommended corrections of Section 5.

Flag 1 — The Gaza remark is the Rav's, not a participant's. The HTML (Section II) says "(A participant adds a remark about the current war ... and the Rav agrees: 'Yes, very good, they talk about that.')" In the Sofer: [“They'll all be gone. One by one, we're bombing, I mean, you know what's going on, in Gaza and in... Yeah, very good, very good. They talk about that”]. The Plaud labels the whole sentence as Speaker 1, the Rav (a Speaker 2 remark follows only afterwards, at 00:03:02, about the addition of the berachah). So the Rav himself said it, in an unfinished sentence, and then answered a voice from the room. Recommendation: either attribute it to the Rav in a half-sentence or (better) cut it; it is a political aside that adds nothing to the lomdus.

Flag 2 — The antisemitism clause is attached to the wrong community (VIII). The HTML has "In Babylonia 'we lived in peace; the authorities liked us,' though there was antisemitism too. In Eretz Yisrael 'they were forced to do terrible things.'" The Sofer: [“We in בבל, we lived in peace, the authorities liked us, and we but they had anti-Semitism, שמד. They were forced to do terrible things”]. The false start ("and we— but they had") shows that the antisemitism and שמד belong to Eretz Yisrael, which is also the whole thrust of Pirkoi's argument. Fix: "In Babylonia we lived in peace; the authorities liked us. In Eretz Yisrael they had antisemitism and שמד; they were forced to do terrible things."

Flag 3 — The "Vumipnei chata'einu" exchange is reshaped (IV). The Sofer has a participant raise ומפני חטאינו גלינו מארצנו, and the Rav reply: [“You ever said a שבת מפני חטאינו? ... יום טוב ... שבת יום טוב, good, בסדר, but not every שבת”]; only then does the Rav say "things you have to be מחלק: collective for the entire Klal Yisrael you could ask for." The HTML drops the Rav's "not every Shabbos; only on Yom Tov" point (which is the answer to the objection as asked) and instead has the objector say that "the galus of the Shechinah and the fact that we ask for Moshiach" are painful. In the Sofer that clause is the Rav's later, hedged reply to a second challenge: [“Are you saying the חילוק that I said before is not 100%? But it's something that's going to remind you of צער. The גלות השכינה and the fact that we ask for משיח, that's I guess something more רוחניות'דיק”]. The HTML also loses the "I guess" and states the point firmly. Fix: restore the Yom Tov reply and the hedge, and attribute the second question as an unrecorded challenge rather than a stated one.

Flag 4 — שבת היא מלזעוק is missing from the body, and note 7 misattributes it. In the Sofer, just before the Tanchuma, a voice says: [“That's why you don't say רפואה שלימה on שבת, you say שבת היא מלזעוק”]; the Plaud has the matching remark on Speaker 2, 00:16:18. The write-up leaves it out of the body, and note 7 calls "Shabbos is from crying out" the Rav's additional phrase "in the room." It is a participant's point and, since it is the classical formula behind the whole Yerushalmi principle, it belongs in IV (attributed to a participant) and in note 7 as a source to be identified.

Flag 5 — The Eretz Tzvi's date (IX). The HTML parenthetical says the Rav "says he lived 'about 85 years ago.'" The Sofer: [“he came up with this about 85 years ago”], that is the chiddush, not the lifetime. Fix to "the Rav says the Eretz Tzvi put this forward about eighty-five years ago" and have note 21 carry the same. (The Rav's "we spoke about him in Lublin" is correctly kept.)

Flag 6 — "A participant: בסדר, הכי נמי" (VII). The Sofer: [“Yeah, בסדר, הכי נמי, but I just find it hard to believe that משה רבינו didn't know”]. The words are the Rav's, answering something a participant said that is not audible. The HTML gives them to the participant. Fix: "A participant offers an explanation (inaudible); the Rav: 'Yeah, fine, but I just find it hard to believe ...'"

Tension A — The Rav's remark about the yahrzeit date. The Sofer reads [“Tuesday, ט' סיון, right”] and the Plaud [“Tuesday Tassain”]. Both are most likely the recorder's rendering of ט"ז חשון (Tuesday, 27 October, is 16 Cheshvan). Note 1 says the date is "garbled" and keeps only "Tuesday." Recommend adding the probable reading, "(ט"ז חשון, as the 27th of October falls on 16 Cheshvan)," and asking the Rav to confirm.

Tension B — Who is the speaker of the guest's proof (VII)? The Sofer runs on from the introduction straight into [“Now, פתח פיך ויאירו דבריך. So, I have a ראיה for ישמח משה. Okay.”] and then [“הפנים יפות in פרשת נח. Beautiful, beautiful!”]. The HTML presents it as the guest's proof relayed and built on by the Rav, and note 15 reports it so. That reading is reasonable but not certain, since the transcript does not mark the change of speaker. The write-up could say so ("apparently the guest").

Tension C — Rav Abba's statement and the Yerushalmi are given as quotation. The Sofer text is the Rav's lively paraphrase ([“נאך א מזל, thank God that by מודים we bow down”], then a second version). The HTML keeps it in the Rav's voice with a "(as the Rav calls him)" for "tanna", which is fine; but the heading "Rav Abba's vulnerability at Modim" presents a located reading, while note 4 says no such statement was located. Soften the heading.

Smaller points. (a) The HTML says the Rav "jokes" that the late Rav would have told them to say תחנון anyway; the Sofer ([“The רב would tell us to say תחנון, I think. But all right, whatever”]) is not clearly a joke; render it plainly. (b) The "Eliyahu HaNavi" question is garbled in the Sofer (two partial versions); the HTML's reconstruction is plausible but should carry "as heard." (c) "Rav David Chai HaLevi," "Reb Yehoshua," "Reb Yehuda," "Reb Chaim Yishai," "Reb Biyatz," "Rav Elchanan," "Alan," "Nadav," "Bernie," "Reb Simcha" and "Rav Adnan" are all as the Sofer has them; the guest's name is a close hearing and the Plaud does not help. (d) The Rav's "I'm not saying you should change it to Rashi" is garbled in the Sofer and correctly shortened. (e) "Ach mai?" after Rav Elchanan's "Why?" is omitted; harmless. (f) The Rav's "the Chida ... old Rishonim" is the Rav's slip and the HTML reproduces it; the editorial review recommends the correction. (g) The Sofer's closing "Can I run in for a berachah?" is kept, as is the Rav's "the rabbis don't have the courage" line.

6.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav, and Bottom Line

Q1.What was the exact date of the yahrzeit (ט"ז חשון?), and what did the Rav mean about תחנון?
Q2.Which Yerushalmi has Rav Abba's statement about bowing at Modim? The located Yerushalmi (Berachos 1:5) has a different statement by Rebbi Abba bar Zavda.
Q3.Which Rishon uses the words מפני שהעם ממהרים לבוא לבית הכנסת בשבת וממהרים לצאת, and where in the Meiri?
Q4.Which edition of the Tanchuma has שאסור לו לאדם לתבוע צרכיו בשבת and נמצא שמחלל את השבת? And what is the Tur's second reading of ישמח משה (the Pharaoh midrash) and the "מדרש מגילות"?
Q5.Where is the Soferim passage the Rav cites (no weekday Kedushah at both Shacharis and Minchah), given that the located text has Kedushah at Minchah? Also the Chida's passage.
Q6.Which Chasam Sofer responsum says nobody knows where אז בקול רעש גדול comes from, and where is the Eretz Tzvi's responsum?
Q7.Please confirm the guest's name (עדנן?) and the name of the Panim Yafos' proof's speaker, and whether the story of the two years of separate Yom Tov dates is the 921-922 dispute.
Against the Sofer.ai transcript, the final write-up is a faithful, carefully hedged and unusually well-sourced account of a long shiur. Its errors are few and local: the Gaza aside given to a participant, the antisemitism clause assigned to Babylonia instead of Eretz Yisrael, the compressed "Vumipnei chata'einu" exchange, a missing שבת היא מלזעוק (and a note that misattributes it), the Eretz Tzvi's "lived 85 years ago," and one participant-attributed line that is the Rav's. What stays open is research, not transcription: the Tanchuma wording, Rav Abba's Yerushalmi, the Soferim text, the Chasam Sofer and Eretz Tzvi responsa, and the Genizah chronology.
