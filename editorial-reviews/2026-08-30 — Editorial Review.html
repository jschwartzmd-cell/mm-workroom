2026-08-30 — The Rosh HaShanah Tefillah: מלכויות · זכרונות · שופרות

Independent review · read fresh, no exposure to the writing process · 30 August 2026

1.Overall Read

A well-structured opening installment of the three-part מחזור arc. The historical spine — משנה and גמרא baseline, then תָּקַע דְּרַב, then the גאונים/ראשונים piyutim layer — is laid out cleanly and gives the reader a real framework for orienting inside the machzor. The Kroining Nacht callout (Section VI) and the Reb Zusha tavern story (Section IX) are the emotional high points. The פחד/שמחה synthesis in Section VII is theologically ambitious and mostly earns its landing. Weakest zone is the middle: Section IV/V occasionally repeats itself on the "nine ברכות" and "combined with קדושת היום" points, and Section VIII feels like a hinge that hasn't quite been engineered.

2.What Works

  • The Nadvorna opening (Section II). "הַסִּדּוּר בִּמְקוֹמוֹ, וְהַמַּחְזוֹר מְסֻדָּר. אַתָּה צָרִיךְ לְסַדֵּר אֶת עַצְמְךָ" is the perfect frame for a series about the machzor, and the piece uses it structurally — the whole arc keeps returning to internal preparation vs. text-mastery.
  • Section III's core-vs-piyut argument. The move — "ask any Yid and they'll say ונתנה תוקף, but אביי ורבא never said it" — is genuinely clarifying, and the follow-up ("what do we race through fastest? the פסוקים — the actual core") is a real chiddush for most readers.
  • The Yerushalmi geography (Section V, fn6). Galilee following ר' יוחנן בן נורי, Judah following ר' עקיבא — concrete, teachable, and rare in a public shiur on this topic.
  • The Kroining Nacht callout (Section VI, fn9). "Yankl the tailor, Berl the shoemaker — every simple Yid gets to ascend to the כסא הכבוד" lands emotionally without sentimentality.
  • The Reb Zusha tavern story (Section IX). Elegantly deployed to illustrate the immovability of the גזר דין — the story does the theological work the section needs.
  • The Practical Suggestion callout on picking one piyut. Concrete, actionable, and consistent with the piece's overall thesis.

3.Where It's Weak or Could Be Sharper

Repetition between Sections IV and V. The "מלכויות combines with קדושת היום — כדי שתמליכוני עליכם" point is made in IV, then re-made in V, then referenced again in VI. One clean statement with cross-references would be tighter.

Section VIII (כוונה and Communal Identity). The tension between "your personal עמידה can be longer" and "you daven as a ציבור" is set up but never resolved. How does a person practically balance both when the tzibbur is finishing? The Hillel citation at the end gestures at synthesis but doesn't deliver one.

Section IX's structure. The Dessler letter and the Reb Zusha story are both about the immovability/weight of the גזר דין, but they make slightly different points (Dessler: תפילה can shift it; Zusha: once decreed, it will happen). Held next to each other without a bridge, they can read as being in mild tension.

זכרונות section is thin (Section VI). מלכויות gets the Chaslavich story; שופרות gets the arc from מתן תורה to תקיעה גדולה; זכרונות gets one paragraph. The imbalance is noticeable.

The ונתנה תוקף discussion (Section III) hedges. The piece says the piyut is "much later" and that "authorship and dating are the subject of significant historical dispute" — but then never returns to what a reader should do with the piyut. Given how much rhetorical weight the section places on the piyut being non-Chazal, some direction ("say it, but know what the core is") would close the loop.

4.Factual / Citation Concerns

"Multshleif Nadvorna Rebbe" (fn1). The spelling — apparently a transliteration heard by ear — should be verified. Standard English spellings for Nadvorna sub-branches include Mishkoltz, Bania, Zutchka. If "Multshleif" is meant to be Miskolc/Mishkoltz, the file should say so; if it's a distinct branch, it needs confirming.

"רבי חיים שמואל לוויט" (fn11). The name as spelled is not one I can identify with confidence — possibly Rav Chaim Shmuel Lopian, Levovitz, or a different figure. The fn itself acknowledges no sefer/page. Worth nailing down before print.

Rav's dating (fn8). "First-generation Amora, c. 175–247 CE" — the birth year is conventionally given as c. 175 but some sources have c. 155 or 180; 247 as the death year is standard. Fine as-is but worth confirming.

"תָּקַע דְּרַב" as a technical term (fn8). The phrase is used in the piece as if it were a fixed חז״ל coinage. The term does appear in later halachic literature for Rav's מוסף establishments, but a reader may be surprised to find it not straightforwardly in the Gemara — a brief sourcing note in the fn would help.

פרי חדש citation (fn14). The fn honestly notes the specific siman was not cited in the shiur and suggests תקצ"ז. Worth checking before print — the מנהג of משלוח מנות on ראש השנה is discussed by several early acharonim and getting the right one matters.

Zohar citation (fn16). "זוהר חלק ג' דף ק״א ע״א (פרשת אמור)" — this daf reference should be verified; the בתוך עמי אנכי יושבת application to communal davening on the Yamim Nora'im is a well-known Zohar passage but the exact location varies by edition.

5.Small Polish Suggestions

  • Section I is very short (one paragraph). Either fold it into the Section II opening or expand — currently it reads as a preamble to a preamble.
  • Section III: "spoiler — not וּנְתַנֶּה תֹּקֶף, however moving it is" in the lead is good; the phrasing "and rightfully so" in the body ("Ask any Yid... And rightfully so") slightly undercuts the argument the section is about to make. Consider trimming.
  • Section IV: "Ten ברכות in principle — but the גמרא rules..." — the "in principle" is doing a lot of work here; a clause explaining why ten would have been the count would help a lay reader.
  • Section V, on Rav's compositions: the bulleted list of introductions is helpful, but the paragraph immediately after ("Rav's introductions became fixed liturgy...") should note that this is the same עלינו that closes every davening — the piece says it, but almost as an aside; it's actually a striking historical fact worth landing.
  • Section VII, Dimension of פחד: "רבי חיים שמואל לוויט" — if this name is uncertain (see fn concern above), consider paraphrasing without attribution rather than misnaming.
  • Section IX callout: "peasants decided to beat Reb Zusha three times" reads oddly precise for a decision made by drunks. Consider "gave Reb Zusha a beating" and letting the "three times" emerge in Reb Zusha's punchline.
  • Practical Takeaways: "the mishloach manos per the פרי חדש" — the שמחה side and the פחד side get listed as balanced items, but the mishloach manos is quite obscure while the תלויים ועומדים is core; consider swapping in something more central to the שמחה side (e.g., בגדי לבן, חדות ה' היא מעוזכם).
  • Colophon still reads "First of three shiurim" — good; but the header meta and the lead say the same thing. One placement would suffice.

6.Bottom Line

Yes — a strong opening to the machzor arc, anchored by the Nadvorna frame and the core-vs-piyut chiddush; tighten the IV/V repetition, beef up the זכרונות section, and verify the "Multshleif Nadvorna" and "רבי חיים שמואל לוויט" attributions before print.
