Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

2026-10-11 — נשמת כל חי: Layers, Legends, and the Question of Authorship

Content-based review · re-done against the PRIMARY Sofer.ai transcript (no timestamps, so passages are cited by quoted phrase, shown as [“…”]) · the Plaud transcript and summary were used only as secondary checks · reviewer also read the current English write-up and the last two Sefaria log entries · 11 October 2026 · 30 Tishrei 5787 (Rosh Chodesh מרחשון) · this review replaces the earlier Plaud-based review

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

The Rav returns to the Shabbos-tefillos track after the Days of Awe and takes נשמת כל חי, the long lead-in to ישתבח, as a question of layers: why the Mishnah Berurah has a latecomer say it, who wrote it, when it entered the seder, and why on Shabbos. The first layer is ברכת השיר on the fourth cup (פסחים קי"ח ע"א); the second is ואילו פינו, tied to the rain-prayer (ברכות נ"ט and "a whole סימן in שולחן ערוך"); the third is the אלהי הראשונים / המשיח אלמים / המפענח נעלמים lines, which the Chida's שמחת הרגל (and R. Yaakov Yisrael Algazi) trace to the miracle of רב קלונימוס בעל הנס and which are absent from the siddurim of Rav Amram and Rav Saadia. A "much, much later" section, וכל הלבבות to שוכן עד, hides the יצחק/רבקה acrostic, which the Machzor Vitry rejects as the Avos. Then come the authorship extremes: the Rokeach (the first tefillah ever, replacing ברכות השחר); Reb Hertz Treves's backwards acrostic שמעון (שמעון בן שטח), followed immediately by "the most controversial thing" in the seder, the legend of שמעון כיפא / Peter as the "double agent," with the Baruch Ta'am gloss on the ninth of Teves, the fury of the Machzor Vitry, and Sefer Chasidim's "Peter Chamor." The reasons for Shabbos are more time, the נשמה יתירה, and (the Tur, "I think") the exodus theme; individuals said it before the tzibbur did. The Rav closes with Nishmas as the שיר השירים of davening, Reb Moshe Wolfson's half-hour Nishmas, the יסוד ושורש העבודה on simchah, and the unanswered question of why the shliach tzibbur descends at שוכן עד ("The answer is קבלה and I don't know"), ending with the רבי חנניה בן עקשיא mishnah. Throughout he says outright that he is "a little confused myself as to the timeline" [“I'm a little confused myself as to the timeline”; “I'm confused as to when they started saying it”].

2.Overall Fidelity — What the Write-Up Does Well

  • The reconciliation to the Sofer.ai transcript has worked. Most of the points the earlier Plaud-based review had to flag as garbled or doubtful are now correct in the HTML and confirmed by the Sofer text (full list in Section 4): Pesachim קי"ח ע"א, Berachos נ"ט and the Shulchan Aruch siman, רב קלונימוס and "the rav of Yerushalayim," burial outside הר הזיתים, ישועה (not "yisurah"), ArtScroll, the Magen Avraham and Shelah, Baruch Frankel-Teomim, Reb David, Reb Yisroel Dovid, Ziskind of Horodna, the Mishnah in Yadayim, and the "we should say Nishmas every day" line, which the Plaud had inverted.
  • The architecture is faithful and proportioned. The twelve sections follow the live order of the shiur. The long Kloynimus story is told in full and in the right order (libel, Pasha, the child, Shabbos, burial, lost kever, the lines in the text, Gaonic siddurim). The Plaud summary's "Assignments" section is not in the Sofer transcript and, correctly, is not in the write-up.
  • The Rav's own stance and hedges survive. "A little confused myself as to the timeline"; "I believe it. I have no problem"; "an urban myth ... but it has sources"; "a lot of things point to the story being true"; "I'm not sure about that exactly" on the Ashkenaz/Sefard difference; "I don't know" on Yom Tov and on שוכן עד. The Kloynimus story is not upgraded to history, and the Peter legend is framed throughout as "the Rav's report of a legend."
  • Several earlier flags are now genuinely closed in the HTML (verified against the Sofer text): the dedication is now placed at the coffee break with the names offered and hedged; "predates Chazal" is gone; "the Rav leaves to the Chida" is now "leaves open"; the Seder Avodah aside now says "the machzor" and is hedged; the Reb Hertz "quotes it" line is present; the Wolfson half-hour is reported as the Emunas Yisrael practice; "his talmid is Rabbi Yehoshua ben Perachya" and the Shimon ben Shetach / Yeshke link are restored, with note 14 honestly saying this runs opposite to Avos; the Rav's remark that the Tur is cited "I think" is kept.
  • The Machzor Vitry, Rokeach, Wolfson, Yesod VeShoresh, and Yitzchak/Rivka sections track the Sofer text closely, including the Machzor Vitry objection ("Yitzchak wrote mincha") and the Rav's "like Shimon Kipa, we want you by the misnagdim" line, which survives in the Ziskind passage.
  • The header date is right. 11 October 2026 is 30 Tishrei 5787, the first day of Rosh Chodesh Cheshvan; the Rav says "a גוט חודש," "welcome to חודש מרחשון," and "Have a wonderful חודש."

3.Content Flags — What Is Still Wrong, Mis-Attributed, Omitted, or Added

Everything below was re-tested against the Sofer text. Items from the earlier review that are now fixed are not repeated here (see the "resolved" list in Section 4).

Flag 1 — The Shimon Kipa legend is still framed as a rival to Reb Hertz Treves. Section IX opens "The 'Shimon' in the acrostic, according to a different and far more explosive tradition, is not Shimon ben Shetach," and a later paragraph says "Reb Hertz Treves says it points to Shimon ben Shetach, and the Baruch Ta'am tradition says Shimon Kipa." The Sofer text shows the Rav does not set them against each other. Right after the reverse acrostic he says [“Then he says the most controversial thing that was ever said about anything in our siddur”], then [“some rishonim are very upset when you say this over, but I'm going to say it because other rishonim do say it, R' Hertz quotes it”], and he concludes [“that's why Shimon, not Shimon ben Shetach ... hence Shimon Kipa”]. In the Sofer, then, Reb Hertz is the one who quotes the Peter version; the Baruch Ta'am adds the ninth-of-Teves gloss afterwards. The HTML does keep "Reb Hertz quotes it," which contradicts its own "different tradition" framing. Who exactly carries the legend (Hertz himself, a midrash "whose source is debatable," other Rishonim, the "Tashbetz" the Rav points at) stays unclear, and the write-up should say so rather than invent a contrast.

Flag 2 — "Sefer Chasidim so fiercely reject[s]" overstates the Rav (new under the Sofer text). The abstract at the top says the legend is one "that the Machzor Vitry and Sefer Chasidim so fiercely reject," and note 15 says "The Machzor Vitry and Sefer Chasidim reject it." The Sofer text gives only [“ספר חסידים also calls him פטר חמור, even though ספר חסידים says he started off as a צדיק. So nobody really knows what the story with this Peter is”]. That is a disparaging epithet with a mitigating clause, not a stated rejection of the Nishmas-authorship claim. Only the Machzor Vitry is reported as rejecting it (and even there, the Rav's version is a paraphrase).

Flag 3 — The "two Shimons" exchange is still in the wrong place, and two of its details are not in the Sofer text. The HTML puts it at the end of the ninth-of-Teves paragraph. In the Sofer it comes after the Wolfson story, right after [“So bottom line is נשמת was incorporated in the סידור דוקא on שבת. Now we have to understand why on שבת. Yes, Reb Mordechai, I'm sorry.”] and before [“Why is it on שבת?”]. (a) The listener's question ("could the two Shimons be one person?") is not on the Sofer recording at all; the Sofer has only "Yes, Reb Mordechai, I'm sorry" and then the answer. The question comes from the Plaud transcript alone (and garbled there). The Rav's answer itself is well preserved. (b) The write-up calls the "maybe Shimon Kipa was a Nusach Sefard guy" line "a quip from the room." In the Sofer it reads as the Rav's own sentence ([“Now, so maybe שמעון כיפא was נוסח ספרד guy. I hear what you're saying, I hear what you're saying.”]), placed immediately after "hence Shimon Kipa," i.e. before the Machzor Vitry paragraph, not at the two-Shimons exchange.

Flag 4 — The closing is partly from the Plaud, and the Rav's actual closing mishnah is missing. The HTML ends "may we merit to thank Hashem, the merciful One, for everything we have." The Sofer text has [“we should be זוכה to thank הקדוש ברוך הוא for everything that we do”] and then the Rav recites [“רב חנניה בן עקשיא אומר, רצה הקדוש ברוך הוא לזכות את ישראל, לפיכך הרבה להם תורה ומצות, שנאמר ה' חפץ למען צדקו, יגדיל תורה ויאדיר”]. "The merciful One" was a Plaud artifact ("our G-d is merciful"); the mishnah is absent from the HTML (the Plaud transcript ended in garbage text).

Flag 5 — "Or for other yeshuos" is still in the HTML. Section XI ("Forty Days at the Kosel") says people go "in order to find a shidduch or for other yeshuos," and the header of note 12 says "(for a shidduch and yeshuos)." The Sofer text says only [“people go to the כותל for 40 days and they say נשמת, and they get a שידוך”], with no other yeshuos. The word came from the Plaud summary ("seeking salvation," "for yeshuos"). Flagged in the earlier review and not yet applied.

Omission A — The Rav's double stance on the Peter legend (still open). The Sofer has [“has to be some part of this legend have to be true”] and, about Peter's dream, [“this is all made up, obviously, according to our tradition”]. The HTML's "What Peter Did" section reports the dream as simply "in the New Testament" and drops both qualifiers. It keeps "a lot of things point to the story being true," but not "made up according to our tradition," which is the qualifier the Sefer Edition most needs.

Addition B — Editorial wording that sits in the Rav's voice (still open). The reconciliation moved many references into the notes, but these remain in the body: (1) "The Mishnah says Hallel is completed and one recites Birchas HaShir"; the Rav says [“So the גמרא says you say, according to one opinion, you say ברכת השיר”], and "the fourth cup is the cup of Hallel" is also the editor's (the Rav only reels off [“צפון, ברך, הלל, נרצה”]); (2) "(59b, where Rabbi Yochanan closes the rain thanksgiving with it)" inside the Rav's sentence on נ"ט; (3) "Although this was entirely permitted" (Kloynimus); the Rav says [“He did חילול שבת in order for פיקוח נפש, but he held he's not on the same מדרגה”]; (4) "so the earlier layers are older than this addition" after "the Geonim already have this tefillah of Nishmas" (the Rav says only "Let's go a little weiter"); (5) in Section V, "These lines also echo Birchos HaShachar ... (releasing the bound, straightening the bent)": the Rav makes that connection only at the Rokeach, so this pulls his point forward; (6) Reason Three: the Rav's claim ([“the טור I think writes, that since שבת is זכר ליציאת מצרים, and we just say שירת הים ... so right after, we say נשמת”]) is replaced in the body by the verified Tur wording (Nishmas itself carries the exodus theme); (7) "with nishmas kol chai in the fullest sense" (not the Rav); (8) "with a smile" appears twice (the yeshuah-with-your-wife line and the Harborview line); the Sofer text has no such cue; (9) "Rav Chaim Kanievsky's daughter" (body and note 22) is presented as the participant's identification; in the Sofer it is the Rav's own question ([“Rebbetzin Kolodetzky, Rav Chaim Kanievsky's daughter you mean, she also says it every day”]).

Chronology — better, but "The Latest Layer" is still asserted as fact. The body now says "(see note 6 on the Rav's chronology)" at the Ottoman/Pasha point, and the Chida paragraph keeps "700 to 800 years ago," which is an improvement. Still open: (a) the heading of Section VI is "The Latest Layer," whereas the Rav says only [“based on my research in ancient סידורים, וכל הלבבות ייראוך is a later part of דאווענען ... authored much, much later”]; by the write-up's own note 10 the Machzor Vitry already knows the Yitzchak/Rivka acrostic, which sits oddly with a 700-year-old Kloynimus insertion being earlier. (b) The body never carries the Rav's other date, [“an obscure נס that happened a couple of centuries ago in ירושלים”]; it is only in note 6(c). A reader of the body will take "700 to 800 years" and "Ottoman" as settled.

Smaller points still open. (a) "Most Rishonim" (Section III; Sofer [“אנשי כנסת הגדולה, most ראשונים understand”]) versus "Some Rishonim" (Section VII; Sofer [“Some ראשונים say אנשי כנסת הגדולה”]): both are in the Sofer, but the write-up should not harmonize silently. (b) Dedication: the Sofer attaches [“פאר אלע אידן א ישועה”] to the first mention of the sponsors; the HTML attaches it to the end of the second, longer dedication. The Sofer's [“לעילוי נשמת, ר׳ יוסי גבאי טהרה, לעילוי נשמת ר׳ Her father ...”] reads most naturally as a further dedicatee (a separate name in the list), not as someone whose role is "not clear"; the HTML is right to hedge, but should show the name. (c) "Famous custom" for סגולה (Sofer [“a famous סגולה ... you go דאוון by that קבר”]); the Rav's word is segulah. (d) Optional omissions that change nothing: "they were נרדפים, the Jews hated them, the Romans hated them, as we know from the story of the cross"; "Peter, obviously, was a Jew, but he was a כופר"; "I didn't think of that" (before "maybe it fits"); the reverse-acrostic is the Rav's "acronym of the tefillah of Nishmas," and the HTML's "first letters of the paragraphs" is the editor's description.

Garbles the Sofer does not resolve (the write-up must stay hedged). (1) "The machzor says you're not allowed to say it" (the Sofer's [“shocking the מחזור it is says you're not allowed to say it”] is as ambiguous as the Plaud's). The HTML's "apparently the claim of Peter's authorship" is a fair, hedged reading, but note 21 slips back into "the Machzor Vitry reference is unverified"; the Rav says only "the מחזור." (2) The "midrash" for the Peter legend and "Tashbetz": unidentified. (3) "Rav Yitzchak's wife": unnamed. (4) "Whether it happened because of the story of רב קלונימוס, huh?": a trailing question, now rendered as "leaves open," which is right. (5) "It comes from the רוקח, one of the ראשונים writes, that נשמת more than the other פסוקי דזמרה ... בקול רם": the HTML gives the בקול רם ובנעימה rule to the Rokeach flatly; the sentence can also be read as the Rokeach being the source of the Shir HaShirim idea and an unnamed Rishon writing the rule. (6) The final lines about שוכן עד ("On every That, yes, according to some נוסחאות"): the Rav's point there is incomplete.

Is the apparatus honest? Yes, and it has improved. The notes separate "the Rav said" from "the editor identified"; they say when the Sefaria check failed (notes 1, 5, 6, 7, 11, 13, 15, 17, 18, 19, 21, 22, 28); they correct wrong dapim (Berachos 59a to 59b; Shulchan Aruch OC 580:2; Beitzah 16a vs Taanis 27b); and they refuse to launder the Rav's weaker claims (the Tur ties Nishmas, not Shabbos, to the exodus; the Tosafos text does not say what the Rav says; the Machzor Vitry passage on Peter was not located). The two Sefaria log entries match the HTML on every item compared (fn1, fn2, fn3, fn4, fn6, fn10, fn14, fn17, fn23, fn24, fn25, fn26, and the UNCERTAIN list). The Tosafos reversal (fn25) is correctly in the body ("whereas in the siddurim of the Geonim ... only individuals"), consistent with the Sofer.

Where it still falls short. (1) The Reason Three Tur sentence and the other body insertions listed in Addition B keep the verified source in the Rav's mouth. (2) The Treves/Kipa framing and "Sefer Chasidim rejects" (note 15) are not flagged as uncertain. (3) Note 15's "a medieval Jewish folk tradition with no scholarly-historical basis" and note 16's "the New Testament itself is not read by scholars as attributing these changes to Peter" are the editor's verdicts; they are labeled "Flag:" and must stay visibly separate. (4) Note 21 should say "the machzor" (not "the Machzor Vitry reference") to match the Rav. (5) Note 22 attributes "Rav Chaim Kanievsky's daughter" to a participant; the Sofer text shows the Rav asking "...you mean?" (6) Note 12 (Kosel): drop "and yeshuos" from the header.

5.Recommended Corrections for the Sefer Edition

Outstanding items only, in priority order (text-correcting first, apparatus last). Locators are phrases from the Sofer.ai transcript.

  1. Fix the Treves / Shimon Kipa framing (abstract, Section IX opening, and the ninth-of-Teves paragraph). Delete "according to a different and far more explosive tradition, is not Shimon ben Shetach" and the sentence "Reb Hertz Treves says it points to Shimon ben Shetach, and the Baruch Ta'am tradition says Shimon Kipa." Follow the Rav: Treves reads the backwards acrostic as Shimon (ben Shetach) and then says "the most controversial thing"; "other Rishonim do say it, Reb Hertz quotes it"; the Baruch Ta'am adds the ninth-of-Teves manuscript; the Rav concludes "that's why Shimon, not Shimon ben Shetach ... hence Shimon Kipa." Add to note 15 that the recording does not identify who carries the Peter version (a midrash "whose source is debatable," Reb Hertz, "the Tashbetz").
  2. Correct the Sefer Chasidim wording. Abstract: "that the Machzor Vitry fiercely rejects and Sefer Chasidim also disparages (calling him 'Peter Chamor')." Note 15: replace "The Machzor Vitry and Sefer Chasidim reject it" with the Rav's actual statement.
  3. Restore the Rav's qualifiers on the Peter legend (Section IX, "What Peter Did"). Add "has to be some part of this legend have to be true" and, for the dream, "this is all made up, obviously, according to our tradition."
  4. Move and correct the "two Shimons" exchange. Place it after the Wolfson passage and the Rav's "Now we have to understand why on Shabbos. Yes, Reb Mordechai, I'm sorry." Do not write "A listener asks whether the two Shimons could be one person" as if on the recording; present it as the Rav's response to Reb Mordechai and (if kept) note the question is reconstructed from the Plaud. Keep his answer in full ("Doesn't really fit, maybe according to one shitah ... with the dates of Christianity, of Yeshke, Yehoshua ben Perachya ... I didn't see anybody that says that. I hear what you're saying" line, as the Rav's own, back in the ninth-of-Teves paragraph, right after "hence Shimon Kipa," and drop "a quip from the room."
  5. Fix the closing.   7. Move editorial insertions out of the Rav's voice. (f) Reason Three: state the Rav's claim ("the Tur, I think, writes that since Shabbos is a remembrance of the exodus, and we just said Shirat HaYam, we say Nishmas right after it") in the body and put what the Tur says in note 24.   8. Mark "The Latest Layer" as the Rav's claim and surface the second date.
