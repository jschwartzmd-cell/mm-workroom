הַמִּנְחָה הָאַחֲרוֹנָה, אֲחוֹת קְטַנָּה, וּשְׁבוּעַת הַנְּשָׁמָה

The Final Mincha, Achos Ketanah, and the Chofetz Chaim on the Primordial Oath of the Neshamah
Studies in the Machzor of Rosh HaShanah — Second Discourse
24 Elul  ·  Yahrzeit of the Chofetz Chaim  ·  Sefer Edition

The מחזור does not begin at the first מעריב of Rosh HaShanah. It begins with the מנחה that closes the year that just was — for הכל הולך אחר החתום.

I. The Final Mincha — Hakol Holech Achar HaChitum

Where the Machzor Truly Begins

The natural instinct on Erev Rosh HaShanah is to reserve one’s kavvanah (כוונה) for the first maariv (מעריב) of the new year — to treat the closing hours of Elul as logistics: the mikveh (מקוה), the final preparations, the last mundane errands of the outgoing year. Yet the true opening of the machzor (מחזור) sits earlier still, in an easily-overlooked place: the Mincha (מנחה) of Erev Rosh HaShanah — the final tefillah (תפילה) of the outgoing year.

This is the last moment the departing year has to close itself. The principle of hakol holech achar hachitum (הכל הולך אחר החתום) — everything follows the seal — is a fundamental in the halachos of berachos (ברכות): an error at the chasimah (חתימה) of a berachah invalidates the berachah in a way that an error in its middle does not.[1] The klal carries beyond the technical halachah: the Ribbono Shel Olam (רבונו של עולם) judges a matter by how it ends. Whatever quality the departing year is finally to have — that quality is set in its closing minutes. The Mincha of Erev Rosh HaShanah is thus not the tail of one year but the chasimah upon which the entire year is judged.

The Skverer Rebbe on Barech Aleinu HaShanah HaZos

An oral tradition preserved through two generations places the weight of this Mincha in unmistakable relief.[2] Reb Yoel Gross of Miami, zichrono livrachah, the eldest einikel (אייניקל) of Reb Shaya’le Kerestirer, made a lifelong practice of davening far’n amud for the Mincha of Erev Rosh HaShanah. Each year, at the same point — the berachah of Barech Aleinu HaShanah HaZos (ברך עלינו השנה הזאת) — he broke into intense weeping. Asked why one should weep so hard over bless upon us this year when only minutes of the year remained, Reb Yoel replied that he had heard the same weeping in the same place from the previous Skverer Rebbe, Rav Yaakov Yosef Twersky, zecher tzaddik livrachah.[3] The Skverer Rebbe had taught: at the final Mincha, when a Jew weeps with the words barech aleinu ha’shanah ha’zos, he may be meraches (מרחיק) the entire outgoing year retroactively. The year is being sealed at that very hour; a single moment of authentic kavvanah can pull the whole year up with it into the seal.

הַמִּנְחָה הָאַחֲרוֹנָה שֶׁל הַשָּׁנָה הִיא הַחֲתִימָה עַצְמָהּ.
The final Mincha of the year is the seal itself.

Beneath the mesorah lies a lomdish question of some depth. Is the chasimah of the year a din b’zman (דין בזמן) — a fixed moment in time that stamps whatever was placed within it — or is it a din b’pe’ulah (דין בפעולה), an act that must be performed to close the year? On the first understanding, Reb Yoel’s tear is an outstanding maaseh caught at the last permissible instant. On the second, that tear is itself the pe’ulas ha’chasimah — the very act of sealing. In either case the operational conclusion is identical: this is the hour to invest.

II. Achos Ketanah — The Piyut of Transition

Between the Departing Year and the Incoming

Between the final Mincha of the outgoing year and the first maariv of Rosh HaShanah, the Sefardim (ספרדים) recite a short piyut (פיוט) that captures the seam with unusual precision — Achos Ketanah (אֲחוֹת קְטַנָּה). Its composition is attributed to Rabbeinu Avraham Chazan of Girona (רבינו אברהם חזן גירונדי), a figure of the Girona school active in the era of the Ramban (רמב"ן).[4] The paytan signed the composition through an acrostic of his name across the opening letters of the stanzas, closing with the formulaic chazak (חזק) traditional to the end of a piyut.[5]

Tichleh Shanah v’Killeloseha

The piyut takes its structural motto from the Gemara’s teaching[6] that the tochachah (תוכחה) of Ki Savo is read on the Shabbos before Rosh HaShanah in order that tichleh shanah v’killeloseha (תכלה שנה וקללותיה) — that the year, together with its curses, may end. Each stanza of Achos Ketanah catalogs a chapter of the tzaros of Klal Yisrael in galus (גלות) and closes with this refrain. The final stanza pivots:

תָּחֵל שָׁנָה וּבִרְכוֹתֶיהָ.
May the year and its blessings begin.

The klalos (קללות) petitioned to end are not only the material burdens of parnasah (פרנסה), health, and shalom bayis (שלום בית). They include the spiritual arur (ארור) attached by the Torah to specific aveiros — the shadow that clings to a Jew from a year’s worth of failures. Achos Ketanah begs that the corridor between the two years be swept clean, so that the incoming year opens with berachah on both planes.

III. Ledavid Mizmor and the Divine Name Cha"tach

The Segulah of the Arizal

The Arizal (אריז"ל) transmitted a well-known segulah for parnasah: the recitation of Ledavid Mizmor (לְדָוִד מִזְמוֹר), Tehillim 24, with kavvanah after maariv on the first and second nights of Rosh HaShanah and again on Yom Kippur, functions as a keli (כלי) that draws down shefa parnasah for the entire year.[7] The kapitel is one that the mispallel says often; on these nights it carries an unusual koach (כוח). In Belz the segulah is honored with a public gathering — the Ledavid Mizmor tish on the second night of Rosh HaShanah — where the mispallelim recite the entire mizmor together, pasuk by pasuk.

The Kabbalistic Name Cha"tach

The mekubalim (מקובלים) tie the segulah to the divine Name of parnasah, Cha"tach (חת"ך) — a Name embedded in two roots within tehillah:

  • In Ashrei, the final letters of פּוֹתֵחַ אֶת יָדֶךָ spell Cha"tach.
  • In Ledavid Mizmor, the opening letters of תֵּבֵל וְיֹשְׁבֵי בָהּ spell the same Name.[8]

Reciting Ledavid Mizmor on Rosh HaShanah is thus a plugging-in to a channel of shefa that the mispallel already touches every day in Ashrei without knowing it. And with the channel comes a responsibility: as the Ribbono Shel Olam is poseach es yadecha (פותח את ידך) toward His creatures, so the mispallel must, in his own parnasah, be masbia lechol chai ratzon (משביע לכל חי רצון). The segulah is not a bare technique; it is an avodah that pledges the recipient to imitate the Giver.

IV. The Chofetz Chaim on the Primordial Oath

The Difficulty in Ledavid Mizmor

The Chofetz Chaim (חפץ חיים), Rav Yisrael Meir HaKohen of Radin, whose yahrzeit is observed on this very date — 24 Elul[9] — was troubled by the peshat of the central pesukim of Ledavid Mizmor:

מִי־יַעֲלֶה בְהַר־ה' וּמִי־יָקוּם בִּמְקוֹם קָדְשׁוֹ׃ נְקִי כַפַּיִם וּבַר־לֵבָב, אֲשֶׁר לֹא־נָשָׂא לַשָּׁוְא נַפְשִׁי וְלֹא נִשְׁבַּע לְמִרְמָה׃
Who shall ascend the mountain of Hashem, and who shall stand in His holy place? He of clean hands and pure heart, who has not borne My soul in vain, nor sworn deceitfully.
Tehillim 24:3–4

Neki Kappayim — Cleanliness in Money

Neki kappayim (נקי כפים) is the classical matbea lashon of financial integrity — the hand that has taken nothing that does not belong to it. There is a certain sheine zach (a fine thing) in the very fact that Jews call with a shailah asking whether they must repay a given debt: the willingness to ask is itself the nekiyus that permits a mispallel to stand before the Ribbono Shel Olam and spread open his palms.

V’Lo Nishba L’Mirmah — The Oath Before Birth

But the clause v’lo nishba l’mirmah (ולא נשבע למרמה) — and has not sworn deceitfully — presents a difficulty. In the era of the Gemara, nedarim (נדרים) and shevuos (שבועות) were woven into ordinary life; today most Jews never make a formal shevuah at all. What then does the pasuk demand of the mispallel who is himself the ascender-of-the-mountain the pasuk describes?

To this the Chofetz Chaim brings a chiddush of considerable beauty. The Gemara in Niddah[10] teaches that before a child is born, a malach (מלאך) teaches the neshamah (נשמה) the entire Torah. Pure and spiritual, at home in the olam ha’ruchani (עולם הרוחני), the neshamah does not wish to descend into a physical body in a physical world; it would remain at the Kisei HaKavod (כסא הכבוד). But a body has been prepared for it, and it must enter. The chevlei leidah (חבלי לידה) that accompany the birth are, on this view, the physical trace of a spiritual struggle: the neshamah’s resistance to descent.

At the moment of descent the malach administers an oath: mashbi’in oso (משביעין אותו) — tehi tzaddik v’al tehi rasha (תהי צדיק ואל תהי רשע).[11] Be a tzaddik; do not be a rasha. The neshamah — knowing full well how difficult tzaddikus in this world will prove — takes the shevuah.

The chiddush of the Chofetz Chaim: ולא נשבע למרמה speaks of that shevuah. Every human being took it. To ascend the har Hashem is to live the life of the tzaddik one committed to before birth. Where a mispallel has, chas v’shalom, come short of that oath until now, the recitation of Ledavid Mizmor on Rosh HaShanah is the moment to be mekabel al atzmo to honor it from this point forward.

The chiddush is not a novelty imposed upon Chazal from without; it draws out what is already latent in the Gemara itself. The very sugya in Niddah that records the oath cites Tehillim 24:4 — asher lo nasa la’shav nafshi v’lo nishba l’mirmah — as the pasuk describing the one who has kept the oath.[12] The Chofetz Chaim thus renders explicit an association Chazal had already forged. This is the Chofetz Chaim’s classical derech: not innovation ex nihilo, but the drawing out of what the sugya itself contains — here converted into the working avodah of the lail Rosh HaShanah at the recitation of the Arizal’s segulah.

V. Shir HaMaalos MiMa’amakim — The Question of Hefsek

The Widespread Minhag and the Severity of the Hefsek

In the Aseres Yemei Teshuvah (עשרת ימי תשובה), a widespread minhag inserts Shir HaMaalos MiMa’amakim (שיר המעלות ממעמקים), Tehillim 130, between Yishtabach (ישתבח) and the berachah of Yotzer Or (יוצר אור). The minhag collides with a well-established rule: the seam between Yishtabach and Yotzer tolerates no hefsek (הפסק).[13] Nusach Ashkenaz (נוסח אשכנז), following the Vilna Gaon (הגר"א), does not insert the mizmor at this juncture.

The severity of a hefsek at this seam is illustrated by a striking comparison in the Rishonim: the mispallel who is mafsik is likened to the soldier sent home from battle. The Torah’s passage of the kohen mashuach milchamah (כהן משוח מלחמה) addressing the army[14] asks: mi ha’ish ha’yarei v’rach ha’levav (מי האיש הירא ורך הלבב) — who is the man who is fearful and fainthearted? One shitah in the Gemara[15] reads the phrase as referring to the soldier who fears the aveiros in his hand: me’aveiros she’beyado (מעבירות שבידו). A Jew burdened with aveiros cannot fight. The severity of a hefsek at Yishtabach-to-Yotzer is, in the classical simile, that of the same order.

The Dagul MeiRevava in Defense of the Minhag

The origin of the minhag is traced to the Arizal. The Noda BiYehudah (נודע ביהודה), Rav Yechezkel Landau, in his commentary on Shulchan Aruch known as the Dagul MeiRevava (דגל מרבבה),[16] mounts a defense grounded in a piece of testimony from the Rambam: in the older minhag Sefarad, the community was accustomed to recite Az Yashir (אָז יָשִׁיר) at precisely this seam — after Yishtabach and before Yotzer.[17] Since the Rambam testifies to that minhag as a legitimate ancient practice, the prohibition on hefsek at this seam cannot pertain to additions per se. What is forbidden is sichah beteilah (שיחה בטלה), idle chatter that has no place in the tefillah’s flow. Additional shevachim of the Ribbono Shel Olam — songs of praise structurally continuous with the enterprise of Pesukei d’Zimra — are of a different halachic category and are permitted. This derech is reinforced by other poskim, notably the Aruch HaShulchan,[18] who reads the sequence from the end of Pesukei d’Zimra into Yotzer as a continuous ascent, not as two hermetically separated units.

Wide swaths of Klal Yisrael have accepted the minhag. Nusach Ashkenaz, following the Gra, holds the line. The Mishnah Berurah records the minhag without a full haskamah.[19] A mispallel should follow the nusach of his community.

VI. Zochreinu L’Chaim and the Additions of the Aseres Yemei Teshuvah

The Halachic Problem

During the Aseres Yemei Teshuvah, four bakashos are inserted into the Shemoneh Esrei: Zochreinu L’Chaim (זכרנו לחיים) in the first berachah; Mi Kamocha Av HaRachamim (מי כמוך אב הרחמים) in the second; U’Chsov L’Chaim Tovim (וכתוב לחיים טובים) in Modim; and U’V’sefer Chaim (ובספר חיים) in Sim Shalom.

The Gemara in Berachos[20] establishes that in the first three berachos of shevach (שבח) and the final three of hoda’ah (הודאה) of the Shemoneh Esrei, one may not insert personal bakashos; requests belong exclusively to the middle berachos of bakashah. The four Aseres Yemei Teshuvah insertions are a takkanah of the Geonim (גאונים). The Vilna Gaon, according to the record of his practices preserved by his talmidim, held that the insertions altered the matbea she’tavu Chazal (מטבע שטבעו חז"ל).[21]

Three Terutzim and Their Weight

Three lines of justification for the takkanah are given in the Rishonim and Acharonim:

  1. Communal, not personal. The prohibition on bakashos in the outer berachos is a prohibition on tzarchei yachid (צרכי יחיד). Tzarchei tzibbur (צרכי ציבור) are exempted. The insertions are formulated in the plural — zochreinu, ka’svenu — and function as communal bakashos, not personal ones. This is the most direct reading of the Shulchan Aruch’s formulation[22] and the workmanlike defense of the takkanah.
  2. The character of the days. The Maharam of Rothenburg (מהר"ם מרוטנבורג)[23] holds that the very ofyan shel yamim — the specific character of the Yamim Nora’im — renders these bakashos appropriate for insertion in the outer berachos. On days when Klal Yisrael’s entire posture is oriented toward the gezar din, the request for life is not an intrusion upon shevach but a completion of it.
  3. Spiritual life, formulated as shevach. The strongest teretz proceeds from the language of the insertions themselves. The mispallel does not merely ask for biological continuance; he specifies l’maancha Elokim Chaim (למענך אלקים חיים) — for Your sake, O living G-d. The bakashah is thereby framed as a bakashah that is itself shevach: a request for life so that one may serve the King. In lomdish terms, this is the chiluk between a bakashah b’geder bakashah and a bakashah b’geder shevach.[24]

The three terutzim do not exclude one another. The first is the halachically cleanest and the one on which the Shulchan Aruch most naturally rests; the second supplies the ta’am proper to the days; the third supplies the deepest lomdish rationale by reclassifying the bakashah itself.

L’Chaim and La’Chaim — a Sharp Kefidah

The poskim, discussed at length in the Mishnah Berurah,[25] observe a kefidah gedolah (קפידא גדולה) that one must recite zochreinu l’chaim (זכרנו לחיים) with a sheva under the lamed — and not la’chaim (לחיים) with a patach. The mekubalim warn that on the Yom HaDin, when every word is combed by the mekatregim (מקטרגים), the patach vocalization may be misheard as lo chaim (לא חיים) — not for life. In these days the mispallel must be careful with every word he lets out of his mouth.

The Bach (ב"ח), Rav Yoel Sirkis,[26] raises a kashya: we say la’chaim tovim u’l’shalom every night in maariv and no one is medakdek. The kashya, however, appears to reflect a nusach in which the maariv phrase is pointed with a patach. In the ascendant nusach the phrase is vocalized l’chaim tovim with a sheva, in which case the difficulty dissolves. Where a competing nusach was current, the Bach’s question stands within its own nusach and admits no easy resolution; the accepted halachah, in any case, follows the sheva.

The Din of One Who Forgot Zochreinu

Because the four insertions are a takkanah of the Geonim and not a matter fixed in the Gemara itself, one who forgets zochreinu and has not yet reached Shem u’Malchus (שם ומלכות) of the chasimah returns to the point of insertion; once he has already uttered Baruch Atah Hashem, he does not go back.[27] This is to be contrasted with the din of HaMelech HaKadosh (המלך הקדוש), which is a change grounded in the Gemara itself and which carries stricter consequences.

VII. Avinu Malkeinu — Rabbi Akiva at the Drought

The Origin in Ta’anis

Following the chazaras ha’shatz of Rosh HaShanah, the tzibbur recites Avinu Malkeinu (אבינו מלכנו). The Gemara in Ta’anis[28] records its origin. A severe drought had struck; one chacham after another rose to daven and no rain came. Rabbi Akiva (רבי עקיבא) stood before the amud and called out:

אָבִינוּ מַלְכֵּנוּ, חָטָאנוּ לְפָנֶיךָ. אָבִינוּ מַלְכֵּנוּ, אֵין לָנוּ מֶלֶךְ אֶלָּא אָתָּה. אָבִינוּ מַלְכֵּנוּ, לְמַעַנְךָ רַחֵם עָלֵינוּ.
Our Father, our King, we have sinned before You. Our Father, our King, we have no King but You. Our Father, our King, for Your own sake have mercy upon us.

The rain fell at once. The Gemara supplies the ta’am: mipnei she’hu ma’avir al middosav (מפני שהוא מעביר על מדותיו) — Rabbi Akiva was one who overlooked his own honor when others slighted him, and in kind was answered from above.

The Gematria — Rabbi Akiva Yesado

The Rishonim preserve a striking remez that binds the origin narrative to the text as we have it. The gematria of the opening phrase — Avinu Malkeinu chatanu lefanecha (אבינו מלכנו חטאנו לפניך) — equals the phrase Rabbi Akiva Yesado (רבי עקיבא יסדו): Rabbi Akiva composed it.[29] The klal is well-known: a mechaber signs his piyut within the composition, most commonly in an acrostic. Rabbi Akiva signed his in gematria.

Correspondence with the Middle Berachos

A careful reading of the successive bakashos of Avinu Malkeinu reveals a correspondence — not one-to-one, but thematically striking — with the middle berachos of the weekday Shemoneh Esrei. The requests for chaim, for parnasah, for selichah, for geulah, for refuah, and for the sealing of the year of goodness recapitulate in Avinu Malkeinu the substance of what is asked in the middle berachos of an ordinary weekday Shemoneh Esrei. Two examples suffice: the requests for selichah and mechilah in Avinu Malkeinu parallel Selach Lanu (סלח לנו); the requests for parnasah parallel Bareich Aleinu. This correspondence is drawn out in the Abudraham and the Kol Bo,[30] and explains a structural puzzle: on Rosh HaShanah the mispallel does not recite the weekday middle berachos of bakashah, and yet the day is the Yom HaDin on which the year’s parnasah, refuah, and selichah are decreed. It is through Avinu Malkeinu — recited immediately after the chazaras ha’shatz — that these bakashos reach their proper expression on the day itself.

On Shabbos, when personal bakashos are not voiced in the Shemoneh Esrei, Avinu Malkeinu is omitted; when Rosh HaShanah falls on Shabbos, Ashkenazim omit it (the Sefardim retain a shortened version). On a weekday Rosh HaShanah, it is central.

VIII. The Thread Through the Sections

The sections traversed — the final Mincha, Achos Ketanah, Ledavid Mizmor with its embedded Name of parnasah, the primordial oath at the heart of Ledavid Mizmor, the seam of Shir HaMaalos MiMa’amakim, the insertions of the Aseres Yemei Teshuvah, and Avinu Malkeinu of Rabbi Akiva — are not disparate topics grouped by the accident of proximity. They trace a single motion: the closing of one year and the opening of the next, section by section, from the last minutes of Elul through the bakashos that carry the mispallel to the chasimah of the new year.

What binds them is the klal of the first section: hakol holech achar hachitum. Every chasimah counts — the chasimah of the departing year at the final Mincha, the chasimah of a berachah of the Aseres Yemei Teshuvah when the mispallel must say l’chaim and not la’chaim, and the chasimah of the new year that will be sealed on Yom Kippur. Between them stands the mispallel, opening his palms in neki kappayim, remembering the shevuah that his own neshamah once took, and joining the words of Rabbi Akiva at a distant drought to his own drought of merit as he stands before the King.

The Arizal on Ledavid Mizmor as a segulah for parnasah. — The mesorah traces to the writings of Rav Chaim Vital in Shaar HaKavvanos and Peri Etz Chaim (Shaar Rosh HaShanah); it is codified in Kaf HaChaim to Orach Chaim 582 and in the Yalkut Yosef. The mizmor is specifically Tehillim 24, which opens with the words Ledavid Mizmor (as distinct from other mizmorim that open Mizmor l’David). ↩
  8. The Divine Name Cha"tach — rashei teivos and sofei teivos. — The rashei teivos / sofei teivos constructions upon this Name appear across the kabbalistic liturgical literature. The standard citation for the Cha"tach hidden in the sofei teivos of poseach es yadecha is Shelah HaKadosh in Maseches Tamid, drawing on the writings of the Arizal; see also the Magen Avraham on Orach Chaim 51:7 on kavvanah in poseach es yadecha. The parallel rashei teivos reading in Tevel v’yoshvei bah is preserved in later kabbalistic collections including the Kaf HaChaim; it is less widely attested than the poseach es yadecha remez. ↩
  9. The Chofetz Chaim — 24 Elul. — Rav Yisrael Meir HaKohen of Radin (1839–1933), whose yahrzeit falls on 24 Elul, author of Chofetz Chaim, Shmiras HaLashon, Mishnah Berurah, and Machaneh Yisrael. The hilula of a gadol whose life-work included the Mishnah Berurah’s guidance for the very tefillos under discussion is not incidental to a shiur on the machzor. ↩
  10. Niddah 30b — the malach teaches the neshamah in utero. — Darash Rabbi Simlai: L’mah ha’vlad domeh bi’mei imo? … U’melamdin oso kol ha’Torah kulah. The Gemara continues that at birth a malach strikes the child on the mouth, causing forgetfulness of what was learned. The oath of tehi tzaddik is on the same daf. ↩
  11. Mashbi’in oso — tehi tzaddik v’al tehi rasha. — Niddah 30b, immediately following the account of the Torah taught to the neshamah: U’mashbi’in oso: tehi tzaddik v’al tehi rasha. Va’afilu kol ha’olam kulo omrim lecha tzaddik atah — heyeh b’einecha k’rasha. ↩
  12. The pasuk in Ledavid Mizmor already cited in the Niddah sugya. — Niddah 30b brings Tehillim 24:4 — asher lo nasa la’shav nafshi v’lo nishba l’mirmah — as the pasuk descriptive of the one who has upheld the primordial oath. The linkage of the shevuah to Ledavid Mizmor is thus already in Chazal; the Chofetz Chaim’s contribution is the drawing out of this linkage as the operative avodah at the recitation of the mizmor on Rosh HaShanah. Compare the Nefesh HaChaim, Shaar I, and the Mesillas Yesharim, opening chapters, on the tachlis established for the neshamah prior to its descent; the same derech animates the Ramchal’s Daas Tevunos on why the neshamah must forget after birth if the aim is to remember. The Chofetz Chaim’s reading is a derech derush layered on top of the classical peshat of the Rishonim (Rashi, Ibn Ezra, Radak, Rabbeinu Bachya), who read shevuas shav in the standard peshat-sense of ordinary shevuos; the two layers coexist. ↩
  13. The severity of hefsek between Yishtabach and Yotzer. — Primary sources: Yerushalmi Berachos ch. 1; discussions in the Rashba and the Mordechai; codified in Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chaim 54:3, and elaborated in the Mishnah Berurah ad loc. ↩
  14. Devarim 20:8 — mi ha’ish ha’yarei v’rach ha’levav. — Part of the address of the kohen mashuach milchamah to the army before battle. ↩
  15. Sotah 44a — me’aveiros she’beyado. — The Gemara records a machlokes tannaim: one shitah holds that ha’yarei v’rach ha’levav refers to actual fear of battle; a second holds that it refers to fear of one’s own aveiros. The stricter reading — me’aveiros she’beyado — supplies the classical simile applied by the Rishonim to the severity of a hefsek between Yishtabach and Yotzer. A Prague chapter in the annals of the Haskalah, in which a play was staged mocking this shitah, elicited the well-known retort attributed to the Chofetz Chaim and to Rav Chaim Brisker: the play was left unfinished — had it been completed, the two gedolim who did not exit the field would have been shown to have won the war. ↩
  16. The Noda BiYehudah’s Dagul MeiRevava. — Dagul MeiRevava is Rav Yechezkel Landau’s commentary on the Shulchan Aruch. His treatment of the hefsek question at the seam of Yishtabach-Yotzer appears in Orach Chaim in the vicinity of siman 51/54. The critical move is the halachic distinction between sichah beteilah (prohibited) and shevachim continuous with the Pesukei d’Zimra flow (permitted). ↩
  17. The Rambam and the Sefarad minhag of Az Yashir after Yishtabach. — The Rambam’s codification of the nusach appears in Hilchos Tefillah ch. 7, with editorial variants noted across the printed mehaduros; the seder ha’tefillah appended to Sefer Ahavah is a further locus of testimony to the sequence of pieces around Yishtabach. The Sefardic-minhag testimony of Az Yashir at this juncture is what the Dagul MeiRevava takes as his primary raiya. Cited here as the Dagul MeiRevava brings it; the precise Rambam locus repays checking against a critical edition. ↩
  18. Aruch HaShulchan, Orach Chaim 54:4 — the continuity of shevachim into Yotzer. — The Aruch HaShulchan reads the trajectory from the concluding shevachim of Pesukei d’Zimra through Yishtabach into Yotzer as a single continuous ascent, not two hermetically-sealed units; on this reading, shevachim occupying that transition are structurally proper to it. A related line is developed by the Eshel Avraham of Butchatch. ↩
  19. Mishnah Berurah to Orach Chaim 54 — the minhag recorded without full endorsement. — The Mishnah Berurah notes the minhag of inserting Shir HaMaalos MiMa’amakim at this seam during the Aseres Yemei Teshuvah but does not offer a full haskamah, which is why Nusach Ashkenaz, following the Gra, does not adopt it. ↩
  20. Berachos 34a — no bakashos in the first three or last three berachos. — Amar Rav Yehudah: L’olam al yish’al adam tzerachav lo b’shalosh rishonos v’lo b’shalosh acharonos, ela ba’emtza’iyos. Codified in Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chaim 112:1. ↩
  21. The Vilna Gaon on the Aseres Yemei Teshuvah insertions. — Reports transmitted by talmidim of the Gra (see Ma’aseh Rav) place him at reservation regarding the insertions on the grounds that they altered the matbea she’tavu Chazal. The exact scope of his practice — whether he refrained from reciting them or recited them b’lis breirah — is transmitted with some variation; the safer formulation is that he held them with reservation, not that he omitted them outright. ↩
  22. The tzarchei tzibbur teretz — Shulchan Aruch 112:2. — The Shulchan Aruch’s formulation limits the prohibition of bakashos in the outer berachos to tzarchei yachid; tzarchei rabbim are permitted. This is the most direct halachic defense of the takkanah. See also Mishnah Berurah to Orach Chaim 112 for the practical application. ↩
  23. The Maharam of Rothenburg — the ofyan shel yamim teretz. — Rabbeinu Meir b’Rabbi Baruch of Rothenburg, in his teshuvos on the Yamim Nora’im, argues that the character of the days themselves generates the heter for the insertions. Cited by later poskim as the classical Rishonim-level defense of the takkanas ha’Geonim. ↩
  24. The chiluk between bakashah b’geder bakashah and bakashah b’geder shevach. — The Brisker-style formulation. Analogous to the Rishonim’s classification of she’eilas geshamim in Bareich Aleinu as belonging in gemara-terms to the material of gevuros. The precise l’maancha Elokim Chaim clause reframes the whole bakashah as a request for life in order to serve, converting the utterance from bakashah to shevach. See also Shulchan Aruch HaRav, Orach Chaim 112, for a related treatment. ↩
  25. Mishnah Berurah to Orach Chaim 582 — l’chaim versus la’chaim. — Mishnah Berurah 582:7 addresses the kefidah of l’chaim (sheva) rather than la’chaim (patach), warning against the latter. The concern of lo chaim traces back through the Abudraham and the mekubalim. ↩
  26. The Bach’s kashya from maariv. — Rav Yoel Sirkis in the Bayis Chadash on Orach Chaim raises the kashya from the phrase l’chaim tovim u’l’shalom in hashkiveinu: if the daily practice were la’chaim and no one is medakdek, the special kefidah on Rosh HaShanah would be difficult to sustain. The kashya presupposes a specific vocalization of the maariv phrase; the accepted nusach uses sheva, and on that reading the kashya dissolves. Where the kashya holds within its own nusach, it stands without a full resolution. ↩
  27. Forgot Zochreinu: the din of returning. — Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chaim 582:5. Since the addition is a takkanah of the Geonim and not from the Gemara, if one has not yet said Shem u’Malchus of the chasimah he returns; if he has already uttered Baruch Atah Hashem, he does not. Contrast Shulchan Aruch 582:1 for HaMelech HaKadosh, which appears in the Gemara itself and is halachically stricter. ↩
  28. Ta’anis 25b — Rabbi Akiva at the drought. — Maaseh b’Rabbi Eliezer… v’lo na’anu. Yarad l’fanav Rabbi Akiva v’amar: Avinu Malkeinu, chatanu lefanecha. Avinu Malkeinu, ein lanu Melech ela Atah. Avinu Malkeinu, l’maancha rachem aleinu — v’yardu geshamim. The Gemara supplies the ta’am: mipnei she’hu ma’avir al middosav. ↩
  29. The Rishonim on Rabbi Akiva yesado — the gematria. — The gematria of Avinu Malkeinu chatanu lefanecha equalling Rabbi Akiva yesado is cited as a remez in the classical liturgical commentators — the Abudraham, Kol Bo, and related early collections. The signature-in-composition klal is standard among paytanim; the gematria variant here is the unusual form. Presented in the spirit in which it is transmitted — as a widely-cited remez. ↩
  30. Abudraham and Kol Bo on the structure of Avinu Malkeinu. — The Abudraham and Kol Bo draw out the correspondence between the successive bakashos of Avinu Malkeinu and the middle berachos of the weekday Shemoneh Esrei. The Gra, in Shenos Eliyahu on Avinu Malkeinu, structures the composition against thematic groupings rather than a strict one-to-one correspondence with the weekday berachos — a slightly different shitah that leaves the underlying insight intact: the day of the gezar din requires the substance of the weekday bakashos, even where their outer form is displaced by the day’s special berachos. ↩
Sefer Edition of Rabbi Ephrati’s Sunday-morning shiur on Tefillah, recorded 6 September 2026 (24 Elul) — second of three discourses on the machzor in the weeks approaching Rosh HaShanah 5787, delivered on the yahrzeit of the Chofetz Chaim. This edition sequences the sections along the arc that closes one year and opens the next; converts the Chofetz Chaim’s reading of v’lo nishba l’mirmah into the operative avodah at the recitation of Ledavid Mizmor; deepens the Dagul MeiRevava’s defense of Shir HaMaalos MiMa’amakim with the further leg of the Aruch HaShulchan; weighs the three terutzim on Zochreinu; addresses the Bach’s kashya in its nusach-conditional force; and closes with Avinu Malkeinu’s structural correspondence to the weekday middle berachos as it appears in the Abudraham and Kol Bo.
