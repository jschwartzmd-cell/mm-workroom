Kaf-Hei Elul, Rosh HaShanah on Shabbos, and the Battle over the Piyutim
Studies in the Machzor of Rosh HaShanah — Third Discourse
25 Elul  ·  The Eve of Rosh HaShanah 5787  ·  Sefer Edition

The last light of Elul falls on the day ר"א assigns to בראשית and on which Nechemiah closed the wall of ירושלים. The mispallel who takes the day seriously is already inside the מחזור.

I. Kaf-Hei Elul — Three Anchors

The Day of Bereishis

The 25th of Elul carries an unusual density. According to the shitah of Rebbi Eliezer (רבי אליעזר) in Rosh HaShanah,[1] the world was created in Tishrei. Adam HaRishon (אדם הראשון) was formed on the sixth day of Creation, which is Rosh HaShanah itself; counting the six days of maaseh bereishis back from that anchor places the first day of Creation — bereishis bara Elokim (בראשית ברא אלקים) — on the 25th of Elul. The dating is a derivation from the Gemara rather than an explicit statement in the sugya, but it is the fixed mesorah that governs the day.

Rashi (רש"י) on the opening pasuk cites the midrash: the world was created bishvil Yisrael she’nikreu reishis (בשביל ישראל שנקראו ראשית).[2] The first day of Creation and the coronation-day of the King three days later thus rest on the same tachlis: the Ribbono Shel Olam (רבונו של עולם) creates a world for the sake of the am whose keser malchus will crown Him at the beginning of every year.

The Wall of Nechemiah

The pasuk in Nechemiah records that the wall of Yerushalayim (ירושלים) was completed on this very date: va’tishlam ha’chomah b’esrim v’chamishah la’Elul, la’chamishim u’shnayim yom (ותשלם החומה בעשרים וחמשה לאלול לחמשים ושנים יום).[3] Fifty-two days of communal avodah against opposition sealed the city’s protection on the 25th of Elul — a communal-historical anchor to the day that matches the day’s cosmological load. On Kaf-Hei Elul the physical enclosure of Yerushalayim was finished; on Rosh HaShanah, three days later, the spiritual enclosure of Klal Yisrael for the coming year begins.

A Hint of the Geulah

A tradition transmitted in the name of the Zohar at the end of Balak[4] associates Kaf-Hei Elul with the fall of malchus Romi (מלכות רומי) and the opening of the geulah (גאולה). The eschatological content is unmistakably in the Zohar; the specific calendar peg to Kaf-Hei Elul is preserved in the later chassidic and kabbalistic transmission of that Zohar. In whichever form it is received, the day is treated as one on which the door of the geulah stands slightly ajar — a fitting hachanah to the days on which the King is to be coronated.

II. Avinu Malkeinu — Harugim and Tevuchim

Two Consecutive Lines

Avinu Malkeinu (אבינו מלכנו), whose origin at the ta’anis geshamim of Rabbi Akiva was traced in the previous discourse and signed in gematria by the composer[5] — Avinu Malkeinu chatanu lefanecha equalling Rabbi Akiva yesado — contains two consecutive lines that appear to say the same thing:

אָבִינוּ מַלְכֵּנוּ עֲשֵׂה לְמַעַן הֲרוּגִים עַל שֵׁם קָדְשֶׁךָ.
אָבִינוּ מַלְכֵּנוּ עֲשֵׂה לְמַעַן טְבוּחִים עַל יִחוּדֶךָ.
Our Father, our King, act for the sake of those slain for Your holy Name. Our Father, our King, act for the sake of those slaughtered for Your Oneness.

Why the doubling? What does harugim al shem kodshecha (הרוגים על שם קדשך) add to — or subtract from — tevuchim al yichudecha (טבוחים על יחודך)?

The Pshevarsker Rebbe on the Banks of the Danube

A pshat preserved in the name of the Pshevarsker Rebbe (הרבי מפשעווארסק)[6] maps the two lines onto two madreigos of mesirus nefesh whose distinction became grotesquely literal in the last century. On the banks of the Danube, Yidden were lined up while reciting Shema (שמע). The bullets moved down the line. Some were shot mid-pasuk, mouth open on the Shem but the word echad (אחד) not yet pronounced — killed al Shem Kadsho, on the Name of Kedushah alone, without the yichud that completes it. These are the harugim al Shem kodshecha. Others reached the word echad, articulating the yichud Hashem, and were shot in the act of pronouncing it. These are the tevuchim al yichudecha.

Two madreigos, two lines in Avinu Malkeinu. The composition is old; the framework that supplies the peshat is of our own generation. The mispallel who reaches these two lines on Rosh HaShanah 5787 — while the emotional distance to that mesirus nefesh is not yet great — is asked to hold both images and to say the two lines as they were separately intended.

Before the events of the last several years the mispallel could bring a great-grandfather to mind at these two lines. He now brings all of them.

III. Fasting Past Chatzos on Rosh HaShanah She’Chal B’Shabbos

The Halachic Setting

Rosh HaShanah 5787 falls on Shabbos — the first day of Yom Tov and Shabbos together, followed by the second day of Yom Tov. This creates a halachic pressure most mispallelim underweight: ta’anis b’Shabbos (תענית בשבת) past chatzos (חצות) violates the chiyuv of oneg Shabbos (עונג שבת).[7] On an ordinary Shabbos this is a non-issue — kiddush and the seudah are complete long before chatzos. On Rosh HaShanah, when chazaras ha’shatz carries the full seder of Malchuyos, Zichronos, and Shofros, Birkas Kohanim (ברכת כהנים), and the piyutim that girdle them — a mispallel can find himself in shul at chatzos with nothing yet in his stomach.

The Question of Drinking Before Tefillah

The Shulchan Aruch prohibits eating and drinking before Shacharis.[8] The Acharonim discuss the status of water, of coffee, and of the shiur at which the prohibition attaches. A machlokes also exists whether the issur of a Shabbos fast past chatzos is de’oraisa — a violation of oneg Shabbos which the Ramban counts among the Torah’s obligations — or d’rabbanan.[9] Even on the softer view the issur retains real weight; on the harder view the mispallel who fasts past chatzos to preserve a chumra of not drinking before tefillah is trading a de’oraisa against an issur that most poskim read as d’rabbanan. The psak falls in an obvious direction: drink.

A bochur once came to the Minchas Elazar (מנחת אלעזר) — Rav Chaim Elazar Spira of Munkacs — with the chumra of never drinking before davening, even on Shabbos Rosh HaShanah. The Minchas Elazar himself brought in a tray with coffee, milk, and sugar. The chumra of not drinking before tefillah is not worth the issur chamur of a Shabbos fast past chatzos.[10]

IV. Tears on the Yom HaDin

The Shulchan Aruch and the Arizal

The Shulchan Aruch[11] rules that weeping on Shabbos is generally forbidden, on the ground that it negates oneg. The exception is tears from which the weeper derives hanaah (הנאה) — the tear that closes the heart around what has been felt is itself a form of oneg and is permitted. The Arizal (אריז"ל) taught that the avodah of Rosh HaShanah is not weeping. The day is one of eimas ha’din (אימת הדין) fused with bitachon (בטחון); the composure appropriate to the Yom HaDin is not the composure of Tisha b’Av. Uncontrolled tears — the neshamah (נשמה) sensing the din on its own — are of a different order and are welcomed.[12]

A Remez in the Tefillah

A compact remez preserves the paradox in the tefillah itself. In the piyut u’vchein tzaddikim yiru v’yismachu, the phrase b’Shimcha yagilun (בשמך יגילון) — they shall exult in Your Name — carries the roshei teivos that spell bechiyah (בכיה). The joy at the coronation of the King on the Yom HaDin and the tear at the eimas ha’din are the same motion of the nefesh; the paytan planted the remez to mark it.[13]

The Reishis Chochmah — a Tear on the Forehead

The Reishis Chochmah[14] preserves a practice: take a tear of teshuvah from the eye and place it on the forehead. The forehead is the place where the tzitz of the Kohen Gadol once bore the Shem; a tear placed there becomes a keli of kapparah. Whether the practice is retained when Rosh HaShanah falls on Shabbos — where the physical wiping of a tear may raise a chashash of memarei’ach or of michak — is a matter to consult with a local Rav; the Arizal’s permission for uncontrolled tears from eimas ha’din stands regardless. Performative or willed weeping is not encouraged even on a weekday Rosh HaShanah; a fortiori not when the day falls on Shabbos.

V. Sleep on the Yom HaDin

The Shulchan Aruch[15] discourages sleep on Rosh HaShanah morning: mi she’yashen b’Rosh HaShanah ba’yom mazalo yashen — his mazal sleeps with him at the head of the year. The Shulchan Aruch itself qualifies the concern with the phrase aval im yashen achar chatzos ein kepeidah — after chatzos there is no objection. The Arizal and the Vilna Gaon converge on this reading: the mazal-concern attaches to the morning specifically, not to the whole day.[16] The Mishnah Berurah[17] presses the point further from an avodah-standpoint: idle awake time is halachically indistinguishable from sleep for purposes of the mazal concern; a mispallel who needs a nap after chatzos in order to daven Musaf or the afternoon tefillah with kavvanah is better off asleep than sitting idle. A brief nap after chatzos, when required for koach, is not a chumrah worth defending against.

VI. Lamnatzeach Livnei Korach and the Absolute Hefsek of the Shofar

The Sevenfold Recitation

Before the tekios, a widespread minhag traced to the Arizal is to recite Lamnatzeach Livnei Korach mizmor (למנצח לבני קרח מזמור) — Tehillim 47 — seven times.[18] The number seven corresponds to the sevenfold naming of Elokim in the mizmor, each recitation cutting a shaar of the Satan’s prosecution. It is worth counting the Names once in a printed Tehillim before Rosh HaShanah in order to say the mizmor with the arithmetic in view.[19]

The Priority of the Mitzvah over the Minhag

The minhag is beloved but recent in the historical scale; the older machzorim of Rodelheim and Heidenheim do not print the sevenfold recitation at all. What is not recent is the issur hefsek (איסור הפסק) between the berachah of lishmoa kol shofar (לשמוע קול שופר) and the fulfillment of the mitzvah. Hearing shofar is de’oraisa; a hefsek between berachah and kiyum is treated with the strictness of a hefsek between birkas ha’mitzvah and mitzvah generally, with implications extending, in some shittos, to whether the berachah must be repeated.[20] The operational rule is unambiguous: if the ba’al tokea (בעל תוקע) has begun the berachos and the mispallel has not yet finished the seventh recitation of Lamnatzeach, he stops mid-mizmor. The Aruch HaShulchan[21] puts it in his own idiom: no minhag from the Arizal displaces a mitzvas asei de’oraisa.

VII. Hataras Nedarim and Mesiras Moda’ah

The Erev Rosh HaShanah Practice

On Erev Rosh HaShanah, the mispallel performs hataras nedarim (הַתָּרַת נדרים) before three — a beis din of ordinary Yidden suffices — using the nusach printed in every machzor.[22] Two levels of reflection belong to the moment. A promise made in the outgoing year to a shul, to tzedakah, to a chavrusa or a family member, that has not yet been fulfilled and was not framed bli neder (בלי נדר), carries the weight of a neder. Hataras nedarim is not a substitute for paying what is owed. Where a mispallel owes money to the shul, he pays it; the hataras nedarim then addresses what remains.

The Mesiras Moda’ah for the Coming Year

After the hataras nedarim before the beis din, a private declaration may be made at home: mesiras moda’ah (מסירת מודעא) — a formal statement that any expression of intention issued in the coming year should not carry the status of a neder unless the word neder is explicitly said.[23] The Gemara in Nedarim[24] discusses this posture — ha’rotzeh she’lo yiskaymu nedarav kol ha’shanah. The same posture is what Kol Nidrei (כל נדרי) performs on the night of Yom Kippur; the two practices are the same instrument at the two ends of the Aseres Yemei Teshuvah. The mispallel who has said the mesiras moda’ah at home on Erev Rosh HaShanah reaches Kol Nidrei having already done, on a personal register, what Kol Nidrei is doing on a communal one.

VIII. The Ownership of Tefillah — Core Over Piyut, HaMelech from the Place

The Order of Priority

The instinct on Rosh HaShanah is to compress Pesukei d’Zimra, Birkas Kerias Shema, and Shemoneh Esrei in order to keep pace with the piyutim that accompany chazaras ha’shatz. The instinct is inverted. These are the core of the tefillah. Where the piyutim of chazaras ha’shatz must wait an extra two minutes so that the mispallel can finish his own Shemoneh Esrei with the kavvanah the day demands, they wait. The piyutim, whose halachic status will occupy the next chapter, are supplements; the berachos of the Amidah are the ikkar.

HaMelech HaKadosh from the Mispallel’s Own Place

A related chassidishe minhag: some shluchei tzibbur begin the opening word HaMelech (המלך) of the chazarah on Rosh HaShanah morning from their own place in the tzibbur, not from the amud.[25] The two moves — refusing to rush the core to reach the piyut, and letting the tzibbur, not the shatz, be the coronating body — are the same claim in two registers: the tefillah belongs to the mispallel and to the tzibbur. The shatz serves it; he does not own it. On the day of the coronation of the King, who does the coronating is not a minor question.

IX. The Battle Over the Piyutim

Origin in a Gezeirah

The centerpiece of the day’s discourse is a documentary discovery that reshaped the modern historical understanding of the machzor. From the Cairo genizah, published only at the end of the 19th century, was recovered the polemical epistle of Pirkoi ben Baboi (פרקוי בן באבוי), a talmid in the circle of Rav Yehudai Gaon (רב יהודאי גאון).[26] The document records the tradition that the piyutim originated in Eretz Yisrael under a gezeiras shmad (גזירת שמד) that had suppressed the standard nusach ha’tefillah. Yidden could not daven the regular berachos publicly; the paytanic form — metrical, acrostic, dense with signature patterns — concealed the halachic content of the day inside a poetic surface that a Yid could recite and that his neighbor understood but that stood outside the reach of the enforcement mechanism. The piyutim were, on this account, substitute-tefillah for a persecution era.[27]

The Geonic Critique

When the persecution ended and the standard nusach could be restored openly, the substitute did not withdraw — the tzibbur had grown attached to it. The great Geonim pushed to strip the piyutim back out:

  • Rav Hai Gaon (רב האי גאון, ca. 939–1038) held that piyutim inserted inside the berachos of Shemoneh Esrei constitute a hefsek.[28]
  • Rav Saadia Gaon (רב סעדיה גאון, 882–942) held that the matbea she’tavu Chazal (מטבע שטבעו חז"ל) is not to be altered from the outside. Rav Saadia himself, it should be noted, is the compiler of one of the earliest known siddurim and did compose piyutim of his own; his position on inserted piyutim inside the outer berachos of the Amidah is stricter than his general practice of piyut-composition, and the two facts are compatible.
  • The Rosh (רא"ש), cited by the Tur in Orach Chaim siman 68,[29] endorses the Geonic critique with respect to piyutim inside Birkas Kerias Shema and inside Shemoneh Esrei.
  • The Vilna Gaon follows the Geonic line, and his operational move — recorded in Ma’aseh Rav — is precise rather than blanket: piyutim that fall inside the berachos are moved outside them or omitted; piyutim that fall at neutral moments of chazaras ha’shatz are treated differently.[30]

The Defense — Shevach, Minhag, and the Maharil

The classical defense of the piyutim against the Geonic critique proceeds on two legs. The first is halachic: piyutim of pure shevach (שבח) — praises of the Ribbono Shel Olam without embedded bakashah — do not fall under the Geonic prohibition, which was aimed at inserted bakashos foreign to the berachah’s matbea. This is the same halachic engine that permits the mispallel to answer amen to a berachah he hears in the middle of Pesukei d’Zimra: continuous shevach is a different halachic category from foreign bakashah. The engine is developed at length in the Rishonim and codified in the Beis Yosef to Orach Chaim 68.[31]

The second leg is the leg of minhag. The Maharil (המהרי"ל), Rav Yaakov Moellin of the early 15th century, is the great codifier of the minhagei Ashkenaz and the most forceful voice against the alteration of established minhag. His argument is not merely institutional. A minhag accepted by klal Yisrael across generations acquires a halachic standing that is not undone by the discovery of a difficulty; the discovery obliges study of the minhag, not its abolition. The Maharil’s specific instruction with respect to the piyutim is remembered in three parts: learn them, understand what one is saying, correct grammar where copyists have introduced error — but do not alter the meaning and do not remove the piyut from the minhag.[32]

A maaseh transmitted with the Maharil closes the argument. The Maharil, in one year, removed a specific piyut from the tefillah of the community; his daughter died in that year, and he retracted, treating the loss as heaven’s response to the alteration. Whether the maaseh is offered as historical record or as parable, its halachic force is the same: on the question of minhag Ashkenaz, the Maharil holds the line.[33]

Where the Argument Rests

The two legs of the defense do not exhaust one another. The shevach-vs-bakashah distinction addresses the halachic engine; the Maharil’s minhag-argument addresses the sociological and metaphysical standing of the community’s established practice. The Geonic critique retains its own force — and the Nusach HaGra’s operational preference for keeping piyutim out of the berachos themselves is a legitimate expression of that force — without dislodging the minhag of Ashkenaz and Sefarad to retain the piyut structure of the machzor. A mispallel follows the nusach of his community; the halachic parshas ha’drachim is settled by minhag ha’makom, not by fresh judgment at the amud.

X. The Mystery of Rabbi Elazar HaKalir

The Question of Identity and Era

The spine of the Ashkenaz machzor is the corpus of Rabbi Elazar HaKalir (רבי אלעזר הקליר). Who he was, and when he lived, is contested in the Rishonim themselves. Rabbeinu Tam in Tosafos Chagigah 13a[34] identifies him with a Tanna — on some readings Rabbi Elazar b’Rabbi Shimon (רבי אלעזר בר" שמעון) — and rests the identification on a chain of mesorah ish mi’pi ish: transmitted, on his own testimony, from Rashi through Rabbi Meir. Later Rishonim including the Shibbolei HaLeket and Rabbi Yitzchak Ibn Ghayyat, and much of subsequent scholarship down to the modern academic study of piyut based on the Cairo genizah, place the paytan in Eretz Yisrael in the 6th or 7th century, some time after the Tannaim but before the rise of the classical piyutim of Ashkenaz.

The stakes of the question are more than biographical. If the Kalir was a Tanna, the Geonic critique that the piyutim are a post-Chazal substitute cannot straightforwardly apply to his corpus — the piyutim would be Chazal’s own composition. If the Kalir is a 6th- or 7th-century figure in Eretz Yisrael, the timing aligns with the persecution era to which Pirkoi ben Baboi traces the piyut form, and the Geonic critique bears more directly on the corpus. The Rishonim hold both positions; the tzibbur, on both, sings the Kalir at the machzor.

Legends and Their Weight

Colorful legends surround the Kalir. One tradition speaks of an insect — recorded in some versions as a bee, in others as a scorpion — stinging his tongue and thereby loosening the koach to compose. Another, from the Aruch, describes a piece of cake and a kameia associated with a fire that would burn around him as he wrote. Rabbeinu Tam transmits these traditions with the framing that he received them ish mi’pi ish from Rashi through Rabbi Meir; the halachic weight lies in the mesorah-chain rather than in the miracle detail, and it is on that chain that his identification of the Kalir as a Tanna rests.[35]

The Language of the Kalir

What is not contested is the character of the language itself. The Kalir’s Hebrew stretches to its limits: rare roots from the far corners of Tanach, dense acrostics, elaborate signature patterns, a lexicon that requires peirush at almost every stanza. It is the language that makes serious study of the machzor a lifelong project, and the Kalir’s corpus that gives the Ashkenaz machzor its particular density. A mispallel who wishes to daven the machzor with kavvanah begins each year by learning a few stanzas he has not yet unlocked.

XI. The Arc

The Kaf-Hei Elul of the day of Bereishis and of Nechemiah’s wall opens onto a Rosh HaShanah that is also a Shabbos. The mispallel enters carrying the two lines of Avinu Malkeinu with the Danube in his ear, the coffee already drunk against the issur of fasting past chatzos, the tear of the neshamah permitted where it comes on its own, a nap after chatzos if koach requires it, the seventh recitation of Lamnatzeach abandoned mid-mizmor if the ba’al tokea has begun, the hataras nedarim and the mesiras moda’ah already accomplished, the ikkar of the tefillah refusing to be rushed for the sake of the piyutim, and the great corpus of the Kalir — whose era is contested and whose language is difficult — sitting in the machzor open before him. The chasimah of the outgoing year happens at that Mincha of yesterday’s discourse; the chasimah of the incoming year lies ten days away. Between them stands the mispallel, whose avodah during these hours is what the machzor is for.

Even on the softer positions, the issur carries real weight. ↩
  10. The Minchas Elazar (מנחת אלעזר) on the bochur with the chumra. — Rav Chaim Elazar Spira of Munkacs (1868–1937), whose teshuvos Minchas Elazar comprise seven chelakim. The specific ma’aseh of the tray with coffee, milk, and sugar cubes for the oiberlender-bochur with the chumra not to drink before tefillah on Rosh HaShanah she’chal b’Shabbos is transmitted in Munkatch mesorah; a specific printed teshuvah is findable in the indexed Minchas Elazar corpus and would strengthen citation. The broader modern psak on drinking coffee before davening when tefillah will run past chatzos on Shabbos is discussed in Igros Moshe (Orach Chaim) and in the teshuvos of Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach. ↩
  11. The Shulchan Aruch on bechiyah b’Shabbos. — Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chaim 288:2 on the prohibition of fasting on Shabbos, with Rama and Mishnah Berurah ad loc. treating the collateral question of bechiyah where it negates oneg. Ta’anugim vs. tzaar is the halachic frame; tears from which the weeper derives hanaah fall outside the prohibition. ↩
  12. The Arizal on the avodah of Rosh HaShanah. — The Arizal’s derech on the day is preserved in Shaar HaKavvanos and in Peri Etz Chaim (Shaar Rosh HaShanah). The specific position that composed weeping is not the avodah, while uncontrolled tears from eimas ha’din are welcomed, is standard mesorah. Its pin against a specific Shaar HaKavvanos paragraph on Rosh HaShanah bears verification against a critical edition. ↩
  13. B’Shimcha yagilun — roshei teivos bechiyah. — The remez reads the roshei teivos of the phrase in the paragraph u’vchein tzaddikim of the kedushas ha’yom. The tradition is widely transmitted in the chassidishe literature on the machzor; a specific printed source would repay verification. ↩
  14. Reishis Chochmah — the tear on the forehead. — Rav Eliyahu de Vidas, Reishis Chochmah, 16th-century Tzfas. The practice of placing a tear of teshuvah on the forehead as a keli of kapparah, evoking the tzitz of the Kohen Gadol that bore the Shem, appears in the Shaar HaTeshuvah section. Specific perek reference bears verification. ↩
  15. Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chaim 583:2 — sleeping on Rosh HaShanah. — V’chen mi she’yashen b’Rosh HaShanah ba’yom mazalo yashen, aval im yashen achar chatzos ein kepeidah. The Shulchan Aruch’s own formulation includes the post-chatzos qualifier. ↩
  16. Arizal and Vilna Gaon — convergence on the post-chatzos permission. — Both traditions read the mazal-concern as attaching to the morning specifically. See Ma’aseh Rav for the Gra’s minhag. ↩
  17. Mishnah Berurah to Orach Chaim 583. — The Mishnah Berurah presses the point on avodah-grounds: sleeping in order to daven Musaf or the afternoon tefillah with kavvanah is preferable to idle awake time, since idle awake time is halachically equivalent to sleep for the mazal-concern. Yoshev battel k’yashen dami. ↩
  18. The Arizal on the sevenfold recitation of Lamnatzeach before tekios. — The minhag is traced to the Arizal via Shaar HaKavvanos and Peri Etz Chaim; codified in Machzor Vitry and in later widespread practice; discussed in Kaf HaChaim to Orach Chaim 585. ↩
  19. The count of divine names in Tehillim 47. — The kabbalistic ta’am for the sevenfold recitation invokes the sevenfold appearance of Elokim in the mizmor. The mizmor contains multiple divine Names, and different enumerations count differently — some include Melech or Elyon to reach seven, others count only explicit Elokim. Worth a literal count in a printed Tehillim before Rosh HaShanah to say the recitations with the arithmetic in view. ↩
  20. Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chaim 592:3 and Mishnah Berurah ad loc. — The Shulchan Aruch and Mishnah Berurah treat the hefsek question between the berachah of lishmoa kol shofar and the fulfillment of the mitzvah with the strictness of hefsek between birkas ha’mitzvah and mitzvah generally — an issur d’rabbanan with real consequences, including in some shittos the obligation to repeat the berachah. Talking after the initial tekios but before the Malchuyos/Zichronos/Shofros sequences of Musaf is a discussion in the Acharonim. ↩
  21. Aruch HaShulchan to Orach Chaim 585 / 592 — no minhag displaces the de’oraisa. — The Aruch HaShulchan spells out the priority in his own posek-idiom: the sevenfold Lamnatzeach is a beautiful minhag from the Arizal, but the mitzvah of shofar is de’oraisa and a hefsek between the berachah and the kiyum is not to be entertained. The Rodelheim and Heidenheim machzorim, which do not print the sevenfold recitation at all, are a historical corroboration. ↩
  22. Hataras nedarim on Erev Rosh HaShanah. — Standard minhag codified in the machzor and in the Kitzur Shulchan Aruch; done before three, with the nusach printed in every machzor. The general halachos of hataras nedarim are in Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh De’ah 228. ↩
  23. Mesiras moda’ah — the private declaration for future nedarim. — The private declaration performed at home — in some ashkenaz traditions on Erev Rosh HaShanah and in others as part of Kol Nidrei on Yom Kippur — that any future statement of intention shall not carry neder-status unless the word neder is explicitly said. Codified in Shulchan Aruch, Yoreh De’ah 211:1–2, with Rama noting that this is the operative function of Kol Nidrei. The question whether mesiras moda’ah for future nedarim requires edim constitutively (as in mesiras moda’ah for gittin and kiddushin) or whether the private declaration suffices is a machlokes in the Rishonim; the accepted practice is the private declaration. ↩
  24. Nedarim 23b — ha’rotzeh she’lo yiskaymu nedarav kol ha’shanah. — The Gemara in Nedarim supplies the source: ha’rotzeh she’lo yiskaymu nedarav kol ha’shanah, ya’amod b’Rosh HaShanah v’yomar: kol neder she’ani asid lidor yehi vateil. Some Rishonim, including the Rosh, tie the nusach of Kol Nidrei directly to this Gemara — nedarim she’ani asid lidor; Rabbeinu Tam famously argues that Kol Nidrei concerns past nedarim and revised the nusach accordingly. This is the classical machlokes Rishonim that lies behind the two placements of the mesiras moda’ah in the Ashkenaz liturgical year. ↩
  25. The chassidishe minhag — beginning HaMelech from the mispallel’s place. — Oral chassidishe minhag; a printed pin is not to be expected. The kavvanah: the tzibbur, not the shatz, coronates the Ribbono Shel Olam; the shatz’s role is to join what the tzibbur has already begun. Whether a shatz raised in a non-chassidishe shul is obligated to adopt the minhag is a minhag ha’makom question rather than a general halachic requirement. ↩
  26. Pirkoi ben Baboi and Rav Yehudai Gaon — the Cairo genizah polemic. — The polemical epistle of Pirkoi ben Baboi, transmitted in the Bavli-centered Geonic derech and treating minhagei Eretz Yisrael as remnants of the gezeiras shmad era, was recovered from the Cairo genizah and published in the scholarly literature only from the late 19th century onward — earlier Acharonim including Rabbi Akiva Eiger, the Ketzos HaChoshen, and the Chida did not know it. Pirkoi writes as a talmid in the circle of Rav Yehudai Gaon and invokes his authority. Modern academic scholarship debates whether Pirkoi is transmitting a verified teshuvah of Rav Yehudai or presenting an independent polemical case under Rav Yehudai’s haskamah. Either way, the historical picture the document establishes — that the Geonim understood the piyutim as a persecution-era substitute that no longer had halachic warrant — is corroborated independently by the direct teshuvos of Rav Hai and Rav Saadia. ↩
  27. The gezeiras shmad-origin narrative and the modern academic pushback. — The traditional understanding, preserved by Pirkoi and by the Geonic critique, is that piyutim in Eretz Yisrael developed as substitutes for the standard nusach during a period of Byzantine, and then early Muslim, gezeiros. Modern academic scholarship — associated preeminently with Ezra Fleischer — argues that piyut was primarily an aesthetic-liturgical development in its own right, not a persecution-driven substitute. Both frameworks appear in the received mesorah; the Pirkoi document is compatible with the older narrative, and the modern academic reading is compatible with the halachic reception the Rishonim gave to the piyut corpus. ↩
  28. Rav Hai Gaon on piyutim as hefsek. — Rav Hai Gaon (ca. 939–1038), the last of the great Geonim of Pumbedisa. His teshuvos on liturgical matters are cited by the Tur and the Beis Yosef as the strongest Geonic pushback against inserted piyutim; specific loci in Sha’arei Teshuvah and in Otzar HaGeonim. ↩
  29. The Tur, Orach Chaim 68 — citing the Rosh. — The Tur in Orach Chaim 68 collects the Geonic critique of piyutim inside Birkas Kerias Shema and cites the Rosh in agreement; the Beis Yosef ad loc. develops the discussion at length. ↩
  30. The Vilna Gaon — nuanced operational practice. — The Gra’s position, as recorded in Ma’aseh Rav and in the Aderes’s reports, is not a blanket rejection of piyut but a precise operational move: piyutim that fall inside the berachos of Kerias Shema or of Shemoneh Esrei are omitted or repositioned outside the berachos; piyutim that fall at neutral moments are treated differently. The implementation in different kehillos that took Nusach HaGra varied; the halachic sevara is uniform. ↩
  31. The shevach-vs-bakashah distinction — Beis Yosef to Orach Chaim 68. — The classical halachic engine for the defense of piyutim against the Geonic critique. Continuous shevach is a different halachic category from foreign bakashah; the parallel is the permission to answer amen to a berachah heard in the middle of Pesukei d’Zimra. The engine is developed in the Rishonim around Orach Chaim 51 and 68 and codified in the Beis Yosef ad loc. ↩
  32. The Maharil — learn the piyutim, correct grammar, do not alter meaning or omit. — Rav Yaakov Moellin (ca. 1360–1427), Sefer Maharil (Minhagim). The Maharil is the great codifier of the minhagei Ashkenaz and issues repeated strong rulings against altering communal practice, including on liturgical matters. The three-part instruction — learn, correct grammar, do not alter meaning — is preserved in his responses to inquiries from various kehillos. Specific siman in the printed Sefer Maharil bears verification. ↩
  33. The Maharil ma’aseh — the removed piyut and the daughter. — The tradition that the Maharil, in one year, removed a specific piyut from the tefillah and that his daughter died in that year, prompting his retraction, is preserved as a foundational anecdote in the Ashkenaz mesorah on the sanctity of minhag. Whether transmitted as historical record or as parable, its halachic force in the mesorah is identical: on the question of minhag Ashkenaz, the Maharil holds the line and the community holds with him. ↩
  34. Tosafos Chagigah 13a — Rabbeinu Tam on the Kalir as a Tanna. — Tosafos Chagigah 13a, s.v. v’raglei ha’chayos, records Rabbeinu Tam’s identification of the Kalir with a Tanna and cites the mesorah ish mi’pi ish from Rashi through Rabbi Meir. The identification is contested by other Rishonim, including the Shibbolei HaLeket and Rabbi Yitzchak Ibn Ghayyat, and by modern academic scholarship, which places the Kalir in 6th- or 7th-century Eretz Yisrael on the basis of linguistic, historical, and Cairo-genizah-corpus grounds. The scholarly picture converges, in its dating, with the Geonic timing of the piyut-origin narrative. ↩
  35. The Aruch, the kameia, and the insect legend. — The Aruch preserves a tradition of a piece of cake and a kameia associated with the Kalir’s composition of piyutim, with a fire that would burn around him as he wrote. Rabbeinu Tam transmits the tradition with the framing ish mi’pi ish from Rashi through Rabbi Meir. The insect-on-the-tongue tradition is preserved in various forms — a common version has a bee, another has a scorpion; the received text bears verification. The halachic weight of the transmission is in the mesorah-chain, not in the miracle detail, and it is on the strength of that chain that Rabbeinu Tam’s identification of the Kalir as a Tanna rests. ↩
Sefer Edition of Rabbi Ephrati’s Monday-morning shiur on Tefillah, delivered on Labor Day, 25 Elul 5786, as the third and last of three preparatory discourses on the machzor before Rosh HaShanah 5787. This edition sequences the material as the ten preparatory halachos and madreigos of the mispallel entering a Rosh HaShanah that falls on Shabbos, and closes with the historical arc of the piyutim from Pirkoi ben Baboi through the Maharil and the contested identity of Rabbi Elazar HaKalir. Citations that in the source were partial — the Zohar’s date-peg, the Kalir insect tradition, the Minchas Elazar coffee-teshuvah, the position of the Vilna Gaon — are received here in their transmitted form with the caveat, historical or textual, appropriate to each.
