פּוּרִים שֶׁחָל בְּעֶרֶב שַׁבָּת קֹדֶשׁ

Purim Falling on Erev Shabbos Kodesh
Shiur of Rabbi Ephrati — Practical Halacha l’Ma’aseh
Recorded 9 March 2025  ·  Sefer Edition · Revised 30 August 2026

In a year in which Purim (פּוּרִים) falls upon erev Shabbos kodesh (עֶרֶב שַׁבָּת קֹדֶשׁ) — a calendar collision that visits the unwalled cities perhaps once in a generation — two valid derachim (דְּרָכִים) stand before us. The simpler is the ruling of the Rema (רְמָ"א): wash and eat the seudas Purim (סְעוּדַת פּוּרִים) before chatzos (חֲצוֹת). The older and more dramatic is the minhag (מִנְהָג) of the Meiri (מְאִירִי) and the Maharil (מַהֲרִי"ל) — “pores mapah u’mekadesh” (פּוֹרֵס מַפָּה וּמְקַדֵּשׁ) — beginning the seudah upon Purim and continuing it, seamlessly, into Shabbos. The present siman lays out the sources of each derech, the mechanics of the second, the bentshen question at the meal’s close, and the two great ma’asim — the Imrei Emes in Gur and Rav Yitzchak Isaac Sher in Yerushalayim — that transform the second derech from a curiosity into a l’chatchilah (לְכַתְּחִלָּה) option.

I. The Rarity of the Year

The luach (לוּחַ) sets Purim of the unwalled cities upon erev Shabbos only at long intervals; the case that occupies this siman will not present itself again to our generation for approximately twenty years. The rarity is itself pedagogically decisive: because most who read these halachos will not have practiced them in the recent past, the material must be laid out with the fullness a halacha l’ma’aseh reader requires.

The parallel case of Yerushalayim — where in such a year Shushan Purim (שׁוּשַׁן פּוּרִים) falls on Shabbos itself, generating the intricate halachos of Purim Meshulash (פּוּרִים הַמְשֻׁלָּשׁ) — is a distinct sugya (סוּגְיָא) and has its own literature. The present siman addresses the unwalled cities alone.

II. The Underlying אִסּוּר — Kevi’as Seudah on Erev Shabbos

The Shulchan Aruch (שֻׁלְחָן עָרוּךְ) in Orach Chaim siman 249[1] rules that one may not establish a seudah (סְעוּדָה) on erev Shabbos. The Gemara (גְּמָרָא) in Gittin 38a[2] is the conceptual root: two prominent families of Yerushalayim in the days of the Beis HaMikdash (בֵּית הַמִּקְדָּשׁ) were nichresu (נִכְרְתוּ) — wiped out from the world — and the sugya explains why. The first family, of great wealth, would gather each erev Shabbos at midday for a substantial family seudah; this consumed the tei’avon (תֵּאָבוֹן) that the seudas Shabbos (סְעוּדַת שַׁבָּת) required. The second stretched the Shabbos-day seudah until three or four in the afternoon, missing the shiur (שִׁעוּר) of Torah that filled the day’s remaining hours. Both were held accountable, and both were nichresu.

Two independent ta’amim (טְעָמִים) emerge from the Shulchan Aruch’s codification. The first is kevod Shabbos (כְּבוֹד שַׁבָּת) — the seudos of Shabbos are to be eaten with an unspent appetite, and any earlier seudah that would blunt that appetite is prohibited. The second, drawn by the Magen Avraham (מָגֵן אַבְרָהָם) from Rabbeinu Tam (רַבֵּינוּ תָּם), is that a heavy meal in the late afternoon of erev Shabbos displaces the hachanos (הֲכָנוֹת) that Shabbos requires: bathing, cutting nails, seeing to the household. The Mishnah Berurah (מִשְׁנָה בְּרוּרָה) accordingly directs that when a substantial seudah must be eaten on erev Shabbos, it is to be scheduled early in the morning; some Rishonim (רִאשׁוֹנִים) limit even that.

The distinction between the two ta’amim is not merely academic. When the Purim-day seudah continues, without a break, into the Shabbos seudah itself — the operation described below as “pores mapah u’mekadesh” — the first concern (kevod Shabbos, the unspent appetite for a separate seudas Shabbos) largely evaporates, because there is no separate later meal being displaced. The second concern (the hachanos) remains live, and the practical warnings in Section VIII — that the telephone be silenced, the muktzeh (מֻקְצֶה) set aside, and the household made ready — are doing structural work of the first order.

III. When the סְעוּדַת פּוּרִים Is Eaten in a Normal Year

The mitzvah (מִצְוָה), properly formulated, is to eat the seudas Purim in the day of Purim itself — not the preceding night and not, in the strict view, after the day’s shki’ah (שְׁקִיעָה) alone. The most rigorous of the poskim (פּוֹסְקִים) hold that the seudah is best eaten in the morning, before chatzos, with netilas yadayim (נְטִילַת יָדַיִם) taken a second time toward evening for the closing of the day. In practice, the universal minhag is to begin the seudah in the late afternoon, an hour or two before shki’ah, and to extend it into the night. This minhag is well founded: the daylight hours are consumed by mishloach manos (מִשְׁלוֹחַ מָנוֹת), matanos la’evyonim (מַתָּנוֹת לָאֶבְיוֹנִים), and kri’as ha’Megillah (קְרִיאַת הַמְּגִלָּה), and a substantial early seudah is difficult to arrange.

Already among the Rishonim the kedushah (קְדֻשָּׁה) of Purim is understood to extend before and after the day proper. Rabbeinu Simchah of Vitry (רַבֵּינוּ שִׂמְחָה מִוִּיטְרִי), a talmid (תַּלְמִיד) and nephew of Rashi (רַשִׁ"י), refers in his Machzor Vitry (מַחְזוֹר וִיטְרִי)[3] to the night preceding Purim as “leil shikorim” (לֵיל שִׁכּוֹרִים) — the night of the drinkers — recording that the yeshivos (יְשִׁיבוֹת) of Ashkenaz (אַשְׁכְּנַז) kept the Purim drinking already on the night before. The day’s seudah is the essential fulfillment; the kedushah of the day surrounds it.

IV. The Collision

These two halachos collide head-on in the year we describe. One may not establish a seudah on erev Shabbos; and the accepted time for the seudas Purim is precisely the hours of the late afternoon that the issur (אִסּוּר) forbids. What is a ben Torah (בֶּן תּוֹרָה) to do?

V. Derech Alef — The Ruling of the רְמָ"א

The Rema in siman 695[4] rules l’halacha (לְהֲלָכָה): in such a year, the seudas Purim is to be washed and eaten before chatzos. The Shelah HaKadosh (שְׁלָ"ה הַקָּדוֹשׁ), as brought by the Mishnah Berurah,[5] holds that even in an ordinary year the seudah before chatzos is preferable. Most do not follow this stringency in ordinary years; but in the year in question there is no dispute at all — once the chatzos hour is passed, the issur of siman 249 is fully in force, and the seudah must be earlier.

The seudah, l’chatchilah, is basar v’yayin (בָּשָׂר וְיַיִן) — meat and wine. Where an early meat seudah is not feasible for the household, some poskim permit discharging the obligation with a chalavi (חֲלָבִי) meal, though the stricter view retains the requirement of meat and wine. In practical outline, one who chooses this derech proceeds as follows: kri’as ha’Megillah and the mitzvos of mishloach manos and matanos la’evyonim are attended to as early as possible; the seudah is washed and begun before chatzos; bentshen (בֶּנְטְשֶׁן) is recited normally, with al ha’nissim (עַל הַנִּסִּים) in its place and without retzeh (רְצֵה); the balance of the afternoon is devoted to the hachanos for Shabbos; and the household prepares and eats a full, separate seudas Shabbos on Friday night. This is the simpler derech, valid without machlokes (מַחֲלֹקֶת), and for many families is the appropriate default.

VI. Derech Beis — The מַהֲרִי"ל and the Old מִנְהָג

The Standing of the Maharil in the Halacha of Ashkenaz

The Maharil — Rabbeinu Yaakov Moellin (רַבֵּינוּ יַעֲקֹב מוֹלִין) — is among the foundational Rishonim of Yahadus Ashkenaz (יַהֲדוּת אַשְׁכְּנַז). Born in Mainz (Magenza — מַגֶּנְצָא) circa 1365, he directed a vast yeshivah there for many years and produced a generation of talmidim whose piskei halacha (פִּסְקֵי הֲלָכָה) shape the Ashkenazic siddur and nusach (נֻסָּח) to this day. He was, as his talmidim attest, himself a ba’al menagen (בַּעַל מְנַגֵּן), and much of the nusach of Shabbos and Yom Tov as we sing it in the great communities of the West traces to him. He was niftar (נִפְטַר) in 1427 and is buried in the ancient Jewish cemetery of Worms (Vermayza — וֶרְמַיְזָא), where two and a half centuries later the Chavas Ya’ir (חַוּוֹת יָאִיר) would also be laid to rest. His era — the late fourteenth into the early fifteenth century in Ashkenaz — was one of unrelenting pera’os (פְּרָעוֹת) and expulsions, of which the gezerah (גְּזֵרָה) of Vienna in 1421 was among the sharpest; Hashem yerachem (ה’ יְרַחֵם).

The Rema, as is commonly observed, cites the Maharil more freely than any other single Rishon, for the Rema is the posek of Ashkenazic halacha and the Maharil is his chief rebbi muvhak (רְבִּי מֻבְהָק) in that mesorah (מְסוֹרָה). This makes the case at hand all the more striking.

The Teshuvah the Rema Did Not Adopt

The Maharil was asked by a talmid:[6] what shall we do when Purim falls on erev Shabbos? Are we bound to eat before chatzos? His answer, in outline, is no. One may wash for the seudah after chatzos — even at the ninth or tenth hour of the day — because the mitzvah of the seudas Purim is bound to Purim, and one may continue the seudah straight into Shabbos. His analogy is to a seudas bris milah (סְעוּדַת בְּרִית מִילָה) upon the eighth day, or a seudas pidyon ha’ben (סְעוּדַת פִּדְיוֹן הַבֵּן) upon the thirtieth day, that fall upon erev Shabbos: the poskim permit beginning such a seudah after chatzos, since the mitzvah is tied to the day. So too, he reasons, for the seudas Purim.

The analogy is the load-bearing move of the entire teshuvah (תְּשׁוּבָה), and it can be pressed in either direction. The seudos of bris and pidyon are widely treated as seudos reshus (סְעוּדוֹת רְשׁוּת) that acquire their chashivus (חֲשִׁיבוּת) only by their association with the mitzvah; the seudas Purim is a full chiyuv (חִיּוּב) in its own right, an integral limb of the day’s mitzvos. Read one way, the kal va’chomer (קַל וָחֹמֶר) is strong: all the more so a full chiyuv permits the extension. Read the other way, there is room to distinguish: perhaps precisely because seudas Purim is a chiyuv demanding its own integrity, it is less naturally fused into a Shabbos seudah that follows.

The Rema, faithful to the Maharil in nearly every other halacha, did not rule with him here. Why the Rema declined to adopt the teshuvah is not stated by him explicitly; three plausible readings suggest themselves. Perhaps he felt the analogy to bris and pidyon did not carry; perhaps he was reluctant to require of the average household the intricate mechanics of “pores mapah u’mekadesh”; perhaps he was uneasy with the kabbalistic reordering discussed below in Section X. In any event, the Rema’s silence does not overturn the Maharil’s ruling; it leaves it standing as an alternative derech for those who wish to follow it. The position was preserved through the generations: the Yad Ephraim (יַד אֶפְרַיִם) of Rabbi Ephraim Zalman Margaliyos (רַבִּי אֶפְרַיִם זַלְמָן מַרְגָּלִיּוֹת) records the Maharil’s ruling in his glosses on the Tanya Rabbasi (תַּנְיָא רַבָּתִי) — the early Ashkenazic halachic compendium of R’ Yechiel b. Yekusiel of Rome, not to be confused with the later Chabad work of the same name — and the Mishnah Berurah in siman 695 brings the ruling from there.

VII. The Mechanics of פּוֹרֵס מַפָּה וּמְקַדֵּשׁ

The Source in the Meiri and the Sugya in Pesachim

The Maharil’s teshuvah establishes the permission; the mechanics of the operation are worked out most fully by the Meiri — Rabbeinu Menachem HaMeiri (רַבֵּינוּ מְנַחֵם הַמְּאִירִי) — among the greatest of the chachmei Provence (חַכְמֵי פְּרוֹבַנְס), writing in Perpignan roughly a century before the Maharil. In his Beis HaBechirah (בֵּית הַבְּחִירָה) on Kesuvos 7a[7] he writes that “ani v’rabbim acherim” (אֲנִי וְרַבִּים אֲחֵרִים) — I and many others — followed an established masorah (מָסֹרֶת) of running the seudas Purim straight up against shki’ah, and, when Shabbos arrived in its midst, performing “pores mapah u’mekadesh”. His formulation places the minhag already kadum (קָדוּם) in his own day; the Maharil’s teshuvah, then, does not innovate the derech but authorizes and codifies a mesorah that had already stood for more than a century in the communities of Provence.

The structural din (דִּין) rests on the sugya in Pesachim 105[8]: Rebbi Yosi (רַבִּי יוֹסֵי) and Rebbi Yehudah (רַבִּי יְהוּדָה) dispute the case of one who began a seudah on erev Shabbos and wishes to continue it into Shabbos. Rebbi Yehudah requires the removal and replacement of the table, to mark visibly the new seudas Shabbos. The Shulchan Aruch in siman 271[9] follows Rebbi Yosi: no interruption of the seudah is required; instead, a cloth is spread over the bread — pores mapah — and kiddush (קִדּוּשׁ) is recited, the covered bread serving as the visible marker of the shinnuy (שִׁנּוּי) in the status of the meal. The bread is then uncovered and the seudah continues, now as a seudas Shabbos.

The Chakirah Beneath the Mechanics

A single chakirah (חֲקִירָה) underlies nearly every practical question that follows. Is the second portion of the meal a continuation of the seudas Purim, now bearing a kiddush-marker to acknowledge that Shabbos has entered? Or is it a seudas Shabbos in its own right, beginning within the frame of a meal already in progress? The Rebbi Yosi-Rebbi Yehudah machlokes can be understood in either direction, and the Meiri’s language leaves room for both. Almost every practical machlokes below — the requirement of lechem mishneh (לֶחֶם מִשְׁנֶה), the berachos (בְּרָכוֹת) of a latecomer, and the bentshen question of retzeh and al ha’nissim — is a facet of this deeper chakirah.

VIII. The Mechanics of the Meal — הֲלָכָה לְמַעֲשֶׂה

The following steps trace the practical shape of a seudah conducted al pi the Meiri and the Maharil. The clock references are given in generic terms; concrete times must be drawn from the local luach for the year and place in question (a sidebar at the close of the section illustrates with the Lawrence, New York times of 5785).

Before the Meal — The Mitzvos of the Day
Kri’as ha’Megillah, mishloach manos, and matanos la’evyonim are all bound to the day of Purim. On the year in question they must be discharged before shki’ah. Once Shabbos enters, the mitzvos of Purim cease; nothing is postponed to Motza’ei Shabbos, and no partial credit is available to one who did not complete them on the day.

1. Mincha — Early

Mincha (מִנְחָה) is davened early, at or shortly after mincha gedolah (מִנְחָה גְּדוֹלָה), before the seudah begins or at its very first stages, so that it is discharged before wine takes effect. Every congregation runs an early mincha on such an erev Shabbos; one goes.

2. Beginning the Seudah

The seudah is begun in the late afternoon, aimed to run for a full two hours or so before shki’ah. L’chatchilah the seudah of Purim is a seudas mere’im (סְעוּדַת מְרֵעִים) — a gathering of friends — and the household should be joined by others where feasible. Netilas yadayim, hamotzi (הַמּוֹצִיא), a seudah of meat and wine, divrei Torah (דִּבְרֵי תוֹרָה), and the customary mishteh (מִשְׁתֶּה) of the day.

3. Shki’ah — The Women Light and the Household Prepares

As shki’ah approaches, the women of the household step aside briefly to complete any remaining hachanos and to light the neros Shabbos (נֵרוֹת שַׁבָּת). Once a woman has lit, she has accepted Shabbos upon herself and may not eat further until kiddush has been made. She may light in the room of the seudah or in another room of the house, as her minhag and the practical situation require.

The men remain seated; the seudah is not interrupted. As shki’ah arrives, the challos (חַלּוֹת) are covered with a cloth — the visible pores mapah.

Stop Being a Weekday Jew
Before the bentshen, and certainly by the moment of shki’ah, every trace of weekday behavior must be set aside. The telephone is silenced and put away. Anything that is muktzeh is set out of reach; the children are checked for the same. Any last hachanos for Shabbos are completed. This is the point of transition that gives Rabbeinu Tam’s concern (Section II) its full weight, and it operates whether the bentshen is at seven at night or at ten. The Shabbos-ness of the second half of the meal is real; the household must be ready for it.

4. Kabbalas Shabbos

Kabbalas Shabbos (קַבָּלַת שַׁבָּת) is recited around the table. No minyan (מִנְיָן) is required; there are no berachos; the recitation is the words themselves, sung with the household. Where the head of the household prefers to walk to the shul (שׁוּל) for kabbalas Shabbos with the tzibbur (צִבּוּר), the Mishnah Berurah and the poskim[10] hold that this is not a hefsek (הֶפְסֵק): one has da’as (דַּעַת) from the start of the seudah to return and continue, and the meal is not interrupted. It is not the most l’chatchilah arrangement, but it is fully permissible.

5. Kiddush — Without Hagefen and Without Hamotzi

Here is the operation that gives the derech its distinctive character. A fresh cup of wine is poured and kiddush is recited — vayechulu (וַיְכֻלּוּ) and the berachah of kiddush ha’yom (קִדּוּשׁ הַיּוֹם). Borei pri ha’gafen (בּוֹרֵא פְּרִי הַגֶּפֶן) is not recited, because the berachah made earlier in the meal still covers this cup of wine; a fresh netilas yadayim is not taken and hamotzi is not repeated, for the same reason. The kiddush-wine is drunk and the meal resumes.

Latecomers
One who joins the meal only after kabbalas Shabbos — not present for the Purim-portion and not having made ha’gafen or ha’motzi earlier — makes both berachos when he is served, exactly as he would at any seudas Shabbos. The reason he makes fresh berachos where the original participants do not is precisely the chakirah above: for him, this is the beginning of a seudas Shabbos, without the covering continuity of the earlier Purim-portion.

6. Lechem Mishneh

Whether lechem mishneh is required at the kiddush is itself a machlokes, and it is a facet of the same chakirah: is the second half of the meal a seudas Shabbos in its own right, which would require lechem mishneh, or a continuation of the earlier seudah under a kiddush-marker, which would not? The prudent practice is l’chumra (לְחֻמְרָא): an additional challah is brought to the table before kiddush so that lechem mishneh is present. It bears emphasis that the reason no fresh hamotzi is recited (Section VIII.5) is unrelated to the lechem mishneh question; the two operate on different tracks and both are honored.

7. Eating a True Seudas Shabbos

The second portion of the meal must not be treated as an afterthought. At least a ke’zayis (כְּזַיִת) of bread is eaten, and real seudas Shabbos food is served — not merely dessert or dishes cleared from the Purim-portion. Whatever zemiros (זְמִירוֹת) the household ordinarily sings on Friday night are sung; the Shabbos-ness of the hour is honored in the ordinary ways.

8. Ma’ariv

After the seudah, or in some households mid-meal at an appropriate pause, ma’ariv (מַעֲרִיב) is davened.

A Local Timetable — Lawrence, N.Y., 5785
By way of concrete example, the times for the case-year of 5785 in Lawrence, N.Y. were: chatzos at 12:04 p.m.; mincha gedolah at 1:34 p.m.; shki’ah at 6:42 p.m., with some machmirim to complete matters by 7:00 for bein ha’shmashos (בֵּין הַשְּׁמָשׁוֹת). The seudah is aimed for a start between 4:00 and 5:00 p.m., kabbalas Shabbos for shortly before 7:00, and the meal continues onward. For any other year or place, the luach is consulted for the local zemanim (זְמַנִּים) and the same structural steps are followed.

IX. The Bentshen — רְצֵה and עַל הַנִּסִּים

At the close of the meal, one is at once within Shabbos (which calls for retzeh) and, in some fashion, still within Purim (which calls for al ha’nissim). The two kedushos appear to be tarti d’sasri (תַּרְתֵּי דְּסָתְרֵי) — two mutually exclusive frames. How does one bentsh?

Two approaches stand in the poskim.

  1. The Meiri: both are said in their standard places.[11] The Meiri, who is the Rishon for the minhag, holds that erev Shabbos retains its kedushas Purim at the close of the seudah, and one says retzeh and al ha’nissim together in bentshen. This is the structural claim proper to the derech: the meal has carried two kedushos from its middle to its end, and both are acknowledged where they belong.
  2. The Mishnah Berurah: retzeh in its place, and al ha’nissim shifted from its standard place to the ha’rachaman (הָרַחֲמָן) at the end.[12] Uncomfortable with the appearance of tarti d’sasri within a single berachah, the Mishnah Berurah proposes a formulation in which al ha’nissim is preserved — framed in the standard “ha’rachaman hu ya’aseh lanu nissim ke’mo she’asa la’avoseinu” pattern — but is not made to sit within the same berachah that houses retzeh. This is the more widely followed practice today, particularly in Litvish batim.

The choice between the two encodes, once again, the deeper chakirah of Section VII. The Meiri’s bentshen reflects a meal that retains its dual identity through its close. The Mishnah Berurah’s reflects a meal whose ikkar chiyuv (עִקַּר חִיּוּב) at the moment of bentshen is Shabbos, with the Purim-recognition preserved in the ha’rachaman as a zecher (זֵכֶר) rather than a co-chiyuv. One follows the practice of his rebbe, his community, or the household in which he finds himself; a guest at a tish that follows the Meiri defers to the arrangement of that tish.

X. The Kabbalistic Concern and the Response of the אַמְרֵי אֱמֶת

The mekubalim (מְקֻבָּלִים) note a structural concern.[13] The order in which one enters Shabbos is calibrated to a spiritual ascent through the mochin (מוֹחִין): mincha of erev Shabbos, then kabbalas Shabbos (the rise of the neshamah — נְשָׁמָה), then ma’ariv with kri’as Shema and Shemoneh Esrei, then the berachah me’ein sheva (בְּרָכָה מֵעֵין שֶׁבַע), and finally kiddush and the connection to the neshamah yeseirah (נְשָׁמָה יְתֵרָה). The order of the second derech is, in this sense, afkah (אַפְכָא): the meal comes first, kabbalas Shabbos in its midst, kiddush mid-meal, and ma’ariv only after. From a kabbalistic standpoint the sequence is not l’chatchilah.

Yet the Imrei Emes of Gur (אִמְרֵי אֱמֶת מִגּוּר) — Rabbi Avraham Mordechai Alter (רַבִּי אַבְרָהָם מָרְדֳּכַי אַלְטֶר), the third rebbe of Gur — who was himself a gadol b’kabbalah steeped in the world of the Chasidus that flows from the Arizal’s mochin-architecture, conducted his Purim tish (טִישׁ) in the reordered manner nonetheless.[14] That the kabbalistic concern was known to him and did not deter him is itself an argument — whether one reads him as holding that the kedushas Purim supplies the ascent that kabbalas Shabbos ordinarily achieves through its ordered sequence, or as ruling that the halachic heter (הֶתֵּר) governs even where the kabbalistic order is not preserved. The concern remains as a note against the l’chatchilah-ness of the arrangement; it is not, in the practice of a gadol b’kabbalah, a bar to the derech.

XI. Two מַעֲשֵׂי רַב

The Testimony of Rav Zalman Nechemiah Goldberg from Rav Yitzchak Isaac Sher

In a written response to a questioner,[15] Rav Zalman Nechemiah Goldberg (רַב זַלְמָן נְחֶמְיָה גּוֹלְדְּבֶּרְג) — one of the great poskim of Yerushalayim of the last generation, chasan (חָתָן) of Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach (רַב שְׁלֹמֹה זַלְמָן אוֹיֶרְבַּךְ) — recorded that his own rebbe had, in a year in which Purim fell on erev Shabbos, eaten the seudah at the table of Rav Yitzchak Isaac Sher (רַב יִצְחָק אַיְזִיק שֶׁר) — the rosh yeshivah (רֹאשׁ יְשִׁיבָה) of Slabodka, chasan and talmid muvhak of the Alter of Slabodka (הַאַלְטֶר מִסְּלַבּוֹדְקָא), niftar in 5712 (1952). Rav Sher performed pores mapah u’mekadesh at the meal, and stated of the derech: “l’chatchilah mamash mamash l’chatchilah” (לְכַתְּחִלָּה מַמָּשׁ מַמָּשׁ לְכַתְּחִלָּה). The tripled emphasis is his own, and it does what no citation of the Yad Ephraim alone could do: it gives the second derech a Litvish, Yerushalmi, mainstream pedigree entirely outside the world of Chasidus.

The Purim Tish of the Imrei Emes in Gur

The Imrei Emes conducted his Purim tish, in years in which Purim fell on erev Shabbos, in the fullest form of the derech.[16] The tish was begun approximately an hour before shki’ah; at shki’ah the pores mapah u’mekadesh was performed there at the tish, upon the same table, with lechem mishneh prepared for the occasion; kabbalas Shabbos was recited at the tish; kiddush was made without ha’gafen and without hamotzi; and the meal continued until nine or ten at night, when bentshen was recited with both retzeh and al ha’nissim, followed by ma’ariv. The image of kabbalas Shabbos received in the midst of a Purim tish is not a rhetorical flourish; it is an actual tableau from an actual tish, and it fixes the derech in the memory of one who has been told of it in a way no Mishnah Berurah footnote can.

Not every household will replicate the precise format of a rebbe’s tish. But the two ma’asim — from a Litvish rosh yeshivah in Yerushalayim and from a chasidishe rebbe in Poland — together demonstrate that pores mapah u’mekadesh is not a b’dieved (בְּדִיעֲבַד) arrangement to which one resorts when no better is available. In the practice of serious gedolim across the great divisions of the Jewish world, it is a l’chatchilah option.

XII. Choosing Between the Two דְּרָכִים

Both derachim are halacha l’ma’aseh. Neither is a b’dieved; neither is a chumra (חֻמְרָא) upon the other. The following observations may guide a household on the fence.

A household of young children, in which the Purim day itself has been consumed by chagigos (חֲגִיגוֹת), mishloach manos delivery, and the ordinary chaos of small children on the day of Purim, may find the Rema’s derech practically difficult and the Meiri’s more feasible: a single seudah in the late afternoon that carries the family into Shabbos without a second full meal at night. On the other hand, the mechanics of pores mapah require an alert head of household who can supervise kiddush without ha’gafen, lechem mishneh, the bentshen-question, and the timing — and where the head of the household has himself been drinking through the Purim-portion, the Rema’s derech is safer.

A ben Torah in a chasidishe community whose rebbe follows the Meiri’s derech should follow his rebbe’s tish; a bochur (בָּחוּר) in a Litvish yeshivah with no established institutional mesorah in either direction may reasonably default to the Rema. A young couple without children who wish to conduct a small seudas mere’im at length may find the Meiri’s derech attractive precisely for its length. In each case the choice is oriented by the mesorah of one’s rebbe and the practical shape of one’s home; it is not a matter for personal invention.

XIII. Sof Davar

Both derachim stand upon poskim and minhagim of gedolim. The simpler is the Rema’s: seudas Purim before chatzos, and Shabbos received in its ordinary shape thereafter. The older and more dramatic is the Meiri’s and the Maharil’s: a single seudah that begins in the sunlight of Purim and crosses, under a cloth spread over the challos, into the light of Shabbos — a meal that carries in its own interior the seam between the kedushos of two days. The Meiri’s testimony from twelfth-century Provence, and the two ma’asim of Rav Sher and the Imrei Emes from the century past, place the second derech firmly among the lechatchilah options of a Yid (יִיד) in galus (גָּלוּת).

A freilichen Purim (אַ פְרֵייְלִיכֶן פּוּרִים).
