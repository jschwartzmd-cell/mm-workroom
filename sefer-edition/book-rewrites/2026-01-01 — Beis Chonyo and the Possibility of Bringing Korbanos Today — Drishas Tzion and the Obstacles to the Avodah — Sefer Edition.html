בֵּית חוֹנְיוֹ וְהָאֶפְשָׁרוּת לְהַקְרִיב קָרְבָּנוֹת בַּזְּמַן הַזֶּה

Beis Chonyo and the Possibility of Bringing Korbanos Today: Drishas Tzion and the Obstacles to the Avodah
Studies in Halachah (הלכה) — From the Temple of Egypt to the Sacrifices of Our Day
12 Tevet 5786 · 1 January 2026 · Sefer Edition

This discourse completes the study of Beis Chonyo (בית חוניו), the temple of Chonyo in Egypt, begun the previous Thursday after the discussion of the temple at Elephantine, and uses it as the bridge to a larger question: what would it actually take to bring korbanos (קרבנות) in our own time?

It sets out the views of the Rambam, Tosafos and the Toras Chaim (תורת חיים) on the status of Chonyo’s temple; the attempt of Rabbi Tzvi Hirsch Kalischer (רבי צבי הירש קלישר) in Drishas Tzion (דרישת ציון) to restore the korban Pesach (קרבן פסח); the dispute of the Rambam and the Ra’avad over kedushah rishonah (קדושה ראשונה) on which it rests; the earlier plan of Rabbeinu Yechiel of Paris (רבינו יחיאל מפריז); and the obstacles, halachic and practical, that stand in the way.

The lecturer gives it, as he says, as an overview: there is more to say, and some of the problems are left for another occasion.

I. Beis Chonyo: The Background and the Artisans of Alexandria

The Recap

Last Thursday the lecturer discussed the temples that coexisted with the Bayis Rishon (בית ראשון) and the Bayis Sheini (בית שני), a piece of history that many do not know. He began with the temple at Elephantine, known principally from archaeology in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries and perhaps from a single hint in Yirmiyahu (ירמיהו), and then turned to the temple of Chonyo, which is discussed at length in Menachos (מנחות) 109.[1] The discourse of this morning finishes that subject.

Chonyo

The Mishnah (משנה) calls it Beis Chonyo (בית חוניו). Shimon HaTzaddik (שמעון הצדיק) had two sons and gave the kehunah gedolah (כהונה גדולה) to the younger, not the elder. The elder was jealous and fled; Chonyo, the younger son, ran off to Mitzrayim (מצרים) and built a Beis HaMikdash (בית המקדש) in Alexandria, where he offered korbanos (קרבנות). For centuries korbanos (קרבנות) were brought in Jerusalem and, at the same time, in this Egyptian house. The lecturer notes that the thought of two such houses standing together at once is hard to grasp. According to Josephus, he adds, Chonyo was a grandson of Shimon HaTzaddik (שמעון הצדיק); according to our tradition, a son.

The Artisans of Alexandria

The Gemara (גמרא) in Yoma (יומא), and the Yerushalmi (ירושלמי), relate that the families of Beis Garmu (בית גרמו) and Beis Avtinas (בית אבטינס) held the secrets, respectively, of baking the lechem hapanim (לחם הפנים) and of preparing the ketores (קטרת), and that artisans were brought from Alexandria to Jerusalem to learn from them, and failed.[2] The lecturer reports that it occurred to him that Chonyo, who built his temple in Alexandria and wished to perform the avodah (עבודה) properly, may have sent artisans of his own to learn these crafts, and that he was gratified to find that Rabbi Yosef Shaul Nathanson (רבי יוסף שאול נתנזון), in his Divrei Shaul (דברי שאול), makes this very connection and sees in it the reason that a temple with kohanim in Alexandria is plausible while the Temple stood in Jerusalem.[3]

He then reports that a talmid chacham (תלמיד חכם) told him it is not necessarily so. In the Targum Sheni (תרגום שני) on Megillas Esther (מגילת אסתר), whose authorship is a question for another time, it is told, as children are told in cheder (חדר), that Achashveirosh (אחשורוש) wished to copy the miraculous throne of Shlomo HaMelech (שלמה המלך) and brought artisans from Egypt and Alexandria to Persia, and that they failed there too.[4] That was long before Chonyo; so perhaps Alexandria was simply the center of craftsmanship in the ancient world, and no connection to Beis Chonyo (בית חוניו) is proved.

How Long It Stood

Josephus has his own opinion on how long the house stood; the figures are 343, 250 or 200 years, and the lecturer is not certain which.[5] In any case it stood a very long time and survived the destruction of the Bayis Sheini (בית שני), having been built about a century into that era.

The Halachic Problem

As the lecturer said the week before, the pasuk (פסוק) and the Rambam teach that while the Beis HaMikdash (בית המקדש) stands in its place in Jerusalem it is forbidden to bring korbanos (קרבנות) anywhere else.[6] This is shechutei chutz (שחוטי חוץ), which anyone who has learned Zevachim (זבחים) knows: shochet bachutz (שוחט בחוץ), ma’aleh bachutz (מעלה בחוץ). How then did Chonyo and his kohanim bring korbanos (קרבנות) there?

II. Three Accounts of Beis Chonyo

First View: It Was Avodah Zarah

One opinion in the Gemara (גמרא) is that it was avodah zarah (עבודה זרה); this is the position of Rabbi Meir (רבי מאיר). Chonyo himself justified the altar by a verse in Yeshayah (ישעיהו), ביום ההוא יהיה מזבח לה׳ בתוך ארץ מצרים,[7] taking it to be the prophet’s vision of a temple in Egypt alongside the one in Jerusalem. No one knows the plain sense of that verse, the lecturer says; that was Chonyo’s justification. If it was idolatry, it needs no justification.

The Question: The Yetzer Hara of Avodah Zarah Was Removed

The Gemara (גמרא) in Yoma (יומא) states that at the beginning of the Bayis Sheini (בית שני) the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah (אנשי כנסת הגדולה) prayed and nullified the yetzer hara (יצר הרע) of avodah zarah (עבודה זרה).[8] How then could a house of idolatry stand for two or three centuries? The lecturer’s answer: it did not vanish overnight. The overwhelming force was destroyed, but the yetzer hara (יצר הרע) remained, especially among the nations, and habit continued. For the Jews, he adds, it was taken care of, and the same Gemara (גמרא) speaks of the removal of the yetzer hara (יצר הרע) of arayos (עריות), after which the hens ceased to lay.

The Rambam: Chonyo Was Not Really an Idol-Worshiper

The Rambam, in his commentary on the Mishnah (משנה), gives a long and vivid account.[9] Chonyo had been rejected by the kohanim of the Temple, because of the story of the first day of the avodah (עבודה), when he made his brother wear women’s garments; he ran to Egypt, angry at his fellow kohanim, and out of ambition built a cult around himself. The lecturer remarks that people pay no attention to such one-liners in the Rambam. Chonyo gathered many Egyptian gentiles, ונאספו לו כת שקורין אותו קבט״ר, the ancient Copts, which the lecturer takes to mean a racial and not a religious group, since Christianity had not yet arisen. He drew them close to the avodah (עבודה) of HaKadosh Baruch Hu (הקדוש ברוך הוא); they honored him and gave him what he wished, and והיו המצרים עובדים בו לשם ומקריבים קרבנות. The Rambam says plainly that it was not avodah zarah (עבודה זרה) as such but illegitimate avodah (עבודה): there is a prohibition to offer outside Jerusalem, and Chonyo erred, but he meant it l’shem shamayim (לשם שמים).

Second View: They Did Nothing Wrong (Tosafos)

Another opinion, developed by Tosafos (תוספות) in Menachos (מנחות) 109, is that according to Rabbi Yehudah (רבי יהודה) (against Rabbi Meir (רבי מאיר)) Chonyo did nothing wrong, for he offered only the korbanos (קרבנות) of gentiles and not of Jews.[10] Jews may not offer outside Jerusalem while the Beis HaMikdash (בית המקדש) stands; but offerings of goyim (גוים) are accepted, and according to most opinions they may offer to HaKadosh Baruch Hu (הקדוש ברוך הוא) even outside the Beis HaMikdash (בית המקדש). The lecturer’s picture: Chonyo missed the avodah (עבודה) he had been denied, and built a complete temple for it, but only to receive the offerings of gentiles, not his own. In the Rambam’s language, the lecturer says, Chonyo had a deep longing, נכספה וגם כלתה נפשי.

The Problem and the Chiddush of the Toras Chaim

The difficulty is that there is an opinion that once the Beis HaMikdash (בית המקדש) stands in Jerusalem, gentiles too may not offer outside it. Here the lecturer brings a chiddush (חידוש) of one of the great Acharonim (אחרונים), Rabbi Avraham Chaim Schor (רבי אברהם חיים שור), in his Toras Chaim (תורת חיים).[11] Even according to the minority view, what was Chonyo doing? He was running a training facility: he taught the Egyptians the avodah (עבודה) and its laws; it was a kind of mock Beis HaMikdash (בית המקדש), in which the gentiles brought their own korbanos (קרבנות) while Chonyo and his kohanim were instructors. The lecturer calls it a very great chiddush (חידוש), and notes that the Toras Chaim (תורת חיים) goes back and forth at length and is not sure.

Questions from the Room

One of those present remarked that the fact that a kohen served in Beis Chonyo (בית חוניו) is not a proof that the offerings were those of a gentile; the lecturer agreed that this is only one shitah (שיטה), and that the Sfas Emes (שפת אמת) takes it up and cites the Gemara (גמרא) in Kiddushin (קידושין).[12] Another asked: if the offerings were gentile, why was a Beis HaMikdash (בית המקדש) needed at all? Why not a bamah (במה)?

The lecturer answered that the bamos (במות) were forbidden: the Gemara (גמרא) says, at least four or five times, that from the moment Israel entered Jerusalem and the Beis HaMikdash (בית המקדש) was built, נאסרו הבמות, even for gentiles. The only way left, he said, and it is a loophole in halachah (הלכה), is to build a real Beis HaMikdash (בית המקדש), which is itself forbidden, in which gentiles might bring their own offerings. He would love some historian to reveal what Chonyo really wanted, and why it mattered so much to him to bring the offerings of gentiles. These are, he says, chiddushim (חידושים) on a sugya (סוגיה) that is difficult to understand.

III. Toward Korbanos Today: Kalischer and the Korban Pesach

The Vision

The point of the discourse, the lecturer says, is the later attempt to bring korbanos (קרבנות) when there is no Beis HaMikdash (בית המקדש). In 1861, he says, Rabbi Tzvi Hirsch Kalischer (רבי צבי הירש קלישר), a talmid (תלמיד) of Rabbi Akiva Eiger (רבי עקיבא איגר), put out the sefer (ספר) Drishas Tzion (דרישת ציון).[13] It is unique in our literature: a book devoted entirely to a genuine attempt to restore korbanos (קרבנות) in our time. The main korban (קרבן) it pursues is the korban Pesach (קרבן פסח). The lecturer says that one reads the book between the lines and is inspired by the longing of the author.

Why Specifically the Korban Pesach?

Why not the tamid shel shachar (תמיד של שחר), the tamid shel bein ha’arbayim (תמיד של בין הערביים), the musafim (מוספים) of Shabbos (שבת) and Yom Tov (יום טוב), all the korbanos tzibur (קרבנות ציבור) and menachos (מנחות) that one learns day and night in Daf Yomi (דף יומי)? The answer is practical. Every korban tzibur (קרבן ציבור) must come from all of Klal Yisrael (כלל ישראל) by way of terumas halishkah (תרומת הלשכה), and so must the mizbe’ach (מזבח) itself (kayitz hamizbe’ach (קיץ המזבח)). That means that every Jew must contribute; as the lecturer remarks, it is hard enough for a fundraiser to collect from every Jew for tzedakah (צדקה), let alone to run an "adopt a korban (קרבן)" campaign. The korban Pesach (קרבן פסח), by contrast, is both a korban yachid (קרבן יחיד) and a korban tzibur (קרבן ציבור), and technically does not require the shekalim (שקלים) of terumas halishkah (תרומת הלשכה).

The Problem of Tumah and the Lost Brothers

The primary problem, as the lecturer puts it, is tumah v’taharah (טומאה וטהרה); without it being resolved, he says, nothing will happen until Mashiach (משיח) comes. (The editor adds that purification from tumas meis (טומאת מת) requires the parah adumah (פרה אדומה), which is lacking; the lecturer did not name it at this point.) The Rambam holds that all of Klal Yisrael (כלל ישראל) must contribute to a korban tzibur (קרבן ציבור), and the Chasam Sofer (חתם סופר) has a long discussion whether we may give on behalf of our lost brothers and sisters, wherever they are; he is not sure.[14]

IV. The Machlokes of the Rambam and the Ra’avad on Kedushah Rishonah

What Is at Stake

Before that, says the lecturer, there is one of the most important machlokesen (מחלוקות), begun the week before, on which we still have no hachra’ah (הכרעה), and which appears throughout the Shas (ש״ס): in Zevachim (זבחים), Menachos (מנחות), Megillah (מגילה), Pesachim (פסחים) and Yoma (יומא). Kedushah rishonah (קדושה ראשונה) is the kedushah (קדושה) with which Shlomo HaMelech (שלמה המלך) consecrated the Makom HaMikdash (מקום המקדש), the altar and the vessels. Did it endure forever, or lapse when Klal Yisrael (כלל ישראל) was exiled to Bavel (בבל)? The dispute concerns not only the Makom HaMikdash (מקום המקדש) but all of Eretz Yisrael (ארץ ישראל), in regard to terumos u’maasros (תרומות ומעשרות) and the mitzvos hatluyos ba’aretz (מצוות התלויות בארץ).

The Rambam: Sanctified for All Time

The Rambam holds that the kedushah (קדושה) of the rest of Eretz Yisrael (ארץ ישראל) lapsed, but that of the Makom HaMikdash (מקום המקדש) and Jerusalem endures.[15] The lecturer reads him: korbanos (קרבנות) may be brought in our time אף על פי שאין שם בית, following Rabbi Yehoshua (רבי יהושע), מקריבין אף על פי שאין בית. One may bring korbanos (קרבנות) at the place of the Temple, eat kodshei kodashim (קדשי קדשים) in the Azarah (עזרה) (provided one knows its boundaries), and kodshim kalim (קדשים קלים) within the walls of Jerusalem.

Why does the Rambam distinguish? The lecturer says that when he writes in this language one sees that it is his own chiddush (חידוש), on which there are many questions from many sugyos (סוגיות). The Rambam’s reason: לפי שקדושת המקדש וירושלים מפני השכינה, והשכינה אינה בטילה. The Shechinah (שכינה) could exile us, but it did not exile itself. It is still there, as it was in the days of the Beis HaMikdash (בית המקדש); the lecturer sees in this why every Jew is overcome with emotion on Har HaBayis (הר הבית). The Rambam brings the verse in the tochachah (תוכחה) of Parshas Bechukosai (פרשת בחוקותי), והשימותי את מקדשיכם, which the Sages expound: even though they are desolate, בקדושתן הן עומדים.[16] The Rambam leaves the details to later generations.

The Ra’avad: The Kedushah Lapsed

The Ra’avad is astonished: אמר אברהם, סברת עצמו היא זו; where did the Rambam get this distinction? He brings proofs that the kedushah (קדושה) of the Temple lapsed too, and says that a person may enter Har HaBayis (הר הבית) even as a tamei meis (טמא מת) without being liable to kares (כרת); the kares (כרת) presupposes kedushah (קדושה), and where there is none, there is none. The Ra’avad adds, כך נגלה סוד ה׳ ליראיו. The lecturer notes that the Acharonim (אחרונים) dispute whether he means literally ruach hakodesh (רוח הקודש), given that לא בשמים היא; the Rim of Levin is cited as holding that he means siyata d’shmaya (סייעתא דשמיא) in deciding the halachah (הלכה).[17]

Going Up to Har HaBayis

The lecturer notes, without wishing to make it political (this being the beis medrash (בית מדרש) of Harbor View), that the main source for those who go up to Har HaBayis (הר הבית) today is the Ra’avad. They are not simply political settlers; some are sincerely devout, chassidim (חסידים) among them, and others of this type also hold that one may go. That is what makes it harder to argue with them. The lecturer counts himself with the majority of Klal Yisrael (כלל ישראל) and of the rabbanim (רבנים), who forbid it. The Briskers do not even approach the Kosel (כותל) closely; Satmar (סאטמאר) does not go in the daytime. The Chief Rabbinate of Israel since Rav Kook (הרב קוק), the lecturer adds, even for those who follow Religious Zionism, itself holds that going up is forbidden.

V. Kalischer’s Argument and the Responses

Either Way

Kalischer, says the lecturer, reasons: I follow the Rambam that one may bring korbanos (קרבנות); but perhaps the halachah (הלכה) is like the Ra’avad, and we have no hachra’ah (הכרעה). Even so, if there is no kedushah (קדושה) in the Makom HaMikdash (מקום המקדש), the bamos (במות) are permitted again, and korbanos (קרבנות) may be brought on a bamah (במה) anywhere, in Jerusalem or out of it, in Eretz Yisrael (ארץ ישראל) or out of it. So ממה נפשך: according to the Rambam bring a korban (קרבן); according to the Ra’avad, gather the tzibur (ציבור) and build a bamas tzibur (במת ציבור). The lecturer notes that the Gemara (גמרא) requires a keren yesod (קרן יסוד) for a large bamah (במה) and that whether it counts as a bamas yachid (במת יחיד) or a bamas tzibur (במת ציבור) is a further question that he does not go into.

Rav Kook’s Response

Rav Kook (הרב קוק) wrote sharply against permitting entry, in his Mishpat Kohen (משפט כהן), which the lecturer praises as a sefer (ספר) of halachah (הלכה), whatever one thinks of his other writings.[18] The lecturer paraphrases: who dared pride himself in deciding like the Ra’avad that there is no longer kedushah (קדושה), and therefore that one may enter without fear of kares (כרת)? Who told you that this is a machlokes (מחלוקת) of the Rambam and the Ra’avad that we are equipped to decide? We have no power to decide it until Mashiach (משיח) comes, and the moment one says like the Ra’avad, every tzaruah (צרוע), zav (זב) and tamei meis (טמא מת) goes up, the kedushah (קדושה) is violated, and Hashem (השם) have mercy on what could follow. Those who go up have protocols for purifying themselves; but this, the lecturer says, is problematic, because according to the Rambam there is no justification for it at all.

Rabbi Akiva Eiger’s Refusal

Before publishing, the lecturer says, Kalischer wrote to his teacher Rabbi Akiva Eiger (רבי עקיבא איגר): this is what I am about to do; do I have your haskamah (הסכמה)? He did not receive it. Rabbi Akiva Eiger (רבי עקיבא איגר), the lecturer says, pulled the rug from under his feet in a famous letter: do not do this, I beg you, do not do it.[19] Kalischer did not actually bring korbanos (קרבנות), but went on to write the sefer (ספר). Another great student of Rabbi Akiva Eiger (רבי עקיבא איגר), Rabbi Eliyahu Gutmacher (רבי אליהו גוטמכר) of Greiditz, one of the great tzaddikim (צדיקים) of nineteenth-century Europe, whose notes appear at the back of the Gemara (גמרא), also pushed in this direction. These were gedolei olam (גדולי עולם), the lecturer insists, not hotheads with political motives; they were moved by halachah (הלכה).

VI. A Precedent: Rabbeinu Yechiel of Paris

Ishtori Haparchi’s Report

Kalischer brought an earlier case of gedolei olam (גדולי עולם) who wished to bring the korban Pesach (קרבן פסח) in their time. In the early fourteenth century, in the transition between the Rishonim (ראשונים) and the Acharonim (אחרונים), lived Rabbi Ishtori Haparchi (רבי אשתורי הפרחי), after whom a street in Geulah is named.[20] He traveled from Europe to Eretz Yisrael (ארץ ישראל) and toured it from end to end, an early Zionist, and wrote in his Kaftor VaFerach (כפתור ופרח) the halachos (הלכות) of the Land: terumos u’maasros (תרומות ומעשרות), shemittah (שמיטה) and more, and also about the Makom HaMikdash (מקום המקדש). He writes: I met a Jew named Reb Boruch (רב ברוך), apparently a student of the Baalei HaTosafos (בעלי התוספות), who testified that Rabbeinu Yechiel of Paris (רבינו יחיאל מפריז), who died about 1270, came to Jerusalem and planned to bring a korban Pesach (קרבן פסח) at the Makom HaMikdash (מקום המקדש) on the basis of the Rambam.

Who Rabbeinu Yechiel Was

He was the teacher of the Maharam of Rothenburg (מהר״ם מרוטנבורג) and a student of Rabbi Yehudah HeChasid (רבי יהודה החסיד), one of the leading Baalei Tosafos of France, with a yeshivah (ישיבה) of three hundred. One of his sons-in-law was the Smak (סמ״ק), the Sefer Mitzvos Katan (ספר מצוות קטן); another, the lecturer says, was the Mordechai (מרדכי), Rabbeinu Mordechai bar Hillel Ashkenazi (רבינו מרדכי בר הלל אשכנזי). His daughter’s name was Zelda, known from his autobiography. They were killed al kiddush Hashem (על קידוש השם) in the Rindfleisch pogroms at the end of the 1200s. He is quoted many times in Tosafos (תוספות).[21]

The Disputation of Paris

He had a student, Nicholas Donin, a brilliant man (an ilui (עילוי)) who denied the Torah she’baal peh (תורה שבעל פה) and went off the deep end. Rabbeinu Yechiel excommunicated him; Donin drew close to the Church and to Pope Gregory IX, and reported to him the passages of the Gemara (גמרא) that mocked Yoshke (ישו) (some of which were removed by the censors and have since been restored; there is a sefer (ספר) called Chesronos HaShas (חסרונות הש״ס) listing what was taken out). King Louis IX ordered a public disputation, in which Donin represented the Church against the Baalei Tosafos; it is recorded in the Sefer HaVikuchim (ספר הוויכוחים), and Rabbeinu Yechiel won it hands down. The Church would not admit it and decreed that the Talmud (תלמוד) be burned; two years later, in the square of Paris, twenty or twenty-four wagonloads of manuscripts were burned. But HaKadosh Baruch Hu (הקדוש ברוך הוא) promised כי לא תשכח מפי זרעו, and we still have the Tosafos (תוספות) and the Gemaros (גמרות).

Aliyah and the Plan

Rabbeinu Yechiel took it as a sign that he must leave France, as Jews today say when antisemitism rises: we do not belong here; we belong in Eretz Yisrael (ארץ ישראל). Seven hundred years ago, the lecturer says, he went up with his entire yeshivah (ישיבה); historians dispute where they settled (Acre, Haifa) and even whether he returned to France, a question the lecturer sets aside. Ishtori Haparchi writes, in a few lines, that he was so excited by the idea that he forgot to ask Reb Boruch about tumah (טומאה) and about bigdei kehunah (בגדי כהונה); but the man left, and he lost him, and never received the answer. The lecturer then reports the reasoning that tumah (טומאה) was no problem, because טומאה הותרה בציבור: when it is a korban tzibur (קרבן ציבור) we do not worry about tumah (טומאה).

VII. The Obstacles Today

Tumah: Hutrah or Dechuyah?

There is a great machlokes (מחלוקת) in the Gemara (גמרא) whether tumah (טומאה) is hutrah b’tzibur (הותרה בציבור) or only dechuyah (דחויה).[22] The Rambam, the lecturer says, rules dechuyah (דחויה). That means one must l’chatchilah (לכתחילה) seek someone who is tahor (טהור), and only if there is no choice may it be done in tumah (טומאה). Many Acharonim (אחרונים) understand that, since it is not wholly permitted, the ritzuy tzitz (רצוי ציץ) is still needed: the Kohen Gadol (כהן גדול) must wear the tzitz (ציץ), and then we look away. Kalischer writes that it is hutrah (הותרה) and nothing is needed; the lecturer says that according to the Rambam this is not so.

No Sanhedrin, No Kohen Gadol, No Tzitz

Where is the tzitz (ציץ)? We have none, and we do not even know how to make one, because of many machlokesen (מחלוקות) in Yoma (יומא) and Zevachim (זבחים). There is no Kohen Gadol (כהן גדול); to appoint one requires a Sanhedrin (סנהדרין), and there is none. So no Sanhedrin (סנהדרין), no Kohen Gadol (כהן גדול), no tzitz (ציץ).

The Study of Kodshim

The lecturer interrupts himself: there are young men in Lakewood and in Brisk in Eretz Yisrael (ארץ ישראל) who learn Kodshim (קדשים) so well that they could stand up today and reenact the avodah (עבודה) in every detail. They exist, he says, mostly because the Chofetz Chaim (חפץ חיים) persuaded talmidei chachamim (תלמידי חכמים) to take up Kodshim (קדשים) again, saying that Mashiach (משיח) is coming, the Beis HaMikdash (בית המקדש) will be rebuilt, and we must know how to bring the korbanos (קרבנות).[23] The Brisker Rov, Rav Yitzchak Zev Soloveitchik (רב יצחק זאב סולובייצ׳יק), the son of Rav Chaim Brisker (רב חיים בריסקער), made yeshivos (ישיבות) devoted to Kodshim (קדשים) alone, because as a young man his father brought him to the Chofetz Chaim (חפץ חיים), who said: you are the future of Kodshim (קדשים) in Klal Yisrael (כלל ישראל). Those yeshivos (ישיבות) have splintered into fifteen or twenty, and there is also an organization in the Old City, Machon HaMikdash (מכון המקדש), which rebuilds the garments and occasionally reenacts the avodah (עבודה), together with Ateret Kohanim; the lecturer found it fascinating, and necessary as the time of Mashiach (משיח) draws near.

The Garments of the Kohanim

Suppose we do not need a Kohen Gadol (כהן גדול) or a tzitz (ציץ) for a korban Pesach (קרבן פסח), and a kohen hedyot (כהן הדיוט) will serve. His garments are not so simple: the avnet (אבנט) of a kohen hedyot (כהן הדיוט) is a great machlokes (מחלוקת) in the Gemara (גמרא): is it like that of the Kohen Gadol (כהן גדול), which contains techeiles (תכלת)? If so, what is techeiles (תכלת) today?

The Chofetz Chaim (חפץ חיים) said, of the Radzyner techeiles (תכלת), that if they gave it to him he would not dare enter the Beis HaMikdash (בית המקדש) to do the avodah (עבודה) wearing it, because he did not know with certainty; we need Mashiach (משיח) to tell us.[24] Some wear techeiles (תכלת) today: the lecturer’s wife’s grandfather, Rav Yisrael Belsky (רב ישראל בעלסקי), and, he thinks, Rav Hershel Schachter (רב הרשל שכטר). (A member of the room added that Rav Elyashiv (רב אלישיב) did too; the lecturer was surprised, and the matter was left.) Most do not; there is a machlokes (מחלוקת) on what it is.

Rabbi Akiva Eiger (רבי עקיבא איגר) wrote to his student Kalischer at once: if the avnet (אבנט) is like the Kohen Gadol (כהן גדול)’s, show me techeiles (תכלת). In his day, in the 1800s, nothing was known of it, not even the Radzyner. Kalischer rejected his teacher’s claim and said: look in the Rambam. When the Rambam describes the avnet (אבנט) he says only that techeiles (תכלת) is a color; only in Hilchos Tzitzis (הלכות ציצית) does he say that it comes from the chilazon (חלזון). So the techeiles (תכלת) of the priestly garments is, for most Acharonim (אחרונים), a color. A member of the room, Rav Benjy (רב בנג׳י), raised the point; the lecturer replied that it is not so simple: the Merkavas HaMishneh (מרכבת המשנה) says that the Rambam relied on what he wrote in Hilchos Tzitzis (הלכות ציצית), and we should not infer from him that it is a mere color, and Rashi (רש״י) on Parshas Terumah (פרשת תרומה) says that the techeiles (תכלת) of the bigdei kehunah (בגדי כהונה) does come from the chilazon (חלזון).[25] So again we are stuck. A smaller problem of the same kind is argaman (ארגמן), purple: no one knows exactly what it is made of.

The Place of the Mizbe’ach

The Rambam in Hilchos Beis HaBechirah (הלכות בית הבחירה), chapter 2, halachah 1, writes that the place of the altar is exact: if it is not built on that very spot it is not a mizbe’ach (מזבח), it does not make the korban (קרבן) permissible to eat, and the sprinkling of the blood is not in the right place.[26] Kalischer said: we have the Kosel (כותל); let us start measuring. After the First Temple the verse says ערו ערו עד היסוד בה, so nothing remained; the Second Temple the lecturer says was different, because HaKadosh Baruch Hu (הקדוש ברוך הוא) left us a wall, either of the Azarah (עזרה) or of Har HaBayis (הר הבית). One could also measure from the Even HaShesiyah (אבן השתייה) under the mosque, using Maseches Tamid (מסכת תמיד) and Maseches Middos (מסכת מידות); the Tannaim (תנאים) dispute the exact location, and where the Ark stood. Kalischer wrote to the leaders in Jerusalem, and to the Ottoman authorities, for permission to measure.

Rav Chaim Sonnenfeld (רב חיים זוננפלד), who lived in Jerusalem at the time, wrote, not to Kalischer but to a friend of his, Rav Shimon Sofer (רב שמעון סופר), a grandson of the Chasam Sofer (חתם סופר), that the Arabs would never allow a foot to be set on Har HaBayis (הר הבית): these are dreams; I live here and I know.[27] The Chasam Sofer (חתם סופר) himself, writing to his father-in-law, Rabbi Akiva Eiger (רבי עקיבא איגר), begins: you are an old man living in Europe and do not know what is happening in Eretz Yisrael (ארץ ישראל); I receive letters from there every day, diagrams and pictures of Har HaBayis (הר הבית); politically it is impossible. (The lecturer notes that in those days the authority was whoever the Ottomans had put up there, not the Waqf.) Rav Tzvi Pesach Frank (רב צבי פסח פרנק) is also cited. And there is a sefer (ספר), Avodah Tamah (עבודה תמה) by Rav Chaim Nosson (רב חיים נתן), printed as a rebuttal of Drishas Tzion (דרישת ציון), rejecting every claim of Kalischer; he says that one can measure from today until tomorrow and the exact place of the mizbe’ach (מזבח) will remain in dispute.

The Zerikah in the Portions of Binyamin and Yehudah

The sprinkling of the blood of the korban Pesach (קרבן פסח), the Gemara (גמרא) says, must take place in the portion of Binyamin and the portion of Yehudah; if it is done in only one, it does not permit the flesh to be eaten, and people would be eating the korban (קרבן) in grave violation of the Torah.

Kehunah: Who Is a Kohen?

The lecturer calls this one of the major problems. Kalischer says כהנים הם מוחזקים: we give a kohen the first aliyah (עליה), we take Birkas Kohanim (ברכת כהנים) from him; why should we suddenly doubt him? The Chasam Sofer (חתם סופר) agrees, in a teshuvah (תשובה). But Rabbi Akiva Eiger (רבי עקיבא איגר) and other Acharonim (אחרונים), on the basis of the Yam shel Shlomo (ים של שלמה) and early authorities on pidyon haben (פדיון הבן), say that although chazakah (חזקה) suffices for the first aliyah (עליה) and for nesias kapayim (נשיאת כפים), we do not rely on it to permit a kohen to do what is forbidden to a zar (זר); they must be kohanim meyuchasim (כהנים מיוחסים). Show me one kohen meyuchas (כהן מיוחס).[28] The Yaavetz (יעב״ץ) wrote this long before Kalischer: show me one kohen with a letter we can trust, from the Bayis Sheini (בית שני). Some say a family is Rappaport or Katz; the lecturer says that he knows many kohanim who are not kohanim, and tells of a family in Lakewood who believed for generations that they were kohanim, researched it, and found that they were not, so that people had to redo their pidyonos (פדיונות). Who knows how many mamzerim (ממזרים) and zarim (זרים) have mixed into the kehunah (כהונה)? For the avodah (עבודה) of the Temple we do not take just any kohen.

As for DNA: it does not help with chalalim (חללים). The lecturer adds that in Djerba, in Tunisia, the "city of kohanim," DNA testing, reported in the National Geographic about a decade ago, confirmed that the community descends from kohanim who settled there after the Bayis Sheini (בית שני); Jews still live there, though most have gone to Eretz Yisrael (ארץ ישראל). The whole subject, he says, is a complex one of its own.

VIII. Rav Kook’s Final Word

Last, the lecturer reports the teshuvah (תשובה) of Rav Kook (הרב קוק) to Rav Ovadiah Hadayah (רב עובדיה הדאיה), one of the great Sephardic poskim who also supported the idea of bringing korbanos (קרבנות) in our time. Rav Kook (הרב קוק) writes strongly: the Rambam, at the end of Hilchos Melachim (הלכות מלכים), writes that Mashiach (משיח) will come, the malchus beis Dovid (מלכות בית דוד) will return, the Beis HaMikdash (בית המקדש) will be built, and venakriv korbanos (ונקריב קרבנות).[29] So the Rambam, though by his own view one may bring korbanos (קרבנות) in our time, as he writes in Hilchos Beis HaBechirah (הלכות בית הבחירה), evidently agreed, because of all these technical difficulties, that they will be brought only when Mashiach (משיח) comes.

The lecturer says (adding that he is a little Satmar but may read Rav Kook (הרב קוק)’s Torah) that Rav Kook (הרב קוק) held that the return of Jews to Eretz Yisrael (ארץ ישראל) is aschalta d’geulah (אתחלתא דגאולה), before there was a State; but it does not happen overnight. First a navi (נביא); then a Sanhedrin (סנהדרין); then a Kohen Gadol (כהן גדול); the recreation of the bigdei kehunah (בגדי כהונה); and the building of the Beis HaMikdash (בית המקדש), which, the Midrash (מדרש) says, comes down from heaven in fire, itself a separate sugya (סוגיה). Only then will korbanos (קרבנות) be offered. No one has dared to make a genuine attempt to bring even the korban Pesach (קרבן פסח), despite all of Kalischer’s leniencies.

Closing

And that, the lecturer says, ended the matter until today. There are people in Eretz Yisrael (ארץ ישראל) who try to slip onto Har HaBayis (הר הבית), motivated, in his own feeling, more by politics than by halachah (הלכה); he says one reads of it in the Israeli news. In practice nothing will happen until Mashiach (משיח) comes. Whether Rav Tzvi Yehudah Kook (רב צבי יהודה קוק) held like his father is a matter on which the students of Rav Kook (הרב קוק) disagree; the Nazir (הנזיר), Rav David Cohen (רב דוד כהן), has his own view in his sefer (ספר), which is not as widely accepted, and the lecturer promised to look into it. The remaining halachic problems were left for another occasion.
