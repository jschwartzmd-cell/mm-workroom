וְלִירוּשָׁלַיִם עִירְךָ וְאֶת צֶמַח דָּוִד

Birkas Yerushalayim and Tzemach Dovid — Ulirushalayim Ircha and Es Tzemach Dovid: The Throne of Dovid, the Cave of Gush Chalav, and the Sprout of Mashiach
Shiur of Rabbi Ephrati — Sunday Morning Series on Tefillah
Recorded 6 July 2025  ·  Sefer Edition

Hakdamah

Two berachos (ברכות) stand at the threshold of the geulah (גאולה) in Shemoneh Esrei (שמונה עשרה): Ulirushalayim Ircha (ולירושלים עירך) and Es Tzemach Dovid (את צמח דוד). In Eretz Yisrael (ארץ ישראל) they were once recited as a single berachah (ברכה); today they stand as two. The inquiry that follows proceeds from the Tur’s (טור) gematria (גימטריא) of 1,800, drawn from the opening letters of the berachos, to the question on mein ha’chasimah (מעין החתימה) put to the Bach (ב״ח) by the blind Rav Shabsai Sofer (רב שבתי סופר); to the kavanah (כוונה) that the Arizal (אריז״ל) received from Shmaya v’Avtalyon (שמעיה ואבטליון) in their cave at Gush Chalav (גוש חלב) — that Mashiach ben Yosef (משיח בן יוסף) not be slain by Armilus HaRasha (ארמילוס הרשע); to Sefer Zerubavel (ספר זרובבל) and the war between Paras (פרס) and Edom (אדום); to the names of Mashiach (משיח) and the acrostic “Chaim” (חיים); and finally to Rav Schwab (רב שוואב) on the tears that water the tzemach (צמח), and Rav Shimshon Raphael Hirsch (רב שמשון רפאל הירש) on the long struggle against the influence of the nations.

I. The Opening Vav and the Third Beis HaMikdash

Two berachos of Shemoneh Esrei open with a vav (ו׳): Velamalshinim (ולמלשינים) and Ulirushalayim (ולירושלים). No other berachah begins so.

The Gemara (גמרא) in Megillah 17b[1] derives the order of the berachos: Yerushalayim (ירושלים) follows Al HaTzaddikim (על הצדיקים) because the keren (קרן) of the tzaddikim (צדיקים) can be raised only in Yerushalayim. The pasuk (פסוק) reads “yishlayu ohavayich” (ישליו אוהביך) — a lashon (לשון) of shalvah (שלוה), tranquillity. The ultimate shalvah of the tzaddikim at the end of days is bound to Yerushalayim; hence the vav of Ulirushalayim, the structural continuation of Al HaTzaddikim.

The Midrash of the Shibbolei HaLeket

The Shibbolei HaLeket (שבולי הלקט)[2] brings a midrash (מדרש): when Shlomo HaMelech (שלמה המלך) completed the building of the Beis HaMikdash (בית המקדש), the malachei hashareis (מלאכי השרת) rejoiced and proclaimed “Baruch atah Hashem boneh Yerushalayim” (ברוך אתה ה׳ בונה ירושלים). The prayer recited today, however, is not for the Yerushalayim of Shlomo but for the Beis HaMikdash HaShlishi (בית המקדש השלישי). The first and second were destroyed; the third, say Chazal (חז״ל), will stand forever — binyan olam (בנין עולם).

A question raised in the course of the shiur — whether the third Beis HaMikdash can be built by human hands — touches a machlokes (מחלוקת) over the manner of its coming: whether it is built by man, or is already built above and will descend whole. Rashi (רש״י) is cited for the second view.[3]

II. The Tur and the Number 1,800

A question of nusach (נוסח) opens the matter. Nusach Edos HaMizrach (נוסח עדות המזרח) begins this berachah not with “Ulirushalayim” but with “Tishkon” (תשכון) — without the opening vav. The difference is not slight.

The Tur (טור) in Orach Chaim (אורח חיים) siman 118[4] defends the vav. He brings a midrash of the darshei reshumos (דורשי רשומות): the roshei teivos (ראשי תיבות) — the opening letters — of the eighteen berachos sum, by gematria, to 1,800 (אלף ות״ת), the number of the malachim (מלאכים) who receive the tefilos (תפילות) of Klal Yisrael (כלל ישראל). The count is exact only according to the nusach that includes the vav of Ulirushalayim. The Zohar HaKadosh (זוהר הקדוש) is also invoked, in the shiur, for the 1,800 angels appointed over the prayers of Klal Yisrael; the exactness of the tally — mamash the opening letter of every single berachah — is itself a cause for wonder.[5]

The Arizal and Nusach Sefard

The Arizal (אריז״ל)[6], however, recited “Tishkon” without the vav. He was not troubled by the gematria. Nusach Ashkenaz (נוסח אשכנז) — and what is called nusach Sefard (נוסח ספרד) today — follows the Tur’s calculation; the Sefardim of Edos HaMizrach follow the Arizal, and have their own mesorah (מסורה).

III. The Question of Mein HaChasimah

The berachah reads: “Return in mercy to Yerushalayim Your city, and dwell in it as You have spoken, and build it…” It then continues “v’chisei Dovid avdecha meheirah l’socha tachin” (וכסא דוד עבדך מהרה לתוכה תכין) and closes “Baruch atah Hashem boneh Yerushalayim.” The final line before every chasimah (חתימה) must be mein ha’chasimah (מעין החתימה) — substantively close to the chasimah itself. Here the preceding line concerns the throne of Dovid (דוד), while the chasimah concerns the rebuilding of Yerushalayim. They appear to be different subjects.

The Answer of the Bach

The Bach (ב״ח)[7] takes up the question, and records the name of the one who put it to him: Rav Shabsai Sofer (רב שבתי סופר).[8]

A Word About Rav Shabsai Sofer

Rav Shabsai Sofer (רב שבתי סופר) flourished at the turn of the seventeenth century and was a talmid (תלמיד) of the Levush (לבוש). He was called Rav Shabsai Sagi Nahor (רב שבתי סגי נהור) — literally “full of light,” the euphemism for blindness — because he lost his sight in old age. Despite the blindness he became one of the most consequential figures in the history of the siddur (סידור), authoring a siddur according to our nusach at the request of the gedolei hador (גדולי הדור).

He lived in Przemyśl (not Premishlan), in southeastern Poland — Galicia, today on the Polish–Ukrainian border — and was called “Shabsai Premishla” after his city. The hakdamah (הקדמה) to his siddur alone runs six hundred pages. In it he relates that he was blind throughout the project and had to dictate everything. The work was undertaken at the instance of, and received haskamos (הסכמות) from, the Maharsha (מהרש״א), the Bach, the Rema (רמ״א) school, and the Shelah (של״ה). Dozens of talmidei chachamim (תלמידי חכמים) assisted in the compilation.

The Vaad Arba Aratzos (ועד ארבע ארצות)[9] — the central rabbinic authority of Polish Jewry, whose standing was such that one hesitates to say Sanhedrin (סנהדרין), yet the idea is very similar — issued a kol koreh (קול קורא) that every beis knesses (בית כנסת) within its jurisdiction should own a copy. Hand-copied siddurim were rare; in many wooden synagogues the nusach of Rav Shabsai was written on the walls so that the kahal (קהל) might compare. The trust placed in his diyuk (דיוק), his exactitude, was unique.

Rav Shabsai put the kushya (קושיא) to the Bach: how can the line before the chasimah speak of the throne of Dovid when the chasimah is “boneh Yerushalayim”? No other berachah in Shemoneh Esrei shows such a discrepancy.

The Bach answered, and Rav Shabsai accepted his answer: the entire tachlis (תכלית) of Yerushalayim is malchus beis Dovid (מלכות בית דוד). Yerushalayim is not what one might imagine today — buildings, holy sites. Yerushalayim is Yerushalayim only when Dovid HaMelech (דוד המלך) sits upon his throne — or whoever will be Mashiach ben Dovid (משיח בן דוד) — and the kingdom of Dovid is intact. Kisei Dovid (כסא דוד) is the rebuilding of Yerushalayim. The line before the chasimah is therefore, in truth, mein ha’chasimah, pointing at the same target from another side.

Tzarich Iyun: The Question of Bentching

A question was put in the course of the shiur, which the maggid shiur (מגיד שיעור) called an excellent one, acknowledged that he had never considered, and left open: if, as the Bach holds, Yerushalayim and malchus beis Dovid are inseparable, why does Boneh Yerushalayim in Birkas HaMazon (ברכת המזון) close without any reference to the throne of Dovid?

The question remains a tzarich iyun (צריך עיון). Two directions suggest themselves, offered here as editorial observations and not as teachings of the shiur. First, the berachah of Bentching may be a request for the rebuilding of the city alone, whereas the berachah of Shemoneh Esrei is a petition for the complete geulah — the city together with the kingdom. Second, the principle of the Bach may operate only at the level of mein ha’chasimah, a technical law in the construction of a berachah; the berachah of Bentching, which has no preceding line about the throne of Dovid that stands in need of explanation, simply never raises the difficulty.

IV. One Berachah in Eretz Yisrael

The answer of the Bach explains a structural fact: in ancient Eretz Yisrael, “Ulirushalayim” and “Es Tzemach Dovid” were one berachah. The Tosefta (תוספתא) and the Yerushalmi (ירושלמי)[10] reflect a tradition that Yerushalayim and Dovid HaMelech were one — the Tosefta permits including Dovid within Boneh Yerushalayim. This confirms the reading of the Bach: they are not two themes but one.

If they were originally one, the older count was seventeen berachos. With the addition of Velamalshinim, the number reached eighteen. Our practice today is otherwise: the two are recited separately, and the berachos number nineteen. The Gemara in Megillah records them as separate, and it is that Gemara that we follow.

The Proof from the Kalir’s Kerovetz for Purim

On Purim (פורים) morning the kerovetz (קרובץ) of the Kalir (הקליר) is recited — a piyut (פיוט) corresponding to each berachah of Shemoneh Esrei, tying that berachah to the events of Purim. The Kalir composed a piece for every berachah except Es Tzemach Dovid. In his mesorah there was no separate berachah of Tzemach Dovid; in his time and tradition the two were combined.

The proof is strong, though not conclusive. The absence of a Tzemach Dovid piece may reflect a liturgical rather than a halachic (הלכתי) merger, or some other reason particular to the paytan (פייטן); and the textual witnesses to the older count of the berachos are not uniform.[11]

Who Composed “Yerushalayim Irecha”?

The paytan who composed “Yerushalayim Irecha” (ירושלים עירך) was an early poet of Eretz Yisrael. A major machlokes surrounds his identity: some attribute the piyut to Rabbi Elazar b’Rabbi Shimon (רבי אלעזר ברבי שמעון), the son of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai (רבי שמעון בר יוחאי). Most scholars today, on the strength of discoveries in the Cairo Geniza,[12] hold that he was an earlier, anonymous poet of Eretz Yisrael. Like the Kalir, he wrote no separate piece for Tzemach Dovid — for him, too, the two were one. The Gemara in Megillah, however, records the two as separate berachos, and we follow it.

The Meaning of Kerovos

The Taz (ט״ז) on Orach Chaim,[13] reporting the teaching of a senior member of his family, explains that any piyut incorporated into Shemoneh Esrei is called kerovos (קרובות). Today kerovos are said principally on Purim; in earlier centuries the practice was wider. The pronunciation evolved into “kroivitz” (קרויביץ).

V. The Kavanah of the Arizal — A Cave in Gush Chalav

Certain siddurim — in particular those of nusach Sefard and Edos HaMizrach — print a parenthetical instruction for kavanah at Tzemach Dovid. It may be thought that the Arizal and Kabbalah (קבלה) are not for us, who are simple Yehudim (יהודים). The reality is that an enormous portion of what is said and done in tefillah (תפילה) rests upon the Toras HaArizal (תורת האריז״ל), even when this goes unnoticed.

The Arizal and the Kivrei Tzaddikim

Rav Chaim Vital (רב חיים ויטאל)[14], the principal talmid of the Arizal, used to accompany his rebbe (רבי) on regular journeys to prostrate himself at the graves of tzaddikim (kivrei tzaddikim, קברי צדיקים). Most of the graves of tanna’im (תנאים) and amora’im (אמוראים) in Eretz Yisrael were identified by the Arizal himself. The blue paint still seen on graves in Tzfas (צפת) goes back to him: when he came to Eretz Yisrael he travelled with his talmidim and pointed to particular spots — “Here this tanna is buried; here this amora is buried.” They marked the sites, and the marking became the basis of what is today an entire enterprise of kivrei tzaddikim travel — buses, hotels, food. Baruch Hashem (ברוך השם), the Arizal has provided parnassah (פרנסה) for many Jewish children.

The Cave of Shmaya v’Avtalyon

The Arizal once visited the cave of Shmaya v’Avtalyon (שמעיה ואבטליון) in Gush Chalav (today, in its Arabic name, Jish), a short distance from Tzfas. Shmaya and Avtalyon were the teachers of Hillel (הלל) and Shammai (שמאי). Chazal say that they were geirei tzedek (גרי צדק), or descendants of geirei tzedek; the matter is a machlokes.

Gush Chalav, Ancient and Modern

In ancient times Gush Chalav was a major centre of Jewish lamdus (למדנות), since Shmaya and Avtalyon lived there. They travelled at times to Yerushalayim for the Sanhedrin, but were buried in Gush Chalav. The area later came to have a Muslim majority; today it is home chiefly to some 13,000 Maronite Christians, a Lebanese sect persecuted under Muslim rule but living peacefully within the democratic framework of the State of Israel, with churches and schools, some of them serving in the IDF.

The Revelation

Rav Chaim Vital records that the Arizal entered the small cave of Shmaya v’Avtalyon — a small space with a few kochim (כוכים) — and that when he emerged he told his talmidim that Shmaya and Avtalyon had revealed themselves to him as he prostrated himself there. Such revelations are not granted to us; they are granted to the gedolei Yisrael (גדולי ישראל).

What did they teach him? A new sod (סוד) concerning the kavanah at “Es Tzemach Dovid avdecha”: that Mashiach ben Yosef not be killed by Armilus HaRasha. The Vilna Gaon (גר״א) taught a related kavanah on this berachah.[15]

VI. Armilus HaRasha and the End of Days

What is the subject here? The genre is Jewish eschatology — the study of acharis hayamim (אחרית הימים). The Armageddon and apocalypse of popular imagination are downstream of it; each tradition has its own version. Ours is to be found in our berachos, in the siddur itself.

Sefer Zerubavel and Armilus

Armilus (ארמילוס) first appears in Sefer Zerubavel.[16] Zerubavel (זרובבל) of Tanach (תנ״ך) is, according to Chazal, none other than Nechemiah ben Chachalyah (נחמיה בן חכליה) — the great leader of Klal Yisrael in the era of the return to Tzion (ציון) from Bavel (בבל), a descendant of the tribe of Yehudah (יהודה) and of the royal house of Dovid. Sefer Zerubavel is allegedly the work of Nechemiah himself; it is not part of Tanach. (The Sefer Nechemiah (ספר נחמיה) that we have in Tanach is a different book; Sefer Zerubavel is a separate, mystical work.) The Ibn Ezra (אבן עזרא) at the opening of Shemos (שמות) dismisses its authority; Rav Saadia Gaon (רב סעדיה גאון) in the ninth century quotes it, and Targum Yonasan (תרגום יונתן) on Devarim (דברים) and elsewhere is said to reflect it. The tradition is divided.

According to Sefer Zerubavel, Nechemiah asked the angel Michael (מלאך מיכאל) what would occur at the end of days, and the text records a long account of an Armageddon-like war. The name Armageddon derives from Har Megiddo (הר מגידו), the mountain in northern Eretz Yisrael that was the site of major wars throughout Tanach and on which Yigael Yadin spent much of his career excavating at Tell Megiddo. According to tradition, the great battle of acharis hayamim is to be fought at Har Megiddo.

The name Armilus itself is understood as Romulus, the legendary founder of Rome, raised by wolves in Roman mythology. In our tradition the leader of malchus Edom (מלכות אדום) at the end of days is associated with Romulus or his gilgul (גלגול) — hence Armilus HaRasha. Edom is Rome; Rome is Western civilization; its end-time leader is Armilus.

The War Between Paras and Edom

The Gemara in Yoma 10a[17] records a machlokes over the fate of the two empires: whether Rome is destined to fall into the hands of Persia, or Persia into the hands of Rome. The understanding followed in the shiur is that at the end of days there will be a war between malchus Edom (the West, in the shiur’s framing) and malchus Paras (מלכות פרס) (Iran and the broader Islamic world), in which Edom will ultimately prevail. The view was voiced that this generation is approaching acharis hayamim: Western civilization sits in malchus Edom, Paras sits in the Islamic world, and the conflict is unfolding.

But the leader of Edom — Armilus HaRasha — will come down to Eretz Yisrael and fight Klal Yisrael. According to Sefer Zerubavel, Armilus will reach Har Megiddo and kill Mashiach ben Yosef.

Mashiach ben Yosef and Mashiach ben Dovid

The notion of Mashiach ben Yosef may sound strange. In our tradition Mashiach ben Yosef falls at the hands of Armilus HaRasha; thereafter Mashiach ben Dovid comes and avenges his death, and this brings the geulah.

The kavanah revealed in the cave of Shmaya v’Avtalyon is that when one says “Es Tzemach Dovid avdecha,” one prays that Armilus HaRasha not have the koach (כח) to kill Mashiach ben Yosef — that he survive, and the geulah unfold without that churban (חורבן). This is real substance in our tefillah: we hope for acharis hayamim, and ask HaKadosh Baruch Hu (הקדוש ברוך הוא) that we somehow prevail over Armilus before he can take the life of Mashiach ben Yosef.

The kavanah raises a question that the shiur did not close. Do we pray for his survival, or for his eventual victory through death and vengeance? The sources — Sefer Zerubavel, the Pesikta Rabbasi (פסיקתא רבתי), the Zohar — are not uniform. If the death of Mashiach ben Yosef is a necessary step in the eschatological drama, a prayer for his survival would seem to stand in tension with the received mesorah. The chiddush (חידוש) of the Arizal is that the death is not inevitable, and that the tefillah of Klal Yisrael can avert it.[18]

VII. Tzemach Dovid — Why “Tzemach”?

The word tzemach (צמח) means a sprout — a flower or blade of grass beginning to grow. Pesukim in Tanach repeatedly refer to Mashiach as tzemach, and there is a shitah (שיטה) that his very name is Tzemach.

The Names of Mashiach

The Gemara in Sanhedrin 98b[19] records the names of Mashiach: Shiloh (שילה), Yinnon (ינון), Chaninah (חנינא), and Menachem ben Chizkiyah (מנחם בן חזקיה). The initial letters of Menachem, Yinnon, Shiloh, Chaninah, rearranged, spell Mashiach (משיח). The Gemara in Pesachim 54a[20] teaches that seven things were created before the world — Torah (תורה), teshuvah (תשובה), Gan Eden (גן עדן), Gehinnom (גיהנם), the Kisei HaKavod (כסא הכבוד), the Beis HaMikdash, and the name of Mashiach. The name represents the tachlis of all of beriyah (בריאה), and the geulah through Mashiach is built into the structure of the world from its inception.

The Acrostic “Mashiach Hashem Shemo Chaim”

An acrostic is brought on the name of Mashiach, in the form “Mashiach Hashem shemo Chaim” (משיח ה׳ שמו חיים), and is read as a hint that a gadol (גדול) who bore the name Chaim carried a nitzotz (ניצוץ) of the neshamah (נשמה) of Mashiach. The written summary of the shiur attributed the remez to the Chida (חיד״א) at the close of Parshas Re’eh (פרשת ראה); the shiur itself, as recorded, names the bearer of the nitzotz as Rav Chaim ben Attar (רב חיים בן עטר), the Ohr HaChaim HaKadosh (אור החיים הקדוש).[21]

The Baal Shem Tov and the Gadol HaDor

The Baal Shem Tov (בעל שם טוב)[22] is said to have testified that Rav Chaim ben Attar bore a nitzotz of Mashiach. One Shabbos (שבת) the Baal Shem Tov was washing for seudah shlishis (סעודה שלישית) when an illumination suddenly came upon him. After the meal he told his talmidim: “The gadol hador (גדול הדור) has just passed away.”

How did the Baal Shem Tov in Mezhbizh know that a tzaddik had passed from the world in Eretz Yisrael at that very moment? They asked him, and he answered: “There is a sod in netilas yadayim (נטילת ידים) for seudah shlishis that is known to only one Jew in each generation — the tzaddik hador (צדיק הדור). I never knew it. Suddenly, as I washed for shalosh seudos (שלוש סעודות), the sod came into my head. That means that the tzaddik hador has just passed; the sod was transferred to me.”

The maggid shiur was himself aware that the account circulates widely among the chassidic traditions — it is told, in his words, as a story “everybody has.”

The Meaning of “Tzemach”

Why tzemach? Mashiach is sprouting even now — like a seed deep in the ground that has begun to germinate. It takes time before it breaks the surface. Throughout the generations we are growing the seed of Mashiach; he germinates underground, and one day we shall see him emerge. “Meheirah tatzmiach” (מהרה תצמיח) — let it sprout quickly. “Bimeinu achishenah” (בימינו אחישנה) — in our days, may it be hastened.

VIII. The Tears That Water the Tzemach

Rav Shimshon Schwab (רב שמשון שוואב)[23] writes, in a passage of deep feeling, that every tzemach requires irrigation. What waters the tzemach of Mashiach? The tears shed at “v’al Yerushalayim ircha berachamim tashuv.” If one wept at “Yerushalayim ircha,” one’s tears irrigated the tzemach.

The Arizal in his kavanos (כוונות) on Shemoneh Esrei[24] asks how a Yehudi could fail to weep at “Yerushalayim ircha berachamim tashuv.” The pasuk in Yeshayahu (ישעיהו)[25] reads “malachei shalom mar yivkayun” (מלאכי שלום מר יבכיון): the malachim weep continuously over Yerushalayim — “tamid” (תמיד). We are seeking to console Rachel Imeinu (רחל אמנו), who weeps for her children. How could a Yehudi not weep? Once a year, on Tishah B’Av (תשעה באב), we sit on the floor and speak of Yerushalayim; the rest of the year we live as though it does not weigh upon us. The geulah shleimah (גאולה שלמה) with Yerushalayim and malchus beis Dovid awaits the tears.

IX. “Ki Lishuasecha Kivinu Kol HaYom U’metzapim LiYeshuah”

Whence come the words “u’metzapim liyeshuah” (ומצפים לישועה)? “Ki lishuasecha kivinu kol hayom” (כי לישועתך קוינו כל היום) is in the standard nusach. “U’metzapim liyeshuah” appears in many siddurim in parentheses or brackets. It is recited by those who follow the Sefardi custom, though many Sefardim do not say it.

The Source — A Printer’s Mistake

The Arizal (or his talmidim in his name) wrote that when one says “ki lishuasecha kivinu kol hayom,” the kavanah should be the following. The Gemara in Shabbos 31a[26] teaches that among the questions asked in the beis din shel maalah (בית דין של מעלה) is “Tzipisa liyeshuah?” (צפית לישועה) — did you await Mashiach?

What shame shall be ours when we are asked, “Did you await Mashiach?” Are we waiting? Mashiach is not a bumper sticker. To await Mashiach in truth means to hope that the world should be a better place — that humanity should be elevated. We shall be embarrassed.

The Arizal accordingly wrote: have kavanah that you truly desire the yeshuah (ישועה), so that when you come to Shamayim (שמים) you may answer, “Yes — every day I anticipated the yeshuah.”

This kavanah was meant to be printed in brackets as an instruction — not as part of the nusach. Somewhere in the printing history, however, a printer brought the bracketed kavanah into the text of the nusach itself. So we say “u’metzapim liyeshuah” — by mistake, according to the Likutei Ari (לקוטי אר״י).[27] Some kehillos (קהילות) noticed and removed it. In either case the kavanah remains the point: whether in brackets or in the nusach, this is what is sought.

X. What the Berachah Asks of Us — Geulah and Separation from the Nations

The tears shed for Yerushalayim cannot be separated from the condition that occasions them: the exile in which Klal Yisrael still lives, not yet fully separate from the nations among whom it dwells. It is here that Rav Shimshon Raphael Hirsch (רב שמשון רפאל הירש) is quoted at length on this berachah, together with the testimony of his successor in the Frankfurt tradition, Rav Schwab.[28] The geulah of Klal Yisrael, with Yerushalayim and Mashiach, is beyond our full grasp. But at that point Klal Yisrael will be separated from umos ha’olam (אומות העולם).

We do not realise, writes Rav Hirsch, how much of the philosophy and hashkafah (השקפה) of the nations has infiltrated our ranks. The pasuk in Tehillim (תהלים) alludes to it: some Jews spend their entire lives fighting off the hashpaah (השפעה) of the surrounding nations.

The Generations Move Down

As the generations draw nearer to the geulah, two things occur: Klal Yisrael becomes more affected by the nations, and the nations themselves grow worse.

The example of Rav Schwab, in the Frankfurt tradition of Rav Hirsch: when he was a child in Germany, before the First World War, there was no such thing as mixed swimming. Even the German gentiles kept that boundary — even the supposedly cultured Germans, full of derech eretz (דרך ארץ), who would later murder six million. This changed after the First World War. The world became more degenerate, more lustful — parutz (פרוץ). After the war it became normalised in Europe; today it is universal. Can it be said that the outlook of the world does not creep into Klal Yisrael?

The measure of bushah (בושה) and tznius (צניעות) in our community is lower than it was a generation ago, and lower than the generation before that. We must remember who we are. We are bnei malachim (בני מלאכים); we are “rachmanim, bayshanim, gomlei chasadim” (רחמנים, ביישנים, גומלי חסדים). Bushah is in the very makeup of Klal Yisrael. We do not see it because we are so deeply influenced by the nations around us.

Up the Ladder and Down

A further observation. In Germany, Jews who wished to integrate with the upper classes went to university and became authors, poets, philosophers, doctors. Later the imitation went down the social ladder as well. In America in the 1920s and 1930s, the lower echelons were copied too: there was a Jewish mafia. The maggid shiur knew a Yehudi in Miami whose grandfather owned a kosher restaurant where Meyer Lansky and his associates used to sit; Lansky was a Jew. Another, Benjamin “Bugsy” Siegel, is credited with founding Las Vegas — so that, as the sardonic remark has it, not only Eretz Yisrael is ours, but Las Vegas as well. How did we sink so low?

Yearning

Says Rav Hirsch: we do not have Yerushalayim; we do not have malchus beis Dovid; we do not have geulah. We should yearn for the days when we shall not be trying to copy the nations — the days when they will try to copy us, when we shall be a light to the nations. One must pause and reflect how low we have fallen, even within the ultra-Orthodox community. Baruch Hashem, we keep Torah u’mitzvos (תורה ומצוות); but the Jewish nation across the generations has been drawn along by what the nations were doing.

For this reason we should truly yearn for the days of an elevated life — with Yerushalayim, with the Beis HaMikdash, with korbanos (קרבנות), with malchus beis Dovid. Meheirah tatzmiach; bimeinu achishenah.
