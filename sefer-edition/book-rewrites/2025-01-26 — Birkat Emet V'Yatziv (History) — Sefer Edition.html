בִּרְכַּת אֱמֶת וְיַצִּיב — הַהִיסְטוֹרְיָה הַנִּסְתֶּרֶת

Birkat Emet V'Yatziv: The Hidden History
Shiur of Rabbi Ephrati — Sunday Morning Series on Tefillah
Recorded כ״ו טבת תשפ״ה · 26 January 2025  ·  Sefer Edition

The longest berachah in the siddur, and the only long berachah that opens without a Baruch. Following the Shibolei HaLeket (שיבולי הלקט) we discover that the opening chain — “אמת ויציב ונכון וקיים…” — preserves, verbatim, an ancient letter of acceptance sent from the communities of the galus back to Eretz Yisrael (ארץ ישראל) after an inquiry about David HaMelech's takanah of one hundred daily berachos. The Shibolei HaLeket's access to this secret runs through one remarkable Roman summer in 1280 — a chavrusa shared with the Riaz (ריא"ז) and with the most controversial figure of the age, Rabbeinu Avraham Abulafia (רבינו אברהם אבולעפיא).

I. The Berachah and Its Structural Puzzle

The Shema (שמע) of Shacharis (שחרית) is bracketed by three berachos: two before — Yotzer Or (יוצר אור) and Ahavah Rabbah (אהבה רבה), or, in the nusach (נוסח) of some communities, Ahavas Olam (אהבת עולם) — and one after: the berachah that opens with “Emet V'Yatziv” (אמת ויציב) and closes with “Baruch Atah Hashem, Ga'al Yisrael” (ברוך אתה ה' גאל ישראל).

This berachah is unlike any other. It is the longest in the siddur. And it carries, on its face, a structural anomaly of the first order. Every long berachah ordinarily opens with the formula “Baruch Atah Hashem” and closes with the same. Emet V'Yatziv closes in the standard form — but where is its opening “Baruch”? The answer, as the Rishonim (ראשונים) develop it, requires first understanding what this berachah in fact is.

II. The Chiyuv: Zechiras Yetzias Mitzrayim

The Gemara (גמרא) in Berachos (ברכות) rules:[1]

כל מי שלא אמר אמת ויציב בשחרית ואמת ואמונה בערבית — לא יצא ידי חובתו.
Whoever did not say Emet V'Yatziv in Shacharis or Emet V'Emunah (אמת ואמונה) in Ma'ariv (מעריב) has not fulfilled his obligation.
Berachos 12a

Which obligation? The mitzvah (מצוה) of zechiras yetzias Mitzrayim (זכירת יציאת מצרים) — the daily commandment to remember the Exodus. The Sefer HaChinuch (ספר החינוך)[2] teaches that of all the zechiros (זכירות) which the Torah commands — the six recited at the close of the siddur: Amalek (עמלק), the incident of Miriam (מרים), the Shabbos Kodesh (שבת קודש), and the rest — only one requires verbal expression. The others may be discharged in the heart, by thought alone. Zechiras yetzias Mitzrayim is the singular exception. Chazal (חז"ל) require it to be articulated aloud, morning and night; and the vessels of that articulation are precisely Emet V'Yatziv and Emet V'Emunah.

The Attachment of Emet to the Shema

The pasuk (פסוק) at the close of parshas tzitzis (פרשת ציצית) in Parshas Shelach reads “Ani Hashem Elokeichem” (אני ה' אלקיכם)[3] — without the word emet. Chazal, however, join emet to the close of the Shema, drawing upon the verse in Yirmiyahu (ירמיהו): “Vahashem Elokim emet” (וה' אלקים אמת).[4] Functionally the word attaches to the tail of Kriyas Shema (קריאת שמע); in truth it is the opening word of the coming berachah. The Shema and Emet V'Yatziv flow as one continuous declaration.

L'hagid BaBoker Chasdecha

The Gemara grounds the twofold structure — verbal remembrance morning and night — in Tehillim (תהילים):[5]

להגיד בבקר חסדך, ואמונתך בלילות.
To relate Your kindness in the morning, and Your faithfulness at night.
Tehillim 92:3

“L'hagid baboker chasdecha” is fulfilled through Emet V'Yatziv, a berachah saturated with the chasadim (חסדים) of the Ribbono Shel Olam (רבונו של עולם) — yetzias Mitzrayim, krias Yam Suf (קריעת ים סוף), the beloved of His youth. “V'emunas'cha baleilos” is fulfilled through Emet V'Emunah — the declaration of faith that as the Holy One rescued us in every generation of history, so will He bring the final geulah (גאולה). The essential mitzvah throughout remains zechiras yetzias Mitzrayim; chesed and emunah are the further dimensions layered upon it.

III. The Five Components of the Yerushalmi

The Yerushalmi (ירושלמי) in Berachos,[6] as codified by the Tur and the Rosh (רא"ש) and ruled to halacha (הלכה) by the Mishnah Berurah (משנה ברורה),[7] requires that Birkat Emet V'Yatziv contain five constitutive components. Omission of any one leaves the berachah deficient and the obligation unfulfilled:

  1. Zechiras yetzias Mitzrayim — the essence.
  2. Malchus (מלכות) — repeatedly: Malkeinu, Melech avoseinu, Go'aleinu, Go'el avoseinu.
  3. Krias Yam Suf.
  4. Makkas Bechoros (מכת בכורות) — the crown of the plagues upon Mitzrayim.
  5. Shiras Yisrael (שירת ישראל) and their geulah — “Shirah chadashah shibchu geulim…”

This is the internal architecture of the berachah: a compressed retelling of the redemption, rendered so that the daily chiyuv (חיוב) of zechiras yetzias Mitzrayim is discharged in full.

IV. The Opening Chain: The Language of a Letter

What arrests the attentive reader is the very first line: “Emet v'yatziv v'nachon v'kayam v'yashar v'ne'eman v'ahuv v'chaviv v'nechmad v'na'im v'nora v'adir u'mesukan u'mekubal v'tov v'yafeh haDavar hazeh” — a chain of fifteen adjectives, each linked by vav (ו), followed by the words “haDavar hazeh” (הדבר הזה). No other berachah from Chazal reads in this way. The cascade of conjunctions, the reaching for one epithet after another, the explicit language of acceptance — all this is unlike the compressed and measured lashon (לשון) of the standard tefillos. It is the language, unmistakably, of a formal document rather than a composed berachah.

What document? For the answer the Rishonim turn to a work whose author is himself one of the more remarkable figures of the Italian Rishonim: the Shibolei HaLeket (שיבולי הלקט) — Rabbeinu Tzidkiyah ben Avraham HaRofeh (רבינו צדקיה בן אברהם הרופא), of thirteenth-century Rome.[8]

V. Rome and the Four Noble Families

The Shibolei HaLeket belongs to one of the most storied communities in our history. Following the Churban (חורבן), the Roman emperor Titus exiled to his capital many Jewish families of distinguished lineage. Among them were four aristocratic families, the most eminent houses of Yerushalayim (ירושלים), who settled together in Rome, intermarried across the centuries, and preserved intact the minhagim (מנהגים), tefillos, berachos, and formulas which they had carried out of the destroyed city. These are the arba mishpachos meyuchasos b'Yisrael (ארבע משפחות מיוחסות בישראל), whose yichus (יחוס) reached directly back to the days of the Bayis Sheini (בית שני):

  1. Mishpachas HaAnavim (משפחת הענבים) — the family of the “humble.”
  2. Mishpachas HaAdumim (משפחת האדומים).
  3. Mishpachas HaTappuchim (משפחת התפוחים).
  4. Mishpachas HaBachurim (משפחת הבחורים).

Each family bore its own seal. Between them they produced the leading poskim, darshanim, and rashei yeshivos (ראשי ישיבות) of Italian Jewry, and shared a single beis knesses (בית כנסת) in Rome known as The Synagogue of the Four Heads, where the heads of the four houses davened together.

From these families emerged Rabbeinu Nasan Ba'al HaAruch (רבינו נתן בעל הערוך),[9] author of the first Talmudic lexicon and among the Rishonim cited constantly by Tosafos — he was of Mishpachas HaAnavim; Rabbi Azariah HaAdumim (רבי עזריה האדומים), of Mishpachas HaAdumim; and the Shibolei HaLeket himself, likewise of Mishpachas HaAnavim. The Roman kehillah (קהילה) to this day preserves its own distinctive nusach, tracing every particular of it back to the Beis HaMikdash (בית המקדש); the poskim have ruled that one whose family minhag follows the Roman rite is not to be disturbed in it.

VI. The Shibolei HaLeket and the Beis Yosef

The influence of the Shibolei HaLeket upon our daily practice is difficult to overstate. In Orach Chaim (אורח חיים) — the very domain in which the sefer concentrates its rulings — the Beis Yosef (בית יוסף) and the Shulchan Aruch (שולחן ערוך) cite him every few pages.[10] An astonishing number of the small details that shape a person's day, from waking to sleeping, rest upon his authority alone. Among the Rishonim who set the halachic architecture of late thirteenth-century Ashkenaz and Italy, he stands among the most consequential.

VII. Rabbeinu Avraham Abulafia

To understand how the Shibolei HaLeket came into possession of the secret behind Emet V'Yatziv, we must pause to attend to one of the most unusual figures of the Rishonim: Rabbeinu Avraham Abulafia (c. 1240 — c. 1291).

The First Thirty Years

For the first three decades of his life, Rabbeinu Avraham was a conventional talmid chacham (תלמיד חכם), a student in the halachic-Tosafist stream of his time.[11] Nothing in his early years foretold the storm that would surround him. Then, at the age of thirty-one, he reports having received a prophetic vision — recorded in his autobiographical Sefer HaEdus (ספר העדות)[12] — directing him to travel to Rome and seek an audience with the Pope, that some reconciliation might be effected between Israel and the Christian world. He spent the next ten years preparing himself for that mission.

The Kabbalah of Meditation and the Cherem of the Rashba

In the years of preparation, Rabbeinu Avraham developed and began to teach a distinctive method of Kabbalah (קבלה). Its character was experiential: a Kabbalah not only studied but practiced, entered through meditative yichudim (יחודים) built upon the sheimos (שמות) of the Holy One, the letters, and the numbers. He opened yeshivos in Sefarad and in Italy and drew students.

The novelty of the method — and, still more, certain messianic overtones in his self-presentation — aroused sharp opposition. The community appealed to the Rashba (רשב"א), the gadol hador and the supreme halachic authority of Sefarad. In a scathing teshuvah,[13] the Rashba issued a cherem (חרם): he forbade the copying, learning, or dissemination of Rabbeinu Avraham's writings, and directed Klal Yisrael (כלל ישראל) to distance itself from him. From that day — roughly the year 1270 — through the end of his life, Rabbeinu Avraham wandered from city to city, refused everywhere on the Rashba's authority. He continued to write, but almost nothing of his was copied openly, nothing entered the mainstream batei midrash.

The Chida's Rehabilitation

His works survived through narrow channels — Rabbeinu Chaim Vital (רבינו חיים ויטאל) cites him a small number of times, the Ramak (רמ"ק) makes reference to him[14] — but for centuries his name was largely absent from the standard shelves. It was the Chida (חיד"א), in his encyclopedic Shem HaGedolim (שם הגדולים),[15] who reopened the door. Acknowledging both the Rashba's cherem and the rejection of the method in its own time, the Chida nonetheless declared, on the basis of his own study, that Rabbeinu Avraham was a giant in Kabbalah and that his sefarim deserved to be learned. From that acknowledgement a slow return began; today his sefarim are being systematically brought to press.

VIII. The Rome Encounter of 1280

The Journey to the Pope

In the summer of 1280, Rabbeinu Avraham arrived in Rome to fulfill what he had understood as his charge ten years earlier. The Pope's officers refused him an audience. The Pope of that summer, Nicholas III, had withdrawn to a papal country residence outside the city.[16] Rabbeinu Avraham resolved to circumvent the officers and approach the country palace directly, though he was warned that if apprehended he would be burned alive. He was moser nefesh (מוסר נפש) — this is not a detail he learned from others but from his own writing in Sefer HaEdus — and he set out. He records the date: Yud-Chet Elul (י"ח אלול), four days before Rosh HaShanah (ראש השנה).

He reached the palace and knocked. The answer at the door was that the Pope had died the previous night, suddenly. Rabbeinu Avraham read this as a siman min haShamayim (סימן מן השמים) — that the forces of concealment had risen to obstruct his mission, and that HaKadosh Baruch Hu (הקדוש ברוך הוא) had stood with him. He returned to Rome for Rosh HaShanah; on Yom HaKippurim (יום הכפורים) he was arrested. Rather than being executed he was held for a matter of weeks, then released with an order to leave. He fled to Sicily, where he lived out his final years. His grave is unknown.

The Chavrusa with the Shibolei HaLeket and the Riaz

Yet the encounter that shaped our daily tefillah was not with the Pope. Before the summer approach to the papal residence — during the weeks Rabbeinu Avraham spent in Rome itself — he was received by the gedolei ha'ir (גדולי העיר). Foremost among them was the Shibolei HaLeket, of Mishpachas HaAnavim. Meeting Rabbeinu Avraham, he was struck by the breadth of his knowledge and resolved to learn with him.

The chavrusa-text they selected was itself a sefer that had, in earlier generations, been the cause of cherem: the Moreh Nevuchim (מורה נבוכים) of the Rambam (רמב"ם).[17] The Shibolei HaLeket had not yet learned it. Joining them was a third chaver (חבר): Rabbeinu Yeshaya ben Mali di Trani — the Riaz,[18] whose pirush upon the Rif (רי"ף) every bachur and ben kollel learns to this day. The Riaz was a cousin of the Shibolei HaLeket, likewise of the four Roman families, and a nephew of the elder Rid of Tosafos (תוספות רי"ד).[19]

The three of them — the leading posek of Orach Chaim, the master commentator on the Rif, and the most controversial mekubal (מקובל) of the age — sat together for perhaps a month and learned the Moreh Nevuchim. The tradition records that shortly after the chavrusa concluded, both the Shibolei HaLeket and the Riaz were niftar (נפטר).

That month with Rabbeinu Avraham left its impress upon the Shibolei HaLeket. Kabbalistic elements which do not appear in his earlier work make their way into the halachic sefer he composed thereafter. What Rabbeinu Avraham could not publish under his own name — his teachings held under the Rashba's cherem — entered the mainstream of halacha quietly, through the pen of the great Roman posek.

IX. The Secret of Emet V'Yatziv

The Question

The Shibolei HaLeket, in his Hilchos Kriyas Shema, reveals what he received. The berachah proclaims: “V'yatziv v'nachon v'kayam v'yashar v'ne'eman…” But what is emet v'yatziv? This thing is true and upright and enduring… — but which thing? The Kriyas Shema just recited? The seal “Hashem Elokeichem emes”? The coming chasimah (חתימה) of “Ga'al Yisrael”?

The Answer through Kabbalah

The Shibolei HaLeket answers — drawing, in the reasonable reading, upon the Kabbalah he had received in that Roman chavrusa — that the long chain refers back to what has just been said: the Kriyas Shema, sealed by “Hashem Elokeichem emes.” The Jew affirms: this declaration — the acceptance of the yoke of Malchus Shamayim and the emes of the Torah that closes it — is emet, yatziv, nachon, kayam… true and upright and accepted upon us.

The Gimatria

The Shibolei HaLeket then discloses what is encoded in the very letters of the passage. Count the vavs in the descriptive chain that follows the opening word: “v'yatziv v'nachon v'kayam v'yashar v'ne'eman v'ahuv v'chaviv v'nechmad v'na'im v'nora v'adir u'mesukan u'mekubal v'tov v'yafeh”:

  • Fifteen vavs.
  • 15 × 6 (the numerical value of vav) = 90.
  • Add the two heis (ה) of “haDavar hazeh” (5 + 5 = 10): 100.

The chain is bounded precisely where the numerical elegance requires: the descriptive adjectives close at “v'yafeh,” and it is the demonstrative “haDavar hazeh” that concludes the frame. Everything from “emes Elokei olam Malkeinu…” onward is a new movement of the berachah. The Shibolei HaLeket himself calls the gimatria “דבר נפלא עד מאד” — a matter of exceeding wonder.

X. The One Hundred Berachos and the Letter of Acceptance

The Takanah of David HaMelech

The Tur at the opening of Hilchos Berachos, citing Rav Natronai Gaon (רב נטרונאי גאון),[20] records: in the days of David HaMelech (דוד המלך) there was a magefah (מגפה) taking one hundred lives each day. David inquired in Heaven; the answer came that Klal Yisrael was to draw more berachos into daily life — one hundred berachos a day. David HaMelech instituted the takanah (תקנה). The berachos which bracket Kriyas Shema in Shacharis and Ma'ariv are counted within that hundred.

The Letter

The Shibolei HaLeket continues. In the days of the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah (אנשי כנסת הגדולה), when the takanah of the hundred daily berachos was being restored and firmly established, an inquiry was issued from the Chachmei Eretz Yisrael (חכמי ארץ ישראל) to the communities of the galus (גלות): Do you accept upon yourselves the takanah of one hundred berachos daily?

The reply came back. The language of the reply was, precisely, what we now recite each morning:

אמת ויציב ונכון וקיים וישר ונאמן ואהוב וחביב ונחמד ונעים ונורא ואדיר ומתוקן ומקובל וטוב ויפה הדבר הזה.
True and upright and correct and enduring and just and faithful and beloved and dear and delightful and pleasing and awesome and mighty and well-arranged and accepted and good and beautiful is this thing.

Every word is the language of formal acceptance. Mekubal — accepted. Nechmad, na'im — delightful, pleasing. “This thing,” the one hundred berachos, “is true upon us, and we receive it forever.” And encoded in the very grammar of the acceptance — the fifteen conjunctive vavs plus the two heis of the demonstrative — is the number one hundred, precisely the number of berachos the letter accepts.

Chazal received that letter of acceptance, and they placed it in the mouth of Klal Yisrael: as the opening of the berachah which itself follows Kriyas Shema, so that twice each day — morning and night — the Jew renews, in the original wording, the original commitment.

XI. The Structural Puzzle Resolved

Now the anomaly with which we began yields. The berachah opens without a “Baruch” because its opening is not, strictly, the opening of a berachah at all. It is the continuation of Kriyas Shema through the seam-word “emes” into the verbatim text of a historical document — the letter of acceptance from the galus. It is a berachah ha'semuchah la'chaverta (ברכה הסמוכה לחברתה), joined to the recitation that precedes it, which for that reason does not require its own opening formula. Only at the far end, when the historical narration reaches its climax in the shirah of Krias Yam Suf, does the berachah assume its standard chasimah: “Baruch Atah Hashem, Ga'al Yisrael.”

And a quiet historical lesson is folded within. The Torah of a Rishon placed under cherem by the Rashba, whose books were shut for centuries, traveled into the daily tefillah of Klal Yisrael through the pen of a Roman colleague and the length of a single summer's chavrusa. Every morning of our lives, we have been reciting words in whose transmission Rabbeinu Avraham Abulafia had a hand.

XII. Conclusion

The longest berachah in the siddur, and the one whose missing opening Baruch troubles the eye of every learner: on the Shibolei HaLeket's reading, its opening is not composition but preservation — an ancient letter of acceptance carried in unbroken transmission from the days of the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah through the four noble houses of Rome into our own siddur. Fifteen vavs and two heis: one hundred, the number of berachos that stand between the Jew and the magefah, and the number he undertakes anew each morning by the mere reciting of the words.

This is one of four ma'halachim that the Rishonim develop upon “v'yatziv v'nachon,” each with its own texture; b'ezras Hashem the coming shiur will take up the remaining three. May it be granted us to daven with kavvanah (כוונה), to discharge the hundred daily berachos as David HaMelech instituted them, and to serve the Ribbono Shel Olam b'simchah u'v'tuv leivav (בשמחה ובטוב לבב).

The narrative of the Rome trip is preserved almost entirely in Rabbeinu Avraham's own writing; he is the principal source for his own biography, and Rabbi Ephrati notes that this material is drawn from Sefer HaEdus rather than from any outside record. ↩
  13. Shu"t HaRashba, חלק א', in the vicinity of סימן תקמ"ח. — The Rashba's cherem against Rabbeinu Avraham Abulafia: prohibition on the copying, learning, or dissemination of his writings; direction to Klal Yisrael to distance itself from him. The precise siman varies by edition; the response was directed principally against Rabbeinu Avraham's messianic self-presentation and its perceived risks. Modern scholarship (Idel and others) has noted that the ban did not fully halt the manuscript circulation of Rabbeinu Avraham's teaching in certain kabbalistic circles, though its effect upon mainstream reception was severe and lasting. ↩
  14. R' Chaim Vital (general reference); R' Moshe Cordovero (Ramak). — The narrow channels through which Rabbeinu Avraham Abulafia's name survived in the mainstream literature between the Rashba's cherem and the Chida's rehabilitation. His teaching continued to circulate in kabbalistic manuscripts throughout the intervening centuries, but was largely absent from the standard printed corpus until the Chida's intervention. ↩
  15. Chida (R' Chaim Yosef David Azulai), Shem HaGedolim, entry on R' Avraham Abulafia. — The Chida's rehabilitation: acknowledging the Rashba's cherem and the rejection of the method in its time, while affirming on the basis of his own study that Rabbeinu Avraham was a giant in Kabbalah and that his sefarim deserve to be learned. The Chida's authority opened the slow return of the sefarim to print; a substantial number are now available, with additional titles continuing to appear. ↩
  16. Pope Nicholas III (Giovanni Gaetano Orsini), d. 22 August 1280 at the papal residence at Soriano nel Cimino. — The standard historical dating places Nicholas III's death on 22 August 1280 at Soriano nel Cimino, a papal country residence north of Rome, rather than at Castel Gandolfo (which entered use as a papal summer estate only in the seventeenth century). Rabbeinu Avraham's own account in Sefer HaEdus speaks of a papal residence outside Rome; the identification is with the summer estate of that era, not with the site the popes have used since the time of Urban VIII. Contemporary sources describe the death as sudden, without specifying a modern medical cause. ↩
  17. Rambam, Moreh Nevuchim. — The chavrusa-text of the Roman trio in 1280: the Shibolei HaLeket, the Riaz, and Rabbeinu Avraham Abulafia. The Rambam's philosophical work, itself in earlier generations the cause of cherem; the Shibolei HaLeket had not yet learned it before this Roman summer. ↩
  18. R' Yeshaya ben Mali di Trani (Riaz), Pesakim on the Rif. — The third chaver in Rome; cousin of the Shibolei HaLeket; nephew of the elder Rid (Tosafos Rid). The Riaz's pirush on the Rif is standard learning material in yeshivos and kollelim. Tradition: he and the Shibolei HaLeket were both niftar shortly after completing the Moreh Nevuchim with Rabbeinu Avraham. ↩
  19. R' Yeshaya di Trani the Elder (Rid), Tosafos Rid. — Uncle of the Riaz; major Rishon of southern Italy; of the same Roman aristocratic context as the four families. The Tosafos Rid is among the standard Rishonim printed alongside the Gemara. ↩
  20. Tur, או"ח, opening of Hilchos Berachos; citing Rav Natronai Gaon. — The institutional account of the hundred daily berachos: a magefah in the days of David HaMelech taking one hundred lives a day; the heavenly response requiring one hundred berachos daily; David HaMelech's takanah; the berachos bracketing Kriyas Shema as part of the count. The framework within which the letter of acceptance from the galus — preserved as the opening of Emet V'Yatziv — sits as the original communal commitment. ↩
