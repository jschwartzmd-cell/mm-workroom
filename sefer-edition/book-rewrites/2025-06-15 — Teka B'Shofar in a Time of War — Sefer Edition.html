תְּקַע בְּשׁוֹפָר — בְּעֵת מִלְחָמָה

Teka B’Shofar in a Time of War — The Hinge of Shemoneh Esrei and the Shofar Gadol
Shiur of Rabbi Ephrati — Sunday Morning Series on Tefillah
Recorded 15 June 2025  ·  Sefer Edition

Delivered two days into the war with Iran. Dedicated to the חייל killed that morning in עזה, and to the welfare of אחינו בני ישראל in ארץ ישראל.

וְתָקַע בְּשׁוֹפָר גָּדוֹל לְחֵרוּתֵנוּ, וְשָׂא נֵס לְקַבֵּץ גָּלֻיּוֹתֵינוּ. Sound the great shofar for our freedom, and raise a banner to gather our exiles.

The streets of בני ברק stood empty. Missiles had fallen in בת ים, and three children were among the dead. The קהילות of Lawrence had begun saying אבינו מלכנו that morning.

The shiur turns in that hour to the ברכה of תקע בשופר: the hinge on which שמונה עשרה turns from the private בקשות to the communal, from רפואה and פרנסה up to קיבוץ נדחי עמו ישראל — which the גמרא installs as the deepest מנוחת הנפש a יהודי can ask for. From there to the two readings of the שופר גדול itself: the Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer reading of the right horn of the איל העקידה awaiting אליהו הנביא's hand, and Rav Shimshon Schwab זצ"ל's reading of the שופר as the universal הכרה moment — the hour when every head of state publicly declares that his throne is מלכות הקדוש ברוך הוא.

I. The Hour

Empty Streets

Someone sent a photograph from בני ברק — the עיר ואם בישראל[1], the city that learns and davens day and night, the city that does not sleep — empty. The streets, empty. ברוך השם, the קהילה is following the instructions of פיקוד העורף; but it is frightening to see those streets as they were. People are sheltering at home.

Word came while we spoke: three children killed in Bat Yam. Missiles falling everywhere. Devastation.[2]

Trust and Hishtadlus

The גאון רבי חיים קנייבסקי זצ"ל, during the first Gulf War when Scuds were flying toward בני ברק, said: "כל טיל יש לו כתובת"[3] — every missile has its address. That is believed absolutely. And in the same breath, the חיוב of hishtadlus (השתדלות) for pikuach nefesh (פיקוח נפש) is not suspended; one goes to the miklat (מקלט). Both halves are the tradition; neither cancels the other.

Several of our Lawrence shuls — the Young Israel of Lawrence and Cedarhurst among them — began saying אבינו מלכנו this morning. The specific halachos (הלכות) governing the recitation of אבינו מלכנו outside the Aseres Yemei Teshuvah require checking;[4] but when כלל ישראל is reaching for אבינו מלכנו, the moment is real.

Seriousness

I saw a picture of three chayalim (חיילים) davening at the Kosel (כותל) — heartbreaking. Michael's son was at the כותל Friday night: he must be told that when there is an azakah (אזעקה), he goes to the מקלט. This is not an hour for heroics; it is pikuach nefesh, and pikuach nefesh is docheh ha’kol.

We dedicate today's divrei Torah (דברי תורה) to the welfare of acheinu Bnei Yisrael (אחינו בני ישראל) in ארץ ישראל, and especially to the chayal killed this morning in Azah (עזה) — we forget there is still a war ongoing in עזה alongside everything else.

רבונו של עולם, we need rachamei Shamayim (רחמי שמים). We need to feel the tzaar (צער). A little less smiles, a little less jokes, a register of seriousness. They are laughing less when they run to the miklatim (מקלטים).

For the Leadership of Medinas Yisrael

Someone sent a video of Yidden singing Kabbalas Shabbos in a bomb shelter. The decisions at the head of Medinas Yisrael are unbearably weighty — when to strike, whom, how. השם ירחם. הקדוש ברוך הוא is with us; we will be victorious; perhaps we are at the threshold of the geulah sheleimah (גאולה שלמה).

II. The Shofar Already Sounding — Hashgachah in This Week's Events

Before we open the ברכה itself, one further observation about the hour. The Hezbollah pager operation of the previous autumn, and the strike this week on the Iranian drone-headquarters: whatever one makes of them as military events, their only honest reading — by any yarei shamayim watching from any angle — is Baruch Hu, not kochi v’otzem yadi (כחי ועצם ידי).[5] I am more nispoel from the drone-headquarters strike than from any battlefield report: no human doctrine of planning accounts for it. Hold this in mind; it returns later in the shiur, when Rav Schwab's reading of the shofar gadol turns out to describe exactly this register of hakarah.

III. ברכת תקע בשופר — From Personal to Communal

There is no ברכה more meaningful at this moment in the tekufah (תקופה) of כלל ישראל. We are in a difficult תקופה; every יהודי who says תקע בשופר wants to hear that שופר.

The Seder in מגילה י"ז

The גמרא[6] gives the seder of the middle brachos of שמונה עשרה. After ברך עלינו we close the arc of personal bakashos (בקשות) — daas, teshuvah, selichah, geulah peratis (גאולה פרטית), refuah, parnassah — and turn to the bakashos of the klal. The Gemara grounds each adjacency in a specific pasuk; underneath those pesukim, the Rebbe here reads a hashkafic ascent: a person first asks for refuah (bri’us as the precondition of everything); then for parnassah (the basis of menuchas ha’nefesh, מנוחת הנפש, and yishuv ha’daas, יישוב הדעת); then for the deepest menuchas ha’nefesh a יהודי can ask for — "וקבצנו יחד מארבע כנפות הארץ". Kibbutz nedachim (קיבוץ נדחים) is the deepest shalom.

In a time of war the frame reads differently than in a time of quiet. Teka B’Shofar is not primarily, in that hour, an eschatological petition about the end of days: it is the bakashah for peace-as-reunion, for the ingathering that is the end of running to miklatim.

IV. The Midrash — The Reunion in מצרים

Each ברכה in שמונה עשרה was first uttered by the malachei ha’shareis (מלאכי השרת) at one moment of biblical history.[7] "ברוך מקבץ נדחי עמו ישראל" was first proclaimed when Yaakov Avinu (יעקב אבינו) and the shevatim (שבטים) arrived in מצרים and were reunited — after Yosef's years in galus Mitzrayim (גלות מצרים) and Shimon's imprisonment. The first time the family — the nucleus of כלל ישראל, the twelve shevatim complete — stood as one.

Note the strangeness of the location. The bracha of mekabetz nidchei amo Yisrael was first uttered in Mitzrayim, at the threshold of the galus — not at its end. The malachim, watching the family stand complete before the long dispersion opened, saw in that moment of reunion the prototype of every later ingathering: the galus yet to come would be measured, in Shamayim, against the first time the family had stood whole. From that reunion the bracha enters the siddur.

May we be zocheh to see the ingathering of כלל ישראל soon, and join the malachim in saying it.

V. What Is the Shofar? — The Pshat Question

The pesukim in Tanach (תנ"ך) we read on Rosh HaShanah (ראש השנה) seem to indicate that we will literally hear a shofar — an actual blast that grows and grows until the entire world hears it and gathers to ארץ ישראל. Is the shofar gadol physical, or metaphorical?

Underneath the dispute lies a classical axis of Jewish thought. The Rambam, in Hilchos Melachim 12,[8] cautions against being mesapek in the aggadic particulars of the days of Mashiach: "a person should not occupy himself in the aggados on this subject, nor spend time on the midrashim... for these matters neither bring to love nor to fear"; the actual course of events will be known only when it happens. On the other pole stands the Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer — which specifies the physical origin of the shofar gadol itself.

The Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer — Physical, and Traceable

The midrash[9]: the shofar is physical, and traceable. When Avraham Avinu (אברהם אבינו) found the ayil ne’echaz ba’svach b’karnav (איל נאחז בסבך בקרניו) — the ram caught in the thicket — and offered it in place of Yitzchak, the two karnos (קרנות) of that ram entered Jewish history:

  • The left horn sounded the kol shofar (קול שופר) at Matan Torah (מתן תורה) — "ויהי במשך בקרן היובל", in the midrash's own quotation; what we hear in the pesukim of Yisro as "וקול שופר חזק מאד".
  • The right horn, which is larger than the left, is the shofar shel Mashiach — held in reserve since the akeidah (עקידה), awaiting the moment of "והיה ביום ההוא יתקע בשופר גדול" (Yeshayahu 27:13).

The connection is dense and beautiful: the tzaar of the akeidah, Avraham's and Yitzchak's mesiras nefesh (מסירת נפש), the ram caught in the thicket — that very horn closes our story. Another midrash adds that Eliyahu HaNavi (אליהו הנביא) will be the one to blow it.

The Tur — Teka B’Shofar and the Chatzotzros

This week's parsha (Beha’aloscha, בהעלותך) contains the pasuk of the chatzotzros:[10] "ותקעתם בחצוצרות על עולותיכם ועל זבחי שלמיכם". The Tur, in Orach Chaim סימן קי"ח, connects teka b’shofar to the moment we return to ארץ ישראל with korbanos in the Beis HaMikdash: both are teki’os. The Tur's point is deepened by the Rambam's treatment of the chatzotzros in Hilchos Taaniyos 1:1 as the sound-signature of communal tzarah: a klal-shaped sound, both for the bakashah and for the eventual return.

VI. Rav Schwab — The Metaphor

Rav Shimshon Schwab זצ"ל[11] proposes a different reading of the shofar gadol: it need not be physical. It is a mashal (משל).

Rav Schwab was niftar in 1995 — this year, thirty years ago — and the sefer Iyun Tefillah (now also published in English as Rav Schwab on Prayer) came to this shiur's hand only recently, when Rabbi Yossi Popack שליט"א gave it as a gift.[12] The timing itself is a hashgachah moment: a sefer arriving in the thirtieth-yahrzeit year, in the week of a war whose pshat is the Rav Schwab reading. The more one reads, the more one appreciates the yid behind it.

The Misconception

Rav Schwab observed something in his day. In the 1980s, when the beeper entered Jewish life — and later WhatsApp and global messaging — the technology offered an unprecedented thing: at the press of a button, the entire globe could receive a single message. Various circles in כלל ישראל began circulating: "משיח is coming now," "We want משיח now." Bumper stickers. Street-corner posters. "משיח is here."

Rav Schwab said clearly: "I don't like it."

I present Rav Schwab's shitah (שיטה) without any zilzul toward another shitah. The Lubavitcher Rebbe זי"ע had a deliberate mehalech here — to dissolve the todaas galus (תודעת גלות) in which כלל ישראל had been embedded for two thousand years and to bring the consciousness of Mashiach to the front of the mind. To make Yidden aware. I am also a Lubavitcher chassid by lineage; we study every shitah, learn from everyone, and think critically. Rav Schwab opposed that mehalech. Both shitos have al mi lismoch (על מי לסמוך).

Rav Schwab's claim: a public campaign of this shape risks turning Mashiach and geulah into a slogan, and the slogan into a zilzul of one of the most sacred ideas in Yiddishkeit — a musag (מושג), in his language, "as holy as the רוח אלקים at the beginning of בראשית". Mashiach is not a man on a white donkey saying "come back home." Those are meshalim. What Mashiach represents is something far more profound.

The Kashya Posed to Rav Schwab

People came to him: "Rebbe, if we do not do something to prepare the world for גאולה, how will we be able to look משיח in the face? What will we say to him when he asks what we did to bring him?"

Rav Schwab's answer: "Even with all your bumper stickers and beeper messages, do you think you will be able to look at משיח? Do you know how afraid we will be? חז"ל describe משיח בן יוסף and משיח בן דוד; הנביא ישעיהו describes the world at that moment — the yiras shamayim so intense that even the greatest צדיקים will be hiding in the nekikei ha’sela’im (נקיקי הסלעים),[13] in the crevices of the rocks, unable to gaze upward."

VII. Two Stories from Brisk

Two stories transmitted in the name of Rav Chaim Soloveitchik of Brisk זצ"ל[14] ground Rav Schwab's reading in a Brisker mesorah that long preceded the beeper era. Both stories illuminate the same point.

The Hallel on Pesach Night

Rav Chaim as a child loved music deeply. (He himself could not sing — he had no voice — but he understood music, perhaps mathematically.) In Brisk they followed nusach Ashkenaz (נוסח אשכנז) per the Rama's codification[15] and did not say Hallel on leil Pesach in shul. The Beis HaLevi (רבי יוסף דוב הלוי סולובייצ’יק זצ"ל, Rav Chaim's father and rav of Brisk) would head straight home from Ma’ariv to begin the Seder.

The chassidim in Brisk — Lechovitch, Kabrina, Slonim — kept their own shtibl where they did say Hallel at night, and sang it beautifully. One Pesach night, walking home with his father, young Rav Chaim heard the Hallel through the windows. He stopped. He wanted to listen. The Beis HaLevi pulled his hand to keep walking.

Rav Chaim protested: "I want to stay for the beautiful music, the beautiful Hallel!"

The Beis HaLevi answered, in characteristically Brisker form: "It is not halachah to say Hallel now. If it is not the proper zman halachti (זמן הלכתי), what is beautiful about it? Beautiful music is beautiful only when it fulfills retzon Hashem (רצון ה')."

The Chazzan's Brachah

Years later, Rav Chaim attended a function with a choir led by a head chazzan who sang like a malach. Rav Chaim approached him: "You moved me deeply. I want to give you a brachah. What would you like — long life? Success? Children?"

The chazzan's answer was unusual. Not wealth, not fame, not a greater hall. "Rebbe, I want a brachah that when Mashiach comes, I should be part of the choir that greets him — to be mekabel pnei Mashiach (מקבל פני משיח) with song."

Rav Chaim began trembling with yiras shamayim. He answered: "Who do you think you are? Even the greatest tzaddikim will be hiding their faces. Mashiach is a complete gilui kevod Shamayim (גילוי כבוד שמים) in this world. We will be hiding in the crevices of the rocks, as Yeshayahu describes. You think you have the audacity to imagine yourself singing in Mashiach's choir? It is beautiful you think of such things — but that brachah I will not give."

Both stories — the child wanting to listen to Hallel at the wrong zman, the chazzan wanting to sing in Mashiach's choir — point to the same Brisker yesod: spiritual experience is real, but always subordinate to the halachic structures that הקדוש ברוך הוא built. Mashiach is not a venue for self-expression. He is overwhelming kedushah.

VIII. Shitos in Klal Yisrael — A Framing

Other kehillos in Klal Yisrael have in our generations done real work to bring Mashiach and geulah into the collective consciousness. Today there is no yid who does not recognize the signs or know the conviction that there will be a Mashiach and a geulah. Each kehillah has al mi lismoch; the appropriate posture is to learn the best from everyone.

Many tzaddikim kept a special beged (בגד) ready — a bekishe (בעקעשע), a frock coat, depending on the mesorah — for Shabbos or for Mashiach. Being prepared is appropriate. The texture of preparation differs across kehillos; each draws on real mekoros.

IX. Rav Schwab on the Shofar Gadol — The Universal Hakarah

Rav Schwab returns to the shofar gadol with this frame: the shofar is the metaphor for the moment when the world recognizes that הקדוש ברוך הוא is המלך — that no power exists except through Him.

Imagine when the President of the United States, the kings and prime ministers and presidents of every country — secular or religious, makes no difference — stand and declare: "I am in this position only through the grace of God. I was elected, I was appointed; I hold this office because הקדוש ברוך הוא placed me here. Everything is malchus HaKadosh Baruch Hu (מלכות הקדוש ברוך הוא)." Imagine the United Nations — all the leaders of the nations — proclaiming together that everything belongs to הקדוש ברוך הוא, that recognition reverberating through every corner of creation.

That is the shofar gadol. The geulah, on this reading, is fundamentally a transformation of consciousness — a universal hakarah (הכרה) that "ה' מלך, ה' מלך, ה' ימלוך לעולם ועד".[16]

Seeds Already Visible

We see fragments today. President Trump, after the attempt on his life, said publicly: "If not for the grace of God..." — a bare acknowledgment, but on a world stage. Days before this war erupted, the Argentinian president Milei stood crying at the Kosel; a goy who is, by Rabbi Anki's description, "a Lubavitcher's best buddy." When a goy is such, it makes one a little nervous — and yet good mitzvah Hashem is going to come out of it. Add to that the Hezbollah pager operation and the drone-headquarters strike mentioned above: on no honest reading is any of it kochi v’otzem yadi. These are brief, partial, seed-shaped — but they are seeds of the hakarah that in Rav Schwab's reading will one day become the shofar gadol itself.

X. Closing

The bracha of Yerushalayim, which the Gemara's seder places next, we defer to the following week. For now, in this hour: may we hear besoros tovos (בשורות טובות) from ארץ ישראל speedily. May the chayalim in עזה and on every front return home safely. May the leadership of Medinas Yisrael have siyata di’Shmaya (סייעתא דשמיא) in every decision they face. May the shofar gadol be heard — physical or metaphorical, however הקדוש ברוך הוא chooses — and may the world know ה' מלך, ה' מלך, ה' ימלוך לעולם ועד. And may the streets of בני ברק fill again.

XI. Postscript — Rabbi Kozak's Organization

The shiur was abbreviated this morning because the rabbanim of Lawrence — Rabbi Asher Stern, Rabbi Aharon, Rabbi Masri, Rabbi Orlian — were co-hosting a breakfast for an extraordinary chassid from Gur, Rabbi Kozak, whose organization provides mental-health therapy in ארץ ישראל for those outside Bituach Leumi's coverage — chayalim returning from עזה with PTSD, families needing therapy for their children, anyone in spiritual or psychological crisis. Hatzalas nefashos mamash (הצלת נפשות ממש). The Tzahal framework offers returning chayalim one therapy session and is done; people fly to Thailand to escape what they saw, or come home shattered. This yid built the bridge.

I checked the organization carefully before lending my name. In a sefer whose spine is the bakashah for the end of chayalim running from the azakah, no appeal belongs more honestly in it than this one.

Rav Chaim Kanievsky, zt”l (1928–2022, Bnei Brak; son of the Steipler; one of the gedolei ha’dor of the previous generation) reportedly responded during the first Gulf War (Tashna, 1991), when Scud missiles were fired toward Israel, with the now-famous formulation "כל טיל יש לו כתובת" — every missile has its predetermined address. The teaching reflects the classical position on individual hashgachah peratis in matters of life and death; pairs in practical application with the chiyuv of hishtadlus for pikuach nefesh.   4. Mishnah Berurah 584; Shulchan Aruch Orach Chaim 579 — Avinu Malkeinu in eis tzarah. — The standard chiyuv of Avinu Malkeinu is during the Aseres Yemei Teshuvah and on ta’anis tzibbur. The Shulchan Aruch Orach Chaim 579, with the Mishnah Berurah's discussion, treats the recitation during communal eis tzarah; the poskim permit (and, in a declared tzarah, encourage) the minhag for a kehillah to institute Avinu Malkeinu during a milchemes mitzvah or similar eis tzarah affecting Klal Yisrael. The question whether the authority sits with the individual rav, the mo’etzes, or the minyan itself is treated in the acharonim. The Rebbe left this halachic question open in the shiur; this footnote identifies the mekoros for the follow-up. ↩
  5. The Hezbollah pager operation of September 2024 and the Israeli strike on an Iranian drone-coordination facility in the opening days of operation Rising Lion are the two live events the Rebbe cites in the shiur as hashgachah-moments beyond ordinary military explanation. "כחי ועצם ידי" — Devarim 8:17 — is the Torah's warning against reading victory as self-generated power. The pasuk in context: "And you say in your heart, my strength and the might of my hand has made for me this wealth"; the next pasuk: "And you shall remember Hashem your God, for it is He who gives you strength to do mighty things."

  6. Megillah 17b–18a — the seder of Shemoneh Esrei; refuah, parnassah, kibbutz galuyos. — The sugya on Megillah 17b opens with "ומה ראו לומר" and runs through 18a. The Gemara's own derivation sets each adjacency on a specific pasuk: refuah at the eighth (keyed to milah on the eighth day, requiring healing); birkas ha’shanim at the ninth (Rabbi Alexandri: against those who raise prices); kibbutz galuyos after birkas ha’shanim (Yechezkel 36:8 — "הרי ישראל ענפכם תתנו...כי קרבו לבוא"); then "once the exiles are gathered, judgment is executed on the wicked"; and so on. The Rebbe's hashkafic reading — refuah as precondition, parnassah as yishuv ha’daas, kibbutz nedachim as the ultimate menuchas ha’nefesh — sits on the sugya's halachic skeleton as its mussar reading. ↩
  7. Bereishis Rabbah on the reunion in Mitzrayim; cf. Tanchuma parallels. — The midrash that the malachei ha’shareis first uttered "ברוך מקבץ נדחי עמו ישראל" at the reunion of Yaakov and the shevatim in Mitzrayim — after Yosef's years in galus and Shimon's imprisonment — circulates in Chazal as the prototypical first instance of kibbutz nedachim. The parallel passages appear in Bereishis Rabbah on Vayigash and Vayechi (parshah 94–95), with Tanchuma parallels; the frame of each brachah in Shemoneh Esrei originating with the malachim at a biblical moment is developed across the midrashei Chazal on prayer. The force of the midrash here: the bracha was first said at the threshold of the galus, not at its end — the moment of family reunion in Mitzrayim as the prototype of every later ingathering. ↩
  8. Rambam, Hilchos Melachim 12:1–2 — humility about the particulars of yemos ha’Mashiach. — The Rambam at the close of Yad HaChazakah famously writes: "אין בין העולם הזה לימות המשיח אלא שעבוד מלכויות בלבד" and continues that "a person should not occupy himself in the aggados on this subject, nor spend time on the midrashim concerning these and like matters, nor make them the עיקר, for they bring neither to love nor to fear; and so too should he not calculate the end-times." The Rambam's metaphorical temperament is one pole; the Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer's specifying temperament is the other. Rav Schwab's reading of the shofar gadol as mashal sits comfortably within the Rambam's axis; the Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer position sits on the aggadic pole. The dispute is a classic one; it is not a Rav Schwab chiddush but a Rav Schwab inheritance of the Rambam-side temperament. ↩
  9. Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer ch. 31 (R. Chananya ben Dosa) — the two horns of eil ha’akeidah. — The teaching, attributed in Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer ch. 31 to Rabbi Chananya ben Dosa: nothing of the ram caught in the thicket was wasted. Its ashes founded the inner altar; its sinews became the strings of David's harp; its skin, the leather girdle of Eliyahu HaNavi; the left horn, blown at Har Sinai (the midrash cites "ויהי במשך בקרן היובל", Yehoshua 6:5, as its prooftext for a yovel-type shofar sound); and the right horn, larger than the left, destined to be sounded in the future — "והיה ביום ההוא יתקע בשופר גדול" (Yeshayahu 27:13). Verified on Sefaria (Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer 31, paragraph 13). ↩
  10. Bamidbar 10:10 (Beha’aloscha); Tur Orach Chaim קי"ח; Rambam Hilchos Taaniyos 1:1. — The pasuk: "ותקעתם בחצוצרות על עולותיכם ועל זבחי שלמיכם". The Tur in Orach Chaim siman קי"ח, in his treatment of birkas teka b’shofar, connects the future shofar gadol's teki’ah to the resumed teki’os of the chatzotzros on the korbanos in the rebuilt Beis HaMikdash: both are sound-events marking the closure of galus and the return to full avodah. The Rambam in Hilchos Taaniyos 1:1 identifies the chatzotzros as the sound-signature of communal tzarah — a klal-shaped sound, which strengthens the Rebbe's framing of teka b’shofar as tefillah of the klal. ↩
  11. Sefer Iyun Tefillah (Rav Shimshon Schwab zt”l; Eng. Rav Schwab on Prayer) — the shofar gadol as universal hakarah of malchus Shamayim. — Rav Shimshon Schwab, zt”l (1908–1995; rav of Kehal Adath Jeshurun in Washington Heights for nearly four decades, from 1958 until his petirah); talmid of the Chofetz Chaim at Mir, of Rav Baruch Ber Leibowitz at Kaminetz, and of the Alter of Slabodka. In his collected derashos and the posthumous Iyun Tefillah / Rav Schwab on Prayer, he develops a non-physical reading of the shofar gadol in birkas teka b’shofar: the shofar is the metaphor for the universal hakarah of malchus Shamayim by the leaders and peoples of the world. The reading sits within Rav Schwab's broader caution against the trivialization of Mashiach and geulah through bumper-sticker culture, grounded on his theological claim that Mashiach is a musag "as holy as the רוח אלקים at the beginning of בראשית." ↩

  13. Yeshayahu 2:19–21 — "ובאו במערות צרים ובמחלות עפר... לבוא בנקרות הצורים ובסעיפי הסלעים מפני פחד ה'". — Yeshayahu's vision of the day of Hashem: people fleeing into mountain crevices and rock fissures from the overwhelming presence of pachad Hashem.   14. Brisker tradition transmitted from Beis HaLevi (Rabbi Yosef Dov HaLevi Soloveitchik, zt”l, 1820–1892, rav of Brisk) and Rav Chaim Soloveitchik (Rabbi Chaim Soloveitchik, zt”l, 1853–1918, son of the Beis HaLevi; rosh yeshiva of Volozhin and rav of Brisk after his father's petirah; founder of the analytical derech ha’limud known as the Brisker derech). The two stories — the Pesach-night Hallel and the chazzan's requested brachah — circulate widely in Brisker oral mesorah as paradigmatic examples of the Brisker subordination of spiritual experience to halachic structure. Parallel traditions attribute versions of the chazzan story also to the Brisker Rav (Rav Yitzchak Zev); the version here follows the mesorah around Reb Chaim.   15. Rama, Shulchan Aruch Orach Chaim תפ"ז — minhag Ashkenaz not to recite Hallel in shul on leil Pesach. — The Rama in his glosses on the Shulchan Aruch codifies the minhag of kehillos Ashkenaz — Lithuanian and German communities including Brisk — not to recite Hallel with the tzibbur on leil Pesach in beis knesses. The Hallel of the night is fulfilled at the Seder within Maggid. Chassidische and Sephardi kehillos recite Hallel at Ma’ariv in shul. The Brisker family's strict adherence to the Rama grounds the story of young Rav Chaim and the music of the Lechovitcher shtibl. ↩
  16. "ה' מלך, ה' מלך, ה' ימלוך לעולם ועד"; "ותקע בשופר גדול לחירותנו". — The triadic declaration "ה' מלך, ה' מלך, ה' ימלוך לעולם ועד" — attributed in part to Heichalos literature and embedded in the siddur in the Kedushah and elsewhere — proclaims Hashem's malchus across past, present, and future. Rav Schwab reads the shofar gadol of "ותקע בשופר גדול לחירותנו" as the moment when this triadic recognition becomes universal across all the nations of the world. The shofar, on this reading, is the sound-form of universal hakarah. ↩
