הָאֵל הַגָּדוֹל הַגִּבּוֹר וְהַנּוֹרָא — שְׁתֵּי הַכְּרִיעוֹת וּבִרְכַּת גְּבוּרוֹת

HaKel HaGadol HaGibor v’HaNorah, the Two Keriyos, and Birkas Gevuros — The Restoration by the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah and the Grammar of Rain
Shiur of Rabbi Ephrati — Sunday Morning Series on Tefillah
Recorded 23 March 2025  ·  Sefer Edition

Continuing the body of Birkas Avos (ברכת אבות) and turning to Birkas Gevuros (ברכת גבורות): the identity of the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah, the great sugya in Yoma, the two keriyos, and the grammar of rain.

I. The Anshei Knesses HaGedolah — The Body Behind the Siddur

Why This Body Governs Every Berachah

The Anshei Knesses HaGedolah (אנשי כנסת הגדולה) — the Men of the Great Assembly — are the institution that stands behind every berachah (ברכה) and every tefillah (תפילה) that has come down to Klal Yisrael. The Gemara (גמרא) formulates the point with the economy characteristic of Chazal (חז"ל): heim tiknu berachos u’tefillos u’kedushos (הם תיקנו ברכות ותפילות וקדושות). Every syllable in the siddur (סידור), from the opening Modeh Ani through the concluding Aleinu, traces its nusach (נוסח) to their formulation. To study the meaning of any berachah of Shemoneh Esrei (שמונה עשרה) is, in the end, to enter the beis midrash of the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah and to receive its teaching.

Era and Membership

From Moshe Rabbeinu (משה רבינו) onward there had stood a body of shivim zekeinim (שבעים זקנים) — the seventy elders — and when one died another was appointed in his place. With the close of the era of nevi’im (נביאים) — Chagai, Zecharyah, and Malachi (חגי, זכריה, ומלאכי) — and with the long stretch reaching from Churban Bayis Rishon (חורבן בית ראשון) through Galus Bavel (גלות בבל) and into the era of Bayis Sheni (בית שני), a shift occurred: the older guf of the zekeinim expanded and reconstituted itself as the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah. The opening mishnah of Pirkei Avos[1] attests to this transition in its terse formula: Shimon HaTzaddik hayah mi’she’yarei Knesses HaGedolah (שמעון הצדיק היה משיירי כנסת הגדולה) — Shimon HaTzaddik was of the remnant of the Great Assembly. He is identified as the last of them; with his passing the era closed and the era of the zugos and the tannaim commenced.

Among the identified members of the Assembly: the greatest talmidei chachomim of the generation; the three closing nevi’im — Chagai, Zecharyah, and Malachi; Mordechai (מרדכי) of Purim; and Daniel (דניאל) together with his three companions Chananyah, Mishael, and Azaryah (חנניה, מישאל, ועזריה).

The Number — 120 or 85?

The number of the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah is itself the subject of a machlokes between the two Talmuds. The Bavli (בבלי) records the number as 120. The Yerushalmi (ירושלמי) records it as 85. Without entering the full sugya here, it is the Bavli’s count of 120 that entered the collective consciousness of Klal Yisrael and that stands behind the historical resonance of the number in later ages.

The Arithmetic Behind 120 — and Its Modern Echo

The mishnah’s arithmetic yielding the number 120 rests on the tripartite structure of the bottei din of Yerushalayim: the Sanhedrin Gedolah (סנהדרין גדולה) of seventy-one sitting in the Lishkas HaGazis (לשכת הגזית), and two Sanhedraos Ketanos (סנהדראות קטנות) of twenty-three each at the entrances to Har HaBayis (הר הבית) and to the Azarah (עזרה). The sum: 71 + 23 + 23 = 117. To this is added the three closing nevi’im — Chagai, Zecharyah, and Malachi — producing the total of 120.[2] The Anshei Knesses HaGedolah were not, in the working sense, a beis din (בית דין): a body of 120 cannot function as a beis din shakul (בית דין שקול), a court whose composition allows for the resolution of votes. Their function was rather institutional and constitutional: the enactment of takkanos (תקנות), the composition of berachos, and the fixing of the nusach of tefillah.

The number 120 casts a long historical shadow. When the State of Yisrael was founded in 1948, a machlokes arose over the size of the parliament. Some proposed 70 or 71, mirroring the Sanhedrin. This proposal met sharp objection: to compare a body whose members were largely not yirei shamayim to the Sanhedrin was felt inappropriate. Dr. Zerach Warhaftig (ד"ר זרח ורהפטיג) — a signatory of Megillas HaAtzma’us (מגילת העצמאות), and at once a legal scholar of academic standing and a talmid chochom learned in Maseches Sanhedrin (מסכת סנהדרין) — proposed instead 120, mirroring the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah. To this day the Knesset sits with 120 members. Whatever one’s judgment of the body itself, the number is an echo audible only against the background of the mishnah’s arithmetic.

II. Yoma 69b — The Restoration of the Crown

Moshe Rabbeinu’s Formula in Va’eschanan

The name Knesses HaGedolah is itself explained by the Gemara in one of the most stirring sugyos in Shas: Yoma 69b.[3] The sugya unfolds in three historical stations.

The first station is Moshe Rabbeinu. In Parshas Va’eschanan (פרשת ואתחנן) he coined the canonical formula for addressing the Ribbono Shel Olam (רבונו של עולם):[4]

כִּי ה' אֱלֹקֵיכֶם הוּא אֱלֹקֵי הָאֱלֹהִים וַאֲדֹנֵי הָאֲדֹנִים, הָאֵל הַגָּדֹל הַגִּבֹּר וְהַנּוֹרָא, אֲשֶׁר לֹא יִשָּׂא פָנִים וְלֹא יִקַּח שֹׁחַד.
Devarim 10:17

This became the standard opening frame for addressing HaKadosh Baruch Hu (הקדוש ברוך הוא) in tefillah. That Klal Yisrael did not extend the frame beyond what Moshe Rabbeinu established is a matter of settled halachic gravity. The Gemara in Berachos 33b[5] preserves the incident of a shaliach tzibbur (שליח ציבור) who stood before Rabbi Chanina (רבי חנינא) and heaped further and further praises upon HaKadosh Baruch Hu. Rabbi Chanina let him finish, then asked him: have you completed all the praises of your Master? The mashal he delivered is famous. A king whose treasuries are filled with thousands of coins of gold — do you praise him by listing his coins of silver? To do so is not to praise but to diminish. So too HaKadosh Baruch Hu is infinite; every additional praise is by definition a limitation. Therefore Chazal did not extemporize beyond the formula given by Moshe Rabbeinu — HaKel HaGadol HaGibor v’HaNorah.

Yirmiyahu’s Omission and Daniel’s Further Omission

Then came the Churban. Yirmiyahu HaNavi (ירמיהו הנביא) beheld goyim mocking HaKadosh Baruch Hu in His own Sanctuary — slaughtering pigs on the Mizbeach (מזבח) and casting the blood upon the Paroches (פרוכת). Confronted with this desecration, Yirmiyahu could not bring himself to say HaNorah (הנורא). His question, as the Gemara formulates it, is unavoidable: where are His nora’os when this is happening in His Heichal? From Yirmiyahu forward, Klal Yisrael omitted HaNorah from the formula.

Then came Daniel in Galus Bavel. He omitted not only HaNorah but also HaGibor (הגבור). His question: with Klal Yisrael scattered and persecuted, with sinas Yisrael pervading every land where His children reside — where are His gevuros? From Daniel forward, only HaKel HaGadol was said.

The Restoration by the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah

Then came the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah. They restored the formula in full: HaKel HaGadol, HaGibor, v’HaNorah. Their sevara (סברא) is a chiddush that recasts the entire question. Precisely the fact that Klal Yisrael continues to exist — under the mockery of the nations, through the defilement of the Sanctuary, through the long galus — that itself is the content of His gevuros and His nora’os. That the nations of the world have not consumed us, that we still stand and daven and rebuild, is not evidence against the gevurah: it is the gevurah. That a single people, alone and hated, endures against the current of history — this is the nora’os. The Anshei Knesses HaGedolah read the survival of Klal Yisrael not as a puzzle for the formula but as its very content.

From this restoration the Gemara derives the name itself: Knesses HaGedolah. There is no gedulah greater than the restoration of the original glory of Moshe Rabbeinu’s toarim. When the mispallel says HaKel HaGadol HaGibor v’HaNorah in Shemoneh Esrei, the kavvanah installed by the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah is precisely this: to declare that through galus and through hester panim (הסתר פנים), He remains Gibor and Norah — because we are still here.

III. Al Elyon — The Register of Hester Panim

Rav Schwab on the Word

Immediately following HaKel HaGadol HaGibor v’HaNorah, the berachah continues: Kel Elyon (א-ל עליון). The phrase originates with Avraham Avinu in his encounter with Malki-Tzedek (מלכי-צדק) at the conclusion of the war of the four kings against the five. Elyon is upper, supreme. But this raises a simple question: above what? Above whom?

Rav Shimon Schwab, zt”l (רבי שמעון שוואב זצ"ל),[6] wrote extensively on this phrase, and his reading is a piece of hashkafah (השקפה) that has entered the mainstream of contemporary machshavah. Many of his pieces first appeared in The Jewish Observer, the English-language publication of Agudas Yisrael of America from 1963 to 2008, in which the mature hashkafah of gedolei Yisrael reached a broad readership. In the decades following the Shoah (שואה), various figures — sometimes even significant figures — were assigning specific reasons to specific tragedies, each in the service of an antecedent agenda. Rav Schwab wrote sharply against this posture. No person has been granted the reshus (רשות) to explain the tragedies of Jewish history unless Chazal themselves supplied the explanation. This is a halachic-hashkafic stricture, not a matter of taste. The Shoah in particular admits of no such explanation; anyone who begins to construct one has already erred.

The Meaning of Elyon in Shemoneh Esrei

The Shoah, on Rav Schwab’s reading, is precisely the register of Kel Elyon. HaKadosh Baruch Hu is showing His hanhagah (הנהגה) in a mode that human hasagah (השגה) cannot reach. The message of the toar, as it stands in the berachah, is: I am above. I am infinite. You are finite creatures. Do your avodah. Do not attempt to comprehend Mine. This is hester panim in its fullness — not the absence of God but the presence of God in a register above the reach of the limited creature. The mispallel stands ready to ask the Ribbono Shel Olam for everything he needs in the middle berachos of Shemoneh Esrei; before he does, he acknowledges through Kel Elyon that the Meshiv to those requests operates in a register above his comprehension. He comes; he asks; he does not insist on understanding the ways in which his tefillos are answered.

IV. The Two Keriyos of Birkas Avos

The Halachic Frame

L’halachah, in Birkas Avos the mispallel bows at two stations: at the opening — Baruch Atah Hashem (ברוך אתה ה') — and at the close — Magen Avraham, Baruch Atah Hashem (מגן אברהם, ברוך אתה ה'). Two keriyos (כריעות); two distinct frames. The two bows are not a duplication of a single act but the marking of two distinct modes of relationship between the Yid and HaKadosh Baruch Hu.

Two Modes of Relationship — Ze Keili and Elokei Avi

Every Yid stands, at every moment, in two distinct relationships with HaKadosh Baruch Hu simultaneously. The pasuk (פסוק) in Shiras HaYam (שירת הים)[7] couples the two in a single line:

זֶה אֵלִי וְאַנְוֵהוּ, אֱלֹקֵי אָבִי וַאֲרֹמְמֶנְהוּ.
This is my God, and I will beautify Him; the God of my father, and I will exalt Him.
Shemos 15:2

The two clauses mark the two modes. Elokei Avi — God of my father — is the kav ha’avos (קו אבות), the transmitted mesorah (מסורה) that reaches the mispallel through his father, his grandfather, and back to Avraham, Yitzchak, and Yaakov. This is the emunah he inherited. Ze Keili — this is my God — is the direct, immediate, personal encounter, in which the Yid forges his own relationship with the Ribbono Shel Olam. Chazal remark on Ze Keili that at the Yam every Yid pointed with his finger and said this: the recognition was direct, personal, unmediated. This is my Elokim, not merely my father’s.

Rav Schwab on the Two Bows

Rav Schwab, drawing on the rishonim, ties the two keriyos of Birkas Avos to these two modes. The opening keriyah, at Baruch Atah Hashem Elokeinu v’Elokei Avoseinu (ברוך אתה ה' אלקינו ואלקי אבותינו), is the hishtabdus (השתעבדות) to the mesorah of the avos: I am the inheritor of Avraham, Yitzchak, and Yaakov. The closing keriyah, at Magen Avraham, Baruch Atah Hashem, is directed to Avraham as he began — alone, standing b’ever echad (בעבר אחד) against his father Terach (תרח), against Dor HaPalagah (דור הפלגה), against an entire civilization. The two bows together install both dimensions: the inheritance, and the personal avodah.

Avraham HaIvri and the Chiddushei HaRim

Chazal ask why Avraham is called Avraham HaIvri (אברהם העברי). The answer given: because he stood on one side, b’ever echad, while the entire world stood on the other. To stand alone against sheker (שקר) for the sake of emes (אמת) is the essence of what it means to be Avraham Avinu. Every Yid, at some moment in his life, is placed by hashgachah in precisely such a position; at that moment he is being asked to be a fragment of Avraham Avinu.

The Chiddushei HaRim, zt”l (חידושי הרי"ם זצ"ל) — whose yahrzeit falls on the calendar day of the delivered shiur — writes in Sefer Alzachos,[8] the principal sefer that Gerrer chassidim possess from him on chumash, a formulation that has become famous in Ger and in Kotzk: kol Yehudi hu Magen Avraham (כל יהודי הוא מגן אברהם) — every Yid is a Magen Avraham; every Yid is, at his moment, an Avraham Avinu. The closing chasimah (חתימה) of Birkas Avos on Magen Avraham alone, without the other avos, is on this reading the flag under which every Yid’s personal avodah stands. The second keriyah is thereby not the marking of an inheritance but the acknowledgment of a personal call: I, too, like Avraham, stand against the sheker of my world for the emes of HaKadosh Baruch Hu — and in that standing, the second bow finds its place.

V. Birkas Gevuros — Why Rain Is Called Gevuros

The Text of the Berachah

The second berachah of Shemoneh Esrei opens: Atah Gibor l’olam Hashem, m’chayeh meisim atah, rav l’hoshi’a. Mashiv haruach u’morid ha’geshem — or, in the summer season, morid ha’tal (מוריד הטל). At the head of the berachah stands the toar of gevurah; immediately following it stands the invocation of rain.

The Question of the Gemara

The Gemara at the opening of Ta’anis[9] raises the question: gishmei berachah (גשמי ברכה) are designated gevuros (גבורות). Why? What is it about rain that constitutes a demonstration of the Ribbono Shel Olam’s gevurah? Rain, viewed superficially, is the most ordinary of natural phenomena; its designation as gevurah demands explanation.

The Gra — Every Drop to Its Address

The Gra (גר"א)[10] supplies the reading that has become the standard pshat in the berachah. The fact that every single drop of rain descends precisely to its appointed location — every field, every furrow, every root — is itself the demonstration of gevurah. What appears to the natural eye as random precipitation is, in emes, the most exact expression of hashgachah pratis (השגחה פרטית) over the finest details of bri’ah (בריאה). There is no seder (סדר) more intricate than this seder, and there is no gevurah more exact than the direction of every drop of rain to its address. On the Gra’s reading, the placement of gishmei berachah at the head of the berachah of gevuros is not a stylistic accident but a doctrinal statement.

VI. HaGeshem or HaGashem — The Grammar and the History

The Halachic Question

How does the mispallel pronounce the word? Is it ha’geshem (הַגֶּשֶׁם) with a segol, or ha’gashem (הַגָּשֶׁם) with a kamatz? Most say what their father said. The question is nevertheless real and rests on a substantive grammatical foundation.

The Rule of Segol and Kamatz

The Gemara in Ta’anis[9] teaches that the hilchos keri’as sefarim (הלכות קריאת ספרים) — the grammatical rules governing the proper reading of sacred texts — were transmitted to Moshe Rabbeinu at Sinai. Among these rules is the following: a word that carries a segol mid-pasuk shifts to a kamatz when the word stands at the end of the pasuk or at a hefsek (הפסק). The classical example, drawn from the Torah itself in Parshas Mishpatim:

עַיִן תַּחַת עַיִן, שֵׁן תַּחַת שֵׁן, יָד תַּחַת יָד, רֶגֶל תַּחַת רָגֶל.
An eye for an eye, a tooth for a tooth, a hand for a hand, a foot for a foot.
Shemos 21:24[11]

The first appearance of regel, mid-pasuk, retains the segol. The second appearance, at the close of the pasuk, shifts to kamatz. The same word, same root, different nikud by virtue of position alone.

The Application to Morid HaGeshem

The application to morid ha’geshem depends on how one reads the syntax that follows. If Mashiv haruach u’morid ha’geshem ends a sentence — with an esnachta (אתנחתא) before the following phrase Mechalkel chayim b’chesed (מכלכל חיים בחסד) — then the grammatical rule yields ha’gashem with a kamatz. If, on the other hand, the phrase flows continuously into Mechalkel chayim b’chesed — construing the sense as HaKadosh Baruch Hu sustains life through the rain — then ha’geshem with a segol is correct. The machlokes ha’poskim (מחלוקת הפוסקים) turns on how the phrase is to be parsed.

The Historical Nusach — Yitzchak Satanov and His Siddur

For centuries, the universal nusach was ha’geshem with a segol. Every ancient siddur — including the Siddur of Rav Amram Gaon (סידור רב עמרם גאון),[12] the earliest comprehensive Jewish siddur we possess — reads with segol, uniformly. The shift to ha’gashem is post-1800, and the proximate source of the shift is one of the more unsettling episodes in the history of printed siddurim.

The figure in question is Yitzchak Satanov (יצחק סאטאנוב), named for the town of Satanów, near Lviv in present-day western Ukraine. (The town itself was decimated in the Shoah; in 2020, the remains of approximately three hundred Yidden — most of them women and children, suffocated to death by the Nazis and their Ukrainian collaborators — were discovered in two basements. After a six-year legal battle they were brought to kevurah in Eretz Yisrael.)

Satanov died in 1804 or 1805. He came from a frum family; he moved to Berlin — then the capital of the Haskalah (השכלה) — and became close to Moses Mendelssohn, the intellectual father of Reform. He worked in Mendelssohn’s printing house. Outwardly he continued to present as frum, but his loyalties were divided. Those who knew him observed that beneath his long kapote (קפוטה) he wore a short jacket — straddling both worlds, and telegraphing the split.

Over time it emerged that Satanov was a serial forger. He forged sefarim. He forged haskamos (הסכמות) — including haskamos in the names of the Chasam Sofer (חתם סופר) and the Pnei Yehoshua (פני יהושע). The Pnei Yehoshua initially cited him in the ordinary way; only later did he discover the deception. To establish credibility in serious circles, Satanov became the first to print the Kisvei HaAri z”l (כתבי האריז"ל) in publicly available form — a step of no small chutzpah, given that ever since Shabbsai Tzvi there had stood a cherem (חרם) against the printing of Kabbalah for the general reader.

He then turned his attention to the siddur. His edition, published under the title Vaye’etar Yitzchak (ויעתר יצחק) — the pasuk of Yitzchak Avinu davening, and Satanov’s namesake — introduced numerous grammatical “corrections” to the received nusach.[13] It is in this siddur that ha’gashem with a kamatz first appears in print, replacing the universal ha’geshem. Approximately twenty-five years before the delivered shiur, a full kuntres (קונטרס) appeared — the work of Rav Krause (רב קראוס) — documenting the entire history and reopening the question for a generation of poskim.

The Poskim on the Question

Rav Moshe Feinstein, zt”l (הגר"מ פיינשטיין זצ"ל), in Shu”t Igros Moshe (שו"ת אגרות משה)[14] rules in favor of ha’gashem. He records, further, that Maran HaGrish Elyashiv, zt”l (מרן הגרי"ש אלישיב זצ"ל), was extremely makpid on ha’gashem; in his minyan on the second floor of Porat Yosef (פורת יוסף) in Yerushalayim, the talmidim corrected any mispallel who said ha’geshem. (A talmid with a sense of humor once came in the summer and said Mashiv haruach u’morid ha’geshem; the room jumped to correct him; his answer: you don’t say ‘geshem’ at all in the summer!) Rav Elyashiv held the phrase Mashiv haruach u’morid ha’geshem to end a sentence with an esnachta before Mechalkel chayim, and the grammatical rule accordingly yields ha’gashem.

Rav Yaakov Yisrael Fisher, zt”l (הגר"י פישר זצ"ל),[15] a Rav in Yerushalayim from the Eidah HaChareidis, took the kuntres on Satanov, set it aside, and articulated a principle of general validity: even if he was a maskil, what he says here makes sense; ha’gashem is correct on its own grammatical merits, regardless of the identity of the first to write it in print. The judgment separates the halachic question from the biographical stigma.

Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach, zt”l (הגרש"ז אויערבך זצ"ל),[15] originally said ha’gashem. When he saw Rav Krause’s kuntres documenting that the older nusach was ha’geshem, he changed for the rest of his life. The change is preserved as a paradigm of the gadlus of an ish ha’emes: he saw that his practice rested on a mistaken premise and altered it.

Among the majority of contemporary acharonim the practice is ha’geshem. Only a genuine medakdek, aware of the underlying question, distinguishes.

VII. Geshem Inside Birkas Techiyas HaMeisim

Why This Berachah

Why is geshem installed inside Birkas Techiyas HaMeisim (ברכת תחיית המתים) rather than in some other berachah? The Gemara supplies the formula in a phrase of programmatic weight: ki hu techiyas ha’meisim — rain is the daily techiyas ha’meisim. Without water there is no life. The dependence of the world on geshem is not an occasional matter but a moment-by-moment reality. The Gemara in Sanhedrin, perek Chelek,[16] develops the indictment of the Dor HaMabul: their corruption included the claim that they did not need HaKadosh Baruch Hu for rain, since they had neharos (נהרות), yamim (ימים), and irrigation systems. The Mabul itself was middah k’neged middah (מדה כנגד מדה): water from above and water from below together demonstrated that the Ribbono Shel Olam alone controls life-giving water.

The Structural Mapping to the Avos

The structural mapping of the first three berachos of Shemoneh Esrei onto the avos is a classical piece of Kabbalah-machshavah that runs through Chazal and the Rishonim:

  • Avos (אבות) ↔ Avraham — chesed (חסד).
  • Gevuros (גבורות) ↔ Yitzchak — gevurah (גבורה).
  • Kedushah (קדושה) ↔ Yaakov — tiferes (תפארת).

The placement of gevuros as the second berachah, corresponding to Yitzchak, deepens the reading of geshem within it. Yitzchak is the avodah of gevurah; rain, directed drop by drop, is the visible expression of that middah.

VIII. Techiyas HaMeisim as Daily Reality

Four Categories of Daily Techiyas HaMeisim

The simple pshat of the berachah — somech noflim, v’rofei cholim, u’matir asurim (סומך נופלים ורופא חולים ומתיר אסורים) — is that each of these is a bechinah (בחינה) of daily techiyas ha’meisim. The berachah is not the future eschatological event alone; it is the present-tense reality of hashgachah.

  • Somech noflim — when a person is depressed and will not rise from his bed, and HaKadosh Baruch Hu sends a ray of hope: this is techiyas ha’meisim.
  • Rofei cholim — the Gemara in Ta’anis[17] teaches that the rising of a choleh from his sickbed is, in a sense, a greater nes than techiyas ha’meisim. The choleh is halachically and existentially considered dead while ill; recovery is therefore a daily techiyas ha’meisim.
  • The liberation of the survivors of the Shoah — they were dead while breathing, sacks of bones; their emergence into the world to build families, to continue Klal Yisrael: techiyas ha’meisim.
  • Every morning: b’yadcha afkid ruchi (בידך אפקיד רוחי) at night, and the Birkas HaShachar (ברכות השחר) upon waking — the daily return of the neshamah to the body: techiyas ha’meisim.

The Ramban on the Berachah

The Ramban,[18] in his treatment of Birkas Gevuros, articulates the position with full force: every single day there is a techiyas ha’meisim, not only the future eschatological one at the close of days. The berachah, on the Ramban’s reading, is a daily hakaras (הכרה) of hashgachah pratis — not only a future emunah. The mispallel who wishes to recite the berachah as it was installed by the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah must hold both frames simultaneously: the future techiyah and the daily techiyah.

IX. Rav L’Hoshi’a — Rav Aharon Kotler on the Second Din

The Structural Question

The berachah continues: Atah Gibor l’olam Hashem, m’chayeh meisim atah, rav l’hoshi’a (רב להושיע). Rav Schwab, drawing on the rishonim, points out that HaKadosh Baruch Hu in the berachah-formulations of the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah is designated Melech, ozeir, u’moshi’a u’magein (מלך, עוזר, ומושיע, ומגן). Of these, moshi’a denotes complete yeshu’ah (ישועה), the final and unqualified vindication. This raises a substantive question: what is the connection between techiyas ha’meisim and complete yeshu’ah? Why should the closing note of the berachah pair the techiyah specifically with the concept of rav l’hoshi’a?

Two Dinim — The Gemara in Rosh HaShanah

The Gemara in Rosh HaShanah 16b[19] (and its parallel sugyos) records that after techiyas ha’meisim there is the Yom HaDin HaGadol v’HaNorah (יום הדין הגדול והנורא) — a second, final din, distinct from the personal din that each neshamah faces immediately upon death. The natural question presses itself: was there not already a din when the neshamah came up after meah v’esrim (מאה ועשרים)? The accounting of mitzvos and aveiros, Gan Eden (גן עדן) and Gehinnom (גיהנם) — all of this took place. Why then a second din?

Rav Aharon Kotler’s Chiddush

Rav Aharon Kotler, zt”l (הגר"א קוטלר זצוק"ל),[20] offers a chiddush that reframes the entire question. The chiddush is preserved in oral mesorah among his talmidim and appears in various forms in the derashos that emerged from Lakewood in the decades following his passing; a definitive published makor in his own seforim could not be identified for this edition.

Rav Aharon draws the point through a mashal. Consider a Yid with serious sefeikos ba’emunah (ספקות באמונה), on the verge of leaving the derech entirely. A person meets him, becomes close to him, mentors him, teaches him, works with him through his confusion until he returns. That returnee has children; he has grandchildren; his descendants are shomrei Torah u’mitzvos (שומרי תורה ומצוות). The person who did the hishtadlus (השתדלות) thinks: I did a small chesed. I sat with someone for an hour or two. I gave him chizuk (חיזוק). The emes is that he saved an entire etz shel doros (עץ של דורות) — a tree of generations.

Why Two Dinim

  • The first din is the din that follows death. At that moment, the consequences of a person’s mitzvos rippling forward through the generations are not yet visible. The Ribbono Shel Olam judges the person on what he did and on the frame within which he understood himself to be doing it.
  • The second din is the Yom HaDin HaGadol v’HaNorah that follows techiyas ha’meisim. Now the full picture is in. The hour of chizuk is now visible as the tikkun (תיקון) of an entire mishpachah across centuries. Now, and only now, the Ribbono Shel Olam can judge with complete information — the ledger in full view.

The Fear of the Tzaddikim

On this reading, the fear of the tzaddikim before the Yom HaDin HaGadol v’HaNorah becomes intelligible in a way that it is not intelligible when the din is conceived merely as a repetition of the first. Who knows? Perhaps something that in the immediate frame appeared as a chesed turned out, in the long generational arc, to carry negative consequences. Perhaps something that appeared modest turned out to be enormous. The full ledger of unintended consequences is a frightening object even for the tzaddik, and the second din is where that ledger is opened.

Rav L’Hoshi’a as Prayer for Final Vindication

This is why techiyas ha’meisim is paired with rav l’hoshi’a. Only after techiyas ha’meisim and the second din, when the Ribbono Shel Olam exonerates the person with full knowledge of all consequences across all generations — only then is the yeshu’ah complete. The daily tefillah of rav l’hoshi’a is not a request to be rescued from an individual tzarah; it is the request to be mezakeh b’din (מזכה בדין) at that final accounting, when the full ledger is unrolled. It is the tefillah for complete and unqualified vindication.

X. Practical Summary

  • The Anshei Knesses HaGedolah stand behind every syllable of the siddur. To learn a berachah is to enter their beis midrash.
  • The restoration of HaKel HaGadol HaGibor v’HaNorah is the declaration that survival through galus is itself the content of gevurah and nora’os. This is the kavvanah of the phrase in Shemoneh Esrei.
  • Kel Elyon installs hester panim as a category of avodah: acknowledge that His ways are above hasagah before you ask for anything.
  • The two keriyos of Birkas Avos mark the two modes: Elokei Avi at the opening, Ze Keili at the close. Kol Yehudi hu Magen Avraham.
  • Rain is gevurah on the Gra’s reading because every drop reaches its address. This is the finest expression of hashgachah pratis.
  • On ha’geshem versus ha’gashem: know the grammatical rule and the historical nusach; most contemporary acharonim say ha’geshem, following the pre-Satanov universal reading; those who follow Rav Elyashiv and the Igros Moshe say ha’gashem on grammatical grounds. Do not change without daas Torah.
  • Techiyas ha’meisim is a daily reality on the Ramban’s reading — the choleh who recovers, the nofel who is lifted, the neshamah that returns each morning.
  • Rav l’hoshi’a is the prayer for the second din — the final vindication, when the ledger of generational consequences is unrolled in full.

Shimon HaTzaddik, identified as one of the last surviving members of the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah, marks the transition from the era of nevu’ah to the era of chochmah and takkanos. ↩

Mishnah Sanhedrin ch. 1 — three bottei din in Yerushalayim; arithmetic 71 + 23 + 23 + 3 nevi’im = 120. — The opening perek of Maseches Sanhedrin describes the three bottei din: the Sanhedrin Gedolah of seventy-one in the Lishkas HaGazis, and two Sanhedraos Ketanos of twenty-three each at the entrances to Har HaBayis and to the Azarah. The arithmetic yielding the Bavli’s 120 for the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah rests on this mishnah, augmented by the three closing nevi’im. ↩
  3. Yoma 69b — Yirmiyahu, Daniel, and the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah on HaKel HaGadol HaGibor v’HaNorah. — The famous sugya, verified against the William Davidson Sefaria text: Moshe Rabbeinu coined the formula in Va’eschanan; Yirmiyahu at the Churban dropped HaNorah (heichan nora’osav?); Daniel in Bavel further dropped HaGibor; the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah restored the full formula on the sevara that the very survival of Klal Yisrael through Churban and galus is the manifestation of His gevurah and nora’os. The Gemara closes by deriving from this restoration the very name Knesses HaGedolah: hechzirah atarah l’yoshnah. The teaching is attributed in the Gemara to Rav Mattana in the West and expounded by Rabbi Yehoshua ben Levi. ↩
  4. Devarim 10:17 — HaKel HaGadol HaGibor v’HaNorah, the source-pasuk. — Moshe Rabbeinu’s formulation in Parshas Va’eschanan: ki Hashem Elokeichem Hu Elokei ha’Elohim va’Adonei ha’Adonim, ha’Kel ha’Gadol ha’Gibor v’ha’Norah, asher lo yisa panim v’lo yikach shochad. The pasuk from which Chazal derive that HaKel HaGadol HaGibor v’HaNorah is the canonical frame for addressing HaKadosh Baruch Hu in tefillah. The pasuk’s pairing of the three toarim with the chiyuv of impartial justice (lo yisa panim) shapes the kavvanos of Birkas Avos and Birkas Gevuros. ↩
  5. Berachos 33b — Rabbi Chanina on excessive praise; the mashal of the king with treasuries of gold. — The sugya: a shaliach tzibbur stood before Rabbi Chanina and added praises to ha’Gadol ha’Gibor v’ha’Norah. Rabbi Chanina waited for him to finish, then asked: have you completed all the praises of your Master? The mashal: a king with thousands of gold coins is praised by mentioning silver — an insult, not a compliment. We address HaKadosh Baruch Hu with the formulation given by the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah; we do not extemporize beyond it. ↩
  6. Rav Shimon Schwab, zt”l — Kel Elyon, rav l’hoshi’a, the two keriyos, and the warning against explaining the Shoah. — Rav Shimon Schwab (1908–1995, Rav of Kehal Adath Jeshurun in Washington Heights), talmid of the Chazon Ish and of Rav Baruch Ber, in his English-language essays in The Jewish Observer and in his collected derashos develops several of the readings brought here: Kel Elyon as the hakarah of hester panim beyond human hasagah (with explicit application to the Shoah and a sharp warning against assigning specific reasons to specific tragedies); the two keriyos of Birkas Avos mapped to kav ha’avos versus Ze Keili; and the meaning of rav l’hoshi’a as complete yeshu’ah. Rav Schwab’s essays on tefillah are collected in Rav Schwab on Prayer (Mesorah/ArtScroll). ↩
  7. Shemos 15:2 — Ze Keili v’anveihu, Elokei Avi va’aromemenhu. — The pasuk in Shiras HaYam juxtaposing two modes of relationship with HaKadosh Baruch Hu: Ze Keili — direct, immediate, personal recognition (Chazal: each Yid at the yam pointed with his finger and said this); and Elokei Avi — inherited mesorah from the avos. The two clauses are the textual basis for Rav Schwab’s reading of the two keriyos in Birkas Avos. ↩
  8. Chiddushei HaRim in Sefer Alzachos — kol Yehudi hu Magen Avraham. — Rabbi Yitzchak Meir Alter (the Chiddushei HaRim, 1799–1866, founding Rebbe of Ger), in Sefer Alzachos on chumash — the principal sefer Gerrer chassidim have from him on parshas ha’shavua — develops the reading that the closing chasimah of Birkas Avos on Magen Avraham alone alludes to the avodah required of every Yid: to be, in the moments that demand it, an Avraham Avinu — to stand alone b’ever echad against the world for the sake of emes. Kol Yehudi hu Magen Avraham entered chassidus idiom and is widely repeated in Gerrer and Kotzker tradition. ↩
  9. Ta’anis 2a and Ta’anis 7a — gishmei berachah as gevuros; hilchos keri’as sefarim from Sinai. — The opening mishnah of Ta’anis places gishmei berachah within the berachah of gevuros; the Gemara there asks why and brings the derashah identifying geshem with techiyas ha’meisim. Elsewhere in Ta’anis, the Gemara teaches that hilchos keri’as sefarim — proper grammatical reading rules including the shift from segol to kamatz at end of verse or pause — were given to Moshe Rabbeinu at Sinai. The two sugyos together frame the ha’geshem/ha’gashem question. ↩
  10. The Gra on tefillah/Ta’anis — every drop of rain to its appointed address. — Rabbi Eliyahu of Vilna (the Gra, 1720–1797), in his bi’ur on the siddur and in his comments on the sugya in Ta’anis, develops the chiddush that gishmei berachah are designated gevuros precisely because HaKadosh Baruch Hu directs every drop to its individual destination. What appears to natural eyes as random precipitation is, in emes, the most precise demonstration of hashgachah pratis over the pratei ha’bri’ah; this is the truest expression of His gevurah. ↩
  11. Shemos 21:24 — ayin tachas ayin… regel tachas ragel. — The classic pasuk in Parshas Mishpatim illustrating the segol-to-kamatz shift: the same word regel appears mid-pasuk with a segol and at the close of the pasuk with a kamatz. The pasuk is the standard textual proof for the rule and grounds the dikduk argument in Mashiv haruach u’morid ha’geshem/ha’gashem. ↩
  12. Siddur Rav Amram Gaon — ancient nusach with segol: ha’geshem. — The siddur compiled by Rav Amram bar Shishna Gaon (d. c. 875, head of the yeshiva of Sura) is the earliest comprehensive Jewish siddur we possess. It and other ancient siddurim — including the siddur of Rav Saadia Gaon and the various minhagei tefillah recorded by the rishonim — read ha’geshem with a segol uniformly. The shift to ha’gashem is post-1800 and traces to the siddur of Satanov. ↩
  13. Siddur Vaye’etar Yitzchak — the Satanov siddur and its history. — The early-nineteenth-century siddur edited by Yitzchak Satanov (Yitzchok Halevi Satanov, c. 1732–1804/5; named for the town of Satanów in present-day western Ukraine), worker in the printing house of Moses Mendelssohn in Berlin. Satanov’s siddur introduced numerous grammatical “corrections” to the received nusach, including the shift from ha’geshem to ha’gashem. Subsequent investigation revealed Satanov as a serial forger — of sefarim, of haskamos (including those attributed to the Chasam Sofer and the Pnei Yehoshua), and as the first to print the Kisvei HaAri z”l in defiance of the post-Shabbsai Tzvi cherem. The kuntres by Rav Krause — published roughly twenty-five years before the delivered shiur — documents the entire history; precise edition and exact publication data were not given in the shiur and could not be verified in Sefaria’s catalog for this edition. The historical outline as given rests on the oral tradition of the delivered shiur together with widely circulated biographical accounts of Satanov and his Berlin milieu. ↩
  14. Shu”t Igros Moshe — ha’gashem; testimony on Rav Elyashiv, zt”l. — Rav Moshe Feinstein, zt”l, in Igros Moshe rules in favor of ha’gashem on grammatical grounds, treating the phrase Mashiv haruach u’morid ha’geshem as ending a sentence with an esnachta. He records that Maran HaGrish Elyashiv, zt”l (1910–2012), was meticulous on ha’gashem in his minyan at Porat Yosef. The exact chelek and siman of the relevant teshuvah were not specified in the shiur; the responsum is in the volumes of Igros Moshe on Orach Chaim. ↩
  15. Rulings of Rav Yaakov Yisrael Fisher, zt”l, and Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach, zt”l, on the ha’geshem/ha’gashem question. — Rav Yaakov Yisrael Fisher, zt”l (1928–2003, Av Beis Din of the Eidah HaChareidis in Yerushalayim; author of Shu”t Even Yisrael), held that the grammatical case for ha’gashem stands on its own merits regardless of its first appearance in Satanov’s siddur. Rav Shlomo Zalman Auerbach, zt”l (1910–1995, Rosh Yeshiva of Kol Torah; posek ha’dor of his generation in Yerushalayim), originally said ha’gashem; upon learning the documented historical nusach, he changed to ha’geshem for the rest of his life — held up as a model of an ish ha’emes changing course on evidence. Both positions are preserved in oral and written teshuvos circulating among Yerushalayim talmidim; precise published sources were not given in the shiur and could not be independently verified in Sefaria’s catalog for this edition. ↩
  16. Sanhedrin, perek Chelek — the Dor HaMabul and the value of gishmei berachah. — The sugya in perek Chelek develops the indictment of the Dor HaMabul: their corruption included a denial of dependence on HaKadosh Baruch Hu for gishmei berachah, on the basis that they had neharos, yamim, and irrigation systems. The Mabul itself was middah k’neged middah — water from above and from below proved that HaKadosh Baruch Hu alone controls life-giving water. The sugya grounds the placement of geshem inside Birkas Techiyas HaMeisim as a daily reminder. ↩
  17. Ta’anis 22b (and parallels) — gedolah refuas cholim yoser mi’techiyas ha’meisim. — The Gemara teaches that the nes of a choleh rising from his sickbed is greater, in a sense, than the nes of techiyas ha’meisim. The choleh is halachically and existentially considered dead while ill; recovery is therefore a daily techiyas ha’meisim. The sugya grounds the placement of rofei cholim within the same Birkas Techiyas HaMeisim in Shemoneh Esrei. ↩
  18. Ramban on techiyas ha’meisim in Birkas Gevuros — daily techiyas ha’meisim. — The Ramban (in his derashah on Toras Hashem Temimah, in his peirush on the berachos of tefillah, and parallels) develops the position that Birkas Techiyas HaMeisim in Shemoneh Esrei is not exclusively a reference to the eschatological techiyas ha’meisim at the close of days. Each daily phrase — somech noflim, rofei cholim, matir asurim, the daily restoration of the neshamah upon waking — is itself a present-tense techiyas ha’meisim. The Ramban’s reading frames the berachah as a daily hakarah of hashgachah pratis, not only a future emunah. ↩
  19. Rosh HaShanah 16b — second din after techiyas ha’meisim; Yom HaDin HaGadol v’HaNorah. — The Gemara in Rosh HaShanah (and parallel sugyos in Sanhedrin) records that there is a final Yom HaDin HaGadol v’HaNorah after techiyas ha’meisim, distinct from the personal din each neshamah faces upon death. The structural kushya — why a second din after the first — is the textual ground for Rav Aharon Kotler’s chiddush on rav l’hoshi’a. ↩
  20. Rav Aharon Kotler, zt”l — the second din and rav l’hoshi’a. — Oral mesorah of the talmidim of Rav Aharon Kotler, zt”l (1891–1962, founding Rosh Yeshiva of Beth Medrash Govoha in Lakewood, NJ; talmid of the Alter of Slabodka; author of Mishnas Rabbi Aharon): the long-arc consequences of one’s mitzvos across generations are not yet visible at the moment of personal din after meah v’esrim; they unfold only after techiyas ha’meisim, when the full ledger comes into view. Rav l’hoshi’a is the prayer for complete vindication at that final din.
