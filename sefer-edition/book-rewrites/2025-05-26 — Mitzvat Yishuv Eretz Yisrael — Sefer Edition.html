מִצְוַת יִשּׁוּב אֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל

Mitzvas Yishuv Eretz Yisrael — A Halachic Survey from the Rishonim to the Post-1948 Poskim
Shiur of Rabbi Ephrati — Yom Yerushalayim Shiur
Delivered כ"ח אייר תשפ"ה  ·  26 May 2025  ·  Sefer Edition

Dedicated לעילוי נשמת יוסף בן שמואל הלוי ז"ל, murdered by the Nazis ימ"ש, and לעילוי נשמת זולמה ע"ה — by a survivor of Bergen-Belsen.

I. Opening Frame — The Halachic Register

A sustained halachic treatment of mitzvas yishuv Eretz Yisrael (מצות ישוב ארץ ישראל) sits uneasily astride the ideological landscape of contemporary Jewish life. The sugya (סוגיא) is one on which the historical currents of the past century — the rise of the chareidi rejectionist position typified by Satmar, the national-religious position typified by the talmidim of Rav Kook, and the broad yeshivish middle inherited from Rav Moshe and the Lithuanian poskim — have each left a mark on the halachic literature itself. A survey that is fully halachic must therefore acknowledge from the outset that a posek's hashkafah (השקפה) sometimes shapes his pesak on this question, and must discipline itself accordingly. The halachah (הלכה) remains the halachah; the Torah remains the Torah; the ratzon Hashem (רצון ה') stands independent of the preferences of any community. The pages that follow are addressed to the halachah; the hashkafic implications are indicated where they bear on pesak, and otherwise set aside.

The structure is linear. The survey begins with the underlying pasuk (פסוק) in Parshas Mas'ei; proceeds through the foundational machlokes (מחלוקת) of Rashi and the Ramban (רמב"ן); the Ramban's own biography and mesiras nefesh for the mitzvah; the Rambam's famous omission from Sefer HaMitzvos and its principal resolutions; the dissenting voice of Rabbeinu Chaim HaKohen cited in Tosafos; the Terumas HaDeshen's historical qualification; the sugya of the shalosh shevu'os (שלש שבועות) at the close of Kesubos and the Avnei Nezer's resolution; the modern voices — the Chasam Sofer, the Minchas Chinuch, Rav Kook, Rav Teichtal, Rav Moshe, the Chazon Ish, the Rav, and the Tzitz Eliezer — and closes with the practical maskana (מסקנא) for the yachid (יחיד) in chutz la'aretz (חוץ לארץ) today.

II. The Underlying Pasuk

The source-pasuk for the mitzvah is in Parshas Mas'ei:

וְהוֹרַשְׁתֶּם אֶת הָאָרֶץ וִישַׁבְתֶּם בָּהּ, כִּי לָכֶם נָתַתִּי אֶת הָאָרֶץ לָרֶשֶׁת אֹתָהּ.
And you shall drive out the inhabitants of the land, and dwell in it; for I have given you the land to possess it.
Bamidbar 33:53[1]

The pasuk contains two verbs: v'horashtem (והורשתם), the dispossession of the seven nations, and vishavtem bah (וישבתם בה), the yeshivah (ישיבה) — dwelling — in the land. The entire first-order machlokes of the Rishonim turns on whether this doubled formulation constitutes a tzivui (ציווי), an affirmative chiyuv, or a havtachah (הבטחה), a descriptive divine assurance.

III. The Classical Machlokes — Rashi and the Ramban

The Shitah of Rashi

Rashi[2] reads the pasuk sequentially and descriptively: v'horashtem osah mi'yoshvehah, v'az vishavtem bah — when the seven nations have been dispossessed, dwelling in the land will follow. On this reading, the pasuk is an havtachah to the generation entering Eretz Yisrael under Yehoshua, not a standing chiyuv upon every yid in every generation. The Ramban, in his hassagos (השגות), understands Rashi's reading as entailing the fuller conclusion that there is no independent mitzvah upon the yachid to live in Eretz Yisrael.

The Shitah of the Ramban

The Ramban[3] emerges as the great champion of the mitzvah among the Rishonim. In his peirush al haTorah (פירוש על התורה) on Mas'ei, in his treatment of Parshas Acharei Mos, in his derashos, and in his hassagos on the Rambam's Sefer HaMitzvos, he develops the position systematically. His lashon on Rashi's reading is sharp: "shegagah gedolah hi be'einai" (שגגה גדולה היא בעיני) — this is a serious error in my eyes. The Ramban's position has three principal components:

  1. The pasuk is a full mitzvas aseh upon every yid in every generation — a chiyuv de'oraisa.
  2. The source is not the pasuk in Mas'ei alone; many pesukim in Sefer Devarim command aliyah (עליה) to the land, reinforcing the chiyuv.
  3. The mitzvah contains three distinguishable chalakim:
    • Kibbush ha'aretz (כיבוש הארץ) — the conquest dimension, dispossessing hostile nations;
    • Yeshivah ba'aretz (ישיבה בארץ) — the affirmative dimension of dwelling;
    • She'lo neizvennah be'yad zulasenu (שלא נעזבנה ביד זולתנו מן האומות או לשממה) — the negative component of not abandoning the land to the hands of foreign nations or to desolation. This third chelek, articulated explicitly by the Ramban in his hassagos, is the operative dimension for the Avnei Nezer's and the Tzitz Eliezer's modern halachic application — and it is the chelek under which the practical eitzos of purchasing property and material support of yishuv (יישוב) qualify as ma'aseh mitzvah.

IV. The Ramban's Proofs from Shas

The Ramban's halachic argument against Rashi's reading proceeds not only from the pesukim but from a series of halachos scattered through Shas that are intelligible only on the premise of a standing mitzvah. A sampling:

Kefiyah on a Spouse

The mishnah at the close of Kesubos[7] rules that where one spouse wishes to make aliyah and the other refuses, the one who wishes to ascend may compel: hakol ma'alin l'Eretz Yisrael (הכל מעלין לארץ ישראל). Either spouse may issue the ultimatum: ascend with me, or receive a get (גט). The Rambam[8] codifies the pesak in Hilchos Ishus. If no standing mitzvah exists, the halachic basis for this unusual kefiyah — which overrides the ordinary presumption against uprooting a spouse — disappears.

Travel Before Shabbos

The ordinary halachah, codified by the Rambam and the Shulchan Aruch,[9] prohibits beginning a long-distance sea voyage within three days of Shabbos if it would predictably entail chilul Shabbos. For aliyah l'Eretz Yisrael an explicit exception is granted: one may set out even on Erev Shabbos. This heter is intelligible only on the premise that aliyah itself carries the halachic weight of a mitzvah grave enough to override the baseline restriction.

Mezuzah

The Gemara in Menachos,[10] codified by the Rambam in Hilchos Mezuzah, draws a structural distinction: in chutz la'aretz, a renter has thirty days before becoming chayav in mezuzah; in Eretz Yisrael, the chiyuv is immediate. The Gemara's reasoning: yishuv Eretz Yisrael is itself a mitzvah, and the immediate chiyuv mezuzah both anchors the new resident and ensures that, should he leave, the mezuzah remains in place to draw the next yid who will take the apartment. The halachah presupposes the mitzvah.

Issur Yetziah

The Rambam rules in Hilchos Melachim[8] that one may not leave Eretz Yisrael for chutz la'aretz except for narrow enumerated purposes — severe famine, marriage, or talmud Torah unavailable in the land. An issur of such force on yetziah is unintelligible if no corresponding mitzvah of yeshivah stands behind it.

These four halachos, taken together, carry the Ramban's argument: the body of practical pesak throughout Shas presupposes a standing mitzvah of yishuv, and the pasuk of vishavtem bah is its source.

V. The Rambam's Omission

The Rambam, in his enumeration of the taryag mitzvos (תרי"ג מצות) in Sefer HaMitzvos, omits mitzvas yishuv Eretz Yisrael from the count. The Ramban in his hassagos[4] places this omission at the head of his list of mitzvos the Rambam failed to include, restoring the mitzvah to the count and declaring it a mitzvah noheges b'zman hazeh. The question remains: on what grounds did the Rambam leave it out, and does the omission reflect a halachic position that there is no mitzvah, or only a structural feature of his count?

The Megillas Esther — and Its Refutation

The Megillas Esther on Sefer HaMitzvos — by Rabbi Yitzchak de Leon ibn Tzur (sixteenth century; the sefer first printed Venice 1592)[15] — defends the Rambam at face value: in the Rambam's view, the mitzvah of yishuv applied only during the era of the Beis HaMikdash; after the churban, the mitzvah lapsed. The Megillas Esther's reading is the most ambitious attempt to take the Rambam's silence at face value, and it is the historical anchor for every later posek who wished to argue that the chiyuv does not apply b'zman hazeh.

The reading cannot be sustained against the Rambam's own body of pesak. The Rambam himself rules — in Hilchos Ishus on kefiyah for aliyah, in Hilchos Shabbos on travel before Shabbos for the sake of aliyah, and in Hilchos Mezuzah on the immediate chiyuv in Eretz Yisrael — pesakim that are explicitly operative b'zman hazeh. Each is a direct refutation of the Megillas Esther's reconstruction. Rav Ovadia Yosef, zt"l, in Shu"t Yabia Omer,[16] rejects the Megillas Esther in characteristically forceful lashon: "dachu kol ha'acharonim es ha'Megillas Esther b'shtei yadayim" (דחו כל האחרונים את המגילת אסתר בשתי ידיים) — all the Acharonim rejected him with both hands. The Rambam's own pesakim settle the matter: the Rambam held the mitzvah applies b'zman hazeh. What remains is the narrower question of why he did not separately enumerate it.

The Umbrella-Mitzvah Resolution

The resolution adopted by the mainstream of Acharonim is structural rather than substantive. The Rambam does not separately enumerate mitzvos that are themselves kollel — umbrella categories under which many enumerated mitzvos sit. Where one mitzvah contains others, the Rambam counts the specific contained mitzvos and does not count the umbrella above them. Yishuv Eretz Yisrael is such an umbrella: it contains the entire seder Zera'im — shemittah (שמיטה), yoveil (יובל), terumos u'ma'asros (תרומות ומעשרות), and the full set of mitzvos ha'teluyos ba'aretz (מצות התלויות בארץ). The Rambam counts those; the umbrella he does not separately count. The omission is formal, not substantive.

The Rambam's Own Visit to Eretz Yisrael

A further proof is adduced from the Rambam's own personal conduct. The Sefer Chareidim — by Rabbi Elazar Azkari, zt"l (1533–1600, Tzfas; member of the kabbalistic circle of Tzfas; author also of the piyut Yedid Nefesh) — preserves a letter of the Rambam[11] in which the Rambam records that he set foot in Eretz Yisrael at Akko (עכו) on gimmel Sivan and establishes that day as a permanent yom tov for his family: a mishteh, abstention from melachah, and festive demeanor across generations. The historical record is that the Rambam did not settle in Eretz Yisrael — he continued to Egypt, where he lived and died — but the force of the raya is undiminished. A yid who establishes a permanent family yom tov upon arrival in the land plainly holds the mitzvah of yishuv to be of the gravest order.

VI. The Dissenting Shitah — Rabbeinu Chaim HaKohen

Intellectual honesty requires acknowledging the Rishonim who diverged. Tosafos at the close of Kesubos[12], on the sugya of compelling a spouse to make aliyah, cites Rabbeinu Chaim HaKohen — a talmid of Rabbeinu Tam, twelfth-century France: "b'zman hazeh einah mitzvah ladur b'Eretz Yisrael" (בזמן הזה אינה מצוה לדור בארץ ישראל). The reasoning rests on two concerns:

  1. Sakanas ha'derachim (סכנת הדרכים) — the perils of twelfth-century travel and of life in Eretz Yisrael under hostile governance.
  2. Chashash kishalon in mitzvos ha'teluyos ba'aretz — the concern that a yid unaccustomed to terumos u'ma'asros, shemittah, and yoveil will stumble.

The Forgery Argument

Beginning in the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries, a cluster of major poskim — the Maharit,[13] the Mabit, the Rashbash, and the Rashbatz — argue, on internal grounds and on an external mesorah, that this Tosafos's citation of Rabbeinu Chaim HaKohen is a talmid toeh (תלמיד טועה) interpolation and does not represent Rabbeinu Chaim HaKohen's actual position. The classical mekor for the forgery argument is Shu"t Maharit on Yoreh De'ah; Rav Ovadia Yosef in Yabia Omer adopts the position of these poskim.

The substantive objection reinforces the textual: the purpose of a yid's halachic life is to fulfill as many mitzvos as possible — "ratzah HaKadosh Baruch Hu l'zakos es Yisrael, l'fichach hirbah lahem Torah u'mitzvos" (רצה הקדוש ברוך הוא לזכות את ישראל, לפיכך הרבה להם תורה ומצות). An argument that would deprive klal Yisrael of the opportunity to fulfill the many mitzvos ha'teluyos ba'aretz on the ground that an individual yid might stumble has struck many poskim as inverted on its face.

Reliance on This Shitah in the Vayoel Moshe

The Satmar Rav, zt"l, in Vayoel Moshe[23] — the single most extended halachic-ideological treatment of the sugya from the opposing side — leans heavily on this Rabbeinu Chaim HaKohen as a central Rishon-source. The reliance is precarious given the cluster of poskim rejecting the citation as a talmid toeh interpolation. The deeper halachic foundation of the Vayoel Moshe's position rests not on this Tosafos but on the sugya of the shalosh shevu'os (treated below in Section VII).

VII. The Shalosh Shevu'os

The sugya of the shalosh shevu'os — the three oaths at the close of Kesubos[25] — is the independent foundation of the position against contemporary mass aliyah. Rabbi Yosi b'Rabbi Chanina teaches in the Gemara that HaKadosh Baruch Hu adjured klal Yisrael with three oaths: that they not ascend b'chomah (בחומה), that is, as an armed collective; that they not rebel against the nations of the world; and (the parallel oath) that the nations not oppress Yisrael excessively. These oaths were the halachic ground of the classical Satmar position that any collective aliyah absent clear mashiach-markers violates a shevu'ah.

The Ramban himself, in his hassagos to the Rambam's Sefer HaMitzvos, treats the shevu'os as a limitation on manner, not on the mitzvah itself: the oaths restrict the mode of return (not b'chomah) but do not abrogate the standing mitzvah of individual yeshivah.

The decisive halachic treatment in the Acharonim is that of the Avnei Nezer,[26] Yoreh De'ah 454. Several yesodos emerge from the teshuvah:

  • The shevu'os are conditional — mutually binding on klal Yisrael and on the nations. The oath that the nations not oppress Yisrael excessively was violated continuously by the nations; a conditional shevu'ah whose counter-condition has been violated is batel.
  • The oath against ascending b'chomah prohibits collective armed revolt against the ruling power of the land; it does not prohibit individual aliyah, nor does it prohibit communal purchase, settlement, and development with the permission of the governing authority.
  • The Avnei Nezer rules affirmatively that the mitzvah of yishuv is a full chiyuv upon the yachid in zman hazeh, and that no shevu'ah-based petur stands.

The Avnei Nezer's resolution is adopted, with variations, by the mainstream of post-churban poskim. Rav Yissachar Shlomo Teichtal, zt"l, in Eim HaBanim Semeichah[27] — a sefer composed in Budapest in the final years of the Churban Europa by a talmid of the Munkatcher school who publicly reversed his earlier position — builds upon the Avnei Nezer and marshals the full force of Chazal's aggados on the ma'alas Eretz Yisrael as a direct halachic challenge to the Hungarian-chassidish position of his teachers. The sefer is cited here not as a pesak that settles the sugya but as the paradigmatic internal reversal from within the chassidish world against the Satmar position; a halachic survey that cites Vayoel Moshe owes its reader the Eim HaBanim Semeichah in the same breath.

VIII. The Terumas HaDeshen — Historical Qualification

The Terumas HaDeshen[14] — Rabbi Yisrael Isserlein, c. 1390–1460, Austria — responded to an inquiry from Eretz Yisrael: is the mitzvah of yishuv Eretz Yisrael fulfilled? His answer: yes, in full. The questioner then pressed: why, then, does the Terumas HaDeshen not himself bring his yeshivah and his talmidim over? The reply is historically revealing. The Terumas HaDeshen writes that the Ashkenazim who made aliyah in his era — the fifteenth century — suffered persecution not only from the surrounding Yishmaelim but also from the long-established Sefardi kehillah in Yerushalayim, where the arrival of newcomers from northern Europe disrupted settled arrangements. The Terumas HaDeshen notes the condition as "kayadu'a" (כידוע).

This is sakanah of a different register from Rabbeinu Chaim HaKohen's — a social-political sakanah rather than a sakanas ha'derachim — but the structural shape of the ruling is the same: the mitzvah exists in full; the conditions of his generation produced a specific petur for his specific situation. The Terumas HaDeshen's teshuvah is a historical artifact of fifteenth-century conditions; the conditions it describes do not obtain today, and the petur it extracted falls away with them.

IX. Chazal on the Ma'alah of the Land

The Ramban's pesak rests not only on the pesukim and the halachos but on the aggadic literature of Chazal on the ma'alah of life in Eretz Yisrael. Three loci bear directly on the sugya.

Kol HaDar b'Chutz La'aretz — Kemi She'ein Lo Elokah

The Gemara at the close of Kesubos[7]:

כׇּל הַדָּר בְּחוּצָה לָאָרֶץ — כְּמִי שֶׁאֵין לוֹ אֱלוֹקַּ.
Anyone who dwells outside the Land — it is as if he has no Elokim.
Kesubos 110b

The lashon is startling — a yid in chutz la'aretz remains, of course, a yid who serves the same Ribbono shel Olam as the yid in Eretz Yisrael. The Ramban and his talmid the Rashba develop the sugya at length. The structural principle is that every land and every nation receives its shefa through a sar (שר) — a specific malach appointed over that land. Yidden in Eretz Yisrael are under the direct hashgachah of HaKadosh Baruch Hu; yidden in chutz la'aretz receive the shefa through intermediate conduits. The Gemara's lashon captures the mediated character of the connection — not chas v'shalom an absence of Elokim but a less direct experience of His presence.

Four Amos b'Eretz Yisrael

The Gemara, on the same amud: "kol ha'mehalech arba amos b'Eretz Yisrael, harei hu ben olam haba" (כל המהלך ארבע אמות בארץ ישראל הרי הוא בן עולם הבא) — one who walks four amos in the land has a share in olam haba. The Ramban brings a parallel teaching: even a shifchah (שפחה) — a maidservant unschooled in Torah — has a portion in olam haba solely by virtue of residing in Eretz Yisrael. The Ramban's argument from these teachings is a kol she'kein: Chazal do not speak this way of the land unless its yeshivah is a mitzvah of the first order.

The Sifri at Platia

The Sifri[21] on Parshas Re'eh records a ma'aseh: four Tannaim — Rabbi Yehudah ben Beseira, Rabbi Masya ben Charash, Rabbi Chananiah ben Achi Rabbi Yehoshua, and Rabbi Yonasan — traveled to a place called Platia (פלטיא) on the border of Eretz Yisrael. As they crossed the border outward, they lifted their eyes, wept until their cheeks were full of tears, tore their garments, and recited the pasuk "virishtem osah vishavtem bah", declaring:

יְשִׁיבַת אֶרֶץ יִשְׂרָאֵל שְׁקוּלָה כְּנֶגֶד כׇּל הַמִּצְוֹת שֶׁבַּתּוֹרָה.
Dwelling in Eretz Yisrael is equivalent to all the mitzvos in the Torah.
Sifri, Parshas Re'eh

Few mitzvos in the Torah receive this designation. The Ramban brings the Sifri as a central proof for his position; the line stands among the most-cited Chazal texts on the ma'alas Eretz Yisrael.

X. The Ramban's Mesiras Nefesh — From Barcelona to Akko

The Ramban's halachic position is reinforced by the arc of his own life — the Ramban is one of the Rishonim whose personal conduct bore most consequential testimony to his pesak. A sketch of the arc follows; the full biographical treatment is in the standard literature.

Rabbeinu Moshe ben Nachman was born in Girona, Catalonia, around 1194 and died c. 1270. He stood as rav, posek, philosopher, mekubal, and rofei — one of the supreme Rishonim of the age.

The Barcelona Disputation

In Tammuz 1263, King James I of Aragon summoned the Ramban to a public vikuach (ויכוח) with the apostate Pablo Christiani, in the presence of king, ministers, clergy, and the head of the Dominican Order (Raymond of Penyafort). Four consecutive days. The Ramban documented the entire disputation in his Sefer HaVikuach[5]. He emerged, by his own record, the clear victor, and made public la'ag (לעג) of the Christian claims. The king personally acknowledged the Ramban's victory and, the Shabbos following, appeared in the main synagogue of Barcelona and addressed the kehal; the Ramban rose to respond respectfully but firmly, and the king embraced him and gave him three hundred dinar.

The Flight and the Arrival at Akko

Two years later the Ramban's talmidim circulated the Sefer HaVikuach. The Christian authorities, scandalized, demanded his prosecution. The king deflected the pressure; the matter escalated to Pope Clement IV, who issued an order requiring the Ramban's appearance to stand trial for la'ag on the Christian claims. Most historians read the record as establishing that the Ramban understood he could not expect to survive the trial. He fled.

The Ramban arrived in Eretz Yisrael in 1267, landing at Akko. From Akko he traveled to Yerushalayim, where he built the beis haknesses known thereafter as the Beis Haknesses HaRamban and gave decisive koach to the renewal of Jewish yishuv in the city. In 1972, archaeologists in Eretz Yisrael recovered the Ramban's personal seal[24], bearing the inscription "Rabbeinu Moshe ben Nachman" — one of the most significant material artifacts of the era of the Rishonim; it is on display at the Israel Museum.

The Derashas Rosh HaShanah at Akko

On the first Rosh HaShanah after his arrival — traditionally dated to Rosh HaShanah 5028, autumn 1267, in Akko — the Ramban delivered the Derashas Rosh HaShanah[6]. The derashah is preserved in his collected derashos and runs to tens of printed pages. In its course the Ramban reiterates his pesak: there is a mitzvah on every yid to come to Eretz Yisrael, and living there raises the yid to a far higher madreigah (מדרגה). He turns autobiographical: he describes his mitzvah-driven commitment as the motive for leaving Spain, invokes the Gemara in Eiruvin[22] on talmidei chachamim who become "achzarim al b'neihem ka'orev" (אכזרים על בניהם כעורב) in pursuit of Torah, and writes openly of his separation from his wife and children in Spain: he had come, in his own lashon, l'chasos b'tzel immi (לחסות בצל אמי) — to shelter in the bosom of his mother, that is, in Eretz Yisrael. The derashah is among the most personal Torah-texts the Rishonim bequeathed.

XI. The Modern Acharonim and the Twentieth-Century Poskim

The arc from the Ramban and the Rambam through the Acharonim to the post-1948 poskim is dense; a halachic survey can mark only its principal stations.

The Chasam Sofer and the Minchas Chinuch

The Chasam Sofer,[28] in his teshuvos on Yoreh De'ah and in his drashos, rules with the Ramban that the mitzvah of yishuv Eretz Yisrael is a mitzvah noheges b'zman hazeh. His position bridges the classical and modern eras and is a central mekor for every nineteenth-century posek on the sugya. The Minchas Chinuch[29], in his treatment of the mitzvah — placed in the structural tradition of Sefer HaChinuch — follows the Ramban's enumeration and develops the halachic implications in sefer hamitzvos-register.

Rav Avraham Yitzchak HaKohen Kook, zt"l

Rav Avraham Yitzchak HaKohen Kook, zt"l (1865–1935; talmid of Rav Naftali Zvi Yehudah Berlin at Volozhin; first Ashkenazi Chief Rabbi of Eretz Yisrael from 1921) held a position of radical ahavas Eretz Yisrael within the halachic tradition. His correspondence — collected in Igros HaRaayah[19] — contains a famous reply to the Ridvaz — Rabbi Yaakov Dovid Willowsky, zt"l (1845–1913, author of the Ridvaz commentary on the Yerushalmi; settled in Tzfas in 1903) — who had accused Rav Kook, in the context of the heter mechirah debate of shemittah 5670, of having "become a Zionist." Rav Kook's reply, in substance:

If to be a "Zionist" means to follow the Gemara — which teaches that a shifchah living in Eretz Yisrael has more direct connection to HaKadosh Baruch Hu than a yid in chutz la'aretz, that four amos in the land bring one to olam haba, and that (per the Yerushalmi[20]) one who dwells in the land and eats chulin b'taharah is as if he has fulfilled the whole Torah — then that is the Gemara's own position, and I will wear it as a badge of honor.
Paraphrased in substance from Igros HaRaayah, correspondence of the shemittah debate of 5670 (1909–10); for the specific igros see note [19].

In the same correspondence, Rav Kook develops the position that even the secular yidden engaged in the building of the yishuv participate, at some level, in the mitzvah itself — a position controversial in the chareidi world of his day and ours, and one which must be understood on its own terms rather than caricatured. The reader is referred to the Igros themselves for the full development.

Rav Teichtal and Eim HaBanim Semeichah

The treatment of the shalosh shevu'os above (Section VII) already brought Rav Yissachar Shlomo Teichtal, zt"l, and Eim HaBanim Semeichah.[27] The sefer deserves placement here as well: for a halachic survey that cites the Vayoel Moshe, the parallel voice from within the Hungarian-chassidish world — a posek who publicly reversed his earlier position and marshaled the Ramban, the Avnei Nezer, and the body of Chazal's aggadic material in defense of contemporary aliyah — stands as a necessary counterweight within the same cultural register.

Rav Moshe Feinstein, zt"l — Mitzvah Kiyumis

Rav Moshe Feinstein, zt"l, in Shu"t Igros Moshe,[17] rules with the mainstream that the mitzvah of yishuv is a mitzvah — the many halachos in Orach Chaim, Even HaEzer, and Yoreh De'ah that presuppose it leave no room to deny it — and introduces a structural chiddush on the character of the chiyuv. His yesod: mitzvas yishuv Eretz Yisrael is a mitzvah kiyumis (מצוה קיומית), not a mitzvah chiyuvis (מצוה חיובית).

The structural analogy is to tzitzis at the de'oraisa level: a yid is not under absolute chiyuv to wear a four-cornered beged; if he does so, the chiyuv of attaching tziztzis triggers. On Rav Moshe's reading, mitzvas yishuv Eretz Yisrael operates on the same structural principle: if a yid is in Eretz Yisrael, he is mekayem a mitzvah de'oraisa; he is not under absolute obligation to relocate. The chiddush establishes a halachic framework within which an American yid who does not make aliyah is not a mevatel aseh — a reading that bore directly on the self-understanding of the mid-twentieth-century American chareidi world. The position is contested; the Tzitz Eliezer treats it below.

The Chazon Ish

The Chazon Ish, in positions attributed to him by his talmidim, is reported to have held a related structural distinction: there is a mitzvah of ha'achzakah (החזקה) of yishuv — of strengthening and maintaining Jewish settlement in the land — which operates somewhat independently of a per-yachid chiyuv to make aliyah. The structural resonance with Rav Moshe's kiyumis/chiyuvis framing is suggestive; the two poskim stand together as the twentieth-century mesorah that treated the yachid's chiyuv aliyah as a kiyum rather than an unconditional affirmative obligation.

Rav Yosef Dov Soloveitchik, zt"l — Kol Dodi Dofek

The Rav, zt"l, in Kol Dodi Dofek,[30] develops a position that the events of 1948 constitute a dofek — a knock of the Dod — to which klal Yisrael is halachically required to respond. The Rav's treatment of the mitzvah is distinctive: he recognizes the halachic weight of the sheish defikos (six knocks) and the standing mitzvah of yishuv, while declining to translate them into a straightforward chiyuv aliyah upon every yachid in galus. The Rav's shape is intermediate between Rav Moshe's kiyumis framing and the Tzitz Eliezer's unqualified chiyuv; the reader is referred to Kol Dodi Dofek itself for the full development.

The Tzitz Eliezer — After 1948

The Tzitz Eliezer[18] — Rav Eliezer Yehuda Waldenberg, zt"l (1915–2006, Yerushalayim; dayan on the Beis Din HaGadol of the Israeli Rabbanus) — disagrees with Rav Moshe. His position: mitzvas yishuv Eretz Yisrael is a full mitzvah chiyuvis upon every yachid, with an additional collective dimension upon klal Yisrael through koach ha'rabim (כח הרבים) — the strength of yishuv deriving from the aggregate numbers of yidden in the land.

The decisive chiddush: after 1948, the historical peturim cited by earlier Acharonim — sakanas ha'derachim, hostile governance, restrictive Ottoman arrangements, intra-communal friction — no longer obtain. The State of Israel actively welcomes Jewish settlement; nefesh b'nefesh and other programs subsidize aliyah; the dangers of the past are gone. The Tzitz Eliezer writes that no petur survives the changed conditions. Every yachid who can make aliyah is under a full chiyuv to do so.

XII. The Question of the Modern Gedolim

A question that cannot be sidestepped: on the position of the Ramban and the Tzitz Eliezer, why did many of the great gedolim of the twentieth century who had the capacity to make aliyah not do so? Rav Moshe, Rav Aharon Kotler, the Rav, the Satmar Rav — the question stands against each.

The sugya admits several candidate mehalchim, which a halachic survey can at least name without pretending to adjudicate among them:

  • Harbatzas Torah as oness. The classical heter of talmud Torah unavailable elsewhere is codified by the Rambam himself in Hilchos Melachim; a gadol whose capacity to build Torah is tied to a specific kehillah in chutz la'aretz may fall within the category.
  • Rav Moshe's own kiyumis framing. On his own halachic position, the absence of chiyuv for the yachid to relocate is dispositive; the question does not even arise.
  • The Chazon Ish's ha'achzakah framing. A gadol whose influence materially strengthens the yishuv from abroad — through tzedakah, talmidim, infrastructure, and pesak — may be fulfilling the chelek of she'lo neizvennah on a collective level.
  • Personal sakanah and family circumstance. The Terumas HaDeshen-type exception is available in principle; a yachid's halachic situation may generate a petur that does not generalize.
  • The Satmar position — where shalosh shevu'os is understood as still binding and Vayoel Moshe's halachic reading is accepted on its own terms.

None of these resolves the question definitively; each is a position within the sugya. The purpose of naming them is to replace the appearance of a shrug with the shape of the actual machlokes.

XIII. Three Shitos in Brief

The sugya, reduced to its three principal shitos:

  1. The Ramban — mitzvah de'oraisa, operative in every generation, upon every yid. Three chalakim: kibbush, yeshivah, and she'lo neizvennah be'yad zulasenu. Followed by the Chasam Sofer, the Minchas Chinuch, the Avnei Nezer, Rav Teichtal, Rav Kook, Rav Ovadia Yosef, and the Tzitz Eliezer, with the Tzitz Eliezer adding that no petur survives the post-1948 conditions.
  2. The Rambam (as read by the mainstream of Acharonim after refuting the Megillas Esther) — agrees there is a mitzvah noheges b'zman hazeh; declines to separately enumerate it because it is an umbrella containing the mitzvos ha'teluyos ba'aretz. On Rav Moshe's reading, the chiyuv is kiyumis rather than chiyuvis: triggered by presence in the land, not standing as an unconditional affirmative obligation to relocate.
  3. Rabbeinu Chaim HaKohen (as cited in Tosafos) — no mitzvah in zman hazeh, due to sakanas ha'derachim and chashash kishalon in the mitzvos ha'teluyos ba'aretz. Rejected by the cluster of fifteenth- and sixteenth-century poskim as a talmid toeh interpolation. The Satmar position's reliance on this Tosafos, as developed in Vayoel Moshe, inherits the textual difficulty; the firmer foundation for that position is the shalosh shevu'os treated in Section VII, which the Avnei Nezer resolves.

XIV. Practical Maskana — The Yachid in Chutz La'aretz

What follows for the yachid in chutz la'aretz today? On the Tzitz Eliezer's reading, the full chiyuv stands and the yachid who can make aliyah is halachically required to do so. On Rav Moshe's reading, no absolute obligation attaches; aliyah remains a mitzvah kiyumis of the first order, and a yid who can make aliyah ought to pursue it, but the halachic weight falls short of unconditional chiyuv. On either reading, a yachid who does not make aliyah is left with a positive halachic question: how does he participate in the mitzvah?

The poskim describe several alternative modes of participation, each of which stands as a ma'aseh mitzvah rather than as a mere hiddur, on the strength of the Ramban's third chelek of she'lo neizvennah be'yad zulasenu:

  • Purchasing property in Eretz Yisrael. A yachid who cannot physically relocate but who purchases a residence — even a modest one, used three or four times a year — participates concretely in the chelek of not abandoning the land. The eitzah is standard among the poskim who treat the practical implications of the mitzvah.
  • Material and financial support of yidden in Eretz Yisrael. The strengthening of the yishuv from without — through tzedakah directed to mosdos, families, and yishuvim under pressure — is itself yishuv Eretz Yisrael. On the Chazon Ish's framing of ha'achzakah, this is the central kiyum available to a yachid abroad.
  • Short-term and repeated visits. On the Gemara's teachings — the four amos, the shifchah's portion in olam haba — even time spent in the land carries halachic significance; the yachid who travels to Eretz Yisrael for yom tov, for simchos, for extended periods of limmud, enters the structure of the mitzvah.

The maskana for the yachid: the mitzvah exists; the chiyuv is contested as to its force; the alternative modes of kiyum are halachically real. A yid who can make aliyah should do so, with awareness of the halachic weight the poskim have placed upon the act. A yid who cannot has concrete eitzos through which the mitzvah is still accessible — and the halachic infrastructure within which to understand that his participation is not peripheral but substantive.

XV. Closing

The mitzvah of yishuv Eretz Yisrael is one of the mitzvos on which the halachic literature records both the firmest Chazal-level anchor and the most varied practical application across the generations. The Ramban's voice, written into his peirush, pressed into his hassagos, and borne out in his own mesiras nefesh, remains the dominant mekor. May klal Yisrael be zocheh to the full kiyum of the mitzvah with the coming of the geulah sheleimah:

וּפְדוּיֵי ה' יְשׁוּבוּן, וּבָאוּ לְצִיּוֹן בְּרִנָּה.
And the redeemed of Hashem shall return, and come to Zion with song.
Yeshayahu 35:10

The Rambam codifies, and the Shulchan Aruch follows, an exception: for aliyah l'Eretz Yisrael one may begin the journey even on Erev Shabbos. The exception is intelligible only on the premise that aliyah itself constitutes a mitzvah grave enough to override the standard restriction — a halachic argument against the Megillas Esther. ↩

The Gemara's reasoning: yishuv Eretz Yisrael is itself a mitzvah; the immediate chiyuv mezuzah in the land incentivizes settlement and ensures that even if the original tenant departs, the mezuzah remains as a draw for the next yid to take the apartment. The pesak is structurally inseparable from mitzvas yishuv. ↩

On yishuv Eretz Yisrael, the Megillas Esther argues that the Rambam deliberately excluded the mitzvah because in his view it applied only when the Beis HaMikdash stood; after the churban, the mitzvah lapsed. Shu"t Igros Moshe, Even HaEzer 1:102 (and related in EH 4:49) — mitzvah kiyumis; the tzitzis structural analogy. — Rav Moshe Feinstein, zt"l (1895–1986), in Shu"t Igros Moshe — principally Even HaEzer 1:102, with related material in Even HaEzer 4:49 — develops the structural distinction between mitzvah chiyuvis (an absolute affirmative chiyuv) and mitzvah kiyumis (a mitzvah one fulfills if and when the conditions arise, but is not under standing obligation to seek out). The analogy: tzitzis at the de'oraisa level — one is not chayav to wear a four-cornered beged, but if one does, the chiyuv of attaching tziztzis triggers. Rav Moshe's reading: yishuv Eretz Yisrael works the same way — if one is in Eretz Yisrael, one is mekayem a mitzvah; one is not under absolute chiyuv to relocate. The position is contested; the Tzitz Eliezer and Rav Ovadia Yosef hold it is mitzvah chiyuvis proper. ↩

Shu"t Tzitz Eliezer (Rav Eliezer Yehuda Waldenberg, zt"l) — mitzvah chiyuvis; koach ha'rabim; the changed reality after 1948. — Rav Eliezer Yehuda Waldenberg, zt"l (1915–2006, Jerusalem; dayan on the Beis Din HaGadol of the Israeli Rabbanus; author of the multi-volume Shu"t Tzitz Eliezer) develops the position that mitzvas yishuv Eretz Yisrael is a full mitzvah chiyuvis on every yachid, with an additional collective dimension on klal Yisrael (the strength of yishuv deriving from koach ha'rabim). After the establishment of the State of Israel in 1948 and the dramatic improvement in conditions for new olim, the Tzitz Eliezer writes that the historical peturim of earlier generations — danger of the journey, persecution by surrounding nations, restrictive Ottoman governance — no longer obtain. The full chiyuv stands. ↩

Rav Kook's reply to the Ridvaz's charge of being a "Zionist" stands as one of the great expressions of his theology of Eretz Yisrael; he embraces the label as a halachic position grounded in the Gemara's and the Yerushalmi's teachings on the ma'alah of life in the land. The specific igros are in the standard editions of Igros HaRaayah for 5670; a precise igros number was not supplied in the source shiur. ↩

The Ramban brings the sugya in his Derashas Rosh HaShanah at Akko as the framework for understanding his own decision to leave wife and children in Spain in pursuit of mitzvas yishuv Eretz Yisrael — a personal mesiras nefesh the derashah records candidly. ↩
  23. Sefer Vayoel Moshe (Rebbe Reb Yoel Teitelbaum, the Satmar Rav, zt"l) — the sustained halachic case against contemporary yishuv, resting on Rabbeinu Chaim HaKohen and on the shalosh shevu'os. — The Satmar Rav, zt"l (1887–1979, Hungary, then Williamsburg), in Sefer Vayoel Moshe (published 1959–1961), develops the Satmar halachic-ideological position against the State of Israel and against the chiyuv of contemporary aliyah. The sefer is in three parts: Ma'amar Shalosh Shevu'os, Ma'amar Yishuv Eretz Yisrael, and Ma'amar Leshon HaKodesh. The position on yishuv rests substantially both on Tosafos's citation of Rabbeinu Chaim HaKohen and on the sugya of the shalosh shevu'os; the former is precarious given the cluster of poskim rejecting the citation as a talmid toeh interpolation, while the latter is resolved in the mainstream Acharonim by the Avnei Nezer's treatment (see note [26]). ↩
  24. The personal seal of the Ramban, recovered in archaeological excavations in Eretz Yisrael in 1972; bears the inscription "Rabbeinu Moshe ben Nachman"; on display at the Israel Museum, Yerushalayim. — The seal is among the most significant material artifacts of the era of the Rishonim recovered to date and substantiates the Ramban's residence in Eretz Yisrael from 1267 onward. Find-year and site should be verified against a scholarly archaeological reference; the ArtScroll Ramban edition reproduces the seal on its cover. ↩
  25. Gemara Kesubos 110b–111a — the shalosh shevu'os of Rabbi Yosi b'Rabbi Chanina. — The sugya at the close of Kesubos develops the three oaths with which HaKadosh Baruch Hu adjured klal Yisrael and the nations at the time of the churban: that Yisrael not ascend b'chomah, not rebel against the nations, and (the parallel oath) that the nations not oppress Yisrael excessively. The Ramban in his hassagos to Sefer HaMitzvos treats the shevu'os as a limitation on manner (prohibiting mass armed collective ascent against the ruling power) rather than as an abrogation of the mitzvah of individual yeshivah. The sugya is the halachic foundation of the Satmar position developed in Vayoel Moshe's Ma'amar Shalosh Shevu'os. ↩
  26. Shu"t Avnei Nezer, Yoreh De'ah 454 (and related, 455–456) — the resolution of the shalosh shevu'os; affirmative chiyuv aliyah in zman hazeh. — Rabbi Avraham Bornsztain of Sochatchov (1838–1910; the Avnei Nezer), in Yoreh De'ah 454 and the surrounding simanim, provides the decisive Acharon treatment of the shalosh shevu'os. The core yesodos: (i) the oaths are conditional on mutual observance by the nations — the nations' violation of their parallel oath renders the Jewish oath batel; (ii) the oath against ascending b'chomah prohibits mass armed revolt, not individual aliyah or communal purchase and settlement with the permission of the governing authority; (iii) the mitzvah of yishuv is a full chiyuv upon the yachid in zman hazeh, with no shevu'ah-based petur. The teshuvah is the single most important Acharon-source on the sugya. ↩
  27. Sefer Eim HaBanim Semeichah (Rav Yissachar Shlomo Teichtal, zt"l, Budapest, 1943) — internal chassidish reversal; defense of contemporary aliyah. — Rav Yissachar Shlomo Teichtal, zt"l (1885–1945; Hy"d, murdered on a death-march in the final weeks of the war; talmid of the Munkatcher school), composed Eim HaBanim Semeichah in Budapest in 1943, in the shadow of the Churban Europa. The sefer is a sustained halachic and hashkafic argument against the Hungarian-chassidish anti-yishuv position of his teachers — a public reversal of his own earlier position. He builds on the Avnei Nezer's resolution of the shalosh shevu'os and marshals the body of Chazal's aggadic material on the ma'alah of Eretz Yisrael. For a halachic survey that cites Vayoel Moshe, Eim HaBanim Semeichah is the parallel voice from within the same cultural register. ↩
  28. Chasam Sofer, Shu"t on Yoreh De'ah and drashos — mitzvah noheges b'zman hazeh, following the Ramban. — Rabbi Moshe Sofer (the Chasam Sofer, 1762–1839, Pressburg), in his teshuvos on Yoreh De'ah and in his drashos (and in his peirush to Chumash), treats mitzvas yishuv Eretz Yisrael as a chiyuv operative in his generation and in the generations to come. The Chasam Sofer's position bridges the classical poskim and the modern period; nineteenth-century poskim on this sugya uniformly take him as the central mekor. ↩
  29. Minchas Chinuch, Mitzvah 425 — sefer hamitzvos-register treatment of the Ramban's enumeration. — Rabbi Yosef Babad (1801–1874; the Minchas Chinuch), in his treatment of the mitzvah within the structure of Sefer HaChinuch, follows the Ramban's enumeration of mitzvas yishuv Eretz Yisrael and develops the halachic implications. The Minchas Chinuch's treatment is the standard sefer hamitzvos-level analysis for later yeshivish lomdus on the sugya. ↩
  30. Kol Dodi Dofek (Rav Yosef Dov Soloveitchik, zt"l) — the sheish defikos and the halachic weight of 1948. — Rav Yosef Dov Soloveitchik, zt"l (1903–1993; the Rav), in Kol Dodi Dofek (delivered as a Yom Ha'atzmaut address at Yeshiva University; subsequently published), develops the position that the events of 1948 constitute a dofek of the Dod to which klal Yisrael is halachically required to respond. The Rav's treatment of the mitzvah is intermediate between Rav Moshe's kiyumis framing and the Tzitz Eliezer's unqualified chiyuv; it recognizes the halachic weight of the sheish defikos and of the standing mitzvah of yishuv, while declining to translate them into a straightforward per-yachid chiyuv aliyah in galus. The reader is referred to Kol Dodi Dofek itself for the full development. ↩
