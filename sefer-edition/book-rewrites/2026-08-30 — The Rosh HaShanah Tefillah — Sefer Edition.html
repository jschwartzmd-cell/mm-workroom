תְּפִלַּת רֹאשׁ הַשָּׁנָה — מַלְכֻיּוֹת, זִכְרוֹנוֹת, שׁוֹפָרוֹת

The Rosh HaShanah Tefillah — The Core of the Day and the Duality of Pachad and Simchah
Shiur of Rabbi Ephrati — Sunday Morning Series on Tefillah
Recorded 30 August 2026  ·  Sefer Edition

First of three shiurim on the מחזור in the weeks approaching Rosh HaShanah 5787. The סידור is already in its place; the מחזור is already arranged. What remains is the אדם himself.

I. Approaching the Yamim Nora’im

The Two Emotions of the Day

The Yamim Nora’im (ימים נוראים) impose upon a Jew a demand that few other times of the year impose: the sustained holding of two apparently opposing emotions at one and the same moment. On the one side stands the pachad ha’din (פחד הדין) — the terror of judgment, the awareness that the books of the living and the dead lie open before the Throne. On the other side stands the bitachon (בטחון) of a child of Klal Yisrael (כלל ישראל) who trusts that his Father, sitting on the seat of judgment, remains his Father still, and will inscribe him for good along with the community that stands before Him. To hold these two together is itself a genuine avodah (עבודה) — and the entire structure of the Rosh HaShanah tefillah (תפילה), from its innermost pesukim to its outermost piyutim (פיוטים), is designed to escort the mispallel through it.

The Nadvorna Rebbe — Sader es Atzmecha

A chassidishe vort circulates in the courts of Nadvorna (נדבורנא) that sets the frame for any serious approach to the machzor (מחזור).[1] A chassid came before his Rebbe to take leave: he had accepted an appointment as shaliach tzibbur (שליח ציבור) for the Yamim Nora’im in a distant town and needed to travel in order to prepare the tefillos. Why leave now, the Rebbe asked, on the eve of Rosh HaShanah? The chassid replied: I must go to be מסדר the machzor — to arrange the tefillos of the day.

הַסִּדּוּר בִּמְקוֹמוֹ, וְהַמַּחְזוֹר מְסֻדָּר. אַתָּה צָרִיךְ לְסַדֵּר אֶת עַצְמְךָ.
The siddur is in its place, and the machzor is already arranged. It is you who need arranging.

The first preparation for Rosh HaShanah is not the machzor but the man. The machzor is a text; it has already been ordered by the chachomim (חכמים) of every generation. The variable in the equation is the nefesh (נפש) of the person who will hold that machzor in his hands. Every study of malchiyos, zichronos, and shofaros that follows in these pages serves that inner arranging — yishuv ha’daas (יישוב הדעת), berur ha’kavvanah (בירור הכוונה), and the alignment of the nefesh to the register of the day.

II. The Core of the Day — Beyond the Piyutim

Where the Emotional Weight Falls

Ask any Jew which portion of the Rosh HaShanah davening moves him most and the answer is almost universally the same: U’Nesaneh Tokef (וּנְתַנֶּה תֹּקֶף). And with justice. Nowhere else in the liturgy is the drama of the day painted in such vivid strokes — the malachim (מלאכים) trembling, the great shofar blast followed by the kol demamah dakah (קול דממה דקה), all creation passing before the Throne like sheep before a shepherd, and the climactic release: Teshuvah, Tefillah, u’Tzedakah ma’avirin es ro’a ha’gezerah (תשובה, תפילה, וצדקה מעבירין את רע הגזרה). The piyut earns its place in the emotional life of the mispaller, and the tradition has always so regarded it.

What Chazal Actually Required

Yet U’Nesaneh Tokef is a much later composition, and its authorship and dating remain the subject of ongoing scholarly discussion.[2] What is beyond dispute is this: Abaye (אביי) and Rava (רבא) never said U’Nesaneh Tokef. The Rosh HaShanah tefillah as depicted in the Gemara (גמרא) consists of pesukim (פסוקים) — nothing more, nothing less. The core of the day’s davening is malchiyos (מלכיות), zichronos (זכרונות), and shofaros (שופרות). This is not a matter of taste or aesthetic preference; it is a matter of what Chazal (חז"ל) established as the obligatory substance of the day.

An unsettling irony follows. The very portion of the davening that Chazal installed as the ikkar is often the portion that shluchei tzibbur and kehillos race through most rapidly — while the piyutim, layered on generations and centuries later, receive the sustained emotional attention. This is a natural human response: drama moves the heart more readily than a bare pasuk. Yet the mispallel who wishes to daven Rosh HaShanah as Chazal designed it must reverse the emphasis. The piyut is the outer wrapping. The pesukim of malchiyos, zichronos, shofaros are the fruit itself. What to do practically with U’Nesaneh Tokef is not to demote it emotionally but to remember, when reciting it, that it is an hisarusa (התערותא) — an emotional overture — that opens the heart for the actual substance of the brachos that follow.

III. The Structure of Musaf — Nine Berachos

The Torah’s Names for the Day

The Torah gives Rosh HaShanah four designations: Yom Teruah (יום תרועה), Zichron Teruah (זכרון תרועה), Mikra Kodesh (מקרא קודש), and Shabbason (שבתון). Notably, the phrase Yom HaZikaron (יום הזכרון) — the formulation that appears in the brachah of Mekadesh Yom HaZikaron (מקדש יום הזכרון) — is nowhere given by the Torah itself. That name is the coinage of Chazal. The Torah tells us little about specific prayers; it tells us that this is a Yom Tov, and that a shofar (שופר) must be sounded.

The Chiddush of the Mishnah

The unique liturgical architecture of the day is the work of Chazal. The Mishnah Rosh HaShanah (משנה ראש השנה)[3] establishes that in place of the standard seven brachos of the Musaf (מוסף) of every Yom Tov — three opening, three closing, and a single middle brachah for the kedushas ha’yom (קדושת היום) — Rosh HaShanah adds three special sections: malchiyos, proclaiming the sovereignty of the King; zichronos, invoking His remembrance; and shofaros, centered upon the shofar.

In principle this would yield ten brachos. The Gemara,[4] however, rules that malchiyos is not to be recited as an independent brachah but is to be merged into the middle brachah of kedushas ha’yom itself. The result is a structure of nine brachos — a count unique in the entire liturgical year. The Gemara gives the reason in a formula of programmatic force: kedei she’tamlichuni aleichem (כדי שתמליכוני עליכם) — so that you crown Me over you. Here lies a chiddush that reaches far beyond a technical halachah: the kedushas ha’yom of Rosh HaShanah is malchus (מלכות). The sanctity of the day is not one thing and the coronation another; they are a single act. This is why malchiyos is not appended as a fourth item on top of kedushas ha’yom, but embedded within it. The very substance of what makes this day kadosh is the enthronement of the King.

IV. Historical Layers — Tannaim, Rav, Geonim

The Machlokes on the Placement of Malchiyos

The precise station of malchiyos within the Amidah (עמידה) was itself the subject of a foundational machlokes among the Tannaim (תנאים).[5] Rabbi Akiva (רבי עקיבא) held — as is the halachah today — that malchiyos is integrated into the middle unique brachah of the Musaf Amidah, thereby constituting the kedushas ha’yom. Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri (רבי יוחנן בן נורי) argued that malchiyos ought instead to be integrated into the third brachah of kedushas HaShem (קדושת השם), the daily Atah Kadosh (אתה קדוש) that on Rosh HaShanah culminates in HaMelech HaKadosh (המלך הקדוש).

Beneath the surface of this dispute lies a deeper conceptual divide. For Rabbi Akiva, malchiyos is the very content of the day’s kedushah — an independent kiyum (קיום) in the kedushas ha’yom. For Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri, malchiyos is a general statement of malchus Shamayim (מלכות שמים) that piggybacks upon the daily proclamation of HaMelech HaKadosh. The halachah follows Rabbi Akiva: on Rosh HaShanah, the sanctity of the day is the coronation, and any structure that separates them dilutes both.

Even after the halachah was fixed, the era of the Mishnah saw practice divided along geographical lines. The Yerushalmi (ירושלמי)[6] preserves the record: in the Galil (גליל) the custom followed Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri; in Yehudah (יהודה) the custom followed Rabbi Akiva. The uniformity that later generations take for granted took centuries of consolidation to achieve.

Ten Pesukim per Section

In the era of the Tannaim, the substance of each of the three special sections was concise and formal. The Gemara[7] specifies the composition: for each of malchiyos, zichronos, and shofaros, ten pesukim were recited — three from the Torah, three from Kesuvim (כתובים) (specifically Tehillim, תהילים), three from Nevi’im (נביאים), and a concluding tenth pasuk once again from the Torah. The Torah brackets the structure; Kesuvim and Nevi’im fill the middle. This is the same design-logic Chazal employ throughout Shas in the binyanei av of scriptural exposition: the Torah is foundation and closing anchor; the Nach is elaboration. In the era of the Tannaim, this was the entirety of the brachah’s substance beyond its opening and closing formulas. There were no introductions and no piyutim — simply the pesukim, framed by the brachah.

Teka D’Rav — The Amoraic Introductions

The next layer was the work of one of the greatest of the Amoraim: Rav (רב), the founder of the yeshiva of Sura. To each of the three sections he composed a thematic hakdamah — a doctrinal introduction to be recited before the pesukim. This series of introductions became known in the halachic literature as Teka D’Rav (תָּקַע דְּרַב) — “the establishment of Rav.”[8] The three hakdamos are:

  • Aleinu L’Shabeach (עלינו לשבח) — for malchiyos.
  • Atah Zocher Ma’aseh Olam (אתה זוכר מעשה עולם) — for zichronos.
  • Atah Nigleisa Ba’Anan Kevodecha (אתה נגלית בענן כבודך) — for shofaros.

A remarkable historical fact deserves its full weight: Aleinu L’Shabeach — the tefillah with which every davening of the year is closed, in every kehillah across the Jewish world — was composed by Rav as an introduction to malchiyos of Rosh HaShanah Musaf. Its later migration into the daily liturgy as the closing of every service is a secondary appropriation. Its original home is here, opening the coronation section of the day of the coronation.

The Piyutim — Geonim and Rishonim

The piyutim of the Rosh HaShanah machzor were composed later still, in the era of the Geonim (גאונים) and the early Rishonim (ראשונים). Great paytanim (פייטנים) took the central themes of the day and wove them into intricate poetic architecture. This layer of the machzor is both a treasure and a challenge. For those to whom lashon ha’kodesh (לשון הקודש) is transparent, the piyutim deepen and beautify the davening. For those to whom the language is opaque, reciting a long and elaborate piyut without comprehension may miss the point of the composition altogether.

A practical suggestion emerges. Rather than racing through the entire catalog of piyutim with comprehension of none, the mispallel is better served by selecting one — preparing its meaning in advance, understanding its imagery, and saying that one with genuine kavvanah (כוונה). One piyut understood outweighs many raced. This is not to dismiss the ancient Ashkenazi tradition of piyutei ha’Kalir as tefillah shel avoseinu (תפילה של אבותינו) — a tradition defended by great poskim including the Chida and the Chasam Sofer — but only to prioritize, within the finite hours of a Rosh HaShanah davening, the comprehended over the uncomprehended.

V. The Three Themes

Malchiyos — The Coronation of the King

The Gemara states the purpose of malchiyos in a single phrase: kedei she’tamlichuni aleichem — so that you crown Me over you. Rosh HaShanah is the day on which Klal Yisrael places the crown upon the head of the Ribbono Shel Olam (רבונו של עולם), kaviyachol (כביכול). And the crown is placed not by malachim and not by tzaddikim gemurim alone: it is placed by every simple Jew who steps into shul on the night of Rosh HaShanah.

A story preserved from the childhood of Rav Yosef Dov Soloveitchik, zt”l (הגאון רבי יוסף דב סולובייצ’יק זצ"ל), the Rav of Boston,[9] captures this idea with the freshness of a child hearing it for the first time. He recounted that on the day of Erev Rosh HaShanah in the chassidishe shtetl of Chaslavich, his first-grade rebbi — a Lubavitcher chassid — walked into the classroom with his face aglow. He said to the children: Do you know what tonight is? Tonight is קרוינונג נאכט — Kroining Nacht — Coronation Night. Tonight we inaugurate the coronation of הקדוש ברוך הוא. We place a crown upon His head, כביכול. And do you know who places the crown? Yankl the tailor, and Berl the shoemaker — every simple Yid ascends to the כסא הכבוד and crowns the רבש"ע. We declare: ריבונו של עולם — You are our king.

This is the essence of malchiyos. The tailor’s crown and the shoemaker’s crown together become the crown of the King. Nothing in the section’s substance requires ancestral zechus or scholarly attainment; it requires only the willingness of a Jew to stand in shul and, from the depths of his own nefesh, to say Melech.

Zichronos — The Dimension of Yom HaDin

The second section, zichronos, opens the dimension of the day as Yom HaDin (יום הדין). The Gemara supplies its reason with an economy parallel to malchiyos: kedei she’ya’aleh zichronchem lefanai le’tovah (כדי שיעלה זכרונכם לפני לטובה) — so that your remembrance rises before Me for good. In the brachah of zichronos, Klal Yisrael reminds the Ribbono Shel Olam — kaviyachol — that He is the One before Whom nothing is forgotten, and that at the head of all things He remembers stands Akeidas Yitzchak (עקדת יצחק).

The theology at the core of zichronos is the theology of the covenant. Klal Yisrael asks to be judged not merely on its own account but against the standing of the fathers — the zechus of the Akeidah that stands eternally before the Throne. On this day, when the books are opened, the same zichron that recorded the ram caught in the thicket at Har HaMoriah (הר המוריה) rises again on behalf of Yitzchak’s children.

Shofaros — The Voice Across History

The third section, shofaros, is the avodah of the day proper: Klal Yisrael blows the shofar, and Klal Yisrael speaks about the shofar. The first great shofar blast in the history of the world sounded at Matan Torah (מתן תורה). The final shofar will sound at the geulah shleimah: Tekiah Gedolah l’Cheiruseinu (תקיעה גדולה לחירותנו). Between the first shofar and the last shofar sits every shofar of Jewish history — the shofaros of Yehoshua at Yericho, the shofaros that will herald the ingathering of the exiles, and, not least, the shofar that will be sounded tomorrow morning in the beis ha’knesses where the mispallel finds himself standing. All of this is folded into the brachah of shofaros. The section is not merely a description of the mitzvah being performed alongside it; it is a placement of the mispallel within the entire arc of Jewish time.

VI. The Duality — Pachad and Simchah

The Dimension of Pachad

Rosh HaShanah is a day of pachad and eimah (אימה). The Gemara[10] teaches that three sefarim (ספרים) are opened before the Throne on Rosh HaShanah: one for the tzaddikim gemurim, one for the reshaim gemurim, and one for the beinonim (בינונים) — the great middle in which most of humanity finds itself. The tzaddikim are inscribed at once for life; the reshaim at once for death; the beinonim are teluyim v’omdim (תלויים ועומדים) — suspended, hanging between the two possibilities, from Rosh HaShanah until Yom Kippur.

The mashal preserved from Rav Chaim Shmuelevitz, zt”l (רבי חיים שמואלביץ זצ"ל), the Mirrer Rosh Yeshiva,[11] renders the tolui v’omeid in language of unmistakable weight: it is as though a noose is placed around the neck of the beinoni, and the ten days of Teshuvah are the interval in which his teshuvah and his maasim must come forward as witnesses to save him. This is what the Aseres Yemei Teshuvah actually are — not a pleasant devotional interlude between two Yamim Tovim, but the interval of a suspended sentence, granted precisely so that the ten days may alter the verdict.

The Dimension of Simchah

And yet the same tradition that establishes the pachad establishes the simchah alongside it. The Gemara asks[12] why Hallel is not recited on Rosh HaShanah despite its status as a major Yom Tov, and answers that when the King sits upon the throne of judgment with the books of life and death open before Him, Klal Yisrael does not sing songs of praise. The suppression of Hallel is itself a form of pachad. And yet the very same sugya records the counter-side: lovshin levanin u’misatfin levanin (לובשין לבנין ומתעטפין לבנין) — Klal Yisrael dresses in white, garbs itself as one preparing for a day of joy, and comes to the day with simchah.

The scriptural anchor is the pasuk in Nechemiah,[13] spoken by Ezra HaSofer to the assembled returnees from Bavel on the very day of Rosh HaShanah:

לְכוּ אִכְלוּ מַשְׁמַנִּים וּשְׁתוּ מַמְתַקִּים... כִּי חֶדְוַת ה' הִיא מָעֻזְּכֶם.
Go, eat rich foods and drink sweet drinks... for the joy of Hashem is your strength.
Nechemiah 8:10

The assembly had wept upon hearing the words of the Torah; Ezra instructed them not to weep, for this is a holy day, and the joy of Hashem is their strength. From this pasuk the tradition draws the white garments, the seudah of Yom Tov, and even the practice, preserved by the Pri Chadash (פרי חדש),[14] of sending mishloach manos (משלוח מנות) on Rosh HaShanah — a minhag that fell into general disuse but that testifies to how squarely the tradition placed simchah alongside the din.

Gilu Bir’adah — The Synthesis

How can the two coexist? The pasuk in Tehillim[15] supplies the formula that has served as the theological anchor for this synthesis across the generations:

עִבְדוּ אֶת ה' בְּיִרְאָה, וְגִילוּ בִּרְעָדָה.
Serve Hashem with fear, and rejoice with trembling.
Tehillim 2:11

The joy is not despite the trembling but within it. Not sequentially — not first pachad, then simchah, when the danger has passed — but simultaneously, held together in the same davening, the same white garment, the same seat in the same shul. The Nefesh HaChaim teaches this simultaneity as the fruit of daas (דעת) — a mature awareness of Whom one stands before, in which the terror of the judgment does not extinguish the trust in the Judge but is animated by it. Only a Jew who knows that his Judge is also his Father can do this. The Judge is real; the Father is real; the trembling and the joy are two facets of the encounter with the One who is both.

VII. Personal Kavvanah and Communal Identity

The Length of Shemoneh Esrei

A common mispallel’s error is to race through the Shemoneh Esrei in order to keep pace with the tzibbur. The truth is the opposite: on Rosh HaShanah, one’s personal Amidah ought if anything to be longer, not shorter, provided that the extra time is being spent in genuine address to the Ribbono Shel Olam. The piyutim of the chazaras ha’shatz (חזרת הש"ץ) are the sidebar. The ikkar is the Shemoneh Esrei itself and the pesukim of malchiyos, zichronos, shofaros within it.

Within the Community

Yet personal kavvanah must be balanced against the truth that Rosh HaShanah is davened as a tzibbur. The Zohar[16] applies to the Yamim Nora’im the pasuk of the Shunamite woman — b’soch ami anochi yoshaves (בתוך עמי אנכי יושבת): I dwell in the midst of my people. The individual who does not seek to be singled out but is embedded in the community is thereby granted the community’s protection. When the mekatreig searches for individuals to indict, the mispallel who has folded himself into the tzibbur is not visible as a target. This is why the tefillos of the day are formulated in the plural — chatanu lefanecha (חטאנו לפניך) — and why the day’s tefillos in general do not admit of solitary formulations.

The tension between personal kavvanah and communal identity is not a contradiction but a genuine synthesis. The mispallel does not choose between the two. He immerses his personal Amidah in the tzibbur’s Amidah: the personal voice inside the communal voice, the individual within the collective. Hillel’s teaching in Avos[17] — im ein ani li, mi li? U’kshe’ani le’atzmi, mah ani? (אם אין אני לי, מי לי? וכשאני לעצמי, מה אני?) — is not a paradox but the exact halachic-hashkafic posture required of every mispallel on Rosh HaShanah. Both halves apply, and both apply at the same moment.

VIII. The Weight of the Gezar Din

The Michtav MeEliyahu on the Year’s Decree

The gravity of Rosh HaShanah is captured in a letter of Rav Eliyahu Eliezer Dessler, zt”l, preserved in Michtav MeEliyahu (מכתב מאליהו).[18] Writing to his son, Rav Dessler observes that all the tzaros, yesurim, and agmas nefesh that a person experiences over the course of the year have been decreed on the previous Rosh HaShanah. Had the tefillah on that Rosh HaShanah been recited with greater kavvanah, had the teshuvah been deeper, the year’s decree could have been fundamentally different. The point of the letter is not fatalism but its opposite: the day carries real causal weight on the coming year, and deserves the investment appropriate to that weight.

This position is not to be read as displacing the Rambam’s classical formulation in Hilchos Teshuvah that teshuvah during the Aseres Yemei Teshuvah genuinely reopens the gezar din (גזר דין). The two teachings inhabit the same reality from different angles. Rav Dessler is describing what happens if a mispallel comes to Rosh HaShanah in his ordinary state; the Rambam is describing the transformative power still available in the ten-day interval that follows. Both are the tradition’s teaching. Together they generate the same practical implication: this is the day to invest.

Reb Elimelech and Reb Zusha

A story preserved in the biographical literature of chassidus, told of Rebbe Reb Elimelech of Lizhensk (רבי אלימלך מליזענסק), author of Noam Elimelech, and his brother Rebbe Reb Zusha of Anipoli (רבי זושא מאניפולי),[19] conveys the immovability of a gezar din once it has been sealed. During the years the two brothers were traveling in self-imposed galus (גלות), they lodged one night at a rough tavern. Drunken peasants took a dislike to Reb Zusha and resolved to give him a beating. Reb Elimelech — unwilling that his brother should suffer alone — quietly switched beds with Reb Zusha, that he himself might receive the blows. When the peasants returned, however, they went not to the bed they had been eyeing but to the other, and gave Reb Zusha the beating — three times in succession.

Reb Zusha, when the peasants had gone, turned to his brother and said: On Rosh HaShanah it was decreed that Zusha should receive three blows tonight. You can switch beds all you wish; if the רבש"ע has decreed something, it will come to pass.

The Practical Consequence

The gezar din is decreed. If a person wishes to prevent the difficulties of the coming year, the day on which the decree is set is the day on which to intervene. The intervention has one shape: focused avodah — proper tefillah, sincere teshuvah, and hearing the shofar. When the mispallel recites malchiyos, zichronos, shofaros, holding within a single davening both the awe of the din and the trust of the ban in his Father, he may merit to be nichtav v’nechtam b’sefer tzaddikim l’chaim tovim (נכתב ונחתם בספר צדיקים לחיים טובים).

IX. Practical Summary

  • Prepare the self, not the machzor. The siddur is in its place; the machzor is arranged. What remains is sader es atzmecha.
  • The core of the davening is the pesukim of malchiyos, zichronos, shofaros. The piyutim — including U’Nesaneh Tokef — are the outer layer. Do not race the inner core.
  • Choose one piyut. Prepare its meaning in advance and say it with kavvanah. One piyut understood outweighs many raced through.
  • Hold pachad and simchah together. The white garments and the Nechemiah pasuk on the one side; the teluyim v’omdim on the other. Gilu bir’adah.
  • Personal kavvanah and communal identity are not opposed. The Shemoneh Esrei may run longer; the b’soch ami must remain. The mispallel is saved as an individual by being embedded in Klal Yisrael.
  • The day carries the weight of the year. Invest in it accordingly.

U’Nesaneh Tokef — late piyut; authorship contested. — The traditional attribution names Rabbeinu Amnon of Mainz (רבי אמנון ממגנצא), preserved in the famous account of the Or Zarua (thirteenth century). Modern scholarship has surfaced pre-Amnon versions in Cairo Genizah fragments, suggesting a Byzantine or earlier origin with the Amnon narrative attaching to the text at a later stage. What is certain and undisputed: the Amoraim did not recite U’Nesaneh Tokef; the tefillah described by the Gemara is the pesukim of malchiyos, zichronos, shofaros. ↩
  3. Mishnah Rosh HaShanah 4:5 — the nine-brachah Musaf. — The Mishnah establishes the unique Musaf structure of Rosh HaShanah: the standard opening three and closing three brachos, with a middle unit consisting of kedushas ha’yom combined with malchiyos, followed by zichronos, followed by shofaros — nine brachos in total. This count is unique in the liturgical year. ↩
  4. Rosh HaShanah 32a — Malchiyos combined with Kedushas HaYom: כדי שתמליכוני עליכם. — The Gemara discusses the merger of malchiyos with kedushas ha’yom and the formulaic reasoning behind each of the three special sections. The formula kedei she’tamlichuni aleichem is the Gemara’s answer to why malchiyos is embedded specifically in the kedushas ha’yom brachah: the kedushas ha’yom of Rosh HaShanah is the coronation. Parallel formulas: for zichronos, kedei she’ya’aleh zichronchem lefanai le’tovah; for shofaros, the avodah and the zecher of the shofar of Matan Torah and of the future redemption. ↩
  5. Rosh HaShanah 34b — Rabbi Akiva vs. Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri on the placement of malchiyos. — Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri holds that malchiyos should be integrated into the brachah of Atah Kadosh, culminating in HaMelech HaKadosh. Rabbi Akiva holds that malchiyos goes into the middle unique brachah, becoming the substance of the kedushas ha’yom. The halachah follows Rabbi Akiva. The deeper conceptual dispute concerns whether malchiyos is the very content of the day’s kedushah (Rabbi Akiva) or a separable kiyum attached to it (Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri). The Rambam’s formulation of the closing brachah — Melech al kol ha’aretz mekadesh Yisrael v’Yom HaZikaron — is a rich locus for the Brisker analysis of this shitah. ↩
  6. Yerushalmi Rosh HaShanah, ch. 4, hal. 6 — regional practice divided. — In the era of the Mishnah, the Galil followed Rabbi Yochanan ben Nuri; Yehudah followed Rabbi Akiva. The consolidation into a single unified practice took generations. A useful historical footnote: uniformity of minhag Yisrael is often the product of centuries of consolidation, not a state that existed from the outset. ↩
  7. Rosh HaShanah 32a — the ten pesukim: 3 Torah + 3 Kesuvim + 3 Nevi’im + 1 Torah. — The Gemara specifies the required composition. Each of malchiyos, zichronos, and shofaros requires ten pesukim: three from the Torah, three from Kesuvim (specifically Tehillim), three from Nevi’im, and a concluding tenth pasuk from the Torah. The Torah serves as bookend; Kesuvim and Nevi’im occupy the middle. This is the substantive core of each brachah in the era of the Tannaim, before the addition of any introductions or piyutim. ↩
  8. Teka D’Rav — Rav’s introductions: Aleinu L’Shabeach, Atah Zocher, Atah Nigleisa. — Rav (Abba Arikha), the founder of the yeshiva of Sura, composed thematic hakdamos to each of the three special sections of Musaf. The term Teka D’Rav — “the establishment of Rav” — is a designation that entered the halachic literature to describe this liturgical layer. The attribution of Aleinu L’Shabeach specifically to Rav is standard among the Rishonim (Machzor Vitry, Kol Bo); its later migration into the daily closing of every davening is a secondary appropriation of a text originally composed to open malchiyos of Rosh HaShanah. ↩
  9. Rav Yosef Dov Soloveitchik, zt”l — the Chaslavich Kroining Nacht story. — The story of the first-grade Lubavitcher rebbi in Chaslavich calling Erev Rosh HaShanah Kroining Nacht (קרוינונג נאכט) — and describing every simple Jew as ascending the kisei ha’kavod to place the crown upon the head of the Ribbono Shel Olam — appears in several published collections of Rav Soloveitchik’s Rosh HaShanah derashos, notably in Days of Deliverance and Before Hashem You Shall Be Purified. It is one of the Rav’s signature stories on the personal-and-universal dimension of coronation. ↩
  10. Rosh HaShanah 16b — three sefarim; teluyim v’omdim. — The Gemara, quoting Rabbi Kruspedai in the name of Rabbi Yochanan: on Rosh HaShanah, three sefarim are opened — one for reshaim gemurim, one for tzaddikim gemurim, and one for beinonim. The tzaddikim are inscribed at once for life; the reshaim at once for death; the beinonim hang in suspension from Rosh HaShanah until Yom Kippur. The ten days of teshuvah are given specifically to those in the middle to shift their standing. ↩
  11. Rav Chaim Shmuelevitz, zt”l — the noose mashal on teluyim v’omdim. — The mashal that the beinoni stands with a noose around his neck through the ten days — his teshuvah and maasim being the witnesses that must come forward to save him — is preserved in the sichos of Rav Chaim Shmuelevitz, Rosh Yeshiva of the Mirrer Yeshiva in Yerushalayim (published as Sichos Musar). The mashal gives concrete emotional weight to the abstract halachic category of tolui v’omeid. ↩
  12. Rosh HaShanah 32b — no Hallel because the books are open. — The Gemara asks why Hallel is not recited on Rosh HaShanah despite its status as a major Yom Tov. The answer: Efshar Melech yosheiv al kisei din, v’sifrei chaim v’sifrei meisim pesuchin lefanav, v’Yisrael omrim shirah? The absence of Hallel is itself a form of pachad. And yet the parallel practice of lovshin levanin and the Nechemiah pasuk of chedvas Hashem hi ma’uzchem coexist within the same day. ↩
  13. Nechemiah 8:10 — חדות ה' היא מעוזכם. — The full pasuk: ויאמר להם: לכו אכלו משמנים ושתו ממתקים, ושלחו מנות לאין נכון לו, כי קדוש היום לאדונינו; ואל תעצבו, כי חדות ה' היא מעוזכם. The context: Ezra HaSofer reads the Torah to the returned Babylonian exiles on the day of Rosh HaShanah; the assembly weeps upon hearing the words, and Ezra tells them not to weep, for the day is holy, and the joy of Hashem is their strength. This pasuk is the textual anchor for the simchah side of Rosh HaShanah — the white garments, the seudah, and the sending of mishloach manos. ↩
  14. Pri Chadash — minhag of mishloach manos on Rosh HaShanah. — The Pri Chadash (Rabbi Chizkiyah di Silva, 1659–1698), commentary on Shulchan Aruch Orach Chaim, records the minhag of sending mishloach manos on Rosh HaShanah, drawing on the Nechemiah pasuk above (v’shilchu manos le’ein nachon lo). The practice has fallen into general disuse — most contemporary kehillos associate mishloach manos exclusively with Purim — but the Pri Chadash preserves it as a legitimate ancient practice for Rosh HaShanah, rooted in the Nechemiah context. The specific siman is generally identified with the hilchos Rosh HaShanah section of Orach Chaim. ↩
  15. Tehillim 2:11 — עבדו את ה' ביראה וגילו ברעדה. — The full pasuk: עבדו את ה' ביראה, וגילו ברעדה — “Serve Hashem with fear, and rejoice with trembling.” The Gemara in Berachos 30b applies this pasuk to establish that intense simchah and intense yirah must coexist in avodas Hashem. The classical elaboration of the simultaneity of the two — not sequentially but as a single unified posture — appears in the Nefesh HaChaim (Shaar III) as the fruit of daas. ↩
  16. Zohar — בתוך עמי אנכי יושבת applied to Rosh HaShanah. — The Zohar (Chelek III, Parshas Emor, in the passages on the Yamim Nora’im) applies the pasuk from Melachim Bais 4:13 — b’soch ami anochi yoshaves, the Shunamite woman’s answer to Elisha — to the theology of communal davening on the Yamim Nora’im. The individual embedded in the tzibbur is not visible to the mekatreig as a solitary target; the plural formulations of the day’s tefillos derive from this principle. ↩
  17. Avos 1:14 — Hillel: אם אין אני לי מי לי. — The famous three-clause teaching of Hillel HaZaken: If I am not for myself, who will be for me? And when I am for myself alone, what am I? And if not now, when? The first clause anchors personal responsibility; the second anchors communal identity; and Rosh HaShanah requires both simultaneously. ↩
  18. Michtav MeEliyahu — prior Rosh HaShanah’s tefillah decrees the year’s tribulations. — Michtav MeEliyahu, by Rav Eliyahu Eliezer Dessler, zt”l (1892–1953), Bnei Brak. In a letter to his son (published in the later volumes of the collection), Rav Dessler writes that all the tzaros, yesurim, and agmas nefesh of the past year were decreed on the previous Rosh HaShanah. The point is not fatalism but the reverse: the day carries real causal weight on the coming year. Read alongside the Rambam in Hilchos Teshuvah 2:6–7 on the reopening of the gezar din through teshuvah during the Aseres Yemei Teshuvah, the two positions describe the same reality from complementary angles. ↩
  19. Rebbe Reb Elimelech of Lizhensk and Rebbe Reb Zusha of Anipoli — the tavern beating. — The two brothers, both talmidim of the Maggid of Mezritch: Rebbe Reb Elimelech of Lizhensk (1717–1787), author of Noam Elimelech, and Rebbe Reb Zusha of Anipoli (c. 1718–1800). They spent years traveling in self-imposed galus, wandering from town to town in disguise, doing teshuvah for Klal Yisrael. The story of the switched beds at the tavern — and Reb Zusha’s punchline that a gezar din once decreed cannot be evaded by switching beds — appears in multiple forms across chassidishe biographical anthologies (Sipurei Chassidim, Ma’asei HaGedolim). The story’s force in this context: the decree is real and it is set; the intervention available is not the switching of beds but the investment of the day itself. ↩
