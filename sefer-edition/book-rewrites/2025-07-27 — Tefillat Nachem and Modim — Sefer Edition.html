תְּפִלַּת נַחֵם וּבִרְכַּת מוֹדִים

Tefillas Nachem and Birkas Modim — The Once-a-Year Chasimah, the Dates of the Churban, the Bow of Modim, and the Hidden Berachah for Children
Shiur of Rabbi Ephrati — Sunday Morning Series on Tefillah
Recorded ג׳ אב תשפ״ה  ·  27 July 2025  ·  Sefer Edition

לעילוי נשמת רבינו ר' נחמן מנדל בן ר' נחמן מנדל הלוי זצ"ל

A treatise in two parts. The first concerns Tefillas Nachem (תפילת נחם), the one berachah whose chasimah (חתימה) is said once a year: its single source in the Yerushalmi (ירושלמי), the structural rule of Rav Muna (רב מונא) and the placement of the Rif (רי"ף), the laws of one who forgot it, the prohibition against altering its nusach (נוסח), the question of why it is said only at Minchah (מנחה), and the dates of the churban (חורבן) from the seventh through the tenth of Av (אב) with the position of Rebbi Yochanan (רבי יוחנן). The second concerns Birkas Modim (ברכת מודים): the bow, the Gemara of the spine and the snake, the double sense of modim, and the berachah for children that lies concealed in “l’dor va-dor” (לדור ודור).

I. The Berachos Said Only Once a Year

It is told that a riddle was passed down in the family of the Rav, posed by his grandfather to the children: which berachos (ברכות) do we say only once in the year? The answer begins with Shehecheyanu (שהחיינו) on the night of Yom Kippur (ליל יום הכפורים); with “al achilas matzah” (על אכילת מצה) — once a year in Eretz Yisrael (ארץ ישראל), where there is a single seder, though twice in Chutz La’aretz (חוץ לארץ), where there are two; with “al bi’ur chametz” (על ביעור חמץ); with “al mikra Megillah” (על מקרא מגילה), according to certain opinions; and, according to Rav Saadia Gaon (רב סעדיה גאון), with the berachah on the immersion of Yom Kippur (טבילת יום הכפורים). One more remains, and it is the subject of the first part of this chapter: the chasimah of Tefillas Nachem at Minchah of Tisha B’Av (תשעה באב) — “Baruch Atah Hashem, menachem Tziyon uvoneh Yerushalayim” (ברוך אתה ה' מנחם ציון ובונה ירושלים).

The Bear and the Honey

The best-known ma’aseh (מעשה) bearing on the matter is told of Rabbi Yonasan Eibeschutz (רבי יונתן אייבשיץ).[1] A nobleman wagered that no one could teach a bear to pray. Rabbi Yonasan smeared honey on every page of a siddur (סידור); the bear learned to open the book and to turn its pages, one after another, in pursuit of the honey. So, too, many a Jew turns the pages of the siddur and moves his lips, with no kavanah (כוונה) at all.

The nimshal (נמשל) is plain. A person who recites Shemoneh Esrei (שמונה עשרה) daily can fall into the posture of the bear: words turning over without engagement. On Minchah of Tisha B’Av the siddur opens to a berachah that has not been said all year — Nachem (נחם) — and at once the worshipper wakes. He begins to think about what he is saying, and the attention of that single moment suffuses the whole year.

II. The Source of Tefillas Nachem

The Yerushalmi Alone

Tefillas Nachem is not found in the Bavli (בבלי). Its source is the Yerushalmi,[2] where the individual (יחיד) is told that on Tisha B’Av he must mention the me’ein ha-me’ora (מעין המאורע) — an addition tied to the character of the day. Rav Saadia Gaon is reported to have written[3] that although the Bavli community did not recite it, since the Yerushalmi records it and the bnei Eretz Yisrael (בני ארץ ישראל) say it, there is “ein hefsed b’amiraso” (אין הפסד באמירתו) — no loss in saying it. The framing is not that of obligation but of availability. The Shulchan Aruch (שולחן ערוך), in any event, rules that it is said.

The Principle of Me’ein HaMe’ora

The Yerushalmi thus establishes a structural principle: on a day with a distinctive character, Shemoneh Esrei is supplemented with content bearing on the event of the day. On Chanukah (חנוכה) and Purim (פורים) this is Al HaNissim (על הניסים); on Tisha B’Av, Nachem.

The Rule of Rav Muna

The passage records that Rabbi Avdima of Tzippori (רבי אבדימא דציפורין) put a question before Rav Muna (Rabbi Mana, רבי מנא): in which berachah of Shemoneh Esrei is the insertion to be said? One might have expected the reply, “v’liYerushalayim irecha” (ולירושלים עירך). The reply was instead a structural rule:

כָּל דָּבָר שֶׁהוּא לָבֹא אוֹמְרָהּ בָּעֲבוֹדָה. וְכָל דָּבָר שֶׁהוּא לְשֶׁעָבַר אוֹמְרָהּ בְּהוֹדָאָה.
Anything that concerns the future is said in Avodah (Retzei); anything that concerns the past is said in Hoda’ah (Modim).
Yerushalmi Berachos 4:3 · parallel in Taanis 2:2

The rule accounts for what we know. Al HaNissim speaks of miracles already wrought, and is therefore placed in Modim. Ya’aleh v’Yavo (יעלה ויבוא) speaks of future remembrance and of Mashiach (משיח), and is placed in Retzei (רצה).

Where Does Nachem Belong?

Nachem looks principally to the future: it asks that HaKadosh Baruch Hu (הקדוש ברוך הוא) console us, rebuild the Beis HaMikdash (בית המקדש), and restore Yerushalayim. By the rule of Rav Muna it would belong in Retzei. Yet it also dwells upon the past churban, and the authorities were therefore divided as to whether it is a matter of the past or of the future — whether it belongs in Retzei or in Modim.[4]

Our Psak — V’liYerushalayim Irecha

We follow neither limb of the Yerushalmi’s rule. The Rif (רי"ף)[5] rules that Nachem is recited in the berachah of v’liYerushalayim irecha, on a different ground altogether: the halachah in Avodah Zarah (עבודה זרה)[6] that one may add, in any berachah, a request that is me’ein inyan ha-berachah (מעין ענין הברכה) — of the nature of that berachah. Nachem speaks of Yerushalayim, and so it belongs in Birkas Yerushalayim.

Hearah — Two Principles, One Placement

The two principles seem to stand in tension: if the rule of Rav Muna reflects the very structure of Shemoneh Esrei, how can a consideration of me’ein ha-berachah override it? It may be suggested that the rule of Rav Muna is a default governing additions for which no berachah is on point. Al HaNissim has no berachah of its own in Shemoneh Esrei concerning Chanukah or Purim, and so the shape of the content — past or future — decides its place. Nachem, by contrast, has a berachah squarely on point in Birkas Yerushalayim, and specificity prevails over shape. On this reading both halachos stand. (This is offered as an analytic suggestion; it is not stated in the sources cited.)

III. One Who Forgot Nachem

An entire siman of the Shulchan Aruch is devoted to this berachah — Orach Chayim 557 (סימן תקנ"ז), Hilchos Tisha B’Av.[7]

Forgotten in Birkas Yerushalayim

By the structural rule of the Yerushalmi (future → Retzei) one might have returned and said it in Retzei. But the Taz (ט"ז) rules otherwise: it is said in Shema Koleinu (שמע קולנו), a comprehensive berachah into which any request may be admitted.[8]

A critical detail: when Nachem is said in Shema Koleinu, the special chasimah — “menachem Tziyon uvoneh Yerushalayim” — is not recited. One concludes as always, “Baruch Atah Hashem, shomea tefillah” (ברוך אתה ה' שומע תפילה). The once-a-year chasimah is forfeited.

Forgotten Entirely

One who completed Shemoneh Esrei and realized that he had omitted Nachem altogether does not repeat. The klal (כלל): an addition for the me’ora of the day, on a day that has no korban Mussaf (קרבן מוסף), does not require repetition if forgotten.[9]

  • Ya’aleh v’Yavo on Rosh Chodesh (ראש חודש): there is a Mussaf — one repeats.
  • Nachem on Tisha B’Av: there is no Mussaf — one does not repeat.

A further question was raised in connection with the forgotten Nachem, concerning the position of Rabbeinu Yitzchak ibn Giyas; it is recorded in the notes.[10]

IV. The Inviolability of the Nusach

The berachah describes Yerushalayim as ruined and degraded. A certain rabbi in Eretz Yisrael proposed to alter the nusach: “What is meant by ‘Yerushalayim charavah’ (ירושלים חרבה)? Today Yerushalayim is built and magnificent. There is no Beis HaMikdash, but the city itself is not destroyed.” He would have rephrased it in praise: “Baruch Hashem (ברוך ה'), we see the great change; only give us the Beis HaMikdash.”

Chas v’shalom (חס ושלום). It is forbidden to deviate from the mesorah (מסורה). Yerushalayim remains destroyed, degraded, and desolate. Without the malchus beis Dovid (מלכות בית דוד), without the Beis HaMikdash, without korbanos (קרבנות), without the kohanim (כהנים) at their stations in song and music (בשירה ובזמרה) — it is destroyed. We are a nation that wakes each day in anticipation of chazon ha-nechamah (חזון הנחמה); and until that day — baruch Hashem for the yishuv (יישוב) in Eretz Yisrael, for the Torah and mitzvos (תורה ומצוות) kept there in such abundance — Yerushalayim remains in the language of churban. To alter the nusach is unthinkable.

The Language of the Nusach

When the berachah is said with its chasimah it is enough to bring tears. The nusach speaks of the city as “ka-ishah akarah she-lo yaldah” (כאשה עקרה שלא ילדה), a barren woman who bore no child, whom the nations devoured; “al kein Tziyon be-mar tivkeh” (על כן ציון במר תבכה). A moving melody on these words was composed by Shlomo Simchi.[11] “Libi, libi al chalalei” (לבי לבי על חלליהם) — let the heart be broken over the slain; for it was HaKadosh Baruch Hu who kindled it, and He will rebuild it with fire.[12]

Yet the very first word of the berachah is Nachem (נחם): now is the time for consolation, the time for Mashiach to be born. Even as the Beis HaMikdash burns we already hold nechamah (נחמה), because we believe that the nechamah will come.

“Ba’esh Hitzatah u’va’esh Atah Asid l’vnosah”

The berachah closes with its great declaration: “ki Atah Hashem ba’esh hitzatah u’va’esh Atah asid l’vnosah” (כי אתה ה' באש הצתה ובאש אתה עתיד לבנותה). HaKadosh Baruch Hu burned the Beis HaMikdash through the nations, with fire, and He will rebuild it with fire: on one view, the third Beis HaMikdash (בית המקדש השלישי) descends from Shamayim (שמים) in fire.[13]

A debate among the acharonim (אחרונים) arises from this. There is a mitzvas aseh (מצות עשה) of binyan beis ha-bechirah (בנין בית הבחירה). If the third Beis HaMikdash descends from heaven, how is the mitzvah to be discharged? Some say that we shall perform the gimurim (גימורים) — the gates, the finishing work, the completion (השלמה).

V. Why Only at Minchah?

The question is a real one. Al HaNissim is said at every tefillah (תפילה) of Chanukah and Purim; Ya’aleh v’Yavo at Shacharis (שחרית), Minchah, and Ma’ariv (מעריב). Why is Nachem said only at Minchah?[14]

The Rosh

The Rosh (רא"ש) writes that all his days he wondered at the custom, for it is illogical: it should be said at every tefillah of Tisha B’Av.

The Mechaber and the Rama

The Mechaber (מחבר) words the halachah without qualification — “b’Tisha B’Av omer bi-voneh Yerushalayim: Nachem Hashem Elokeinu es avelei Tziyon” (בתשעה באב אומר בבונה ירושלים נחם ה' אלהינו את אבילי ציון) — which is understood to mean at every tefillah; many Sefardim (ספרדים) follow this. (Practice among Sefardim varies by community. The Rav had at first been under the impression that they did not say it at every tefillah, and confirmed the Mechaber’s wording only when pressed.) The Rama (רמ"א) glosses: the practice is widespread to say Nachem only at Minchah of Tisha B’Av.[15]

The Reason of the Rama

The Rama, citing the Rokeach (רוקח) and the Avudraham (אבודרהם), gives the reason: the fire was set in the Beis HaMikdash only at the close of Tisha B’Av — late afternoon, into the night — and therefore Nachem belongs only at the close of the day. Earlier, there is no nechamah yet: the Beis HaMikdash is, so to speak, still burning. The Beis Yosef (בית יוסף) gives the same reason in explaining the practice, which he traces to Rabbeinu Saadia (רבינו סעדיה).

The Tur (טור) records that there were places in which Rachem (רחם) was said at night and in the morning, and Nachem at Minchah; and that the matter depends upon minhag (מנהג).

Hearah — A Question on the Reason

If the reason is the burning, one might ask why Nachem is not also said at Ma’ariv of the following night, when the fire was at its height. It seems that Nachem is anchored to the day of the fast itself, said in its latest tefillah; the Mishnah Berurah (משנה ברורה) on the siman may be consulted for the working-out. (Offered as a question, not a resolution.)

VI. The Dates of the Churban

It is a striking fact that “Tisha B’Av” is nowhere stated in Tanach (תנ"ך) as the date of the churban.

Two Pesukim

  • Yirmeyahu (ירמיהו)[16]: Nevuzaradan (נבוזראדן), servant of Nevuchadnezzar (נבוכדנצר), burned the Beis HaMikdash, the king’s palace, and Yerushalayim on the tenth of Av.
  • Sefer Melachim (ספר מלכים): a similar but not identical pasuk (פסוק) records the seventh of Av.

Neither says the ninth; yet we fast on the ninth.

The Karaim

The Karaim (קראים), who reject Chazal (חז"ל) and follow only the text of Tanach, fasted on both — the seventh and the tenth, as the pesukim say. We follow Chazal, and Chazal resolved the contradiction.

The Process — Taanis 29a

The Gemara in Taanis[17] teaches that there was a process. Nevuzaradan, sar ha-tabbachim (שר הטבחים), yemach shemo (ימ"ש), came on the seventh of Av, as Melachim records, and with him the nations entered the heichal (היכל):

בְּשִׁבְעָה נִכְנְסוּ נׇכְרִים לַהֵיכָל, וְאָכְלוּ וְקִלְקְלוּ בּוֹ שְׁבִיעִי שְׁמִינִי. וּתְשִׁיעִי סָמוּךְ לַחֲשֵׁיכָה הֵצִיתוּ בּוֹ אֶת הָאוּר, וְהָיָה דּוֹלֵק וְהוֹלֵךְ כׇּל הַיּוֹם כּוּלּוֹ.
On the seventh the gentiles entered the Sanctuary, and on the seventh and eighth they ate in it and desecrated it. On the ninth, adjacent to nightfall, they set fire to it, and it burned on through the whole of the day.
Taanis 29a

In the language of the tradition, they were “ochlin v’shosin u’mekarkerin” (אוכלין ושותין ומקרקרין) — the word for revelry. On the seventh and eighth there was desecration but not yet fire. Only on the ninth, toward nightfall, was the fire set; it burned on throughout the tenth.

Some opinions held that one should fast on the tenth as well; we no longer do. A parallel halachah remains: a restraint from meat and wine extends into the tenth until midday.[18]

A Family Note — and the Basis of the Midday Kulah

One halachah: after midday on Tisha B’Av one may sit upon a chair. The Rav tells a family story. When his younger brother was a small child he came home from gan (גן) or first grade convinced that after midday Tisha B’Av was over — not only sitting on chairs, but eating and drinking; he put on shoes and began a meal. To this day the family teases him.

The basis of the kulah (קולא) after midday: there is a mesorah that Mashiach was born on Tisha B’Av.[19] The midrash (מדרש) says that ben Dovid Mashiach (בן דוד משיח) was born — whatever precisely that means. Since Mashiach was born around midday, half the day already carries nechamah.

VII. Rebbi Yochanan’s Shitah — Why Not the Tenth?

The same passage[20] records a striking statement of Rebbi Yochanan:

אִלְמָלֵי הָיִיתִי בְּאוֹתוֹ הַדּוֹר לֹא קְבַעְתִּיו אֶלָּא בָּעֲשִׂירִי, מִפְּנֵי שֶׁרוּבּוֹ שֶׁל הֵיכָל בּוֹ נִשְׂרַף. וְרַבָּנַן — אַתְחַלְתָּא דְפוּרְעֲנוּתָא עֲדִיפָא.
Had I been in that generation I would have fixed it only on the tenth, for the greater part of the heichal was burned on it. And the Sages: it is preferable to mark the beginning of the calamity.
Taanis 29a

We do not follow Rebbi Yochanan. Chazal mark the day of techilas ha-pur’anus (תחילת הפורענות), the beginning of the punishment, when the fire was set: the ninth.

A Tzeiruf for the Lamdanim — Bava Kamma 22

A famous machlokes (מחלוקת) in Bava Kamma[21] concerns the verse “ki seitzei esh” (כי תצא אש): when fire goes out and damages, one pays. Why? Mishum mamono (משום ממונו) — because it is one’s property, like one’s animal that did damage; or mishum chitzav (משום חיציו) — because it is one’s arrow, one’s own action? Rebbi Yochanan holds mishum chitzav; Resh Lakish (ריש לקיש) holds mishum mamono.

It has been suggested that Rebbi Yochanan is consistent with himself (רבי יוחנן לשיטתו). If fire is an arrow shot by its owner, the damage where the arrow lands is his action; the fire ignited by Nevuzaradan on the ninth continued as arrows in flight, and the principal damage of the tenth is part of the same act. On that reading the severity of the arrows landed on the tenth, and the fast should mark the tenth. Chazal hold that the primary moment is the beginning of the punishment, the ninth.

Hearah — The Connection Is a Suggestion

The Gemara in Taanis gives its own reason in the mouth of Rebbi Yochanan: “mipnei she-rubo shel heichal bo nisraf” (מפני שרובו של היכל בו נשרף) — the greater part of the heichal burned on the tenth. That is a consideration of rov (רוב), of the majority of the damage, and not as such a consideration of chitzav. The link to Bava Kamma is therefore a tzeiruf and not a settled connection: perhaps Rebbi Yochanan locates the day by where the damage fell, rather than where the cause began, precisely because he reads fire as chitzav rather than mamon. It should be treated as a lomdish suggestion.

VIII. Birkas Modim — The Bow

The seder of Shemoneh Esrei resumes: “Modim anachnu lach” (מודים אנחנו לך).

The Obligation of Bowing

At Modim the halachah requires a bow: at the opening (“Modim”) and at the chasimah (“Baruch Atah Hashem”). The four bows of Shemoneh Esrei are at the opening and close of Birkas Avos (ברכת אבות) and at the opening and close of Modim.

The Gemara in Bava Kamma

A frightening Gemara[22] states of the one who does not bow at Modim:

שִׁדְרוֹ שֶׁל אָדָם – לְאַחַר שֶׁבַע שָׁנִים נַעֲשֶׂה נָחָשׁ. וְהָנֵי מִילֵּי דְּלָא כָּרַע בְּ״מוֹדִים״.
A person’s spine, after seven years, becomes a snake — and this applies to one who did not bow at Modim.
Bava Kamma 16a

What does this mean? Tosafos[23] takes it up: the punishment is measure for measure (מדה כנגד מדה) — one who rose from his bow like a snake, as is told of Rav Sheishes (רב ששת) in Berachos 12b, is made into one; being made a snake is itself the disgrace. A gloss printed alongside the Tosafos adds an explanation offered by “some”: there is a small bone in the spine — the etzem ha-luz (עצם הלוז) — that, according to Chazal, never decomposes. (The Rav devoted an entire exposition to it; physicians debate which bone is meant.) From this bone HaKadosh Baruch Hu will reconstruct the body at techiyas ha-meisim (תחיית המתים). Were that bone to become a snake, there would be no techiyas ha-meisim — and the gloss rejects this as too harsh a punishment for this sin, since every Jew has a share in the Olam Haba (עולם הבא). In the Rav’s formulation, there is some lesser punishment, with snake-like characteristics.

Why a Snake?

Because the nachash (נחש) was the original kafui tov (כפוי טובה). HaKadosh Baruch Hu created the nachash as the most clever of creatures, and the nachash repaid Him by leading Adam and Chavah to the Eitz HaDaas (עץ הדעת). We are still engaged in repairing that sin; Chazal say that every soul was present at it. One who cannot bring himself to bow at Modim — who cannot acknowledge the goodness bestowed upon him at every breath — is turned into the very creature that was the first to deny it.

The Halachic Posture

The halachah[24] is to bow all the way, “ad she-yispakeku kol chuliyos she-ba-shidrah” (עד שיתפקקו כל חוליות שבשדרה) — until the vertebrae of the spine loosen. One bows at “Baruch” and stands erect at the Name: “Baruch” bowing, “Hashem” (ה') erect, in the opening of Modim and again at the chasimah. The bow is the body’s expression of total acknowledgment: “kol atzmosai tomarnah” (כל עצמותי תאמרנה).[25] Not only the mouth gives thanks, but the body itself in the act of acknowledgment.

L’ma’aseh (למעשה): those who suffer from back trouble must take care, and the poskim (פוסקים) address it. The halachah is lechatchilah (לכתחילה); no one is asked to injure himself. But one who is able should do it the right way every day.

Hearah — The Continuation of the Pasuk

The verse “kol atzmosai tomarnah Hashem mi chamocha” continues: “matzil ani me-chazak mimenu” (מציל עני מחזק ממנו) — Who rescues the poor from one stronger than he. If the bow of Modim is the body’s voice of “kol atzmosai,” it may be that the hoda’ah acknowledged by the bow is specifically the thanks of the weak who were rescued from the strong — a theme that fits the closing of Modim, “mei-olam kivinu lach” (מעולם קוינו לך). (An observation for further study; not developed in the sources cited.)

IX. The Meaning of “Modim”

In plain speech, modim is “thanks.” In the Mishnah (משנה) and the Gemara, however, “modeh” (מודה) means “acknowledges, agrees, admits”: “modim chachamim l’Rebbi” (מודים חכמים לרבי) — the Sages agree.

The two senses fold into one. The essence of todah (תודה) is acknowledgment — the recognition that HaKadosh Baruch Hu gives us life at every moment and protects us at every moment; that recognition obligates thanks. So “modim” says both at once: we acknowledge that You are God and that You sustain us — and therefore we thank You.

X. “L’dor va-Dor” — The Hidden Berachah for Children

The Missing Berachah

Shemoneh Esrei contains no explicit berachah for children. The most important thing in a person’s life, the continuity of his family, the nachas (נחת) of grandchildren in the beis knesses — and there is no formal berachah for it? We add such a request, in our own words, within Shema Koleinu. But why is there no formal berachah?

One candidate presents itself in Birkas Avos, in the words “u’meivi go’el li-vnei v’neihem” (ומביא גואל לבני בניהם), which has been proposed as a reference to the continuing generations. But Birkas Avos is shevach (שבח), praise, and not bakashah (בקשה), request: it is an expression of faith that Hashem will bring redemption to the grandchildren, not a petition on their behalf. It does not answer the question.

One acharon has proposed that the berachah for children is hidden in “l’dor va-dor” here in Modim.[26]

Davening Now for the Children To Come

If one is a bachur (בחור), one should daven now for one’s future children. Why wait? One may daven for a good shidduch (שידוך) and for good children at the same moment.

A personal recollection of the Rav illustrates it. Before returning to America after three years in Eretz Yisrael, knowing it was over, with the shidduchim process ahead, he asked himself how to take leave of Eretz Yisrael. He went to Meron (מירון) and spent the entire night at the grave of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai (רבי שמעון בר יוחאי). He began by davening for an easy shidduch — the “freezer” of the process is unpleasant; he went through it nonetheless, so apparently the kavanah was not enough. But in the course of the night he asked himself: why stop here? Who knows the next time I will be in Meron? He began to daven for good children, and he ended up davening more for good children than for the shidduch itself.

It is permitted to daven for good children before one is married. Every Jew should daven that his children, grandchildren, and great-grandchildren be good Yidden.

The Three-Year-Old’s Berachos

The Rav was preparing a Pirkei Avos (פרקי אבות) class upstairs. The other children were away at camp; only the youngest was home, a three-year-old just past his upsherin (אפשערניש) and beginning to learn berachos. He played with magna-tiles on the floor and began to say — not to eat, only to say — “Baruch Atah Hashem Elokeinu melech ha-olam…” (ברוך אתה ה' אלקינו מלך העולם), running through “shehakol” (שהכל), “mezonos” (מזונות), “ha-adamah” (האדמה) — every berachah, without eating anything.

It pained the Rav to hear the Name spoken in vain, but he chose not to silence the child. Let him practice. It is chinuch (חינוך). When a child practices, it is not a berachah levatalah (ברכה לבטלה); it is the berachah in its highest form. There is no greater fulfillment of a berachah than the practice of a child learning to say it.

Seated upstairs, hearing him, the Rav was struck by the words “l’dor va-dor nodeh lecha” (לדור ודור נודה לך): we are privileged that our children — small children, in this generation, exposed to a world with everything in the palm of the hand — still come to shul, still say a berachah, still recognize HaKadosh Baruch Hu. We should dance.

One should therefore have kavanah at “l’dor va-dor”: although there is no explicit berachah for children, the tefillah is held in those words — the generations continuing; nachas from them; the chain unbroken.

XI. Epilogue

From Tefillas Nachem at Minchah of Tisha B’Av, where the mourning of a year rises in a single chasimah; through Modim, where the body bows to acknowledge what the mind already knows; into “l’dor va-dor,” where every Jew prays for the children he has and the children he has not yet had — tefillah follows a single arc: churban answered by nechamah; kafui tov answered by hakaras ha-tov (הכרת הטוב); a broken generation answered by a faithful one. B’ezras Hashem (בעזרת ה'), may we be privileged to hear besoros tovos (בשורות טובות) very soon.
