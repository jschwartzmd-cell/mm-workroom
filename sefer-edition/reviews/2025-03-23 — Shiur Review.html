23 March 2025 — “הָאֵל הַגָּדוֹל הַגִּבֹּור וְהַנָּורָא,” the Two Bows, and בִּריַכַת גְּבוּרוֹת

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · 7 September 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

A wide-ranging Sunday morning shiur that continues the walk through the first two berachos of Shemoneh Esrei. The spine is the famous Gemara in Yoma ס"ט on Yirmiyahu's dropping of “הַנָּורָא,” Daniel's further dropping of “הַגִּבֹּור,” and the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah's restoration of the full Moshe Rabbeinu formula on the sevara that the very survival of Klal Yisrael through churban and galus is the manifest content of His gevurah and nora'os. From there the Rav layers in Rav Shimon Schwab on “Kel Elyon” as the halachic-hashkafic ground for refusing to explain the Shoah; the two kri'os of Birkas Avos mapped to mesorah versus personal “zeh Keili,” capped by the Chiddushei HaRim's “kol Yehudi hu Magen Avraham”; and, after crossing into Birkas Gevuros, the Gra on why geshamim are called gevuros, the Geshem/Goshem controversy with its extraordinary Yitzchok Satnov back-story, and Rav Aharon Kotler's chiddush on why techiyas ha-meisim is paired with “rav le-hoshia.”

2.What Was Strongest

  • The Yoma sugya as spine. The Rav uses Yirmiyahu / Daniel / Anshei Knesses HaGedolah not as a devar Torah appendix but as the load-bearing wall for what “הַגִּבֹּור וְהַנָּורָא” actually means in davening. The move — that our very survival in exile is not a puzzle to defend against but is itself the substance of the two tearim we say — is the shiur's central hashkafic engine, and it is delivered with real conviction.
  • The two kri'os of Birkas Avos. Rav Schwab's reading — opening bow for “אֱ-לֹהֵי אַבוֹתֵינוּ” as mesorah, closing bow at “מָגֵן אַבְרָהָם” as personal “זֶה אֱ-לִי” — is well anchored in Shemos ט״ו:ב and immediately actionable. Pinning the two modes to the two textual clauses (“zeh Keili” and “E-lokei avi”) gives the whole structure a pshat spine that keeps it out of the “nice vort” category.
  • The Chiddushei HaRim capstone. “Kol Yehudi hu Magen Avraham” is placed exactly where it needs to sit — after the two-bows structure has been built — and the biographical timing (delivered on the Chiddushei HaRim's yahrzeit) is not incidental. It converts the closing kri'ah from a piece of choreography into an existential statement about every davener.
  • The Yitzchok Satnov narrative. This is the shiur's single most memorable stretch. The Rav does not merely note that geshem/goshem is a machlokes; he traces the actual print-history of the shift, ties it to the Mendelssohn printing house, to forged haskamos of the Chasam Sofer and Pnei Yehoshua, and to the first post-Shabsai Tzvi printing of Kisvei HaAri. Whether or not one accepts the halachic nafka mina, the intellectual honesty of surfacing where the nusach shift actually came from is a real service.
  • The Rav Aharon Kotler chiddush on “rav le-hoshia.” The two-dinim framework — that the personal din at petirah cannot yet see the downstream consequences of one's ma'asim, and only the Yom HaDin HaGadol after techiyas ha-meisim works with the full ledger — is the shiur's most theologically satisfying moment. It explains a structural puzzle (why a second din) and it explains a liturgical puzzle (why “rav le-hoshia” is embedded in techiyas ha-meisim) with a single stroke.
  • The Ramban on daily techiyas ha-meisim. The catalogue — somech noflim, rofei cholim, matir asurim, the Shoah survivor rebuilding, the morning “בְיָדְךָ אַפְקִיד רוּחִי” — is well curated. The Ramban's framing of the berachah as a daily hakarah of hashgachah pratis, not only a future emunah, gives the whole Birkas Gevuros section teeth.

3.What Was Weaker or Worth Pushing Back On

The shiur tries to cover too much ground in one sitting. Between Anshei Knesses HaGedolah, Kel Elyon and the Shoah, the two kri'os, Chiddushei HaRim, the Gra, the Satnov episode with its pesakim, the dor ha-mabul, Ramban, and Rav Aharon Kotler — each of these deserves its own hour. The Kel Elyon-and-Shoah section in particular is deeply weighty and gets under three minutes; the Satnov narrative gets ten. The proportionality of coverage does not match the weight of what is being said.

The “survival is the gevurah” reading is presented without the countervailing voice. The Anshei Knesses HaGedolah restored the formula — but Yirmiyahu and Daniel were themselves nevi'im, and their hava amina that during active churban and open hester panim one cannot say these words has real theological standing. The shiur reads their omission as something to be corrected rather than as a permanent voice inside the tradition — a voice that Rav Schwab's own Kel Elyon reading, four paragraphs later, is essentially reactivating. The two sugyos are in productive tension; the shiur only names the resolution.

The Satnov narrative and the pesak narrative do not fully reconcile. The Rav spends real time establishing that Satnov was a meshumad-adjacent zayfen, then reports that Rav Moshe and Rav Elyashiv nonetheless said Goshem, and that Rav Fisher explicitly discarded the kuntres and said Goshem on the merits. The story ends with Rav Shlomo Zalman changing back. But the halachic lesson — if Goshem is grammatically correct and gedolei ha-dor hold that way independent of who first printed it, does the Satnov origin actually matter? — is left implicit. The narrative and the halachah want the same conclusion but the shiur does not force the meeting.

The Rav Aharon Kotler section is the strongest theological stretch and gets the smallest slice of source-time. The chiddush is presented as an insight and a story; the underlying Gemara in Rosh Hashanah ט״ז is barely engaged, and the Ramban's and Rambam's classical accounts of the eschatological din are not brought in as either support or contrast. For a chiddush this large — a second, deferred din whose ledger cannot close until the last downstream talmid's last talmid is born — the audience deserves the primary sources spread on the table.

4.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The “survival is the gevurah” chiddush has a halachic edge that was not developed. If the sevara for restoring “הַגִּבֹּור וְהַנָּורָא” is klal Yisrael's survival, then in principle the kavvanah the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah require in the first berachah should include an active hakarah of collective persistence — not merely the historical fact of Sinai and the Avos. Whether this is a chiyuv mi-de'oraysa flowing from the berachah's own ta'am, or a hashkafic overlay a ba'al nefesh may adopt, is a real question the shiur bypasses.

The Geshem/Goshem question is fundamentally a ta'amei ha-mikra question inside tefillah. Rav Elyashiv's position — that “מְשִׁיב הָרוּח וּמוֹריד הַגָּשֶׁם” is an etnachta before “מְכַלְכֵּל” — is doing serious grammatical work, but Shemoneh Esrei itself has no trop. The question is really: which printed hava amina about the phrase-boundary correctly reflects the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah's original havanah. The shiur brings the grammatical rule and the historical siddur record but does not name that the underlying dispute is about the reconstructive project itself.

The Gra's reading of “geshamim yordim be-gevurah” as hashgachah pratis on every drop is elegant but leaves a lomdish question standing: is the Gemara in Ta'anis saying that geshem is gevurah because it is a species of hashgachah pratis (in which case every act of directed providence would be gevurah), or specifically because life-giving water is the paradigm case of techiyas ha-meisim in the physical world? The Gra's reading and the “ki hu techiyas ha-meisim” reading are not identical — they may even be in tension — and the shiur treats them as complementary.

The two-dinim framework and hilchos teshuvah. If the second din works with a ledger the person could not have seen — downstream consequences, distant talmidim's talmidim — the chiyuv of teshuvah in this world faces a boundary. One can do teshuvah on what one did; one cannot do teshuvah on the gilgul effects one never lived to see. Does “rav le-hoshia”, as Rav Aharon reads it, function as a category of selichah for consequences the actor could not have controlled? The Rambam in hilchos teshuvah would want a word here.

5.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.The Yirmiyahu and Daniel voices are treated as a hava amina the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah overturned. But is there a residue of their position that survives — e.g., that during moments of active hester panim (the Shoah being the paradigm), the bais knesses's recitation of “הַגִּבֹּור וְהַנָּורָא” carries something of their protest even while formally restoring the words?
Q2.On the two kri'os: the closing kri'ah at “magen Avraham” represents the personal mode. But halachically, both kri'os are equally mechuyavos; there is no hava amina that a ba'al mesorah could skip the second bow. Does the Rav Schwab framework mean that both modes are equally chiyuv, or does it introduce a hierarchy in which the inherited mode is prior and the personal mode supervenes on it?
Q3.The Chiddushei HaRim's “kol Yehudi hu Magen Avraham” — is this a chiddush he brings for a particular parashah (e.g., Lech Lecha) or is it a general Kotzk-Ger yesod that shows up in multiple places? A published siman in Sefer Alzachos would strengthen the sefer's citation.
Q4.On Geshem/Goshem: does the Rav himself have a pesak for the tzibbur? The shiur reports the machlokes and the historical narrative without landing. For a sefer intended to guide tefillah, a one-sentence practical hadrachah — even “follow your father” — would be valuable.
Q5.The Rav Aharon Kotler two-dinim chiddush: is the second din a wholly separate proceeding, or is it the completion of the first — the first din's verdict held provisionally in abeyance pending the full ledger? The former is a stronger structural claim; the latter is closer to the Rambam's language in hilchos teshuvah about “shomer le'ish” in gan eden. Which does Rav Aharon mean?
Q6.The Ramban's reading of Birkas Techiyas ha-Meisim as fundamentally about present daily reality is a substantial revision of the Rambam's eschatological framing in the ikkarim. Does this create a machlokes ha-rishonim on what the chiyuv kavvanah in the second berachah is — future-oriented (Rambam) or present-oriented (Ramban)? And does the halachah lema'aseh follow one, both, or an amalgam?

6.Bottom Line

A shiur of very broad scope, held together by a single hashkafic thread: that klal Yisrael's persistence is not a datum despite which we say “הַגִּבֹּור וְהַנָּורָא” but the very content of those tearim. The Yoma sugya, the two kri'os, the Chiddushei HaRim's “kol Yehudi hu Magen Avraham,” and Rav Aharon Kotler's two-dinim chiddush are the shiur's four load-bearing walls, and each is worth returning to. The Satnov narrative is the most memorable stretch but the least halachically consequential; the Rav Aharon section is the reverse. The shiur tries to fit an hour and a half of material into an hour, and some sections — especially Kel Elyon and the Shoah, and the two-dinim framework — will need their sources spread out more fully for the sefer version. But the central hashkafic move — that we are the gevurah, that our persistence is the chasima — is worth the whole hour on its own.
