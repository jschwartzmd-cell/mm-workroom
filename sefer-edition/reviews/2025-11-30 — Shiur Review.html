Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

30 November 2025 — קריאת התורה: Sacred Protocols and the Legacy of the יקעס

Content-based review · reviewer read the Sofer.ai-style transcript (the long file with italic glosses), the Plaud transcript, the AI summary, and the finished write-up (the version before the Sefaria corrections) · 18 October 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

A Sunday-morning shiur in the tefillah series, continuing from the previous week's opening of the aron kodesh and Brich Shmei. The lecturer begins by sponsoring the breakfast and shiur himself, because on Zayin Kislev (Thursday 27 November) he forgot the yahrzeit of his grandfather, a former Satmar student who became a Bar-Ilan professor of Talmud, until a stranger delivering a book from Rabbi Travis reminded him an hour before shkiah; he dedicates the shiur to his grandfather and to his great-grandfather, who was murdered in the Holocaust. He then answers a question raised the week before: why Frankfurt Jews do not say Brich Shmei. The answer is a history of German Jewry and of Ashkenazic suspicion of kabbalah: the Divrei Koheles of Rav Shlomo Zalman Geiger, his brother Abraham Geiger the Reformer, Rav Hirsch and the secession from the Reform community; Shabsai Tzvi and the Amsterdam cherem on the Shelah; the Chasam Sofer, the Maharsha, the Noda BiYehuda against Leshem Yichud, and the Rema's rule that halachah overrides kabbalah; the Baal Shem Tov and the change in our generation. Two further objections to the text follow: bar elahin (with the Yerushalmi on Nebuchadnezzar) and ana avda dekudsha brich hu (with the Chofetz Chaim's private practice and the Yaavetz). The second half goes through the order of the Torah service: Gadlu laShem iti and the six steps of the Ark (Avudraham), escorting the scroll, the Arizal's gazing at the letters, why Ashkenazim lift after the reading, an uncommon honor (handing the mantle), the custom of giving pesichah to an expectant father (Chida; Rav Chaim Palagi), silence during the removal of the scroll, Hakol havu godel, and a proposal, from the Or Zarua/Mordechai, to divide the honors. He closes with a mishnah from the end of Makkos.

2.Overall Fidelity — What the Write-Up Does Well

The write-up follows the lecturer's order closely and keeps most of the argument intact: the structure of the Frankfurt answer, the chain from Shabsai Tzvi to the Chasam Sofer to the Baal Shem Tov, the two "dangerous" phrases of Brich Shmei, the six-steps story, and the Ashkenazic reason for lifting after the reading are all rendered accurately and in good proportion. The historical detail the lecturer develops (the two Geiger brothers, Hirsch and KAJ, the eighty families, the Amsterdam cherem) is preserved rather than compressed. The pattern to note is that nearly every error in the write-up was inherited from the AI summary rather than from the recording: a change of subject from Hakol havu to Barchu, an invented source title, a misunderstood family relationship, and a prayer attributed to a Tanna. These are corrected below. The write-up also loses the opening story's texture (the stranger, the gabbai's lists, the great-grandfather) and several asides that give the shiur its voice.

3.Content Flags — What Is Wrong, Misattributed, Omitted, or Added

A. Misattributions and overclaims

Flag 1 — The grandfather's chavrusa was the Satmar Rav, not the Chazon Ish. The write-up has "When he came to ארץ ישראל he became close to the חזון איש and was a חברותא of his." Neither recording mentions the Chazon Ish. The Sofer-style transcript has: he "learned by the סאטמאר רב … by the holy דברי יואל, he learned with him בחברותא, he prepared a שיעור with him, he's one of the best guys in ישיבה in סאטמאר, in דהיים [back home]. And then he moved to ארץ ישראל"; and later "he always held himself a תלמיד of the סאטמאר רב." The AI summary, which has the Chazon Ish, is the source of the error. Corrected in the HTML. The write-up also omits "as a child" for the Yom HaAtzmaut visit to the shul in Petach Tikva (restored).

Flag 2 — "The phrase is in fact a פסוק in תהלים." The write-up says that אנא עבדא דקודשא בריך הוא "is in fact a פסוק." The lecturer's point is narrower: he recalls אנא עבדך בן אמתך from Hoshana Rabbah and says "that's a פסוק in תהלים; we're quoting דוד המלך." [Sefaria check: Tehillim 116:16, אנה ה' כי אני עבדך אני עבדך בן אמתך; the Aramaic phrase of Brich Shmei is not a verse.] Corrected to a parallel and footnoted. The lecturer also names the rav who changed the text ("Reb Avraham Shor, my rav in Flatbush," heard as "Schorr"); the write-up omits the name (it has "my רב in פלאטבוש").

Flag 3 — A prayer attributed to Rabbi Yochanan ben Zakkai that the recording does not contain. Section XIV has "Say רחמנא ירחם עמא מסכנא — a beautiful תפילה composed by רבי יוחנן בן זכאי for עמא דמסכנא." The Sofer-style text has: "say לך ה' הגדולה, say אב הרחמים הוא ירחם עמוסים. Beautiful תפילות. ויחון אותנו לפליטת עולמים"; the Plaud transcript has "say over rachmanu yirachem amamusim beautiful tfiloh, so yochnei son of light." The AI summary turned אב הרחמים … עמוסים and ויחון into a prayer and its author. Corrected.

Flag 4 — Section XV is about הכל הבו גודל לאלהינו, not ברכו; and the verse is דברים ל"ב:ג', not האזינו. The write-up has the gabbai announcing "ברכו" as a call for silence, and Moshe calling "האזינו — 'listen with me'" before "אז ישיר." The recording has the lecturer address the gabbai: after ותגלה ותראה מלכותו עלינו בזמן קרוב he calls הכל הבו גודל לאלקינו; the ראשונים (מחזור ויטרי, אבודרהם, רי בר יקר, רוקח) read this as the call for silence before the ברכות; and Moshe, "before he said the שירה of האזינו," says כי שם ה' אקרא הבו גודל לאלהינו. ברכו and ברכת התורה are the topic "next week." [Sefaria: Devarim 32:3, "For the name of the Eternal I proclaim; give glory to our God!"; 32:1 is האזינו השמים.] Corrected.

Flag 5 — "Say Yitzchak." The write-up has the sage answering the husband of a woman in difficult labor "Say יצחק," because "יצחק brings forth children easily." The Sofer-style text has "Say יזכור. Why יזכור? Because יזכור, klap kinder aroys [the children should come out]." The AI summary supplied יצחק. The word יזכור is probably the verse ויזכר אלהים את רחל … ויפתח את רחמה [Sefaria: Bereishis 30:22, "God remembered Rachel; God heeded her and opened her womb"], but that is a reviewer's inference. Corrected, with the inference in the note.

Flag 6 — The title מחזה כהונה תמרי תקניין. The write-up bolds this as the Chida's book for the expectant-father custom. It appears only in the AI summary; the Sofer-style text says only "the חיד"א brings it" and "the חיד"א says it's a סגולה that the רחם should open"; the Plaud text says "the chidah." No work of that name is known to this review. Removed; the endnote's guesses (Machzik Berachah, Moreh BaEtzba) are also removed, as the recording does not support them.

Flag 7 — "The שם אריה." The write-up identifies the מהרש"ג as "the שם אריה," and a note says the שם אריה is a different author's work. The Sofer-style text has "רב שמעון גרינפלד, the סמי דעירב" (a garble, probably a place-name for his rabbinate); the Plaud text has nothing close to שם אריה. The identification was added by the AI summary. Removed.

Flag 8 — Zaks family relationships. The write-up has "Hillel Zaks … one zaks is a son-in-law of the Chofetz Chaim, and Mendel Zaks was a ראש ישיבה in YU." The Sofer-style text: "his grandson רב הלל זקס … one of them is [eidim] by the Chofetz Chaim. רב מנדל זקס. Right, רב מנדל was his son-in-law. So his son, רב הלל זקס, was ראש ישיבה in חברון in ארץ ישראל." So Mendel (the son-in-law, at YU) and Hillel (Mendel's son, the Chofetz Chaim's grandson, at Chevron) are separate. Corrected.

Flag 9 — German rabbis named by the summary. The write-up lists "רב יעקב עטלינגער (the ערוך לנר) and רב שלמה זלמן בעמברגער (the ווירצבורגער רב)" as the opponents of Hirsch's course. The recordings name only Geiger and "רב Bamberger" (the Würzburger Rav): "Rav Bamberger and Rav Geiger were very upset." Ettlinger appears in neither recording, and "Shlomo Zalman" is not said of Bamberger (the lecturer gives it only to Geiger). The first name of the Würzburger Rav is left open (the reviewer believes it was Seligmann Baer, but this is not in the recording and was not checked on Sefaria). Corrected to Geiger and Bamberger.

Flag 10 — Details that the recording gives differently. (a) Kollel not kehillah: "our adopted כולל from ארץ ישראל" (the stranger is sent by Rabbi Travis of that כולל). (b) "גואל" for the person who rolls the scroll should be גולל (Sofer-style: "the גולל, the guy that does גלילה"). (c) The Amsterdam חרם covered the Shelah "and all other ספרי קבלה זוהר," not only the Shelah. (d) The חתם סופר's three רבנים are named: the הפלאה, רב נתן אדלר, and רב זלמן חסיד (the write-up has "רבי זלמן, and others"). (e) The wife's "sextuplets, his daughters": see Flag 13. All corrected.

Flag 11 — The Magen Avraham is stated as a fact; the lecturer was unsure. The write-up has "The מגן אברהם explains" the reason for lifting after the reading. The recording: "I don't know if it's the מגן אברהם or one of the other אחרונים." [Sefaria: Shulchan Aruch OC 134:2 and the gloss confirm the Ashkenazic practice; Mishnah Berurah 134:8 confirms it without giving this reason; the reason was not found in the Magen Avraham as retrieved.] Hedged in the HTML. The write-up's "There is a known מעשה of a פסול discovered after וזאת התורה had been said" is also firmer than the recording ("maybe because of a maaseh shehayah…").

Flag 12 — The division of honors is over-extended. The write-up has the Mordechai split the actions as "one person opens the ארון; another removes the ספר; the שליח ציבור carries it," and a note adds replacing and closing as "the basis of selling each item separately." The recording: "A separate person … takes out the ספר תורה, gives it to the שליח ציבור, and the שליח ציבור carries it to the בימה." The attribution is also indistinct: "the אור זרוע writes in the name of the מהר"ח … is brought down in the מהר"ח," then "the מרדכי writes." Corrected to the three-step division and the attribution left open.

Flag 13 — Obed-Edom's household; the Gemara's own words. The lecturer says "all his daughters had sextuplets." The write-up repeats it with "sextuplets every birth." [Sefaria: Berachos 63b, זוֹ חָמוֹת וּשְׁמוֹנֶה כַּלּוֹתֶיהָ שֶׁיָּלְדוּ שִׁשָּׁה שִׁשָּׁה בְּכָרֵס אֶחָד: a mother-in-law and her eight daughters-in-law, each bearing six at a time.] The lecturer's "daughters" is flagged and not silently changed; the write-up's "ס"ד ע"א" is dropped (not found there). Also, "the גמרא discusses different שיטות" about the offerings is Sotah 35b [Sefaria: Rav Pappa bar Shmuel, Rav Chisda]; the lecturer says "סוטה דף ל"ה."

Flag 14 — The cross-reference to Pesachim 118a, and the targum remark. The note to the Yerushalmi adds "the בר אלהים source is also in פסחים קי"ח ע"א." Not in the recording. [Sefaria: Pesachim 118a has Gabriel's offer to cool the furnace but no "בר אלהין"; the Yerushalmi Shabbos 6:9 has "similar to a son of gods," and the angel who "hit that evildoer on his mouth, and told him, correct your words."] Separately, the lecturer's claim that "at the end of Bereishis" the targum renders בני האלהים as דיינא does not match Onkelos at Bereishis 6:2 (בני רברביא); Onkelos renders האלהים as דַיָנַיָא at Shemos 22:8. Both are corrected in the endnote; the lecturer's remark is reported, not silently changed.

Flag 15 — The Maharsha and Lublin. Both recordings have "the מהרש"א, the רמ"א's cousin. We were by the מהרש"א in Lublin last year." The write-up keeps "whose קבר we visited in Lublin" and adds a note praising Lublin. The מהרש"א, as far as the reviewer knows, is buried in Ostroh; the grave in Lublin is that of the מהרש"ל, the better-known critic of kabbalistic customs and a friend of the רמ"א. This may be a slip in the recording (or a misheard letter). Left as reported, with a note; unverified.

Flag 16 — "My father … nine months ago." The write-up has "nine months ago." Both transcripts read "about nine years ago." Since the lecturer says that this is the first year his father is not there to remind him, "months" is the sensible reading, but it is a reading. Left as "nine months," flagged in the Open Questions.

Flag 17 — The dedication. The header in the write-up reads "לעילוי נשמת ר' יעקב אליהו בן ר' אשר אנשיל הי״ד," which a reader may take as saying that the grandfather was killed. The Sofer-style text says: "השם ינקום דמו is on רב אשר אנשל, my great-grandfather who was killed in the Holocaust. I was there by the crematoriums two years ago." The הי״ד belongs to the great-grandfather only; the grandfather (רב יעקב אליהו, born Weiss, later Ephrati) is "זכר צדיק לברכה." Clarified in the Sefer Edition.

B. Omissions

  • The stranger story in full. Rabbi Travis (who, the lecturer says, expects to be excommunicated for his book, which deals with Even HaEzer questions including transgender issues) sent the book with a man flying to America; the man met Rabbi Travis once on a plane ten years earlier; he texted from Flatbush, came to the lecturer's house an hour later, and said, as he left, "You're Avishai Efrati's son? … I daven vasikin with your father every Shabbos [in a shul on the lecturer's block]… Isn't today your grandfather's yahrzeit? I'm the gabbai; gabbaim have yahrzeit lists." The write-up keeps the outline only. The lecturer's remark that he has always been "jealous" of others' hashgachah pratis stories is omitted.
  • The grandfather's work. His books are "not popular because he had a different derech halimud… more into mechkar": he taught gemara with girsaos and nuschaos and "who is the man dehamar," and was "the trailblazer" who gave the high-school (tichon) system a method of studying gemara. The write-up has the general point only.
  • The great-grandfather, Hy"d, "a holy Jew, a chashuva Jew," and the visit "by the crematoriums two years ago" (Flag 17).
  • Why Hirsch's critics were suspicious. Hirsch introduced hakafos with dancing on Simchas Torah; the lecturer says Geiger and Bamberger called it a chutzpah and that "until today certain kehillos" do not do it. The write-up keeps the fact of the innovation but not the reaction or the present-day echo.
  • The Reform "are gone." The lecturer says the Reform are "assimilated," that of the old German community "a lot ended up dead because of the war," and ties it to a warning: "Lawrence is not our Yerushalayim… The ideal Jewish life is in Yerushalayim, ki miTzion teitzei Torah." The write-up keeps only "Berlin became their Jerusalem."
  • Geiger's book and the lecturer's asides. "You should not have the book in your home… probably apikorsus"; the exchange with an audience member about the Geiger family tree; the Hirsch Chumash is kept. None of this belongs in a sefer except possibly a sentence on Geiger's Bible criticism, already kept.
  • The Sanhedrin source for Shabsai Tzvi. "He was megaleh panim baTorah shelo kehalachah, which is one of the worst things Chazal say in Perek Chelek in Sanhedrin: ein lo cheilek le'olam haba." [Sefaria: Sanhedrin 99a.] Restored with a note. The lecturer also forgets Shabsai Tzvi's Muslim name ("it's not important"); omitted.
  • The pinkas hakehillah. A short explanation of the genre (the community notebook of customs and decrees) is reduced to "the communal record."
  • Two Rishonim-level points. (a) Rav Nosson Adler "was a big mekubal" yet never taught it in public. (b) Sephardim say Brich Shmei only on Shabbos, Yom Tov and Rosh Chodesh, "because according to kabbalah you're only supposed to say [it then]"; Ashkenazim say it daily "because the pshat of the Zohar says every day." The write-up has the first as a reason for the nuschaos but not the second.
  • The Chasam Sofer and Mitpachas Sefarim. "The Chasam Sofer was maskim to the sefer Mitpachas Sefarim" (heard; the write-up turns it into "reservations on certain components"). Left as an open question; the reviewer finds the claim surprising, since the Yaavetz died in 1776 and the Chasam Sofer was born in 1762.
  • Ending. The lecturer closes with the mishnah of Rabbi Chananya ben Akashya, "the Holy One wished to make Israel meritorious, therefore He gave them much Torah and many mitzvos, as it is said, Hashem chafetz lema'an tzidko yagdil Torah veyadir" [Sefaria: Makkos 3:16, citing Yeshayahu 42:21]. The write-up's closing סיכום replaces it.
  • Live interludes. The sponsor negotiation, the bagels, the gabbai (Reb Yossi) who needs water, and the questions from the floor ("Was the sefer Torah pasul the week before also?" — "yes, hachi nami") are properly omitted, except that the last is worth keeping as a note.

C. Added or editorial material not in the shiur

The concluding סיכום is composed. "May we all be זוכה to honor the ספר תורה with the כבוד and יראת שמים it deserves — silent at the open ארון…" is the editor's voice; the lecturer closes with "Have a wonderful week" and the mishnah above.

6.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav, and Bottom Line

Q1.Was the father's passing nine months or nine years before the shiur (the first yahrzeit of the grandfather without him)?
Q2.Which teshuvah of the Noda BiYehuda (two are mentioned) and which teshuvah of the Chasam Sofer are meant? Is it the Maharsha or the Maharshal who wrote against learning Zohar, and whose grave was visited in Lublin?
Q3.What did the Chasam Sofer say about Mitpachas Sefarim? Where does the Amsterdam pinkas record the cherem on the Shelah?
Q4.Which acharon gives the reason for lifting after the reading (the Magen Avraham?), and what was the maaseh shehayah?
Q5.In which work does the Chida write about the expectant father and the aron, and what is the word יזכור (a verse?) said for a difficult birth? Which work of Rav Chaim Palagi has the ruling that the same person closes the aron?
Q6.Where does the Maharshag discuss stopping pesukei dezimra, and what is "סמי דעירב"?
Q7.Which passage of the Or Zarua or Mordechai has the dispute over pesichah, and is "מהר"ח" correct?
Q8.The Sephardic and Ashkenazic practice for Brich Shmei (Shabbos only vs. daily): what is the source for the daily recital? Was Hirsch's own community's practice recorded?
