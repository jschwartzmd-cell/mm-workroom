Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

26 January 2025 — The Hidden History of בִּרְכַת אֱמֶת וְיַצִיב

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · 23 August 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

This shiur uses one structural puzzle — why does Birkat Emet V'Yatziv, the longest berachah in the siddur, open without a Baruch? — as the doorway into a remarkable historical excavation. The Rav frames the halachic layer efficiently: the Gemara in Berachos 12a establishes the obligation, the Sefer HaChinuch singles out zechiras yetzias Mitzrayim as the one zechirah requiring verbal expression, and the Yerushalmi lays down five structural components codified through Tur, Rosh, and Mishnah Berurah. The heart of the shiur then pivots to Italy: the Shibolei HaLeket, one of the four aristocratic Roman families exiled by Titus, hosts R' Avraham Abulafia in the summer of 1280 — the same summer of the attempted papal audience at Castel Gandolfo and the sudden death of Nicholas III — and learns Moreh Nevuchim in chavrusa with him and the Riaz. From that encounter, the Shibolei HaLeket transmits Abulafia's kabbalistic gematria: the fifteen vavs plus two heys of the opening chain equal 100, and the entire cascade is nothing less than the preserved letter of acceptance sent by the diaspora communities to Eretz Yisrael agreeing to David HaMelech's takanah of one hundred daily berachos. The structural anomaly is resolved: this is not the opening of a new berachah but the verbatim text of an ancient historical document, embedded in our morning tefillah so that Klal Yisrael renews the acceptance twice daily. The shiur closes by flagging that this is one of four ma'halachim the Rishonim develop, with the remaining three promised for the next installment.

2.What Was Strongest

  • The four Roman families as a scholarly framework. The Rav does not simply drop the Shibolei HaLeket onto the page; he reconstructs the entire Italian aristocratic context — the Anavim, Adumim, Tappuchim, and Bachurim; the shared Synagogue of the Four Heads; the Ravivon lineage of the Aruch; the family connections binding Shibolei HaLeket and Riaz. This structural framing gives the encounter in 1280 real texture, and it retroactively enriches every future encounter with the Aruch, Rabbi Azariah min HaAdumim, and the Roman nusach.
  • The Abulafia biography. This is the shiur's most memorable stretch. The vision at 31, the ten years of preparation, the Mongol / Sambatyon hypothesis, the trek to Akko, the meditation-based kabbalah, the Rashba's cherem, the five centuries of eclipse, and the Chida's brave rehabilitation — each step is a small essay in itself, and together they compose an unforgettable portrait. Few audiences would leave with only a vague sense of Abulafia; here they leave with a working framework.
  • The gematria discovery. Fifteen vavs × 6 = 90, plus two heys = 100. This is the kind of pashut-once-you-see-it observation that makes a person want to count the letters in the siddur themselves the next morning. The Rav lets the numerical elegance speak, then couples it to the Rav Natronai Gaon narrative about David HaMelech's takanah, so the number is not a curiosity but the historical hinge of the whole shiur.
  • The Castel Gandolfo detail. Anchoring the 1280 story in the same papal summer estate still in use today is exactly the kind of touch that makes distant history sit inside the audience's own world. The Rav's aside — “this story I do not know from Google; he writes it himself in Sefer HaEdus” — is also a valuable methodological note about how we know what we know.
  • The opening loose end on the Minchas Elazar. A short, satisfying tie-off of the women / ramach eivarim question from two weeks earlier. It models intellectual accountability — the Rav does not let a sharp question drop — and doubles as a small piece of unusual bekiyus (a question apparently unaddressed for a thousand years).

3.What Was Weaker or Worth Pushing Back On

The central historical claim rests on the Shibolei HaLeket alone, and the shiur does not stress-test it. The narrative — that Emet V'Yatziv's opening is verbatim a diaspora acceptance letter for the hundred-berachos takanah — is beautiful and structurally satisfying, but it is one Rishon's account, presented with kabbalistic-historiographic confidence. The shiur mentions in passing that this is one of four approaches the Rishonim take; it does not indicate whether any other Rishon accepts or contests the letter-of-acceptance framing, or whether other Rishonim connect the chain of adjectives to a different referent entirely. A single sentence locating the Shibolei HaLeket's position on the spectrum would raise the audience's trust in the payoff.

The Abulafia detour, gorgeous as it is, threatens to swallow the shiur. Sections VIII–X occupy nearly half the running time, and the connective tissue back to Emet V'Yatziv is a single sentence: “drawing, presumably, on Abulafia's kabbalah…” The word presumably is doing a lot of work. Does the Shibolei HaLeket himself attribute the gematria to Abulafia? Or is this a modern historical reconstruction that seems fitting given the timing? Either answer is legitimate, but the audience deserves to know which claim they are being asked to accept.

The gimatria is presented as decisive but is not quite airtight. Fifteen vavs to 100 works only if one counts through “v'yafeh” and stops there. But the very next word — hadavar hazeh — is what supplies the two heys, and the passage keeps going: emet Elokei olam malkeinu…, and further vavs follow. The shiur does not address why the counting boundary sits exactly where the numerical elegance requires it to sit. This is not fatal — the Rishon's authority carries it — but a word on how the Shibolei HaLeket justifies the boundary would preempt an obvious question.

The Rashba / Chida rehabilitation arc deserves a caveat. The narrative as presented — Rashba's cherem, five silent centuries, Chida's reopening — is elegant, but scholars of the Rashba's cherem (Idel and others) have argued that it was aimed at Abulafia's specific messianic self-presentation, not at the whole corpus, and that pieces of his teaching circulated continuously in kabbalistic underground channels. The shiur's “vanished for five centuries” framing is dramatic but somewhat overstated. A more calibrated line would only strengthen the underlying point.

4.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The berachah-hasmukhah-l'chaverta dimension is stated but not developed. If Emet V'Yatziv opens without Baruch because it is samukhah to Kriyas Shema, then a familiar sugya lurks: does samukhah l'chaverta apply only to berachos that follow another berachah, or can it extend to a berachah that follows a Torah recitation? The shiur treats this as obvious; the sugya in Berachos 46a about birkas hazan and the analogous discussions in the Rishonim would be worth surfacing. And if the seam Hashem Elokeichem emes makes the two into one continuous declaration, is hefsek between the third parshah and emes more or less severe than an ordinary hefsek mid-berachah? Practical Mishnah Berurah cases turn on exactly this framing.

The five Yerushalmi components raise a bediavad question the shiur does not touch. The Rav cites the Yerushalmi that omission of any of the five means the berachah is lo yotzei. Does this apply only when one entirely omits a component, or also when one recites a modern shortened nusach that alludes to (say) makkas bechoros without an explicit mention? The various nuschaos (Ashkenaz, Sefard, Edot HaMizrach, Italian) differ here, and the Shibolei HaLeket's own community's nusach presumably reflects one resolution.

The verbal-only status of zechiras yetzias Mitzrayim in the Chinuch invites a lomdishe question. If verbal expression is halachically constitutive of this zechirah alone, is a person who thinks the words but does not vocalize them yotzei bediavad? What about someone who says the berachah in a whisper too quiet for their own ears — the classic hishmi'a l'ozno problem, which cuts differently for krias shema (Shulchan Aruch OC 62:3) than it might for Emet V'Yatziv. The shiur asserts the verbal requirement without engaging its edges.

The hundred-berachos framework raises a status question about Emet V'Yatziv specifically. If the berachah is structurally an acceptance of the hundred-berachos takanah, then reciting it functions doubly — as one of the hundred, and as a meta-berachah about the hundred. Does this affect the halacha where one is short of one hundred berachos on a fast day (MB 46 in the various siman discussions)? The theoretical question is inviting.

5.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.What are the other three ma'halachim in the Rishonim on what “v'yatziv v'nachon” refers to? Do any of them offer a competing historical or textual account that contests the Shibolei HaLeket's letter-of-acceptance framing, or are they complementary explanations operating at different registers (peshat, drash, sod)?
Q2.Does the Shibolei HaLeket himself credit R' Avraham Abulafia for the fifteen-vavs-plus-two-heys gematria, or is the attribution a historical inference from the timing of the Rome encounter? If the latter, is there any earlier or contemporaneous source (perhaps in the Ravivon family tradition, or in the Aruch's own kabbalistic material) where the gematria appears independently?
Q3.The shiur mentions in passing that the Roman kehillah preserves a unique nusach traced back to the Beis HaMikdash, and that the poskim rule such a family is to be left undisturbed. Does the Italian nusach of Emet V'Yatziv itself preserve any variant of the fifteen-adjective chain — a different word count, a different order — that would either confirm or complicate the gematria? If our Ashkenaz nusach gives exactly 15+2 but the Italian gives a different figure, the historical picture would need adjustment.
Q4.The Sefer HaChinuch's claim that zechiras yetzias Mitzrayim is the only zechirah requiring verbal expression — is this universal among the Rishonim, or do the Rambam and other Manim-of-mitzvos differ? And is there a corresponding halacha for hilchos Purim (zechiras Amalek), where the practical minhag is verbal even though the Chinuch would classify it as internal?
Q5.Given that the Rashba placed Abulafia in cherem and the Chida rehabilitated him — but the intervening centuries had already absorbed Abulafia's teaching invisibly through the Shibolei HaLeket — how do the poskim conceptualize halacha that traces to a source under cherem? Is there a category of halacha that has, in effect, been laundered through a legitimate carrier, and does the Rashba's cherem retroactively touch such halachos?
Q6.The Abulafia trip records his own account in Sefer HaEdus; do we have any corroboration from the Roman side — ecclesiastical records of Nicholas III's sudden death, or Italian Jewish communal records mentioning Abulafia's Rome visit? The Shibolei HaLeket's ma'aseh in particular would be more historically anchored if a second independent source confirmed the chavrusa.

6.Bottom Line

A tour de force of the Rav's characteristic method: begin with a small textual anomaly, follow it patiently through the Rishonim, and end up standing inside a vast historical landscape one had not realized was there. The Abulafia material alone is a hidden-gem shiur; the Roman-families framework is the kind of context that changes how the audience reads a shelf of familiar Rishonim; and the gematria discovery is genuinely startling. The weaknesses are secondary and structural: the Abulafia biography threatens to dwarf the halachic core, the “presumably” hinge connecting Abulafia to the Shibolei HaLeket's gematria wants firmer sourcing, and the promise of three more ma'halachim leaves the audience knowing that they have heard only a quarter of the map. Still, judged as one installment in a series, this is exactly the shiur the reader will remember years later — the one that answered why the longest berachah in the siddur has no opening Baruch, and did so by taking us to Castel Gandolfo in the summer of 1280.
