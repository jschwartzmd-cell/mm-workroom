Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

28 December 2025 — קדושה דסידרא, ובא לציון, and שיר של יום

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcripts, summary, and finished write-up · 1 November 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

A Sunday-morning shiur in the Seder HaTefillah series (the first after a gap of several weeks), picking up at Kedushah DeSidra. It begins by finishing Lamnatzeach: the nineteenth chapter after the eighteen blessings; the yahrtzeit of Ezra HaSofer (“tomorrow”); the midrash on comforting a woman in labor and the oath of the yoledes; Yaancha on Yom Tov and at a bris; Dovid HaMelech and the sages who came for parnassah (the Pishtu Rebbe and his garlic); Poseiach es Yadecha as request, not praise; and the vessel (keli): the Alshich story, a Lubavitcher story told by a congregant, and the segulah of Hei lachem zera. It then takes up Uva LeTzion: Sotah 49a, Rashi, Rav Natronai and the original learning, the Targum, the individual, Shabbos and Yom Tov; the Sefer HaPardes and the decree against Kedushah (with the Yom Tov Sheni and the Shema of Musaf parallels); the latecomers and the nine o’clock minyan; Baruch Hu Elokeinu. The last quarter opens Shir Shel Yom: Tamid, why only the morning, the Ramban on Zachor, the pagan names of the days, and Wednesday’s Lechu Neranenah. It stops with the promise to continue and the closing mishnah (Makkos 3:16).

2.Overall Fidelity

The finished write-up follows the order of the talk and keeps its best moments: the labor midrash, David and the sages, the guards who leave after Sim Shalom, and the latecomers. Three sources were read: the bilingual Plaud transcript (clearest; it glosses each Hebrew word and is the authority on every disputed point below), the Sofer-style “Speaker 1” transcript (garbled: “Rav Nachmanoy,” “Lusenau,” “kaddish, kaddish, kaddish” for kadosh), and the AI summary. No Sofer .docx exists for this date. As in earlier weeks, the write-up inherited its misstatements mostly from the summary, which departs from both transcripts in several places (a wrong Hebrew year and day, the Zohar, a Beis Yosef for a Gemara, “Roman authorities,” Kaddish and Barchu for Kedushah, a single book title for “the holy books”). The HTML added its own errors in the footnotes (a Rambam halakhah that does not exist, an inflated note on Tisha B’Av, a mis-cited Ramban). All are listed below and were corrected in the final HTML in the same pass except where marked.

3.Content Flags — Misstatements and Overclaims

Flag 1 — The Hebrew date and Ezra’s yahrtzeit. The header and colophon give כ״ח טבת תשפ״ו, and the callout says Ezra’s yahrtzeit is “today.” Computed: 28 December 2025 is ח׳ טבת תשפ״ו (25 Kislev = 15 December; 10 Tevet = Tuesday 30 December; 28 Tevet is 17 January 2026). The Plaud transcript has the Rav catch himself: the message says “today is the yahrtzeit of Ezra … tomorrow? Oh, good he told me” — it is tomorrow, 9 Tevet. The summary also had year תשפ״ה. The HTML even contradicts itself: “this week’s parashah” (Vayigash) cannot fall on 28 Tevet. Corrected.

Flag 2 — The verse in the rebuke is reversed. The write-up and fn 10 quote בערב תאמר מי יתן בקר ובבקר תאמר מי יתן ערב. Deuteronomy 28:67 (and the Plaud transcript) read בבקר תאמר מי יתן ערב ובערב תאמר מי יתן בקר. Corrected.

Flag 3 — The Zohar was never cited. Section XIII says “according to historical sources and the זוהר” and fn 25 elaborates a Zohar mapping of the days to the sefiros. The Plaud transcript says only that the names are עבודה זרה; the Sofer transcript’s “all avoid the Zohar” is a mishearing of that. The Rav said Babylonia and mentioned Constantine. “Not so גלייך” is a garble of “not so gevaldig.” Corrected.

Flag 4 — What was forbidden, and by whom. The write-up says Jews could not say קדושה, קדיש or ברכו, that the disciples went to “the Roman authorities,” and that after the guards left the Jews recited קדיש and קדושה. The Plaud transcript has: could not say קדושה, קדוש קדוש קדוש, ברוך כבוד ה׳ ממקומו; “the authorities back then in Eretz Yisrael” (no “Roman”); and the Jews “said קדושה קדושה קדושה” at ובא לציון. The Sofer transcript’s “kaddish” is its rendering of kadosh (it does the same at the Sotah passage). Barchu appears in neither. Corrected.

Flag 5 — Beitzah, not the Beis Yosef. The Rav asks “what is the לשון of the Gemara in ביצה דף ד׳? מנהג אבותיכם בידיכם, שמא תבוא המלכות.” The Sofer transcript heard “Beit Adav Dalit,” the summary turned it into the Beis Yosef, and the write-up (body and fn 20) gave “בית יוסף, או״ח תצ״ו.” Checked on Sefaria: Beitzah 4b has the instruction to keep the custom of one’s fathers “because at times the monarchy will issue decrees.” Corrected.

Flag 6 — The carriage story. The write-up has the waiting chassid bring an empty carriage to the rebbe. The Plaud transcript is a congregant’s story (“Lubavitcher Rebbe story”): a Lubavitcher chassid without children for years; “somebody else came from France or wherever with a baby carriage … the carriage, there was nothing in the carriage,” and they had the baby that year; the chassid went to the Rebbe. “When you come with the vessel.” The direction of the empty carriage is not fully clear, but the HTML’s version is not what is recorded, and the attribution to a congregant and the word Lubavitch were dropped. Corrected, with the uncertainty stated in the text.

Flag 7 — “Holy books,” and the acronym. The write-up and fn 8 say “ספר המקדושים” and that הא hints at “הכנסת אורחים and (the ק of) א…” — a sentence that does not parse. The Plaud transcript: “it says in the ספרים הקדושים… הא is ראשי תיבות הכנסת אורחים.” The speaker is Yosef (Gen. 47:23, verified). The “should be verified” working note in fn 8 was removed. Corrected.

Flag 8 — Lechu Neranenah is not “at Maariv.” The write-up says “At ערבית we add לכו נרננה on רביעי.” The recording says the verses are added at the end of Wednesday’s song, in answer to why רביעי ends with לכו נרננה, because the psalm ends with ויצמיתם and we do not want to end on a bad word. Corrected.

Flag 9 — יומא דפגרא is not a יום הילולא. Section III says that when Tachanun is omitted “because of a יום הילולא” Yaancha is omitted too. The Rav said יומא דפגרא, it's יום טוב (a day without Tachanun). Corrected. The Rav’s hedge about Sephardim is kept in the Sefer Edition.

Flag 10 — The footnote apparatus. (a) fn 1 gives Berachos 28b and Megillah 17b for the eighteen chapters; the chapters are Berachos 9b (28b gives other reasons; checked). (b) fn 6 adds, for “קרא עלי מועד,” the prohibition of tefillin and of study; not in the recording. (c) fn 7 cites Ramban on Exodus 13:16, which as retrieved treats hidden miracles, not effort and trust. (d) fn 13 gives Rambam Tefillah 9:17, which does not exist (the relevant halakhos are 9:5–6), and ends “Verify exact halakhah.” (e) fn 18 gives Hilchos Melachim 11:14 (the chapter has twelve halakhos in Sefaria’s numbering). (f) fn 16 adds “of Vitry,” “Babylonia,” and that the Rav “flags scholarly debate”; none was said. (g) fn 19 adds Lechem Shamayim; (h) fn 24 adds a Hasagos claim; (i) fn 23 quotes אין אומרים שירה אלא ביום and a Divrei HaYamim derivation (Arachin 11a, as retrieved, has R. Elazar’s derivation from the blood and R. Yonatan on the wine). All corrected or softened.

Flag 11 — Hedging the Rambam, the Tur and the Shulchan Aruch. Section VIII says the Rambam “paskens accordingly” (the Rav: “it seems that Rambam also holds”); fn 14 adds that the Mishnah Berurah “codifies” the individual’s saying it (not said). Checked: Tur 132 cites Rav Tzemach that the individual should not, and then that he may; the Shulchan Aruch’s gloss at 132:1 allows it. Corrected.

4.Content Flags — Omissions

The following were said and left out of the write-up. Item 1 is substantive.

  • The question on the oath. A listener asked when a woman last made such an oath; the Rav answered that it is a recognized question on R. Shimon bar Yochai, that there are other opinions (a korban todah; childbirth impurity), and that it was not the time. Restored in the Sefer Edition.
  • The Gemara’s two answers to the sages (“go and support one another” then “לכו ופשטו בגדוד”) and the Pishtu Rebbe, who gives garlic on the departure of Shabbos (the summary says “on Shabbos”); the Rav’s remark that it was “not politically correct” but understood from the sugya.
  • The Rav’s uncertainty about Sephardic practice on Yaancha at a bris, and the Sephardim saying the Targum quietly (the Arizal not strict).
  • The Rav’s statement that he has no good answer to why Uva LeTzion is said at Mincha on Shabbos, and the deferred question of Motzei Shabbos (with Vihi Noam).
  • The Sefer HaPardes reports the reason “in the name of the ר״ש, in the name of רש״י,” and the Rav’s statement that the decree reason is “totally different” from the one before.
  • The digression on the date: a congregant asked about Nishmas (the legend of its composer, a disciple of Yeshu); the Rav said “it is apropos” since that figure’s yahrtzeit is tomorrow (9 Tevet; corrected to 8), citing a הגה בשולחן ערוך סימן תק״פ and a work he called “Baruch Taam.” The summary and HTML omit this entirely. The transcript is garbled; SA OC 580 does list 8 and 9 Tevet. Restored only as an Open Question.
  • The Rav’s refusal to name or discuss a rabbi in Eretz Yisrael who had spoken that week about Yeshu; it is correctly omitted from the HTML (the Sefer Edition mentions only that he declined).
  • The Shulchan Aruch dispute about praying early on Shabbos (בבוקר בבוקר); Young Israel of Far Rockaway at 8:30; the MTJ / Rav Dovid Feinstein biography point (the summary has it); the Lubavitch 11:00 exchange.
  • Constantine as the one who fixed the order of the week; the Ramban on the months (החודש הזה לכם); the three-year-old’s answer is kept.
  • That the Rav says some Acharonim explain Baruch Hu Elokeinu on the basis of this Rashi.
  • The closing: the Rav ends with Mishnah Makkos 3:16 (רבי חנניה בן עקשיא אומר). The HTML ends with a composed sentence.

5.Added or Editorial Material Not in the Shiur

Most is background and plausible; some is stated as the Rav’s view.

  • fn 2, 12, 15: Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer “used in critical-historical study”; Rav Natronai “ca. 853–858” and the compressed account of the responsum; the Arizal in Shaar HaKavanos — none retrieved; the dates are plausible and the responsum is as the Rav says.
  • fn 3: the HTML says the midrash was “recited to a woman in labor as nechamah — the same Hakadosh Baruch Hu who answered her own mother will answer her now.” Midrash Tehillim 20, as retrieved, has a parable of a mother crying with her laboring daughter (applied to God), but not the Rav’s version. Hedged.
  • fn 5: David then recited Lamnatzeach for parnassah is the Rav’s reading; the Gemara (Berachos 3b, verified) does not say it, and does not name the Philistines. Added to the note.
  • fn 17: the account of Chagigah 14b is accurate and verified.
  • fn 21: Avudraham “brought in the Beis Yosef” is the Rav’s; not retrieved.

6.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

  • Which verse, which direction. Sotah 49a (verified) places the verse in the rebuke in the order of Deuteronomy; the explanation of “prefer yesterday’s known troubles” is right in the prose; only the quotation was reversed.
  • Two Kedushos for the price of one. The Pardes reason makes the Targum a make-up for two missed Kedushos (Yotzer and Chazaras HaShatz); Rashi’s reason makes the Targum for the unlettered. The Rav is explicit that these are two unrelated reasons, a third being the latecomers; the HTML keeps them separate and the Sefer Edition does the same.
  • The individual. The Tur cites Rav Tzemach against and then permits; the Shulchan Aruch’s gloss permits. The Rav’s statement that it is “not actually Kedushah” is the standard reason.
  • The Ramban on Zachor. Verified on Sefaria. The statement that the daily remembrance is not counted among the 613 is the Rav’s and is left unattributed to the Rambam.
  • The historical claim. The decree and the role of the disciples are reported from the Pardes; the HTML’s “the Rav flags scholarly debate” is not in the shiur. The Sefer Edition records it as his report of the Pardes and leaves the book to the Open Questions.

Q2.Which Lubavitcher chassid, which Rebbe, and who carried the empty carriage?
Q3.Where does the Pardes say this in the name of Rashi? Which text, which siman?
Q4.Which Rishonim give the Musaf Shema reason, and where does the Rambam on Yeshu appear (halakhah)?
Q5.What was the digression on the date: whose yahrtzeit, and what is “Baruch Taam”?
Q6.Which Ramban speaks of gevohei madreigos and trust without effort?
Q7.Is Rabbi Yisrael Belsky the Rav’s grandfather or his wife’s grandfather?

8.Bottom Line

A faithful, well-shaped write-up of a discursive shiur whose two best ideas (the comfort of the laboring woman and the decree that explains the Targum) are present and accurate. The errors came, as before, mostly through the summary (the Hebrew date, the Zohar, Beis Yosef for Beitzah, Kaddish and Barchu for Kedushah, “Roman”), and, from the editor, a reversed verse, a non-existent halakhah and several footnotes that say more than the Rav did. Fix those, restore the question on the oath, the Lubavitcher attribution and the closing mishnah, hedge the unsourced footnotes, and the sefer edition can stand. The material that is the Rav’s own and not the sources’ (David reciting Lamnatzeach; the decree) should be shown as his report until located.
