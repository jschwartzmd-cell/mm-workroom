22 June 2025 — הָשִׁיבָה שוֹפְטֵינוּ and בִּרְכַּת הַמִּינִים

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

Two consecutive brachos of Shemoneh Esrei inside the bakashos for the future ge'ulah. The first half of the shiur centers on Hashivah Shofteinu: its structural place after Teka b'Shofar; the Mahari Beirav's sixteenth-century attempt in Tzefas to renew semichah and reconstitute the Sanhedrin; Rav Saadia Gaon's observation that this is the only bracha in Shemoneh Esrei whose chasimah contains “melech”; and the Tur's kushya on that uniqueness, answered in the name of the Tur's older brother Rabbeinu Yechiel from the pasuk in Mishlei, “melech b'mishpat ya'amid aretz” — only a melech delivers true mishpat. From there: the Rama/Aruch dispute on b'dieved in Aseres Yemei Teshuvah; the Rambam's chiddush that Mashiach ben David cannot arrive until the Sanhedrin is reconstituted, with Eliyahu as the vehicle through Achiyah HaShiloni; and Rav Schwab on “v'haser mimenu yagon va'anachah” as the tefillah for the end of agudos agudos, extended into his skeptical treatment of the brachos on chachmei umos ha-olam, melachim, and even gedolei ha-dor. The shiur then pivots to the second bracha, Birkas HaMinim: the Rambam's framing of it as a nineteenth addition that leaves the name Shemoneh Esrei intact as a tefillah for its own disappearance; the Gemara in Berachos and Megillah on Shimon HaPakuli and Rabban Gamliel in Yavneh; the historical context of the Judeo-Christian minim; the mechanism by which the bracha flushed them from the beis knesses; the censored history of the nusachos; and Shmuel HaKatan's uniqueness as the author, closed by the two readings — the standard warning and Rav Kook's inversion — of his single mishnah-contribution, “bi-nfol oyivcha al tismach.”

2.What Was Strongest

  • The Rabbeinu Yechiel answer on “melech” in the chasimah. This is the shiur's cleanest yesod. The Tur's kushya is sharp — brachah ha-semuchah la-chaverta should obviate the need for “melech” here as it does everywhere else — and the answer is both structural and reyach ha-pasuk: mishpat is specifically the midah that only a melech can deliver, and the chasimah is calibrated to that yesod. The biographical note on Rabbeinu Yechiel, built out of the Chida's Birkei Yosef on the Rosh standing fully for his son, is the kind of maggid shiur move that converts an obscure citation into a human picture. It also lets the Rebbe land a mussar point on kibud av taught through ma'aseh, not drashos, with his own household as the live example.
  • The halachic economy around “melech”. Tying the Rama/Aruch machlokes on b'dieved in Aseres Yemei Teshuvah back to the Rabbeinu Yechiel yesod is tight: the Rama can afford not to repeat because the bracha already carries “melech”; the Aruch's stricter view testifies to an older nusach that did not. One yesod, three halachic outputs — the uniqueness, the b'dieved, and the testimony of the Aruch's girsa — is the economy that a maggid shiur aims for.
  • The Rambam's Sanhedrin-before-Mashiach move anchored in Achiyah HaShiloni. The sequence — Rambam's Perush HaMishnayos on Perek Chelek for the pesak, and his Hakdamah for the mesorah chain through Achiyah to Eliyahu — converts “v'ashivah shofetayich” from a wish into a halachic mechanism. The reader walks away with a concrete answer to “how does Eliyahu actually give semichah?” and with the Mahari Beirav episode now reread as an attempt within the same mechanism.
  • The Rav Schwab stretch on agudos agudos. The move that “v'haser mimenu yagon va'anachah” is not a loose addendum but the operational chasimah of the bracha — the yagon of fragmentation that only a reconstituted Sanhedrin can lift — is a real pshat that organizes the bracha. The extension to the brachos on chachamim and melachim (Einstein as the solitary chochmas umos who still stands; the Satmar picture with the Queen; the safek on whether any contemporary gadol ha-dor warrants the bracha) is a sharp — even startling — application of the same point: the entire sedarim of authority in our generation are impoverished, and the bracha is a tefillah for all three to be restored at once.
  • The historical reconstruction around Birkas HaMinim. The shiur does the real work: the pre-tekanah chaos of nusachos described by the Rambam; Shimon HaPakuli's unification before Rabban Gamliel; the crisis of indistinguishability in the beis knesses; the operational mechanism of the bracha as a filter run by the shaliach tzibbur; the censorship history of “minim” to “malshinim” and the scatter of competing nusachos (malchus ha-risha'ah, kol ha-resha'im, malchus zadon) as external pressure, not internal variation. This is the shiur's most historically rich stretch.
  • The Rav Kook inversion on Shmuel HaKatan. The two pshatim are both strong, and juxtaposing them lets the shiur close on the right note: the bracha directed most sharply against enemies could only be composed by the man whose single mishnah warns against simchah at their fall. The Rav Kook move — that Rabban Gamliel chose him because of that middah, not in spite of it — is the shiur's quiet chiddush and lands cleanly.

3.What Was Weaker or Worth Pushing Back On

The shiur runs out of runway on Birkas HaMinim. The Rebbe himself flags it in the room — “we have a couple of minutes… this is a long bracha… three more minutes.” The result is that the nusach history, the Shmuel HaKatan material, and both pshatim on “bi-nfol oyivcha” are compressed into the closing minutes. The Rav Kook inversion in particular is asserted rather than developed. For the sefer this is the obvious seam to open up: either split the two brachos across two chapters, or let the shiur that follows carry the full Birkas HaMinim treatment and let this one close cleanly on Rav Schwab.

The Rav Schwab extension to the brachah on gedolei ha-dor is sharper in the room than in the write-up. In the transcript the Rebbe pushes the point hard — Rav Schwab earned “license to talk like this” from his Shabbos with the Chofetz Chaim in 1930; after the Chofetz Chaim there has not been another leader on whose stature Klal Yisrael uniformly agrees; the chaburah pushes back with Rebbe Kanievsky and Rabbi Feiner, and the Rebbe answers that “everyone has their rebbe,” which is precisely the point — it has to be someone accepted by the entire Klal Yisrael. The HTML keeps the structural claim but drops the Chofetz Chaim frame and the back-and-forth, and so softens what in the room is one of the shiur's most pointed observations.

The two pshatim on “bi-nfol oyivcha” are presented as alternatives when they are better read as complementary. Pshat alef reads the pasuk as a self-warning that Shmuel HaKatan attaches to the bracha; Rav Kook's pshat reads the same pasuk as the credential that qualified him to compose it. These are the same midah looked at from opposite ends of the tefillah — before and after. The shiur presents them as a machlokes; the sefer could note that the two pshatim together form a single arc, which would also sharpen the chiddush.

The Mahari Beirav episode is used only as atmosphere. The Mahari Beirav's project is cited to make the point that semichah cannot be reconstituted b'zman ha-zeh, but the halachic basis for the attempt — the Rambam's ruling in Hilchos Sanhedrin that unanimous consent of the chachmei Eretz Yisrael can reconstitute the chain — and the Ralbach's halachic opposition are not developed. Given that the whole Rambam-Eliyahu mechanism later in the shiur traffics in exactly the same question, folding the Mahari Beirav's halachic argument into the main line would turn a sippur into a sugya.

The “Trump / Chuck Schumer / World War III” aside dates fast and does halachic damage. The excursus is used as a case study for the bracha on a nasi, but it drifts into contemporary politics (“was Trump authorized to bring the world into World War Three”) that will read as period-piece color within two years. The halachic question — whether the bracha on melachim applies to a modern constitutional president, and whether “she-nasan” vs. “she-chalak” is the dividing line — is worth preserving; the geopolitical framing is not. The HTML wisely trims most of this, which is the right call.

4.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The Rabbeinu Yechiel yesod raises a question the shiur does not quite close. If “melech” is in the chasimah because mishpat structurally requires malchus, then one would expect “melech” to surface in the chasimah of “ha-el ha-kadosh” during Aseres Yemei Teshuvah for the same reason — and in fact we do change there to “ha-melech ha-kadosh,” and chozer if we forgot. So the yesod is sound, but the asymmetry is interesting: year-round, mishpat gets “melech” and kedushah does not; in Aseres Yemei Teshuvah, both do. The Rabbeinu Yechiel yesod is actually load-bearing year-round for one bracha and seasonally for a second, and the two together probably sharpen the pshat rather than complicate it. Worth naming.

The Rambam's “Mashiach requires Sanhedrin” move is not universally accepted. The Raavad and others read the sequence of ge'ulah differently, and the Mahari Beirav's own project rested on the inference that Sanhedrin must come first, which others precisely contested. The shiur presents the Rambam's pesak as straightforward; it is in fact one pole of a live machlokes, and the Mahari Beirav episode is comprehensible only when the machlokes is on the table.

The Rav Schwab extension to chachmei umos ha-olam is halachically strained. The halachic status of the bracha on seeing a chacham from the umos does not depend on whether the chacham's theories will be falsified by the next generation; it depends on contemporary recognition of chochmah. Otherwise no chacham would ever warrant the bracha, since every generation's wisdom is in principle subject to revision. Rav Schwab's point is really a hashkafic observation about the arc of chochmas ha-teva dressed as a halachic safek. The Einstein exception, charming as it is, reinforces this: the bracha's halachic criterion cannot be “the theory will still hold a century later.”

The operational mechanism of Birkas HaMinim deserves a halachic frame the shiur does not supply. The Gemara's picture — the min skips, the shaliach tzibbur notes it, the man is expelled — is halachic machinery, not pastoral practice. There are real questions here (what is the chezkas status of someone who stumbles over the bracha? one skip or three? what if he says it inaudibly?), and the Rishonim treat them. The shiur presents the mechanism as self-evident; the sefer could gain by naming the halachic structure — essentially a bedikah bedarkei amitah for minus — that makes the mechanism legitimate rather than merely clever.

5.Fidelity to the Shiur — Flags for the Editor

Major omission: the entire meraglim / chagavim / nemalim mehalech is missing from the write-up. In the room, immediately after Rav Schwab's agudos agudos point and before the pivot to Birkas HaMinim, the Rebbe runs an extended derech agav that is one of the most substantive stretches in the shiur. The thread (from the transcript, 00:22:27–00:27:19):

  • “Va-nehi b'eineinu ka-chagavim” — the meraglim saw themselves as grasshoppers, and were therefore seen as grasshoppers; the famous Kotzker — “if you look at yourself like a chagav, you are a chagav.”
  • The pasuk says “chagavim”; Rashi renders “nemalim.” The source is the Gemara in Sotah: the anakim in the vineyards saw the meraglim as insects, and the Aramaic term “kamsi” is ambiguous between ants and grasshoppers.
  • Why does Rashi prefer nemalim? The Rebbe cites a gilyon he saw this past week (Sholal Alter's gilyonos, distributed in the shul in Flatbush) that two pesukim in Mishlei link ants and locusts — both species that lack a melech.
  • The Gemara in Shabbos: R' Shimon ben Chalafta's famous experiment covering an ant nest to simulate shade, watching one ant exit, report back, and then be killed when the shade is removed — proof that ants have no king (since otherwise they would not have executed one of their own without royal sanction). The Gemara ultimately defers to Shlomo HaMelech's testimony: ants have no katzin, shoter u-moshel.
  • The mehalech's punchline: both chagavim and nemalim share the chisaron of leaderlessness. The chet ha-meraglim, on this reading of Rashi, was precisely the rejection of Moshe Rabbeinu's leadership — which is why the ma'apilim immediately followed, going up without Moshe. “When you lack leadership you descend into the pits” — which is exactly the Rav Schwab point, now carried in through the week's parshah.

This whole stretch anchors Hashivah Shofteinu in parshas ha-shavua, carries a vintage Kotzker, a Gemara Shabbos sippur, and the Rebbe's own synthesis — and reinforces the yesod of the bracha. Its complete absence from the HTML is the single most important thing to fix for the sefer. It should be restored as a “Derech Agav” subsection between the Rav Schwab section and Birkas HaMinim.

Minor softening: the “yasmi d'yasmi” framing. In the room the Rebbe calls ours “a yasmi d'yasmi generation — an orphan generation”, said twice and with real feeling, in direct connection to the Rav Schwab point. The HTML has “diminished structure of authority,” which is accurate but drains the charifus. Restore the Rebbe's own phrase.

Minor omission: the Chofetz Chaim frame around Rav Schwab's “license”. The Rebbe grounds the sharpness of Rav Schwab's comments on contemporary gedolim in his famous Shabbos with the Chofetz Chaim in 1930 (“he didn't stop talking about it for the rest of his life”). This gives the halachic observation its biographical weight. The HTML drops it.

Factual flag on footnote 2 (Rav Saadia Gaon). The footnote confidently places the observation in Siddur Rav Saadia Gaon. The transcript simply attributes the observation to Rav Saadia Gaon without a precise citation; whether the uniqueness-observation is in the Siddur itself, in a later commentary on the Siddur, or elsewhere in the Rasag corpus, deserves a verifier's check before print.

Factual flag on footnote 1 (Mahari Beirav's musmachim). The footnote lists four musmachim: Beis Yosef, Mabit, R' Yosef Sagis, and Ramak (Rabbi Moshe Cordovero). The transcript names only the Beis Yosef and the Mabit explicitly and says “perhaps three or four other people.” The specific identification of R' Yosef Sagis and the Ramak is historically defensible but is editorial addition rather than from the shiur; a verifier should confirm (the standard historical lists vary — some include Ramak, some do not).

Transcription correction worth noting. In the raw transcript, Rabbeinu Yechiel's name is garbled as “Rebbe Hillel” and the Rosh's other son “Yehudah” is said to have written “Zichron Yehudah.” The HTML correctly identifies Rabbeinu Yechiel as the oldest son (the one quoted by the Tur) and lists R' Yehudah separately. This is a correct editorial repair of a transcription error, not an addition.

6.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.On Rabbeinu Yechiel's yesod: why does “melech” appear in the chasimah of Hashivah Shofteinu but not in the chasimah of “ha-el ha-kadosh” year-round — given that kedushah no less than mishpat is a middah uniquely associated with malchus? Is the yesod specifically that mishpat is operational-of-malchus in a way kedushah is not?
Q2.On the Rambam's Sanhedrin-before-Mashiach: does the Rav read this as a halachic pesak or as a structural sevara? And how does the Rav treat the Raavad's pushback and the alternative mehalech that Mashiach himself can reconstitute semichah?
Q3.On Rav Schwab's treatment of the bracha on chachmei umos: is the halachic criterion really “will the theory survive the next generation,” or is it “does Klal Yisrael presently recognize this person as a chacham”? If the latter, the Einstein-vs-Hawking contrast is a hashkafic remark rather than a halachic one.
Q4.On the meraglim stretch: is the Kotzker on “ka-chagavim b'eineinu” compatible with the Rashi-nemalim reading, or do the two readings cut against each other — the first about self-perception, the second about objective leaderlessness?
Q5.On Birkas HaMinim's operational mechanism: what is the halachic status of someone who skips the bracha in our day for reasons unrelated to minus (distraction, forgetfulness, unfamiliar nusach)? Is the Gemara's rule a chezkas minus or a bedikah standard that requires further investigation before action?
Q6.On Rav Kook's inversion: if Shmuel HaKatan was chosen because his middah of “al tismach” qualified him to author the bracha, does this generalize — is there a halachic or hashkafic principle that tefillos against enemies must be authored or led by those who are internally free of sinah?

7.Bottom Line

A tight, well-architected shiur on Hashivah Shofteinu — Rabbeinu Yechiel's yesod, the Rambam-Eliyahu mechanism, and Rav Schwab's agudos agudos reading of “v'haser mimenu yagon va'anachah” all land cleanly — followed by a compressed, under-time pass at Birkas HaMinim that nonetheless lands the historical reconstruction, the operational mechanism of the bracha, and the Shmuel HaKatan/Rav Kook inversion as its closing zinger. The HTML write-up is faithful to the halachic armature but drops one substantial mehalech — the entire meraglim / chagavim / nemalim / R' Shimon ben Chalafta stretch — that in the room served as the parshah-anchored support for Rav Schwab's point. For the sefer: restore that section as a “Derech Agav” subsection, verify the Rav Saadia Gaon and Mahari Beirav footnote attributions, restore the Rebbe's “yasmi d'yasmi” language and the Chofetz Chaim frame around Rav Schwab's license, and consider splitting the two brachos across chapters so that Birkas HaMinim is not strangled by the clock.
