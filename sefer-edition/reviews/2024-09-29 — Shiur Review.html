Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

29 September 2024 — סְלִיחוֹת — Chesed v'Emes Before Yom HaDin

Content-based review · reviewer read the finished write-up · drashah delivered opening night of Slichos, during the Lebanon call-up

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

Delivered at 1:46 AM Motzaei Shabbos as the first Slichos of 5785 began — and as reservists' phones were lighting up with call-ups for the Lebanon ground operation — this drashah weaves five sources into a single argument about how to enter Yom HaDin. It opens with the Brisker Rav's mashal of the smuggler, the wagon-driver, and the horse, calibrating three levels of preparation. It grounds the Yud-Gimmel Middos in the Gemara in Rosh HaShanah (nisatef HaKadosh Baruch Hu k'shliach tzibbur) and immediately turns the knife: if reciting them worked automatically, why hasn't the geulah come? The answer — ya'asu lefanai ka'seder ha'zeh, walk the path, don't just say it — is joined to Tanna D'Bei Eliyahu's demand for agudah achas. The heart of the shiur is the pasuk chesed v'emes yikadmu fanecha: emes = Torah, chesed = tzedakah, and via the Leket Yosher's bold reading of the Rambam's malveh/loveh venue rule, tzedakah compels Hakadosh Baruch Hu to move from Kisei HaDin to Kisei HaRachamim. The Rav closes with Rachmana d'anei le'aniyei read through kafin v'ani v'lo yada — we don't even know how poor we are — and a raw invocation of the moment: last year we didn't know what Simchas Torah would bring; this year we do.

2.What Was Strongest

  • The Brisker Rav's mashal, told with restraint. The three-tier structure — smuggler, balagulah, horse — lands harder than any abstract taxonomy of hachanah would. Especially effective is refusing to soften the third tier: to enter Yom HaDin like the horse is not a lesser preparation, it is a category error. The mashal earns its place because it names something the audience already half-knows about their own Elul.
  • The pivot on the Gemara in Rosh HaShanah. The move from nisatef HaKadosh Baruch Hu k'shliach tzibbur straight to "so why hasn't the geulah come" is the single sharpest turn in the drashah. It refuses the pious fiction that saying the middos is enough, and it does so from inside the Gemara's own language (ya'asu, not yomru). That is the correct place to press, and the Rav presses it.
  • The Leket Yosher chiddush. Genuinely striking. Taking a technical Rambam in Hilchos Sanhedrin about venue and transposing it into the din Torah we have with Hakadosh Baruch Hu gives the audience something halachically anchored to carry into Slichos — not a slogan but a koach. The Rav is properly self-conscious about how daring the formulation is, and that self-consciousness is itself part of what makes it credible.
  • The kafin v'ani v'lo yada reading of Rachmana d'anei. This resolves an obvious mispallel's difficulty (we're not actually poor or broken-hearted) with a category that stays with the listener: we are so impoverished we no longer register it. The tefillah works because Hakadosh Baruch Hu sees what we cannot.
  • The historical anchoring. The line that a phone lit up mid-drashah with news of the ground operation — and the reminder that last Erev Simchas Torah we would have davened differently had we known — is the sort of moment that fuses a shiur to a date. It is also disciplined: the Rav does not exploit the war, he uses it once, at the end, as a reason to take the four remaining days seriously.

3.What Was Weaker or Worth Pushing Back On

The Chasam Sofer's gematria is load-bearing and hedged. The write-up itself flags that chesed + emes equals Slichos "with one extra letter, but the connection holds." That is a soft foundation for a section heading. Either the gematria works (in which case give the count) or it doesn't (in which case the point stands on the pasuk alone and doesn't need the gematria at all). Using it as scaffolding for the central section undersells the pasuk's own force.

The Leket Yosher chiddush needs a small halachic guardrail. The malveh's power to compel venue in the Rambam is not unrestricted — there are conditions (relative size of the community's beis din, whether the malveh is himself in the wrong, etc.). Transposed to our din Torah with Hakadosh Baruch Hu, the parallel raises an obvious question: does the tzedakah-malveh retain standing if he is himself the defendant on other counts? The drashah's rhetorical power would not have suffered from one sentence acknowledging that the koach is real but not unconditional.

The Tanna D'Bei Eliyahu section moves fast. "Agudah achas" is translated as unity and quickly cashed out as more tzedakah — but the source is doing something more specific: it is answering David HaMelech's question about a post-Mikdash age, and the agudah achas is offered as a substitute for the avodah. That substitution logic (unity replaces the korban tzibbur) is the strong claim; collapsing it into "be nicer to each other" leaves the source under-mined.

The horse tier in the mashal invites a follow-up the shiur doesn't take. If the horse is pulled across the border regardless, the mashal implies that the unaware Jew is still judged — awareness or not. Is that so? The Rambam in Hilchos Teshuvah and the whole tinok she'nishba tradition suggest awareness meaningfully changes the standing of the din. The mashal is powerful; it may also be overstated.

4.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The shliach tzibbur's tallis at night. The drashah cites the Gemara in Rosh HaShanah as the source but doesn't engage the halachic anomaly: night is normally lav zman tzitzis, and even a chazan is patur. The minhag to wear the tallis anyway is precisely because he is enacting the image of Hakadosh Baruch Hu as shatz — which raises the classical question of whether he makes a brachah, and the standard answer that he borrows a tallis or wears one without a brachah. Worth a sentence.

The Rambam's malveh/loveh venue rule. The citation to Hilchos Sanhedrin is right, but the underlying machlokes Rishonim on zabla and the borrower's right to demand a local beis din under certain conditions is what makes the Leket Yosher's move interesting halachically. The chiddush is not merely "malveh picks the venue" — it is that even against the borrower's strongest procedural objection, the malveh prevails. That is the halachic force being transposed.

The status of ya'asu ka'seder ha'zeh. The drashah reads ya'asu as behavioral (walk the middos), but there is a long parallel line — the Rosh and others — reading ya'asu as strictly liturgical (say them b'tzibbur, with the proper seder, including the introductory pesukim). The Rav's reading is the deeper one, but the liturgical reading has real weight and explains, e.g., why a yachid skips certain phrases. Flagging the two readings would strengthen the deeper claim by choice, not by default.

Tzedakah as the exception to sechar mitzvah b'hai alma leikah. The Rav notes tzedakah as an exception but doesn't distinguish the two Talmudic frameworks: the Gemara in Kiddushin 39b on peiros vs. keren, and the specific mechanism of malveh Hashem chonein dal as a loan (repayable in olam ha'zeh) as opposed to reward for mitzvah (deferred). These are two different halachic-hashkafic engines producing the same result, and the Alter Rebbe's letter is specifically leaning on the loan model — which is what makes the Leket Yosher's malveh-status argument coherent rather than merely poetic.

5.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.The Leket Yosher's chiddush effectively grants the tzedakah-giver standing to compel Hakadosh Baruch Hu to move from Kisei HaDin to Kisei HaRachamim. Is this a purely rhetorical kviyochol, or does the Terumas HaDeshen (or the Leket Yosher himself) draw practical halachic-hashkafic consequences from it — e.g., a specific formulation to have in mind when giving tzedakah on Erev Rosh HaShanah?
Q2.The Gemara's ya'asu ka'seder ha'zeh is read as demanding behavior, not recitation. Does that mean a tzibbur that recites the Yud-Gimmel Middos with kavvanah but without corresponding middos-work accomplishes nothing, or does the recitation retain some koach as a down-payment or a peticha? The Ari and the Nefesh HaChaim seem to differ on this — where does the Rav land?
Q3.The Tanna D'Bei Eliyahu presents agudah achas as the post-Mikdash substitute for avodah. Is the mechanism zechus (unity as a merit) or chalos (the united tzibbur itself becomes the vessel that avodah used to be)? The difference matters for what counts as "enough" unity to trigger the response.
Q4.The Brisker Rav's mashal casts the unaware Jew as the horse — pulled across the border regardless. In light of the Rambam's treatment of the shogeg and the tinok she'nishba, does the mashal mean to teach that awareness doesn't change the din, only the ability to be moche the din? Or is the horse imagery specifically about the Jew who could have prepared and didn't?
Q5.The drashah closes by tying the Lebanon call-up to the imperative of these four days. Is there a specific tefillah, kabbalah, or seder Slichos-emphasis the Rav recommends for a year like this one — something distinct from an ordinary Elul? The write-up gestures at "differently" but doesn't specify.
Q6.The Chasam Sofer's chesed+emes=Slichos gematria is invoked with a hedge. Does the Rav know the precise formulation the Chasam Sofer used — whether the extra letter is treated by the kollel rule or by some other standard gematria convention — or is this best treated as a homiletic association rather than an exact numerical claim?

6.Bottom Line

A tightly-built drashah that earns its emotional weight. The architecture is unusually clean for a Motzaei Shabbos short: mashal for calibration, Gemara for the mechanism, Tanna D'Bei Eliyahu for the communal dimension, pasuk for the practical structure, Leket Yosher for the koach, closing pasuk for the emotional shift — each source doing distinct work. Its central strength is the refusal to let the Yud-Gimmel Middos function as a password; its central risk is the reverse over-reach in the Leket Yosher section, which is stated more absolutely than the underlying Rambam quite supports. That's a good problem to have — a shiur that says too much rather than too little, given the moment. Anyone reading it years later will know why the first night of Slichos 5785 felt different in the room.
