Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

15 June 2025 — תְּקַע בְּשׁוֹפָר: In a Time of War

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · 5 October 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

An abbreviated, visibly shaken shiur delivered two days into the war with Iran — the opening twenty minutes consumed by the matzav itself: a photograph of an empty Bnei Brak, the three children killed in Bat Yam, a chayal killed in Azah, the Gra"ch Kanievsky's “kol til yesh lo ktoves” paired with the chiyuv of hishtadlus, several Lawrence shuls beginning Avinu Malkeinu that morning, and a direct instruction to Michael's son at the Kotel to go into the miklat when the azakah sounds. The Rebbe explicitly moves everyone into a register of seriousness: “a little less smiles, a little less jokes — they are laughing less when they run to the miklatim.” A long call-to-action for Rabbi Kozak's Ger-based organization providing PTSD and mental-health therapy for chayalim returning from Azah and for families outside Bituach Leumi's coverage, with the breakfast about to start downstairs — which forces the shiur itself to be cut short. The learning piece opens with the Gemara in Megillah yud-zayin on the seder of Shemoneh Esrei: Barech Aleinu closes the personal bakashos, Teka B'Shofar opens the communal, and the internal logic is refuah → parnassah → kibbutz nidchei amo Yisrael as the ultimate menuchas hanefesh. The midrash that the malachei ha-shareis first uttered “Baruch mekabetz nidchei amo Yisrael” at the reunion of Yaakov and the shevatim in Mitzrayim, after Yosef's galus and Shimon's imprisonment, grounds the bracha's historical origin. Then the pshat-question of the shiur: is the shofar gadol physical or metaphor? The Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer position: two horns of the eil ha-akeidah, the left blown at Mattan Torah, the right reserved for Mashiach — Eliyahu HaNavi to blow it. The Tur's connection to the chatzotzros on korbanos in Parshas Beha'aloscha. Against this, Rav Shimshon Schwab's Iyun Tefillah (gifted to the Rebbe by Rabbi Yossi Popack, via the Yisroel Schwab bar mitzvah connection): the shofar gadol is mashal — the moment when world leaders themselves declare that all power is bechasdei Hashem, that the Presidency, the Chancellorship, every throne, is malchus Hakadosh Baruch Hu. Rav Schwab's broader shitah — opposed to the beeper-era, bumper-sticker “Moshiach is here” campaign as a zilzul of a musag we barely understand — is framed with explicit respect for the Lubavitcher Rebbe's countervailing shitah, the Rebbe (“I am a Lubavitcher chassid by lineage”) acknowledging “both have al mi lismoch.” Two Brisker stories anchor Rav Schwab's position: young Reb Chaim stopped at the Lechovitcher shtibl wanting to listen to Hallel on Pesach night and the Beis Halevi's rebuke (“if it is not the proper zman halachtis, what is beautiful about it?”), and the chazzan who asked Reb Chaim for a brachah to sing in Mashiach's choir and Reb Chaim's trembling refusal (“even the greatest tzaddikim will be hiding their faces in the nekikei ha-sela'im”, per Yeshayah). Closing: contemporary glimpses — President Trump's “if not for the grace of God”, the Argentinian president Milei crying at the Kotel days before the war erupted — as seeds of the universal hakarah that will become the shofar gadol. Time ran out mid-arc; the Rebbe deferred the bracha of Yerushalayim to next week.

2.What Was Strongest

  • The opening twenty minutes as a mussar event rather than a preface. The Rebbe did not treat the war as framing; he treated it as the primary content of the opening third. The sequence — empty Bnei Brak, three children in Bat Yam, the chayal in Azah whom “we forget there is still a war ongoing in Azah”, Michael's son at the Kotel told by name to go to the miklat, the explicit instruction to tell fewer jokes — builds a register of seriousness that gives the entire Teka B'Shofar excursus its weight. The writeup preserves this architecture correctly as Section I (“The Hour”).
  • The seder-logic from Megillah yud-zayin. Refuah → parnassah → kibbutz nidchei as an ascending ladder of menuchas hanefesh, with kibbutz galuyos as the ultimate menuchah, is a crisp framing that reorients Teka B'Shofar away from an eschatological petition and toward a bakashah for peace-as-reunion. It is the structural yesod of the whole shiur.
  • The Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer two-horns midrash as continuity. The akeidah-ram as the source of both the Mattan Torah shofar and the Mashiach shofar is one of those rare midrashim that stitches three distant points in Jewish history into a single physical object. The Rebbe lands it with real excitement (“it's amazing how everything is interconnected”), and the writeup captures that in Section IV.
  • Rav Schwab's metaphor — the United Nations as shofar gadol. The image is bold and memorable: not an eschatological trumpet but a world-wide hakarah moment where every head of state publicly acknowledges “ani b'punished”, “I am here only through the grace of God.” The move reframes “ve-sheh-zocheh li-shmoa kol shofaro” as a consciousness event rather than an acoustic one, and it does so without displacing the physical reading — the Rebbe presents both b'kavod.
  • The deliberate b'kavod framing of the Lubavitch disagreement. The Rebbe's self-identification — “I am also a Lubavitcher chassid by lineage” — before presenting Rav Schwab's critique of the beeper-era campaigns is the shiur's most delicate pedagogical moment. He gives the Lubavitcher Rebbe's shitah its full positive statement (“to eliminate the exile mentality and bring the consciousness of Mashiach to the forefront”) before Rav Schwab's objection lands. The writeup's callout preserves this with the right balance.
  • The two Brisker stories as a matched pair. The Pesach-night Hallel and the chazzan's requested brachah point to the same core yesod: beauty and kavvanah are real but subordinate to halachic structure and to the overwhelming yiras shamayim that Mashiach represents. Positioning them together in Section VI is the writeup's cleanest structural move.
  • The Rabbi Kozak appeal as substance, not interruption. The PTSD material — chayalim given one therapy session and sent home, flying to Thailand to escape what they saw, families outside Bituach Leumi's coverage — is itself mussar: it operationalizes the “we need to feel their tzaar” of the opening. The writeup's Section I subsection on it is the right length.

3.What the Writeup Omitted or Softened

The Hezbollah pager operation aside is a serious loss. At the exact moment the Rebbe sets up Rav Schwab's beeper-era critique, he pivots to the just-completed Hezbollah pager operation and the Iran drone-headquarters strike: “I am more nispal from this drone headquarters in Iran... it is all Baruch Hu, not kochi v'otzem yadi.” This is not a digression — it is the shiur's live demonstration of the Rav Schwab point: a world-stage event whose only honest reading is Hashem's hand, happening right now while the Rebbe speaks. The writeup cuts it entirely. For a sefer-edition, even one sentence restored would make the Rav Schwab metaphor land not as abstract theology but as live hashgachah-reading of this exact week.

The Milei vignette is cut and should be restored. The Rebbe closes the shofar gadol arc with the Argentinian President Milei story: “Rabbi Anki is mentioning this Argentinian president who left Israel at the last minute, not knowing what was about to hit Israel — he went to the Kotel and the guy was crying... he is a Lubavitcher's best buddy... when a goy is such, it gets me a little nervous, but good things in mitzvah Hashem are going to come out of this guy.” This is a concrete, dated, named world leader doing exactly what Rav Schwab describes — and it had happened days before the shiur. The writeup's “Glimpses” paragraph collapses it into a bland “leaders sometimes acknowledge divine providence”, losing both the name and the specificity of the Kotel-tears moment.

Rav Schwab's yahrzeit timing is lost. The Rebbe explicitly notes: “Rav Schwab was niftar nineteen ninety-five — thirty years ago, exactly.” The 30-year yahrzeit frame is the reason this particular sefer came to the Rebbe's hand through Rabbi Yossi Popack right now, and it gives the whole Iyun Tefillah section its devotional weight. Easy restoration — one line in Section V would do it.

The dedication to the chayal killed today in Azah is softened in the writeup. The transcript has: “we dedicate whatever divrei Torah we are learning today... especially to the chayal who was killed today in Azah — we forget there is a whole war going on in Azah.” The writeup preserves the dedication but moves it toward the ambient “welfare of acheinu Bnei Yisrael” framing; the explicit “this specific chayal, this morning, Azah” hits harder in the live telling.

The “Rabbi Anki” credit for the Milei story is cut. Minor, but for the sefer's pattern of in-room attributions (“someone sent me”, “Michael's son”, “Rabbi Yossi Popack”) a brief “Rabbi Anki mentioned” would preserve the shiur's room-aware texture.

The “spirit of God at the beginning of Bereishis” framing of Mashiach's sanctity is thinned. Rav Schwab's claim that Mashiach is “one of the most sacred ideas in Judaism, like the ruach Elokim at the beginning of Bereishis” is the theological load-bearing claim beneath the critique of bumper stickers. Without it the critique reads as merely stylistic. The writeup trims it; one sentence of restoration would reground the critique.

The “serenity now” New York joke is correctly cut. The Seinfeld-adjacent aside about New Yorkers' prayer for serenity (“give me serenity now”) is a nice live beat but out of register for the sefer.

The tie-borrowing interlude is correctly cut. The entire “I forgot my tie, Vava has a Fav” business belongs out of the sefer.

The Reb Chaim/Zambul Kvartin dating aside is correctly cut. The audience member looking up that Zambul Kvartin was born in 1874 to check whether Reb Chaim Brisker could have heard him personally is a wonderful live moment but trims cleanly.

Bibi Netanyahu-not-following-home-front-command joke is correctly cut. Belongs out.

4.Where the Writeup Added or Framed Beyond the Shiur

The Rav Schwab attribution is corrected — and this is important. The summary document twice mis-attributes Iyun Tefillah to Rav Shimshon Raphael Hirsch (both in the body under the Rav Schwab heading and in footnote 7). This is the same transcription-era conflation we flagged in the 20 July 2025 review on the Shema Koleinu shiur, where the summary also mis-named Rav Schwab as Hirsch. The transcript is unambiguous: “Sefer Iyun Tefillah from Reb Shimon Schwab”, “Rav Schwab was niftar nineteen ninety-five”. The writeup correctly identifies the author throughout, and footnote 7 adds biographical context (Khal Adath Jeshurun, Washington Heights, talmid of the Chazon Ish and Reb Baruch Ber). Flag for the editorial pass: the summary pipeline is systematically mis-naming Schwab as Hirsch; this should be fixed at the pipeline level before more shiurim inherit the error.

The “Rabbi Kozak from Gur” cleanup is correct. The transcript has “Rabbi Kozak from Gary, he's a Gerer Chassid” — “Gary” is clearly a transcription garble of “Gur”, and the writeup resolves it correctly.

The “Rabbi Masri” identification is editorial reconstruction. The transcript says “Rabbi from Shari" — the writeup identifies the speaker as Rabbi Masri, which is almost certainly right from community context but is an inferred correction. Reasonable; worth a note in the editorial log.

The “Young Israel of Lawrence and Cedars” cleanup is correct. The transcript's “young Israel Lawrence and Cedars” is tidied; no content added.

The lead-paragraph framing (“the hinge of Shemoneh Esrei”) foregrounds a structural claim. In the shiur the hinge-point emerges organically from the Gemara Megillah discussion; the writeup signals it in the lead. This is appropriate for a sefer-reader and does not distort.

The Section VI framing — both Brisker stories as a “matched pair” pointing to “the same Brisker frame” — is editorial synthesis. The Rebbe tells them sequentially without the explicit pairing-move. The writeup's synthesis (“spiritual experience is real, but always subordinate to the structures Hakadosh Baruch Hu built”) is a fair reading but is the editor's voice, not the Rebbe's. Fine as a sefer-ism; worth flagging as such internally.

The “Rav Schwab's broader caution against trivializing Mashiach and geulah through bumper-sticker culture” in footnote 7 is editorial compression. Accurate but slightly sharper in judgment than the Rebbe's own hedging in the room.

Footnotes 1, 2, 3, 7, 8, 9, 12 add biographical and textual context not in the shiur. All are reasonable editorial enrichments. Footnote 8's framing of Yeshayahu 2:19–21 as grounding both Rav Schwab's and Reb Chaim's reading is a nice interpretive bridge.

5.Nuances and Chiddushim Worth Pulling Into the Sefer Edition

  • The Hezbollah pager and Iran drone-headquarters as live illustrations of Rav Schwab's thesis. This is the single most important restoration. The Rebbe himself used these very events to say “Baruch Hu, not kochi v'otzem yadi” — which is the exact substance of Rav Schwab's shofar gadol. Pulling even one sentence in at the hinge between Sections VII and VIII would make the metaphor land as current hashgachah-reading rather than abstract theology.
  • The Milei-at-the-Kotel vignette. A named, dated, specific head of state crying at the Kotel days before the war — and “he is a Lubavitcher's best buddy” — is a vivid closing beat that would replace the writeup's generic “leaders sometimes acknowledge divine providence”. The Rebbe's own ambivalence (“when a goy is such, it gets me a little nervous”) is itself a chiddush worth preserving.
  • Rav Schwab's yahrzeit at thirty years. One line in Section V (“niftar in 1995, exactly thirty years ago”) grounds the whole Iyun Tefillah encounter in right now.
  • The “ultimate menuchas hanefesh = kibbutz nidchei” frame. The writeup has this but could foreground it as the shiur's yesod: Teka B'Shofar is not primarily about Mashiach, it is about peace-as-reunion. In a time of war the bakashah for ingathering reads as a bakashah for the end of running to miklatim.
  • The “ruach Elokim at the beginning of Bereishis” as the measure of Mashiach's sanctity. Rav Schwab's load-bearing theological claim deserves to be stated in his terms, not merely paraphrased as “far more profound.”
  • The Rambam's rule the Rebbe invokes in passing. “We could talk a lot about what's going to happen, when it's going to happen, how it's going to happen, but we don't know until it will happen” — a Rambam-grounded humility clause that gives the whole physical-vs-metaphor dispute its appropriate frame. Worth a footnote citing Hilchos Melachim 12:2.
  • The Eliyahu-blows-it midrash. Preserved in the writeup but worth a footnote identifying the mekor (Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer parallel passages, Yalkut Shimoni).
  • Michael's son at the Kotel told by name to go to the miklat. The writeup preserves this. For the sefer, consider italicizing the direct address: it is one of the shiur's most human lines and a lived instance of pikuach nefesh docheh ha-kol.

6.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The Avinu Malkeinu question is left open in the shiur and would benefit from editorial resolution. The Rebbe says “I don't know all the halachos of Avinu Malkeinu — we need to check”, and reports that two Lawrence shuls began saying it that morning. The halachic question is real: Avinu Malkeinu outside the Aseres Yemei Teshuvah and formal ta'anis tzibbur is governed by Mishnah Berurah 584 and Shaarei Teshuvah, with a minhag permitting its addition during communal eis tzarah. A footnote identifying the mekoros would answer what the Rebbe left pending.

The Megillah yud-zayin seder argument has an alternative reading in the Avudraham. The Rebbe presents refuah → parnassah → kibbutz nidchei as an ascending ladder of menuchas hanefesh. The Avudraham and Beis Yosef on Shemoneh Esrei have alternative sedarim keyed to the geulah-process rather than the menuchas hanefesh-process. Worth a footnote noting the alternative.

The Tur's linkage of Teka B'Shofar to chatzotzros. The Rebbe mentions it quickly. The Tur in Orach Chaim 115 actually develops the point more fully: the chatzotzros-shofar parallel runs through the Rambam's Hilchos Taaniyos 1:1, where chatzotzros are the sound-signature of communal tzarah. This strengthens the “teka b'shofar as tefillah of klal” framing.

Rav Schwab's metaphor and Rambam Hilchos Melachim 12:1–2. The Rambam's own position — that “ein bein olam ha-zeh li-yemos ha-mashiach ela shibud malchiyos bilvad” and that we should not “yesapek b'devarim ha-aggadiyos” about the particulars — is the halachic ground for Rav Schwab's metaphorical reading. The Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer position is the aggadic counter-pole. A footnote identifying the Rambam-Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer axis would frame the dispute as a classic one rather than a Rav Schwab chiddush.

The chazzan's request is doing double work the shiur does not fully draw out. The chazzan asked for a brachah to sing in Mashiach's choir — which is both a middas chassidus (asking for ruchnius rather than gashmius) and a gaavah (presuming one's own fitness). Reb Chaim's refusal lands on the second axis, but the first axis is also real and worth noting: not every bakashah for a spiritual gift is “clean”; the yiras shamayim test cuts across the ruchnius-gashmius distinction.

The Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer two-horns midrash has a kashya. If the left horn was blown at Mattan Torah, what has become of it? The midrash as the Rebbe cites it leaves this open. Some mekoros have the left horn consumed in the sound itself; others preserve it in the Beis Hamikdash. Worth a footnote.

7.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.On the “ultimate menuchas hanefesh” frame: does kibbutz nidchei as menuchah survive even if the geulah itself is delayed indefinitely — i.e., is the bakashah primarily for reunion (which could happen in partial, present-tense ways through aliyah, through klal-wide events like this war) or specifically for the eschatological ingathering?
Q2.On Rav Schwab's metaphor: is the “universal recognition” reading exclusive of the physical shofar, or consistent with it — i.e., will the shofar blow and the leaders proclaim, or does the proclamation itself count as the shofar sound?
Q3.On the Hezbollah pager and Iran drone-headquarters operations: does the Rebbe hold that these are already partial shofar-events in Rav Schwab's sense — or merely hashgachah-moments that point toward the shofar without being it?
Q4.On the Milei vignette: the Rebbe said “when a goy is such, it gets me a little nervous.” What is the nervous-register pointing to — a concern about oso ha-ish-style theological confusion, a concern about Esav sonei l'Yaakov being bent in an unnatural direction, or something more structural about goyish warmth toward Yidden?
Q5.On Avinu Malkeinu outside Aseres Yemei Teshuvah: in a communal eis tzarah like milchemes mitzvah for Eretz Yisrael, is the chiyuv on the individual minyan to institute it, on the rav, on the mo'etzes? And is there a limit — i.e., when does a “temporary addition” become a b'kviyus issue of the sort the Aruch HaShulchan warns about in Shema Koleinu?
Q6.On the chazzan story: did Reb Chaim suggest an alternative brachah after refusing the Mashiach-choir one? The transcript ends on “that brachah I will not give”; the audience asked “did he ask for something else?” and the Rebbe said “I don't know the end of the story.” Is there a mesorah about the resolution?
Q7.On the Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer midrash: given the Rambam's general “do not get particular about aggadic details of Mashiach” stance in Hilchos Melachim 12, how do we weight a midrash this specific (identifying the physical origin of a future Mashiach-era shofar) against the Rambam's own metaphorical temperament?

8.Bottom Line

A shiur whose register is as important as its content: two days into the war with Iran, the Rebbe opens with twenty minutes of mussar on the matzav — Michael's son instructed by name to go to the miklat, a dedication to the chayal killed that morning in Azah, an explicit instruction to tell fewer jokes — before turning to the bracha itself. The learning piece pivots on Rav Schwab's metaphor: the shofar gadol as the moment when the world's leaders themselves declare “everything is malchus Hakadosh Baruch Hu.” The Rebbe grounds the metaphor in two contemporary illustrations the writeup regrettably dropped: the Hezbollah pager and Iran drone-headquarters operations as “Baruch Hu, not kochi v'otzem yadi”, and Milei crying at the Kotel days before the war. The writeup is structurally faithful, correctly resolves two summary-document garbles (“Gary” → Gur; the systemic Hirsch/Schwab mis-attribution), and handles the Lubavitch-Schwab disagreement with real kavod. The sefer-edition pass should restore the pager and Milei live-events, note Rav Schwab's thirty-year yahrzeit as the reason the sefer arrived at this moment, cite the Rambam-Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer axis as the structural dispute underneath the physical-vs-metaphor framing, and resolve the Avinu Malkeinu halachic question the Rebbe deliberately left open. The bracha of Yerushalayim deferred to next week should be flagged for continuity in the following shiur.
