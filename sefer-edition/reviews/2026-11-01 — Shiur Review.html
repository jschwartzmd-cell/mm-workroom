Content-based review · checked against the PRIMARY Sofer.ai transcript (no timestamps, so passages are cited by quoted phrase, shown as [“…”]) · the Plaud transcript (this time with no usable speaker labels: every line is “Speaker 1”) and summary were used only as secondary checks · reviewer also read the final Sefaria-corrected English write-up and the Sefaria log entry for this date · 1 November 2026 · 21 Cheshvan 5787 · reviewed on the final (Sefaria-corrected) draft; the shiur HTML was not edited, so every correction below is a recommendation

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

A Sunday-morning shiur on the Shabbos morning service, opened with a dedication (breakfast sponsored by a friend of the shul for his mother’s yahrzeit, מרים בת חיים יצחק). The Rav finishes Shacharis with the שיר של יום and asks why Ashkenazim say it after Musaf when the Levites sang it with the morning תמיד: the Magen Avraham finds the Ashkenazic custom illogical, the Aruch HaShulchan asks why the Musaf song is not said, the Magen Avraham explains why there is no song after Mincha (the libation could fall after שקיעה), and Nitra and the Young Israel of Woodmere (Rabbi Billet, for decorum) moved it earlier. He turns to taking out the Torah: the Simchas Torah sale of הוצאת ספר תורה (Or Zarua), giving פתיחה to a man whose wife is in her ninth month (Chida, מנהג ותיקין) and to a חתן; Masechet Soferim as the ancient minhagim of Eretz Yisrael, with its very long series of verses; the Or Zarua’s dream (the verse אור זרוע לצדיק and עקיבא / עקיבה in a גט), the Rhineland custom of reciting nothing, and the Shibbolei HaLeket’s family, the four families exiled to Rome, and Rav Avraham the doctor who revived the Soferim verses. אתה הראת (Tur 281, a Spanish custom) and ויהי בנסע הארן follow. Then the מי שברך after each aliyah (“for money”; בעבור שיתן מתנה; the warning on unpaid vows; the Beis Yosef’s ברכה versus תפילה for the sick). The longest part is the haftarah, the “very radical machloket”: Rashi in Sefer HaPardes (a chapter of Prophets every day, lapsed because רבתה העניות; later haftaros for the day, the chasan, the Rambam, Rav Sherira) against the Avudraham (a Greek decree; twenty-one verses), with Elijah Levita’s Sefer HaTishbi (Antiochus) and the Levush (no Kesuvim), an excursus on teaching Torah to non-Jews, and three questions from the room (the triennial cycle and Benjamin of Tudela; why seven aliyos; מחר חודש).

2.Overall Fidelity — What the Write-Up Does Well

  • The Sofer.ai text is followed over the Plaud wherever they differ. Plaud’s “nighter,” “Udmur,” “Leo Bacher,” “the Lubavitcher” (for the Levush), “Rabbi Yonba Chai,” “Arba Chayim,” “Rabbi Zviel David” and the summary’s “Rav Drum” are all correctly not carried over; the HTML has Nitra, Woodmere, Eliyahu Bachur, the Levush, the Avudraham and (in the Plaud summary’s place) the Sofer’s אור זרוע. The Plaud summary’s “[Insert Name]” header and its “assignments” (a decorum committee, a study sheet, consulting Rabbi Langer) are correctly not carried over.
  • The architecture is faithful and proportioned. The eight sections follow the live order closely: dedication and the שיר של יום; the ark; Soferim, the Or Zarua and the Roman families; אתה הראת; the מי שברך; Rashi; the Avudraham, Levita and the Levush; the room’s questions. The one reordering is VIII, where three questions that were put in the middle of the Avudraham passage are collected at the end (see Section 3, Flag 5); the move is sensible but not disclosed.
  • The Rav’s hedges mostly survive. “I don’t know if they read two chapters a day,” “I suspect that Rashi is quoting a מנהג that was before the Gemara,” “I don’t have a good answer; the Magen Avraham doesn’t like it,” “קען זיין” on the chasan, and “maybe bad things happened as a result of it, it’s very hard to tell” on Levita are all kept. The Rav’s own errors (the Rambam’s yahrzeit “somewhere in Shevat,” בבל corrected to Rome for the exile, “daf צ”) are reported without being put right in his mouth, and corrected in the notes.
  • Tangents that would embarrass the Rav are left out. The Cuomo remark about who may attend a shiur, the greeting of Rav Shaul HaKohen, the roll-call for the sponsor’s mother’s name, the sponsor’s name, and the Hebrew letters of Rabbi Billet’s name are all properly omitted or kept in a note.
  • The corrections to the Rav’s citations are reported without being put in his mouth. Shabbos 32b (not Nedarim), Menachos 99b (not daf צ), the Tur at 281 (confirmed from the Plaud summary), the Rambam’s 20 Tevet, and the Rema passage that could not be found are all in the notes; the Sefaria log (fn1 through fn25) matches the HTML note for note.
  • Sofer-over-Plaud choices checked and confirmed. Every Sofer reading used in the HTML is the better one where the two disagree (see Section 4).
  • The header date is right. 1 November 2026 is 21 Cheshvan 5787 (14 Cheshvan was 25 October).

3.Content Flags — What Is Wrong, Mis-Attributed, Omitted, or Added in the Final Draft

Everything below was tested against the Sofer text. The Plaud transcript for this recording labels every line “Speaker 1,” so it cannot settle who is speaking; attributions here rest on the Sofer wording and context. None of these has been applied; they are the recommended corrections of Section 5.

Flag 1 — The logic of the inference about “before the Gemara” is broken by the list (VI). The HTML gives Rashi’s five-part list as מקרא, נביא, משנה, ברייתא וגמרא and then says the Rav “suspects Rashi is quoting a custom from before the Gemara was even written, which would explain why he lists מקרא, משנה, ברייתא, תלמוד together.” In the Sofer the inference is: [“I suspect that רש״י is quoting a מנהג that was before the גמרא was written בכלל, so he spoke about משנה, מקרא, נביאים וכתובים, משנה and ברייתא”]. The Rav’s second list has no Gemara, which is exactly why he says the custom predates it; the first list ([“מקרא ... נביא ... משנה ... ברייתא, גמרא”]) is ambiguous (Plaud: “Brisa Gemara”), and his walk-through in fact fulfils only mikra, mishnah, beraisa and navi. The HTML reads a Gemara into the inference that the Rav has just removed. Fix: give the list as the Rav does, note that the first mention of “ברייתא גמרא” is unclear, and make the inference “Rashi’s list has no Gemara, so the custom may be older than the Gemara.”

Flag 2 — The non-Jews exchange is softened and partly mis-assigned (VII). The HTML: “Another congregant raises the Rambam’s ולא שבט לוי בלבד אלא כל איש ואיש, wondering whether that includes non-Jews; the Rav answers that it does not and cites סנהדרין דף נ״ט.” In the Sofer: [“Yeah, it could be it's including גוים also, the רמב״ם says... That's what I heard once. Wow! Don't say that ever publicly again.”], then [“What's with the גמרא in סנהדרין דף נ״ט, you're not allowed to teach גוים תורה? Wow, פלא עצום. Okay, makes sense.”]. The Rav’s reaction to the Rambam point is alarm (“don’t say that ever publicly again”), not a reasoned answer, and the Sanhedrin 59a objection is put as a question in the room that the Rav accepts; he does not cite it himself. The next line, [“These people became even bigger Christians”], is the Rav’s own (the Plaud agrees). Fix: either give the reaction faithfully (“the Rav: ‘Wow! Don’t say that ever publicly again.’”) or, better for the book, drop the Rambam exchange and keep only the Igros Moshe and Sanhedrin 59a, in the Rav’s voice.

Flag 3 — The Sefardic-shul question is the Rav’s, not a participant’s (VI). The HTML (Haftaros that match the day): “A participant asks what would happen in a big Sefardic shul with a חתן every week.” The Sofer: [“You do it? So you don't read the הפטרה of that week? What if there's a חתן every single week in שול, in a big ספרדישע שול? They don't לייען the הפטרה? You hear this?”]. The speaker is the Rav, addressing the room (and apparently a Sefardi member, since he later says “we don’t even do what רב דן says they do by the ספרדים”). Only the “חיי שרה, the פרשה of שידוכים” is a participant’s joke (the Rav: “don’t be a wise guy!... very good”). Fix: give the first question to the Rav and say that Reb Dan informed him that the Sefardim do it.

Flag 4 — The girl’s blessing is a voice from the room (II). The HTML puts in the Rav’s practice: “...and if it’s a girl, that she should marry a תלמיד חכם.” The Sofer: [“or if it's a girl, it's going to be a girl that should, you know, whatever. I don't know what you say by a girl. Okay. Yeah, to marry a תלמיד חכם. Okay.”]. The Rav said he does not know what to say for a girl; someone supplied the words and he agreed. Fix: “...for a girl, the Rav is not sure what to say; a participant suggests that she marry a תלמיד חכם.”

Flag 5 — Three interruptions have been moved out of the middle of the Avudraham (VII, VIII) and one point is lost. In the Sofer the order is: the Avudraham’s decree and the twenty-one verses; a voice from the room ([“Yes, we spoke about it yesterday, שלש סעודות”]); [“there's different מנהגים exactly where to end... but you have to have at least 21 פסוקים, מה שאין כן on יום טוב, when the ליינינג is shorter, when there's less עולים, you have to have less פסוקים”]; Reb Berel’s triennial question; Benjamin of Tudela; the seven-aliyos question; מחר חודש; and then, [“Oh, we'll get to that in a moment... I want to say the other opinion. The other opinion was that it was a decree... according to the אבודרהם, there was never a ענין to ליין הפטרה every day like רש״י”]. The HTML collects the questions into VIII, which reads as a clean list, but it (a) does not say it has moved them, and (b) drops the Yom Tov point (fewer aliyos, fewer verses), which is the Rav’s own confirmation of the 3 × 7 logic and answers the Editorial Review’s question about why the number of aliyos fixes the verse count. The Plaud summary has the Yom Tov point. Fix: restore it in VII, and keep the VIII reordering with a one-line note. If the sentence about “yesterday at Shalosh Seudos, the haftarah of Parshas...” is ever restored, the correct parsha is וירא (Plaud “Vayero”); the Sofer’s וארא is a mis-hearing.

Flag 6 — מחר חודש: the HTML moves “not like our minhag” to the wrong clause (VIII). The HTML: “According to many מנהגים, you read מחר חודש only when Shabbos is ערב ראש חודש (Sunday being ראש חודש), and when Shabbos is itself ראש חודש, ‘not like our מנהג,’ we read the haftarah of ראש חודש.” The Sofer: [“according to a lot of מנהגים, you ליין מחר חודש only when it's שבת ראש חודש and Sunday is ראש חודש, not like our מנהג. Our מנהג is we ליין when שבת ראש חודש, we ליין the הפטרה of ראש חודש, not מחר חודש.”]. The Rav’s first sentence is itself garbled (“Shabbos Rosh Chodesh and Sunday Rosh Chodesh” is probably “Shabbos erev Rosh Chodesh”), and the “not like our minhag” follows it. The HTML has tidied the Rav’s sentence into something he did not say. Fix: report the garble (“the recording is unclear here”) or say only what is clear: our practice reads the Rosh Chodesh haftarah when Shabbos is Rosh Chodesh, and the Magen Avraham raises the מחר חודש question.

Flag 7 — The Musaf-song answer is guessed (I). The HTML: “Someone offers an answer, and the Rav points out that Masechet Soferim... spells out the songs for every Musaf... ‘Every time there’s a Musaf, they say, now we can learn Torah’ is how the Rav (somewhat tongue in cheek) summarizes the point.” The Sofer: [“The ערוך השלחן has no answer. Anybody has an answer? Yes, we do! Very good question, נתן. If you look in מסכת סופרים at the back... every time there's a מוסף, they say, 'Oh, now, now we can learn תורה.' יישר כח. As our מנהג is, we take a 60-second pause”]. Nobody offers an answer in the text; the Rav himself says “Yes, we do,” complimenting Nasan’s question, and points to Soferim. The fragment “now we can learn Torah” is unintelligible (it may be the Rav’s joke about the break). The HTML invents a summary that the Rav did not give. Fix: “the Rav answers that Soferim, at the back of Avodah Zarah, spells out the songs of every Musaf (Rosh Chodesh, Shabbos, Yom Tov, Shavuos, Sukkos, Pesach)”; drop the “now we can learn Torah” gloss or flag it as unclear. Note 3 already reports that the chapter in Soferim was not retrieved.

Tension A — Who said “Europe added יודים” (III). The HTML says “in Europe, a participant notes, they added יודים, אלפים, עינים and ואוים.” The Sofer: [“Sometimes, sometimes it could be both. I don't remember what... Correct, correct, like in Europe, in Europe they added יודs and אלפs and עיןs and ואוs, yeah. You should know it's not within the scope of the שיעור”]. The words after “Correct, correct” are the Rav’s, continuing from a participant’s remark about names in gittin (the “קכ״ט” of note 9); the participant’s contribution is the part the recording does not carry. Recommend: “the Rav agrees with a remark from the room, and adds that in Europe they added יודים...”.

Tension B — “This morning” and “just now” (I, VI). The Woodmere story is introduced with “I just learned this now” after someone beside him told him (the Plaud has a name, garbled): the HTML says the Rav “shares something he ‘just learned this morning’.” The Rambam / ark haftarah is the one he says he found this morning ([“I only found it out this morning”]). Minor; correct the first to “just now.”

Tension C — Antiochus was supplied (VII). The Sofer has the Rav begin [“אליהו בחור says that in the times of נבוכדנצר, I think... no, no, yeah, יון, מלכות יון, אנטיוכוס, thank you”]; the Plaud has the same. The Rav does not remember who the king was and someone supplies the name. The HTML states “מלכות יון, in the days of אנטיוכוס” without qualification; since this is the answer to the section’s question, it should read “as the Rav relays Eliyahu Bachur.” The Rav’s own wording (“Eliyahu Bachur says...”) is kept; the point is only that the name was prompted.

Tension D — “Bnei Bavel” and the triennial cycle (VIII). The Sofer and the Plaud agree that the Rav (and Reb Berel’s question) says [“the מנהג של בני בבל to ליין the ספר תורה every three years”], and the Plaud summary repeats “Bavel.” The HTML is faithful to the recording. But the triennial cycle was Eretz Yisrael’s, and the Cairo shul in the very next sentence is the “ancient minhag.” This is the Rav’s slip or a mishearing. For the Sefer Edition, put it right in the body and ask the Rav.

Smaller points. (a) The sponsor’s name (רב שמואל פרוינד) is omitted, which is right for a book but should be kept in the editorial file; the Plaud has “Reb Shmuel Freund,” confirming it. (b) The HTML says the Rav “jokes about his own outstanding balance”; the Sofer: [“I owe I owe some money to the שול, טאקע. What's the מתנה? נישט א געשעפט”]. It is closer to a pointed joke at the gabbai’s expense than a confession; the Sefer should drop it. (c) The Rav’s name for the Shibbolei author ([“רב אברהם בן צדקי I think צדקיהו בן אברהם הרופא”]) is a hesitant hearing and the HTML correctly puts it in quotation marks. (d) The question “what’s the מקור for someone whose wife is in the ninth month?” and the Chida are as the Sofer has them; the Plaud summary’s “Rabbi Avraham ben Tzidkiyah (father of the Shibbolei HaLeket)” as a co-advocate with the Or Zarua differs from the Sofer (Rav Avraham alone, in Rome), and the HTML follows the Sofer. (e) The “Torah is not in heaven” rejoinder after the Or Zarua’s dream is a participant’s and is kept as such. (f) The closing “חג כשר ושמח” (“a kosher and happy holiday”) at the end of the Sofer has no holiday in this week’s calendar; the HTML omits it, correctly flagged in the Sefaria log.
