0.Preliminary Flag — Attribution

The write-up names the maggid shiur as “Rabbi Ephrati”, both in the sub-title and in the mareh mekomos back-references (“The Rebbe brings…”, “The Rebbe's frame…”). The commissioning note for this review identifies the speaker as Rabbi Daniel Z. Feldman. The transcript itself does not name the speaker. One of these two attributions is wrong, and before the piece ships for the sefer this must be resolved. If the write-up is correct, the review brief needs correcting; if the brief is correct, every “Rabbi Ephrati” / “The Rebbe” reference in the HTML and footnotes must be swapped. Nothing in the shiur's internal voice (the Krasnoyarsk opener, the “Rabbi Yisrael” / “Ravoysai” address, the “I always like to say — a Jew walks around with a portfolio”) decides the question for the reviewer; flagging it to the editor.

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

A pre-Tishah B'Av walk through Tehillim kuf-lamed-zayin — the perek we say daily before benching on weekdays — built around a sustained reading of the Malbim, bracketed by the Radak and the Alshich HaKadosh, and anchored in the long midrash tehillim on Nebuchadnezzar's cruise. The Malbim's chiddush — “sham yashavnu” as “we settled”, “aravim” as “sweet things” as well as willows, and “gemul” as in-kind rather than vengeance — recasts the perek as a structural analysis of galus as the seduction of olam ha-zeh. The Radak supplies the pshat of tears that interrupt song; the Alshich supplies the shiur's single most quotable line (“the worst part of galus is when we cannot cry”). The midrash narrative — the brutal march, the loads of stones, the mal'achim' plea rebuffed by tanchumin shel hevel, the demand to play the harps, the mashal of the queen and the mukkeh shechin, the bitten thumbs, and Nebuchadnezzar's rage — carries the shiur's emotional engine. From the midrash the Rav lifts the “sacrilegious” question about Divine silence in Auschwitz and in Kfar Aza / Moshav B'eri, and answers it with the shiur's governing thesis: the mal'achim' descent is a response to the Yidden's refusal to sell their shir Hashem, and the question is thrown back at us (“are we ready to bite our fingers off?”). Lands on Bavli-abstention-from-meat-and-wine (Bava Basra samech amud bet), the eifer on the chasan's head, the Edom inversion, and the Malbim's gemul — closing with a specific reading list (Perfidy; a Holocaust book; a book about Shemini Atzeret) and the Olam Haba-dik'e / Yerushalayim-dik'e / Shir-Hashem-dik'e Yidden refrain.

2.Fidelity Assessment — Overall

The write-up follows the shiur's architecture faithfully. The eighteen Roman-numeraled sections map cleanly onto the transcript's unfolding: Malbim on pasuk alef, kinoros, Radak, Malbim on aravim as sweet, Bavel's demand, eich nashir, the midrash, the Kotel vignette, the pride-of-the-exiles sequence, the mal'achim and the question, the demand to sing, the mashal, the bitten thumbs, Bava Basra, im eshkachech, Edom, gemul, and the Perfidy close. The Malbim's three load-bearing moves — “sham yashavnu” as settled, “aravim”-as-sweet, and “gemul” as in-kind — are all present and correctly stated. The Alshich's core line is preserved verbatim. The midrash's key beats (tanchumin shel hevel, gidufin hen, the mukkeh shechin, the right-thumb detail, tilei tilim shel harugim) all survive the transfer.

The problems are of three kinds: (a) one substantive content omission that softens the shiur's hardest contemporary edge; (b) a systematic pattern of editorial additions in the mareh mekomos footnotes — biographical dates, publication histories, scholarly framing — that were not said in the shiur and should be clearly marked as editorial or struck; (c) minor mis-recalls inside the Kotel vignette footnote.

3.Gaps — What the Rav Said That the Write-Up Dropped

  • The “Palestinian babies” aside on “ashrei shye'achez v'nipetz”. At 00:49:48, inside the shock-reading of the closing pasuk, the Rav explicitly says: “What an image seared into our heads — of people taking Babylonian babies, Palestinian babies — I am using the mashal — and smashing them against the rocks.” The write-up (section XVI) renders this as “a searing image of murder” and drops the “Palestinian babies” clause entirely. This is the shiur's single most contemporary and most controversial sentence — a deliberate (and self-flagged, “I am using the mashal”) rhetorical move by the Rav. Whether it belongs in the sefer is an editorial judgement, but the write-up cannot simply disappear it. Either keep the line with the Rav's self-flag intact, or add a footnote recording that it was said and omitted; silent sanitization is the wrong move.
  • The “every five seconds another siyum on Zoom” line survived — good. Flagging it as the kind of contemporary aside the write-up correctly retained, as a contrast to the above.
  • The Rav's self-identification with the “Rabbi Yosef” / “Ravoysai” address. The transcript repeatedly uses direct second-person address to the chaburah (“Doesn't it bother you?”, “Rabbi Yisrael, I know it bothers every single one of us”). The write-up's prose voice is more distanced and sefer-like, which is appropriate for the finished product — but the shiur's live register of a chaburah being addressed in the second person is a texture worth preserving in at least one or two spots.
  • The “one, ten minutes overtime, I apologize” sign-off is dropped. Correctly — mentioned only for completeness.
  • The transcript's half-fragment on “the strength of Jerusalem, I will hold on to you. Loyalty to Jerusalem is my joy” (at 00:46:30, immediately before “And then the last two psukim”) is a scrap of what sounds like a translation of “im lo a'aleh es Yerushalayim al rosh simchasi” and is treated by the write-up as integrated prose in section XIV. The treatment is defensible, but the “rosh simchasi” clause itself is never fully unpacked in the write-up the way the Rav telegraphs it; one more sentence in section XIV naming “al rosh simchasi” explicitly as the climactic clause would help.

4.Fabrications & Additions — What the Write-Up Says That the Rav Did Not

The mareh mekomos notes carry substantial editorial content that is not in the shiur. This is the review's most consequential finding. The mareh mekomos apparatus is a reference tool for the reader, and some editorial contextualization is appropriate — but a reader will reasonably assume that what is attributed to the Rav in those notes was said by the Rav, and that is not the case here. A clean division between “what the shiur cited” and “what the editor added for the sefer” is needed, either by a convention (a bracketed [Ed.] tag) or by splitting the entries.

Specifically:

  • fn1 (Malbim): adds “1809–1879, Romania and Russia” — not said in shiur; adds the gloss “The Malbim's reading transforms the perek from a literal historical lament into a structural analysis of galus as the seduction of olam ha-zeh” — this is the editor's framing, not the Rav's; it is a fair summary but should not be presented as the Rav's.
  • fn4 (Yirmiyahu / Eichah): adds the specific chapter (perek chaf-tes) and quotes the igeres — the Rav did not quote it; he said generally that Yirmiyahu describes Yidden with homes, farms, vineyards.
  • fn5 (Radak): adds “1160–1235, Narbonne” — not in shiur.
  • fn6 (Alshich): adds “1508–1593, Tzfas; talmid of Rabbi Yosef Karo and contemporary of the Arizal” — not in shiur. The Rav called him “the Alshich HaKadosh” without biographical framing.
  • fn7 (Midrash Tehillim): names the midrash as Shocher Tov — correct identification — and adds “redacted in Eretz Yisrael across the early amoraic and gaonic periods” — editorial addition.
  • fn8 (Bava Basra): adds the specific examples “chavitei kohen gadol” for oil — the Rav said only “you shouldn't eat any oil” without specifying the korban; this is editorial elaboration. The entire closing sentence of fn8 (“The Rebbe brings the sugya as the structural answer…”) is editorial synthesis, not the Rav's framing.
  • fn9 (Shulchan Aruch / Mishnah Berurah): adds that the Mishnah Berurah “elaborates the minhagim, including the minhag to recite perek kuf-lamed-zayin”. In the shiur the Rav explicitly says “the Mishnah Berurah brings the minhag that on Shabbos and Yom Tov we say Shir Hama'alos before benching” — so the Mishnah Berurah is cited for the Shabbos / Yom Tov substitution, not for the weekday minhag; the footnote blurs this. The Rav himself hedges on his sourcing (“I am not sure if I am quoting it properly”, “I don't know if the oilam is noheig to say it before benching”) — that candor is lost in the write-up.
  • fn10 (Perfidy): this is the most expansive editorial footnote. It supplies Ben Hecht's dates (1894–1964), the 1961 Julian Messner imprint, a one-paragraph description of the book's thesis (Mapai, the Kastner trial, Jewish leadership failures under Nazi atrocity), and an interpretive gloss on why the Rav recommended it. None of this is in the shiur. The Rav simply said: “Open up a Perfidy. You ever read Perfidy? Every year, that's the read. Perfidy.” The footnote may be useful to the reader, but it is the editor's content, not the Rav's — and the gloss “the Rebbe brings it as recommended reading not as an endorsement of every claim but as a corrective” attributes a nuanced editorial position to the Rav that he did not voice.
  • fn11 (Kotel 1967): adds specifics not in the shiur — “Mota Gur”, the date June 7, 1967, Rabbi Shlomo Goren's shofar-blowing, and the full title “Chief Rabbi of the IDF.” The transcript has only: “Rabbi Goron in the famous words: 'Har ha-bayis b'yadeinu.' … I don't remember about Har ha-bayis. There was a line about Har ha-bayis also, but ha-Kotel b'yadeinu same thing.” The Rav himself is uncertain about which line Rav Goren said; the footnote confidently supplies the version the Rav was uncertain about. The footnote also adds, “Specific attribution and verification of the dialogue varies; the substance is taken as expressive…” — a scholarly caveat the Rav did not voice. Accurate as editorial context; needs the [Ed.] flag.
  • fn2 (The Eternal Question): the note attributes to the Rav the framing “the question is uncomfortable and not fully resolvable in human terms.” The Rav's own framing is cruder and more pastoral: “I know we're not allowed to ask such a question, but it always bothers me.” He does not hedge toward unresolvability; he gives a definite answer (“the mal'achim come down when we're ready to bite our fingers off”). The footnote softens the Rav's actual position.

5.Misattributions and Mischaracterizations

  • The Rav's “I never understood this as a child” is correctly captured in the write-up's section XVI (“The Rebbe never understood it as a child”) — but the write-up third-persons an explicitly first-person line, which flattens the shiur's register. Preserving the first person (“As a child I never understood this pasuk”) would be truer and would also soften the “Rebbe” framing (which is the attribution question flagged in section 0 above).
  • The write-up's section I attributes to “the Mechaber in siman alef” the halachah of saying Al Naharos Bavel before benching on weekdays. The Rav himself is explicitly uncertain: “I am not sure if I am quoting it properly”, “I don't know if the oilam is noheig to say it before benching. It's halacha. The Mechaber brings it actually in siman alef in Shulchan Aruch, where the Mechaber says… I am not sure if I am quoting it properly.” The write-up presents the sourcing as definite. For the sefer, the sourcing should be made definite (verify the siman and the Mishnah Berurah cross-reference), but either flag it as verified-by-editor or acknowledge the Rav's hedge.
  • The Kotel vignette's “religious soldier” framing is slightly tightened. The transcript says the secular soldier is “chozer-in-fleibin, nebach” — a sheigetz, in the Rav's words — a specific sociological register (a chozer she-nifrad or wayward returnee). The write-up renders him as “a secular soldier from a non-religious upbringing”, which is accurate as to secularity but misses the specific texture. Not load-bearing; mentioned for the editor's awareness.
  • The “Rabbi Yosef” in the transcript at 00:50:08 (“Never understood this pasuk, Rabbi Yosef said. Doesn't bother you?”) is almost certainly a mis-transcription of “Rabbosai”, as it appears elsewhere in the transcript at 00:38:00 and 00:48:38. The write-up correctly treats it as the Rav's own voice; worth noting only for the editor to double-check the audio at these spots.

6.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The Malbim's “aravim”-as-“arevim” wordplay deserves one source note the write-up could supply. The dual-meaning move is beautiful, but Hebrew-grammatically it leans on the orthographic identity (same consonants, different nekudos) of aravah / arev. A one-line editor's note on the vowel distinction (“arevim” ayin-kamatz-reish-cheirik-yud-mem vs. “aravim” as the plural of aravah) would help a careful reader see the chiddush as a chiddush in lashon ha-kodesh rather than a loose homonym.

The Malbim's “gemul” move is doing more halachic work than either the shiur or the write-up credit. The Rav glosses gemul as “receiving back in the exact manner you perpetrated it,” which is the sense of middah ke-neged middah. But gemul in Chazal's technical usage carries overlapping registers — gemul chessed (as in gomel chasadim tovim), gemul-as-reward and gemul-as-recompense — and the Malbim's reading is picking out one technical sense. A footnote distinguishing “gemul”-as-in-kind-recompense from “gemul”-as-chessed would strengthen the chiddush.

The Alshich's “worst part is that they did not let us cry” is cited without inside-source location. The Rav says “the Alshich HaKadosh”; the write-up adds “in his peirush al Tehillim.” For the sefer the specific Rommaniah edition page or at least the specific pasuk under which the Alshich develops the point should be supplied by the editor. (The Alshich's running commentary on Tehillim is dense; locating the exact passage would be a chessed to the reader.)

The Bava Basra samech amud bet sugya on abstention from meat and wine is correctly cited, but the Rav glides past a subtlety the sefer should name: Rabbi Yehoshua's rebuttal does not quite reach the Yerushalmi's formulation of “ein gozrin gezeirah al ha-tzibbur”, and the reductio (grain, oil, fruit) is itself the limiting principle rather than a defeater of the ascetic position. The Rav's conclusion — “we have a different way of looking at it; that's what you are supposed to think” — is pastorally correct but under-theorized; a note on the Ramban's “kedosh atah” / “naval bi-reshus ha-Torah” frame would situate the Rav's move inside a bigger discussion.

The “eifer ba-rosh” for the chasan is drawn from im eshkachech without the standard sugya reference (Bava Basra samech amud bet again, actually — the same sugya continues with the eifer and the chatzi amah be-chatzi amah unfinished wall). The write-up could tighten the structure by naming this as a single continuous sugya rather than two separate gemara citations.

7.Structural Suggestions for the Sefer

  • Collapse the two “Eternal Question” sections. The write-up has “VIII. The Eternal Question” and then “XII. The Answer to Our Question”, with sections IX, X, XI (the demand, the mashal, Nebuchadnezzar's rage) interposed. This is faithful to the shiur's live unfolding — the midrash-material supplies the answer — but for the sefer, consider framing sections IX–XII as a single arc under the header “The Question and Its Midrashic Answer”, with the question-sections as hinge paragraphs rather than full H2s. Right now the reader loses the throughline across four section breaks.
  • Mark the mareh mekomos footnotes' editorial additions. Per section 4 above. The cleanest convention: each entry begins with the Rav's exact citation (as he gave it), then an [Ed. note:] block for editor-supplied biography, publication data, and framing. This also protects the sefer against later readers who return to the audio and discover the Rav never said “Mota Gur”.
  • Decide on the “Palestinian babies” line. Three options: (a) include it with the Rav's self-flag (“I am using the mashal”) intact; (b) include it with a translator-footnote on the political register; (c) omit with a visible [...] mark and an editor's note. Silent omission (the current state) is the one option that should not be chosen.
  • The “portfolio” metaphor could be a sidebar. The Rav's “a Jew walks around with a portfolio” — and the enumeration (Torah, avodah, sechar va-onesh, gan eden, gehinnom) — is one of the shiur's warmest moments and lands near the end. The write-up folds it into section XVII in-line; a short sidebar under the heading “The Yid's Portfolio” would give it the airtime it deserves and would also serve the sefer-reader looking for a shabbos-table takeaway.
  • Verify the Shulchan Aruch siman alef citation. The Rav himself hedged. The actual mekor for the minhag to say Al Naharos Bavel is in the Mishnah Berurah's commentary on siman alef (the opening shelo yevosh complex on morning-rising) rather than in the Shulchan Aruch's own text. The editor should verify this and either confirm or amend.
  • Section XI (“Nebuchadnezzar's Rage and the Secret of Galus”) carries a load-bearing chiddush that would benefit from italics or a pull-quote. The sentence “You can take the Bais HaMikdash from him; you can take Eretz Yisrael and Yerushalayim; you can take ruchniyus — and he is still a Yid” is one of the shiur's lines a reader will want to find again. Lift it into a callout.

8.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.The shiur's governing move is the Malbim's “aravim-as-sweet” re-reading. But the Malbim's pshat here is not the Radak's and is not the standard mefaresh. Does the Rav hold the Malbim is the correct pshat, or is this a drush-reading laid alongside the Radak's pshat? The question matters because section IV reads the shiur through the Malbim as if it were the pshat; a clarification is owed.
Q2.On the “Eternal Question”: the Rav's answer is that the mal'achim' descent is contingent on the Yidden's refusal to sell their shir Hashem. Does this mean, read strictly, that the mal'achim did not descend in Auschwitz or on Simchas Torah 5784 because the generation was not ready to “bite its fingers off”? That is a hard reading; a softer reading would be that the Rav is drawing a mussar-point from the midrash rather than extracting an answer. Which does the Rav mean?
Q3.On the Malbim's “gemul”-as-in-kind: the chiddush rescues the pasuk from brutality by making it descriptive rather than prescriptive. But the pasuk opens with “ashrei”: “fortunate is he who…”. Ashrei is a term of commendation. How does the Malbim handle the ashrei? Is the “fortunate” the descriptive-fact-happiness of middah ke-neged middah-realized, or is it a residue that the Malbim's reading leaves unresolved?
Q4.On the Alshich's “worst part is that we cannot cry”: does the Rav extend this to a halachic or hashkafic chiyuv on Tishah B'Av to induce tears — the mussar-tradition of reading Kinos slowly, of visualizing the churban, of saying the Nachem aloud? Or does the Alshich stop at sensitivity, with “actual tears” as a siman rather than the obligation itself?
Q5.On Perfidy: the recommendation is unusual — the book is controversial and (as the editor's footnote correctly notes) was and is contested by establishment Zionist historiography. Why specifically Perfidy and not, say, Lucy Dawidowicz, Yaffa Eliach, or a specific chassidish memoir? Is the recommendation about the content, the polemical register, or the specific lesson about Jewish leadership choices under duress?
Q6.On the Kotel vignette: the Rav himself is unsure which line of Rav Goren he is quoting (“ha-Har ha-bayis b'yadeinu” vs. “ha-Kotel b'yadeinu”). The historical record is that Rav Goren's famous proclamation was “Har ha-bayis b'yadeinu” (over the two-way radio to Dayan). Does the Rav want the sefer to lock the line down, or to preserve his own uncertainty as part of the shiur's live register?

9.Bottom Line

A faithful, well-structured sefer-draft of a strong pre-Tishah B'Av shiur whose chief innovation is the Malbim's re-reading of “aravim” as the sweetness of Bavel, carried through to its re-reading of “gemul” as middah ke-neged middah. The write-up gets the Malbim's three load-bearing moves right, preserves the Alshich's core line, and transmits the midrash's central beats intact. The three things to fix before the piece ships: (a) resolve the attribution question — Rabbi Ephrati or Rabbi Feldman; the current state is a show-stopper; (b) decide on the omitted “Palestinian babies” line one way or the other — do not sanitize silently; (c) mark the mareh mekomos footnotes' extensive editorial additions (biographical dates, publication data, scholarly framing) with an [Ed.] convention, since nothing from “Rabbi Shlomo Goren, Chief Rabbi of the IDF, blew the shofar at the wall” through “Ben Hecht (1894–1964), published 1961 by Julian Messner, Inc.” was said in the shiur. With those three fixed and the Shulchan Aruch siman alef citation verified, the piece is ready.
