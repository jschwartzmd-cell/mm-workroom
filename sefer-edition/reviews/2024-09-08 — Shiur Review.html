Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

8 September 2024 — אָז יָשִׁיר, יִשְׁתַבַּח, and the Throne of Shlomo

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · 23 August 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

Delivered the morning after the Skulener Rebbe zt"l was niftar, the shiur opens with a personal tribute — the Rebbe on oxygen at a Mir gathering insisting the drashah be given in lashon ha'kodesh, the five-minute Asher Yatzar — and then moves into the end of Pesukei d'Zimra: the halachic problem of inserting Az Yashir between Baruch She'amar and Yishtabach; the Geonic reluctance versus the Italian/Roman practice preserved since the Churban and imported to Ashkenaz by R' Moshe ben Kalonymos; the Aruch HaShulchan's kashya on the inverted Torah → Nevi'im → Ketuvim order, resolved by the Zohar's reframing of Az Yashir as a gesher into Yotzer Or; the three-way symmetry of fifteens (shevachim in Shiras HaYam, praises in Yishtabach, generations from Avraham to Shlomo); the eighteen Names / ע״ב issue and the Vilna Gaon's skepticism of the added pesukim; the multiple attributions of Yishtabach's authorship, culminating in the Rokeach's tradition that Shlomo composed it at the dedication of his throne (with a long detour through the Targum Sheni's mechanical marvel, Pharaoh Necho's lameness, Achashverosh's obsession, and even Napoleon refusing to sit); and R' Chaim Vital's tradition of an אברהם acrostic hidden in the closing chasimah. The shiur closes with the Zohar's vivid rebuke for interrupting between the fifteen praises, the Shir HaMaalos tension, and a synthesis of the four converging currents — halachic, historical, Kabbalistic, and compositional.

2.What Was Strongest

  • The Skulener Rebbe petichah. Two images do more work than a paragraph of tribute could: the Rebbe interrupting a Yiddish drashah — from Satmar-affiliated Skulen, no less — to insist on lashon ha'kodesh so the Mir bachurim could understand; and the five-to-six-minute Asher Yatzar facing the wall. Both are the shiur's real thesis in miniature: tefillah as literal, weighed, refused-to-be-rote.
  • The three-way fifteen symmetry. The alignment of the Mechilta's fifteen shevachim, the fifteen praises of Yishtabach, and the fifteen generations from Avraham through Shlomo is genuinely striking, and the Rav uses it not as a numerological curiosity but as the compositional scaffolding that unlocks why Shlomo (fifteenth generation) is a plausible author and why the Avraham acrostic in the chasimah is not decorative. That structural payoff is the finest moment in the shiur.
  • The Aruch HaShulchan → Zohar move. Framing the whole Kabbalah-vs-halachic-order tension through the Aruch HaShulchan's own admission — “I had no resolution until I found that the Zohar HaKadosh asked the same question” — is elegant. It lets a nigleh posek be the one who legitimizes the sod-based reordering, which is far more persuasive than starting from the Kabbalistic side.
  • The Italian provenance narrative. Tracing daily Az Yashir to the post-Bayis Sheni exiles, through Minhag Roma, to R' Moshe ben Kalonymos, and then noting that the same paytan gave us Kol Mekadesh Shevi'i and Menuchah v'Simchah is the kind of connective tissue that makes a familiar liturgy suddenly historically located.
  • The Avraham acrostic. Naming the letters — Adon, Borei, Ribon, HaBocher, Melech — and letting the audience hear אברהם emerge from a chasimah they have said thousands of times is the sort of detail that changes a person's davening the next morning.

3.What Was Weaker or Worth Pushing Back On

The eighteen-Names section is asserted but not shown. The claim — that the Names in the broader Shiras HaYam narrative total eighteen, that י״ח × ד yields the ע״ב, and that extra pesukim were appended to complete the count — is a strong claim on which the Vilna Gaon's dissent turns. But the shiur never actually counts. Which pesukim are the disputed ones? How many Names does the un-augmented Az Yashir contain? Without the arithmetic, the Gra's rejection sounds like a matter of taste, and the mekubalim's defense sounds like inertia. This is the one section of the shiur where a concrete list of pesukim on the board would have changed the audience's understanding.

The Shlomo-as-author narrative and the R' Chaim Vital acrostic tradition sit uneasily together. If Shlomo composed Yishtabach at the dedication of his throne, and Avraham Avinu composed the chasimah, then Avraham's chasimah predates Shlomo's body by roughly fifteen generations — and the composite tefillah only came into existence when Shlomo somehow appended his praises to a pre-existing Avrahamic closing (or vice versa). The Rav notes that R' Chaim Vital records this not in the name of the Arizal, which is honest, but the mechanical implausibility of the two traditions coexisting deserves at least a sentence. The Rishonim who attribute Yishtabach to Chazal, the Geonim, or the Rabbanan Saberoyim are lined up as alternatives, but the shiur does not really argue for any one over the others — it presents four and drifts toward the Shlomo attribution because the Shlomo story is the best one.

The throne excursus is long relative to its payload. The Targum Sheni material — mechanical lions, circling eagles, roaring animals, the electricity speculation, Shishak, Pharaoh Necho's limp, Achashverosh's obsession, Charlemagne's replica, Napoleon standing on the steps — is dramatic and audience-pleasing, but its function in the shiur is a single sentence: Shlomo refused honor at the dedication and diverted it upward with the words of Yishtabach. The Napoleon line is memorable; ten minutes of throne-lore to set up one hodaah moment is a lot. Tightening this would have left room for the Shir HaMaalos tension, which is currently disposed of in a paragraph.

4.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The Rambam's post-Yishtabach placement is dismissed without engagement. The Rambam rules that one who wishes to say Az Yashir should do so after Yishtabach. The shiur observes that no community follows this and moves on, but the halachic reasoning is worth pausing on. If the brachah-frame from Baruch She'amar to Yishtabach is a single unit (per Tosfos's brachah ha'semuchah l'chaverta), then the Rambam's placement is not merely conservative — it is the position that best preserves the structure Tosfos identifies. That the universal practice overrides it on Zohar-based grounds is precisely the halachah/Kabbalah tension the Rav flags elsewhere; here it applies with unusual force and is not drawn out.

The Magen Avraham's amen mid-brachah leniency needs a fence. The rule that certain leniencies apply to Baruch She'amar and Yishtabach because they are post-Talmudic sits in real tension with Tosfos's brachah ha'semuchah l'chaverta structure and with the Mishnah Berurah's severe ruling on repeating from the beginning if one spoke. Are these leniencies limited to answering amen, or do they extend more broadly? The shiur cites the Magen Avraham cleanly but does not resolve where the leniency stops.

The Shir HaMaalos justification is under-argued. The heter offered — that divrei tefillah with established minhag are not classified as hefsek — is the standard answer, but it invites a follow-up the shiur does not ask. If divrei tefillah are categorically not hefsek, why does the Mishnah Berurah treat speaking between Yishtabach and Yotzer Or so strictly even when what one wishes to add is itself divrei tefillah? The distinction likely lies in fixed tzibbur-authorized additions vs. individual initiative, but the shiur leaves the line undrawn.

The dispute over Yishtabach's authorship is treated as a matter of history rather than halachah. If the Rif and Rambam hold tikkenu chachamim, and the Rokeach's tradition holds Shlomo, the halachic weight of the brachah may differ — Chazal-instituted brachos are treated more stringently than post-Chazal minhagim even when both are universally practiced. The Magen Avraham leniency and the hefsek stringency together suggest a hybrid halachic status that would repay explicit treatment.

5.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.Can we get the concrete count of the eighteen Names in Shiras HaYam — which occurrences the Rishonim include, and specifically which pesukim at the end are the disputed additions the Vilna Gaon refused to say? Without the count, the machloikes between the Gra and the mekubalim is opaque; with it, an audience can actually take a position.
Q2.How do the various attributions of Yishtabach's authorship — Rabbanan Saberoyim, Chazal proper (Rambam / Rif), Geonim (Meiri), and Shlomo HaMelech (Rokeach) — actually cohere? Is the traditional view that Shlomo composed the language and Chazal later fixed it as brachah-text? Or are these mutually exclusive positions, in which case which one does the Rav consider most defensible?
Q3.If Avraham Avinu composed the closing chasimah (per R' Chaim Vital) and Shlomo composed the body, what is the historical mechanism by which the two became a single tefillah? Was the Avrahamic chasimah preserved orally in the mesorah and appended by Shlomo, or is this a purely sod-level claim about spiritual sources rather than literal composition?
Q4.Given the Zohar's extreme rebuke for interrupting between the fifteen praises of Yishtabach, how does the Rav understand the universal minhag to answer amen, Kedushah, and Barchu during that section? Is this the same category as Shir HaMaalos on Yamim Noraim, or is there a sharper distinction?
Q5.The Aruch HaShulchan's kashya on the Torah → Nevi'im → Ketuvim order was resolved by the Zohar's pairing of Az Yashir with Yotzer Or. Does that resolution imply that Az Yashir is functionally not part of Pesukei d'Zimra at all — that it belongs, structurally, to the Birchos Kriyas Shema unit and merely precedes them? And if so, does this affect how one thinks about the brachah-frame that Yishtabach closes?
Q6.The Rambam records that some communities recited Ha'azinu daily in place of Az Yashir, and some substitute it even on Tisha B'Av. Does any community preserve this practice today, and is there any halachic reason a person facing a rushed davening could rely on the Ha'azinu substitute in a she'as ha'dechak?

6.Bottom Line

A structurally satisfying shiur that earns its scope. The Skulener Rebbe petichah lands, the three-way fifteens are the kind of pattern one does not forget, and the Aruch HaShulchan → Zohar resolution is genuinely elegant. The weaknesses are secondary: the eighteen-Names section is asserted rather than shown, the throne excursus outruns its liturgical payload, and the four competing attributions of Yishtabach's authorship are laid out but not adjudicated. What the shiur does supremely well is demonstrate that a familiar three-minute stretch of siddur — Az Yashir into Yishtabach — is the sedimented product of Geonic caution, Italian mesorah, Ashkenazic transmission, Kabbalistic reordering, and midrashic composition, and that the Avraham acrostic in the chasimah is the fingerprint on the whole. A person who hears this shiur says the closing praises differently the next morning — which is what a shiur on tefillah is for.
