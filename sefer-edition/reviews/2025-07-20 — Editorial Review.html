Mareh Mekamos · Independent Editorial Review

20 July 2025 — שמע קולנו: The Culmination of Our Petitions

Independent editorial review · craft, prose, and citation pass · 5 October 2026

1.Overall Read

This is a warm, well-shaped shiur with an unusually clean two-movement structure — a historical/halachic first half on nusach and personal additions, and an emotional second half built around “reikam al teshiveinu” and Rav Schwab's loaf of black bread. The writeup follows that shape faithfully. The Shmuel ha-Katan double-maaseh (Section IV) is handled with real craft: both meshalim land, and the pivot in Section V (“Pleasant תפילה”) converts them into a reframing of what “shema” actually asks for. The Aruch HaShulchan callout (Section VI) is the document's single strongest set-piece — it preserves the polemic's heat without endorsing it l'maaseh.

Where the document is weaker is at its seams. The opening dedication to Rabbi Yaakov Yosef is a strong lead but is never structurally bracketed back to the shiur's subject; the closing paragraph (Section XIV) ends on a vivid but under-explained metaphor (“Do not be a bear licking honey through the page”) that will stop readers cold. Section IX (“The Whole Point of Studying תפילה”) shifts voice from first-person to third-person (“The Rebbe steps back”) in a way that momentarily breaks the register the rest of the document has established. And one blockquote in Section VI contains a nested-italic rendering issue that will need to be flattened before type.

2.What Works on the Page

  • The Section IV diptych. The decision to give each Shmuel ha-Katan year its own h3 — The First Year, The Second Year — is editorially right: it makes the parallel structure of the Gemara visible to the eye before the reader even parses the words. The three-sentence close (“Both stories… are not the שמע we want”) lands the point without over-explaining it.
  • The ערוך השלחן callout (Section VI). Pulling the polemic into a shaded box, letting the shouting voice stand ("they are guilty," "עמי הארץ", "corruption of our סידורים"), and then returning to normative paragraphing with the quiet “No one else holds this way למעשה” is textbook pedagogical staging. The reader absorbs the full weight of the Aruch HaShulchan's objection before being told it is not the pesak.
  • The Section XI set-piece (Rav Schwab). The callout box containing the Kommissar-Brot memory is the emotional core of the piece, and the paragraphing within the box is excellent — one paragraph of context, one paragraph of the exchange, one paragraph of the tear and the recognition. The bolded final clause (“everything was for his benefit”) is used sparingly enough in the document that it still carries force when it appears.
  • The תהלים ל״ז:כ״ה integration. Introducing the pasuk as its own h3 inside Section XI (rather than inline) was the right call: it gives Rav Schwab's pshat the architectural weight it needs. The sequence pasuk → question (“surely there are צדיקים”) → reread (“who felt abandoned”) → application to the mother is cleanly built.
  • The two-sentence ב״ח coda (Section XIII). The paragraph that lands “Both גדולים died within the year” is one of the most restrained in the document — the maaseh is told flat, the halachah l'maaseh (“consult a רב; do not improvise”) follows immediately, and no commentary intrudes. Good editorial discipline.
  • The footnote apparatus is more consistent here than in several recent writeups. The two-tier short/expanded structure is applied uniformly across the first fourteen notes; the four academic-flag notes (fn10, fn15, fn16, fn18) are correctly marked as such and their under-specified sources are explicitly declared (“precise published references not specified in the שיעור”). That is the right pattern.

3.Where It's Weak or Could Be Sharper

The Rabbi Yaakov Yosef bracket never closes. The sponsored lead is one of the strongest openings in the series — the Satmar Rebbe's tribute, the Cypress Hills burial, the Mir-era מדבר in Torah. But the closing paragraph (Section XIV) is an abstract summary of insertion categories that never returns to the man whose yartzeit the shiur was dedicated to. One or two sentences at the close — “Reb Yankev Yosef, in a מדבר of a city, did precisely this: he kept coming to the door”, or whatever equivalent — would turn the lead from a dedicatory note into a frame. As it stands, the Chief Rabbi disappears after the lead and the document reads as two self-contained pieces stapled together.

Section IX reads as a seam. The paragraph opening “The Rebbe steps back: this is why we have spent every Sunday morning since שמחת תורה…” is the only moment in the document where the voice shifts out of the shiur's register and into third-person meta-commentary. In a live shiur this is where the rebbi pauses to recentre the room; on the page it reads as a seam between movements. Either rewrite in the shiur's voice (“This is why we have spent…”, with no “The Rebbe steps back” frame), or move the whole paragraph into a different section where it does not have to carry the hinge.

The closing metaphor dies on the page. “Do not be a bear licking honey through the page” — without the oral setup that presumably accompanied it in the shiur-room, this image will stop the reader cold. Either restore the metaphor's source (if there is one — is this a צוואה הלב? a Chasidishe mashal?), or replace it with a plain-prose version of what it is meant to convey (do not go through the bracha mechanically; stop and taste it). The current phrasing feels like a transcript survival.

The Section VI callout has nested italics inside italics. The current markup places <em>-wrapped quotations inside an already-<em>-wrapped paragraph (the whole callout is set italic by convention here), producing visually ambiguous rendering. The polemic's inner quotations (“they are guilty!”, “Who are you to insert…”) should be rendered in roman inside italic surround (or vice-versa), with explicit <q> or quote-mark treatment. On the page as currently coded the reader cannot cleanly see where the ערוך השלחן's words end and the parenthetical gloss begins.

Section VIII (רב יבא סבא) folds in too much, too fast. One paragraph does three jobs: it tells the Zohar story, delivers the halachah l'maaseh (“three times a day… add a תפילה for פרנסה”), and introduces רבי יונה לנדסופר by name without ever having set him up (he reappears in Section VII as a parallel to the יעב״ץ, but is first named inside fn10). Give רב יבא סבא his own paragraph for the maaseh, and a second paragraph for the take-away; and make sure רבי יונה לנדסופר is introduced in the body (not only in a footnote) before he is cited.

The Rebbe's father section (XII) is powerful but structurally orphaned. “The Power of Tears” sits between the Rav Schwab climax (XI) and the ב״ח coda (XIII) without a connective sentence to either. The maaseh of the father's weekday tears is the natural extension of Rav Schwab's pshat — the tzaddik who stays connected — but the current paragraphing does not surface the link. One lead sentence (“What Rav Schwab describes was lived in our own generation”, or similar) would make the section earn its place.

Section VII (יעב״ץ) has a long parenthetical that should be lifted into a footnote. “(long pages of it; people in those generations had patience)” is a lovely aside in the room; on the page it interrupts the sentence between “a דרשה in Prague” and the footnote reference. Either promote it to a sentence of its own, or move it into fn10 where it fits naturally with the discussion of the דרשות.

4.Hebrew / English Integration

  • Nikud is inconsistent. The title bar renders שמע קולנו plain; the body uses plain unpointed Hebrew throughout; but the Shiur Review's title (and some of the parallel editorial reviews in the Sefer Edition series) use fully-pointed forms. For sefer type-setting this will need to be decided once — either all pointed, all unpointed, or pointed only for liturgical quotations — and applied consistently. Current drift is live-document, not sefer-grade.
  • Transliteration is handled almost entirely by preserving Hebrew in the Hebrew alphabet, which is a clean choice and works. One lapse: “Rav Yeiva Saba” in Section VIII appears in English transliteration in some sentences and as רב יבא סבא in others. Standardize to one form — preferably the Hebrew throughout, with the English name only on first mention and in parentheses.
  • Translation accuracy — two spots to double-check. In Section XII the citation “בא נשלום” looks like a transcription artifact; the standard Shemoneh Esrei-era bakashah language is בוא בשלום, and even that is primarily a Lecha Dodi locution rather than a weekday Shemoneh Esrei one. Confirm what the Rebbe actually said and whether the writer meant השיבנו לשלום (end of השיבה) or something else. Similarly, “השיבנו בחיים” in the same paragraph — the normative נוסח is השיבנו אבינו לתורתך, and “השיבנו בחיים” is from the תפלה זכה, not the weekday Shemoneh Esrei. If the Rebbe quoted these as examples of tefillos one might cry at, the context needs to be clarified in prose; otherwise the pairing should be dropped. Flag for Agent 3.
  • The Av HaRachman / Av HaRachamim spelling in Section III. The nusach opening phrase of Shema Koleinu in נוסח ספרד is usually printed Av HaRachaman (אב הרחמן, singular), as in the current document. In נוסח אר״יזל the parallel insertion elsewhere in Shemoneh Esrei is Av HaRachamim (plural). The Tur's gematria argument turns on the leading א' being worth 1 either way, so the pesak is unaffected — but if different sources use different spellings, the current blanket use of “Av HaRachman” across all three paragraphs flattens the distinction. Flag for Agent 3 to verify which spelling the Arizal's שער הכוונות actually gives.
  • Mixed-direction punctuation. Several sentences end a Hebrew span mid-clause and then resume English with a hanging quotation mark on the wrong side — e.g. in Section V the construction “תפילות and תורה are supposed to ascend” survives as valid HTML but the typesetter will have to tune each one. The underlying issue is unicode-bidi: embed on the heb-inline class; for sefer type, consider switching to isolate so each Hebrew span is a sealed unit.

5.Citation and מראה מקומות Flags

fn1 (מגילה י״ז ע״ב). The body of the writeup cites “the גמרא in מגילה” for the structural placement of Shomea Tefillah after Tzemach David. The classical reference is in fact Megillah 17b–18a, and the primary derivation of this order is in the beraisa there. Confirm whether י״ז ע״ב is the exact starting daf or whether י״ח ע״א is the fuller citation. Flag for Agent 3.

fn2 (טור או״ח קי״ח). The 1,800-sum claim rests on summing the roshei teivos of the eighteen (nineteen?) ברכות. Two things to verify: (a) the Tur actually gives this count explicitly in סימן קי״ח (and not elsewhere); (b) the arithmetic itself holds for a standard Ashkenaz Shemoneh Esrei — whether the count presumes eighteen brachos (pre-bracha-la-minim) or nineteen. The 1,800 figure is load-bearing for the entire Av HaRachman argument; the number should be verified rather than taken on footnote. Flag for Agent 3.

fn3 (שער הכוונות). Shaar HaKavanos is cited for the Arizal's nusach. Specific drush or page is not given. For a sefer-grade citation this should be pinned (there are multiple places in Shaar HaKavanos where Shemoneh Esrei nusach is discussed).

fn5 (עבודה זרה ז״ ע״ב — ח״ ע״א). The body refers to “the גמרא in עבודה זרה ח'”; the footnote gives ז״ ע״ב — ח״ ע״א. Decide which to lead with in the body and keep the two consistent. The bepras-vs.-bikviyus distinction, which the Taz and Aruch HaShulchan both hinge on, should be pointed to a specific sugya line within the daf if possible.

fn7 and fn8 (ט״ז and משנה ברורה on סימן קי״ט). The Taz and the Mishnah Berurah here are cited to the whole siman. Pin the Taz to a specific s"k and the Mishnah Berurah to a specific s"k (likely Mishnah Berurah s"k 1 or s"k 2, with Shaar HaTziyun cross-referenced). The polemical temperature of the body paragraph warrants the exact citation.

fn10 (יעב״ץ / רבי יונה לנדסופר). Correctly flagged as academic-note, but the pairing of the Yavetz with Rabbi Yonah Landsofer and the Chida as if they are three parallel voices on the same point should be unpacked. The Yavetz's chiddush is the primary source; Rabbi Yonah Landsofer and the Chida are brought as corroboration. Clarify in the note which teaching is attributed to whom. Also: the Yavetz's dates (1697–1776) and the Prague-drashah framing should be checked — the Yavetz was רב of Emden/Altona, not Prague; the drashah may be Rabbi Yonah Landsofer's (he was from Prague). The current footnote may have fused two different mekoros. Flag urgently for Agent 3.

fn13 (ב״ח). The “two גדולים who died within the year” is a dramatic claim; the Bach's exact language and whether he names the two גדולים should be verified. The citation to “ב״ח על טור או״ח” is to the siman of Shemoneh Esrei structure (likely סימן קי״ח or קי״ח?) — pin it.

fn14 (זוהר הקדוש). Three separate Zohar teachings are bundled into one footnote (1,800 malachim, viduy specification, Rav Yeiva Saba). Each should get a specific daf or at least a parshah reference. The Rav Yeiva Saba maaseh in particular is a recurring Zohar motif (פרשת משפטים, etc.) and should be pointed cleanly. Flag for Agent 3.

fn15 (Rabbi Yaakov Yosef). The dates and biographical frame are specific and well-told, but two details should be sanity-checked: (a) the Satmar Rebbe quote (“every good מחשבה a יהודי has in New York…”) is attributed to oral מסורה; it would strengthen the note to point to any published source (Divrei Yoel? a Kuntres?). (b) Union Field Cemetery is the published burial location; the Rebbe said simply “Cypress Hills”. The specificity in the footnote is appreciated but should be confirmed.

fn16 (Brisker Rav). Correctly flagged as academic-note. The attribution of a one-line bakashah for a business meeting to the Brisker Rav is unusual — it is not the kind of ruling associated with the Brisker derech's classic published chiddushim. Confirm whether this is in Chiddushei HaGriz-circle oral mesorah or whether a published kuntres source exists. Flag for Agent 3.

fn18 (Rabbi Avraham Schorr). The parenthetical in the main body says “the Flatbush רב, with whom the Rebbe's father davened in his later years on the East Side”. The geography is unclear — Rabbi Avraham Schorr is the Flatbush rav; the Rebbe's father davened with him on the East Side? Either clarify (did they both daven at a specific East Side shul? did Rabbi Schorr speak at a hesped on the East Side?) or restructure the clause. Also: Rabbi Avraham Schorr shlit"a should be checked for current status so the honorific is right.

Rav Schwab citation (fn11). Iyun Tefillah / Rav Schwab on Prayer is the right source, but the exact page or chapter where this pshat appears should be pinned — it is a well-known passage and findable.

6.Small Polish Suggestions

  • The lead paragraph (הלעדה) contains the sentence “Two senses of “שמע” — the warm hearing and the cold”, which is excellent but prefigures Section V's pshat too strongly for a lead. Consider softening to “Two faces of שמע” or “Different kinds of שמע”, letting the paragraph itself reveal the chibbah/cold dichotomy.
  • Section I, second paragraph: “People do not always realize” is colloquial in a document that is otherwise formally voiced. Rewrite to “It is often overlooked that” or “A point sometimes missed”.
  • Section III ends on “preserving the ש' at the opening”; the ש glyph in the body sometimes carries a sin/shin dot and sometimes does not. Standardize.
  • Section V, closing sentence: ““שמע קולנו” is the תפילה for that very lift”. Clean, but the word “lift” trades on English informality that reads uneven next to the Baal Shem Tov story. Consider “that very ascent”.
  • Section VI, the second callout paragraph starts “The world says the ערוך השלחן is the opposite…”. This parenthetical about the ערוך השלחן's general stature is good context but structurally interrupts the polemic. Lift it into a footnote or a sidebar; the main-text flow should run polemic → printers quote → למעשה ruling without the biographical aside.
  • Section X: the two sub-headings “The Simple פשט” and “A Deeper פשט” are good, but Section XI opens with “Rav Schwab's פשט”, implying three co-ordinate pshatim. Decide: is Rav Schwab's reading the deeper pshat (in which case fold Section XI into Section X as a third sub-heading), or is it a separately-structured set-piece (in which case rename Section X's second sub-heading so the parallel is not misleading)?
  • Section XII, the callout opening “The Rebbe shares about his father ע״ה” — the English “shares about” is spoken-register. Rewrite: “Of his own father ע״ה, the Rebbe tells this”, or “On his father ע״ה, the Rebbe recalls…”.
  • Section XIV: the “bear licking honey” metaphor aside (already flagged in Section 3 above), the enumeration “Add a וידוי… Add a תפילה for פרנסה… Add for your שידוכים…” is strong closing scaffolding and should be preserved. Consider promoting it to a numbered or bulleted list for the sefer; the parallel structure deserves visual weight.
  • The footer repeats the title and summary. For sefer type-setting the footer can be dropped (print layouts handle running heads differently).

7.Opportunities to Expand for the Sefer

  • Open the “shema”-double-meaning more fully. Section V delivers the chibbah/cold contrast in two sentences; the sefer version should give it a full paragraph. The underlying claim — that “shema” can mean hear with love, hear to be rid of, or hear only after exhaustion — is the whole shiur's chiddush, and in the present draft it is delivered too compactly.
  • Give the Yavetz his own section. Currently Section VII does two things (introduces the Yavetz's chiddush; folds in the Brisker Rav and the Chida). The Yavetz deserves a free-standing section with the shidduch-crisis quote developed in his own voice (“we suffer greatly…”), and the Brisker/Chida material promoted to a separate “Practical Framework” section. The current compression serves time, not reading.
  • Section XIII (ב״ח on גשמים) deserves a contemporary hook. The Shiur Review (fn section 3) notes that the Rebbe mentioned, in the live shiur, a batei din letter from Eretz Yisrael instituting Aneinu during a recent drought. That contemporary application is exactly what makes the Bach's maaseh land — the batei din's letter is the right way to do what the two unnamed גדולים did wrong. Restore the hook.
  • Bracket the Rabbi Yaakov Yosef opening with a closing return. See Section 3 above. The Rebbe's point about Reb Yankev Yosef is implicitly that he kept coming to the door of a city that would not hear him — which is itself a lesson about Shema Koleinu. Make that explicit at the close.
  • Add the Bernie Nagelberg siddur vignette (from the Shiur Review) alongside the father-tears material in Section XII. Both are about yerushah through tefillah, and together they would do real work closing the bracha on the human side.

8.Bottom Line

A structurally strong writeup whose two movements — the Shmuel ha-Katan / ערוך השלחן arc and the Rav Schwab “ריקם אל תשיבנו” arc — hold together on the page as cleanly as they did in the room; the top editorial priorities are (1) close the Rabbi Yaakov Yosef bracket at Section XIV, (2) replace or explain the “bear licking honey” closing metaphor, (3) rewrite the Section IX voice-shift, (4) flatten the Section VI nested-italic rendering, and (5) have Agent 3 verify the Megillah daf, the Tur's 1,800 arithmetic, the Yavetz/Landsofer/Chida attribution tangle in fn10, and the two liturgical phrases (“בא נשלום”, “השיבנו בחיים”) in Section XII against Sefaria.
