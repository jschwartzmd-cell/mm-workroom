9 March 2025 — פורים Falling on ערב שבת קודש

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · 30 August 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

Delivered five days before Purim tashpeh, when the day fell on erev Shabbos for the first time in twenty-odd years, this is a self-consciously practical halacha l'maaseh shiur that pauses the running series on hilchos tefillah to address the moment. The Rav lays out two valid derachim: the simpler pesak Rema in siman 695 — wash and eat seudas Purim before chatzos — and the older, more dramatic minhag of the Maharil and the Meiri: “pores mapah u'mekadesh”, beginning the seudah in the late afternoon of Purim and continuing it straight into Shabbos with a cloth over the challos, a fresh cup of kiddush, and no repeated hamotzi. The structural issur from Gittin 38a and siman 249, the mechanics from the machlokes of Rebbi Yosi and Rebbi Yehuda in Pesachim 105, the bentshen question (retzeh and al hanissim as tarti d'sasri), and two vivid maaseh rav anchors — Rav Yitzchak Shor in Yerushalayim and the Imrei Emes in Gur — carry the argument that pores mapah is l'chatchilah, not b'dieved. The shiur closes with a Lawrence-specific timetable, latecomer and women's halachos, and the simpler Rema alternative for those who prefer it.

2.What Was Strongest

  • The dual-track structure. The Rav does not pretend there is only one right answer. He lays out the Rema's straightforward route and the Maharil/Meiri route as coequal derachim, gives the sources for each, and lets the listener choose by community and situation. That is the honest halachic picture, and the shiur models it cleanly.
  • The Rav Zalman Nechemiah Goldberg letter. An extraordinarily well-placed piece of testimony. The escalating rhetoric — “l'chatchilah mamash mamash l'chatchilah” — transmitted from Rav Yitzchak Shor (the chasan of the Alter of Slabodka) through RZN Goldberg (the chasan of RSZ Auerbach) does what no citation of the Yad Ephraim alone could do: it gives the pores mapah option a Lithuanian-Yerushalayim pedigree that neutralizes the natural suspicion that this is a chasidic or Sefardic minority practice.
  • The Imrei Emes tableau. The set-piece of Purim tish in Gur — kabbolas Shabbos recited at the tish, kiddush without hagefen or hamotzi, the meal continuing until 9 or 10 at night, bentshen with both retzeh and al hanissim — is the kind of image that anchors a piece of halacha in the talmid's memory in a way that no Mishnah Berurah footnote will.
  • The Maharil biography and the Worms detour. The excursus on who the Maharil was — Mainz, Worms, the chavas yair nearby, the Rema's citation-count, the Maharil as musician and baal menagen behind so much of our nusach — is not filler. It gives the reader the sensory feel for why a teshuvah of the Maharil carries the weight it does even where the Rema declined to follow him, and it justifies the whole second half of the shiur.
  • The Meiri's “ani v'rabbim acherim”. A well-chosen source. The Rav's observation that the Meiri is already reporting an established masorah a full century before the Maharil quietly demolishes the impression that pores mapah is a late chumra; it locates the minhag in twelfth-century Provence and gives it real depth of field.
  • The Lawrence timetable. Concrete chatzos, mincha gedolah, and shkiah times woven into the shiur turn abstract halacha into a practical checklist. This is what a halacha l'maaseh shiur is supposed to do.

3.What Was Weaker or Worth Pushing Back On

The Rema's silence is never explained. The Rav flags — correctly — that the Rema follows the Maharil more than any other single source, yet in this halacha he pointedly does not. That is a real puzzle, and it is dropped. Why did the Rema reject his own rebbi muvhak here? Was it discomfort with the kabbalistic reordering he flags in section XI? Concern that most baalei batim could not execute pores mapah properly? A sense that the analogy to bris and pidyon haben does not really hold because those seudos are reshus while Purim's is arguably chovah? Any of these would deepen the shiur; without one, the reader is left with a curiosity rather than an understanding.

The analogy to bris and pidyon haben is asserted, not defended. The Maharil's kal v'chomer is the load-bearing move of his entire teshuvah, and the shiur presents it as self-evident. But seudas bris and seudas pidyon are widely considered seudos reshus that acquire chashivus only by association with the mitzvah; seudas Purim is a full chiyuv in its own right. That asymmetry could cut either direction — strengthening the Maharil (all the more so a chiyuv) or weakening him (the Rema's reticence). The shiur uses the analogy without examining it.

The kabbalistic caveat in section XI is buried. The mekubalim's concern — that pores mapah reverses the mochin order of entering Shabbos — is presented as a footnote after the enthusiastic maaseh rav from the Imrei Emes. But the Imrei Emes is himself a gadol b'kabbalah. If a rebbe steeped in Chassidus and its kabbalistic infrastructure ran his tish that way anyway, that is itself an argument, and it belongs alongside the concern rather than after it. The two datapoints should be brought into conversation.

The choice between the two derachim is under-guided. The Rav says “choose by community and family situation,” but a listener genuinely on the fence gets little help. Who really should default to which? A young family with small children? A single bochur? A shul with a rav who has no fixed mesorah? A concluding paragraph of practical guidance — not pesak, but a framework — would have made the shiur substantially more useful.

4.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The issur in siman 249 has two independent ta'amim, and only one is really at stake. The Rav brings both: kavod Shabbos (the tei'avon reason of the Gemara in Gittin) and the practical hachanos reason from Rabbeinu Tam in the Magen Avraham. When one continues seamlessly into the Shabbos seudah itself by pores mapah, the tei'avon concern arguably evaporates — there is no separate Shabbos meal being displaced — while the hachanos concern remains fully live (which is why section XII's warning to shut the phone and clear the muktzeh before bentshen is doing structural, not decorative, work). The shiur could have named this dynamic explicitly.

The status of kabbalas Shabbos mid-seudah deserves a fuller treatment. The shiur allows either the home kabbalas Shabbos around the table or a walk to shul, and cites the Mishnah Berurah that the walk is not a hefsek because of daas to return. But the deeper chakirah is whether the seudah retains its Purim identity across kabbalas Shabbos or becomes something new. If the former, the hagefen and hamotzi from earlier really do carry — but then why is a fresh kiddush needed at all? If the latter, why not new berachos on the wine and bread? The pores mapah mechanism finesses this rather than resolving it.

The lechem mishneh question is the sharpest lomdish point in the shiur and is not developed. The Rav notes the machlokes and rules l'chumra: bring an extra challah. But why is there a machlokes at all? It turns on exactly the question above: is the second half of the meal a seudas Shabbos in its own right (which would obligate lechem mishneh) or a continuation with a kiddush-marker (which would not)? Naming the chakirah would have oriented the listener to what is actually at stake.

The bentshen compromise via harachaman has a subtle halachic shape. The Mishnah Berurah's solution — retzeh in its place, al hanissim only in the harachaman — is treated as a diplomatic middle path. But it also encodes a real pesak: the ikkar chiyuv of the seudah is Shabbos at the moment of bentshen, and the Purim-recognition is being demoted to a reshus mention within the harachaman. The Meiri's position that both are said in their standard places is a much stronger structural claim — that the seudah retains its dual identity through its close. Which position one adopts is inseparable from the chakirah above.

5.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.Why did the Rema, who follows the Maharil more than any other source, decline to follow him here? Is there a Rema in Darkei Moshe, an acharon, or a mesorah among his own talmidim that gives a reason — whether the kabbalistic mochin-concern of section XI, the difficulty of executing pores mapah at the baal habayis level, or a substantive rejection of the analogy to bris and pidyon haben?
Q2.How does the chakirah on the status of the second half of the meal — a continuation of the seudas Purim with a kiddush-marker vs. a new seudas Shabbos beginning mid-meal — resolve? The lechem mishneh machlokes, the berachos for a latecomer, and the retzeh/al hanissim question all seem to hinge on it, but the shiur treats each as its own sugya.
Q3.The Imrei Emes ran his tish with both retzeh and al hanissim at bentshen, following the Meiri. Given that the Mishnah Berurah's harachaman compromise is more widely followed today, is there guidance for someone at a Litvish seudah who is himself machmir like the Meiri — does he say both anyway, or defer to the tzibbur?
Q4.The shiur closes with a practical halacha that all mitzvos Purim — mishloach manos, matanos l'evyonim — must be completed before shkiah. But if the Meiri holds that erev Shabbos retains a kedushas Purim such that al hanissim is still said, would he also hold that these mitzvos can still be discharged after shkiah within the continuing seudah? Or is his position specifically limited to the bentshen question?
Q5.For a family in the standard American situation — children, chagigah at the shul in the early afternoon, no established family mesorah — which of the two derachim would the Rav actually recommend as the default? And does that recommendation change for a young couple without children, for a ben Torah in kollel, or for someone who will be joining a Chassidish tish?
Q6.The kabbalistic concern in section XI — that pores mapah reverses the mochin-order — is a serious Arizal position, yet the Imrei Emes proceeded anyway. Are there kabbalistic tzidukim for pores mapah on Purim specifically — perhaps that the kedushas Purim itself supplies the mochin ascent that kabbalas Shabbos normally does — or is the Imrei Emes simply overriding the kabbalistic concern with a halachic-chasidish priority?

6.Bottom Line

A model halacha l'maaseh shiur for a rare-calendar moment: two derachim laid side by side, real sources for each, and two maaseh rav anchors that convert what would otherwise feel like an exotic chumra into a live l'chatchilah option. The Meiri's twelfth-century witness and the RZN Goldberg letter are the shiur's most valuable pieces of source-work; the Lawrence timetable is its most valuable piece of pastoral work. What the shiur does not do is press on the fundamental chakirah that lurks beneath every mechanic — whether the second half of the meal is a continuation with a kiddush-marker or a new seudas Shabbos beginning mid-course — and once that question is named, several of the shiur's isolated puzzles (the lechem mishneh machlokes, the retzeh/al hanissim tension, the latecomer ruling, even the Rema's silence) start to look like facets of a single deeper sugya. The shiur equips the listener to execute either derech with confidence; the next pass would equip him to understand why the whole structure hangs together as it does.
