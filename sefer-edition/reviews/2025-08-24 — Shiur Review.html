Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

24 August 2025 — בִּרְכַת מוֹדִים and נְשִׂיאַת כַּפַּיִם

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · 5 October 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

A two-part shiur on the chasimah of Shemoneh Esrei. The first half picks up Birkat Modim: the Yerushalmi on the Amora's body bowing me'eilav; the Chofetz Chaim's pause at Modim because he cannot get enough of thanking HKB"H; the Torat Chaim's kavvanah of thanking for every breath; the Midrash that all korbanos and tefillos will be batel la'asid lavo except Toda and Modim; the acronym Chayim for the four who bring a Korban Toda; Modim de-Rabbanan as Rav Papa's “nimrinhu le-tarvaihu”; the unforgettable Rav Elyashiv-at-102 story about learning to say “thank you” in English; and the halachic mistake of the shaliach tzibbur saying Modim silently. The second half is the long pele of Nesias Kapayim in chutz la-aretz: the Rambam/Chinuch/ra'ayos for daily duchening; the Rama's parnassah-and-simcha ta'am; the failed attempts of the Gra (jail), Reb Chaim Volozhin (the fire that burned half the town), and the Ba'al Ha-Tanya; Rav Nosson HaKohen Adler in Frankfurt as the only success; the chezkas kehunah-is-lost shitah (grounded in the “Pinsk kohen” joke, Djerba, and the post-9/11 DNA teshuvah); and the Galil regional variation (Tzfas, Tiberias, Haifa; Kfar Hasidim as the exception; Rav Kook in Haifa). The shiur ran out of time before “Look-in Look-out”, to be continued the following week.

2.Overall Fidelity

The write-up tracks the transcript closely and preserves the shiur's two zingers — the Rav Elyashiv English “thank you” story and the three failed attempts to restore daily duchening — with the right proportions. The architectural reshuffle into twelve numbered sections is a real improvement on the live flow, which doubled back several times (the chezkas kehunah discussion in particular). Hebrew terms are consistently preserved in the Hebrew alphabet; the mareh mekomos apparatus is substantial. The content gaps and misattributions below are localized, not systemic.

3.Content Flags — Misattributions and Overclaims

Flag 1 — The “Shaalos Teshuvos Margolios” on chezkas kehunah is attributed to the wrong Margolios. Footnote 13 and footnote 14 identify the author as Rabbi Reuven Margoliot (1889–1971, Lvov/Jerusalem; author of Sha'arei Zohar). The transcript twice says the author is Ephraim Zalman Margolios (“the Rav Fra[nk] [and] Zalim Margolia”; “Ephraim Zalim Margolius, one of the great poiskim, has a tshuva called 'Shals Tshuva' based on Ephraim”). This is R' Efraim Zalman Margolios of Brody (1760–1828) — the same figure whose chiddush on Atah Chonen and Motzai Shabbos opened the 18 May 2025 shiur. The two are different mechabrim of different centuries. The misattribution should be corrected in both footnotes, and the body line “Rav Frank and Rabbi Margoliot” re-written accordingly.

Flag 2 — The alternate reading of “al she-anu modim lach” may be mis-cited. The write-up (and the summary) attribute the reading “in addition to what we thanked You in tefillas lachash” to the Ran in a teshuvah (fn 5). The transcript says: “I saw one of the rishonim says I think to read megash in a tshuva.” “Read megash” reads much more naturally as Ri Migash (R' Yosef ibn Migash) than as the Ran. Both wrote she'eilos u-teshuvos, and the two are easy to confuse in an audio transcription. Worth verifying the source directly before the sefer locks the Ran in print; if the shiur in fact said Ri Migash, the attribution should be corrected.

Flag 3 — Confident Mishnah Tamid citation launders a live hedge. Footnote 8 confidently cites “Mishnah Tamid, Perek 5 Mishnah 1; Mishnah Tamid, Perek 7” for daily Nesias Kapayim in the Mikdash, including the “bnei ma'amados” standing in the azarah. The transcript is explicitly uncertain: “In the mishnais, it seems like nesius kapayim did take place in the bei's hamikdash. In the bei's hamikdash they said nesius kapayim. I don't know where exactly. I don't remember if it was Azor or whatever that a platform. I don't remember exactly which part of the base of English, but yeah, I am not sure.” The citation to Tamid 5:1 and 7:2 happens to be correct me'ikkar ha-din, but the write-up has invented a certainty the shiur did not carry. Either keep the citation and drop any pretense that the shiur named it, or (better, for faithfulness) retain a footnote-level “cf. Mishnah Tamid 5:1 and 7:2” and leave the body text in the Rebbe's hedged voice.

Flag 4 — The pasuk for Nesias Kapayim as avodah is given confidently where the shiur hedged. Section VII's halachic ground — “Vayedaber Hashem el Moshe v'el Aharon... va-yevarech es ha-am” — is quoted as if settled. The transcript: “By Yitzu, I have to look at that pasuk when it says by Yitzu.” The Rebbe flagged the pasuk as something to check (he is reaching for Vayikra 9:22, “va-yisa Aharon es yadav el ha-am va-yevarchem”) and did not finish the reach. The write-up quietly fills in the citation without noting the live uncertainty.

Flag 5 — The Avudraham hedge is dropped. On the question of why the tzibbur says its own Modim and does not rely on the shaliach tzibbur, the transcript records: “I think it comes from the Avudraham, although I am not sure. I think the Avudraham says something different.” The write-up presents the “one cannot thank through a shaliach” principle as a settled teaching with no attribution issue. The Avudraham question deserves either a cleaner source-location (if it was tracked down between recording and write-up) or a parenthetical acknowledging that the mekor is unverified.

4.Content Flags — Omissions

  • The Mishnah Berurah on “mi she-eino yachol b'yad, ya'aseh b'pe”. On the Sefardi daily-duchening practice, the transcript has: “The Mishna Berurah says if you can't do it with your hands, you shouldn't do it with your mouth.” (The Rebbe is likely referring to MB 128:150-­ish on duchening vs. birkas kohanim b'peh.) The write-up omits this entirely from Section VIII. It is a load-bearing bridge in the shiur's argument.
  • The Chayim acronym's live confusion is cleaned up without acknowledgement. The Rebbe worked through the acronym live and visibly fumbled: “Which one is the jail? Maybe I forgot”; and he gave the second yud only as “released from prison or great difficulty” without naming yissurim. The write-up smooths this into a clean four-row list in Section IV, with “yissurim” supplied in the footnote. Fine for a sefer; worth noting here only because it is the pattern — the write-up systematically launders hedges.
  • The “Beit Yosef's praise for the Egyptians and bnei Eretz Yisrael” is thinner than the transcript. The Rebbe explicitly notes that in Egypt — “Sfardim that lived in Mitzrayim in the times of Yosef, about four hundred and fifty, five hundred years ago, they did do birkas kohanim every single day” — grounding the Beit Yosef's approval in a specific continuous Sephardic practice. The write-up (Section VIII) nods at the Beit Yosef but drops the Egypt detail.
  • The chaburah-interaction moments are flattened. Several of the shiur's most useful ta'anos come from questions in the room — the Mike Huckabee video, the “Jewish people in Eretz Yisrael also have parnassah worries” pushback, the Yud Yitzchak-Yoisef question on why we have a parallel nusach at all. The write-up preserves the content but strips the dialogic frame, so the reader loses the sense that the Rebbe is working these out live rather than delivering a prepared text. That is a stylistic choice, but for a shiur whose charm is partly its back-and-forth, worth naming.
  • The upcoming trip to Pressburg is used as context but not identified as time-sensitive. The write-up (Section IX, Rav Nosson HaKohen Adler callout) opens “We are traveling in two weeks to the Chasam Sofer in Pressburg.” That is the Rebbe's live voice from August 2025 preserved in the first person. For the sefer, this will date badly. Either remove the frame or re-cast it as a reported detail.

5.Added or Editorial Material Not in the Shiur

Section XII (“Closing”) is a composed synthesis, not transcript. The transcript ends with the Rebbe noting the shiur ran long and that “Look-in Look-out” will have to wait for next week. There is no closing devar hisorerus. The write-up supplies one: “Tefillah moves through structures we did not invent and cannot dismantle, and through mitzvos we did not impose and cannot easily restore...” — a lovely paragraph that genuinely captures the shiur's underlying arc, but it is the editor's voice, not the Rebbe's. Flag it either as an editor's coda or as a reconstruction, so a careful reader knows the register has shifted.

The “Hirsch Museum in Frankfurt” parenthetical is added. The transcript does not mention the Hirsch Museum in connection with Rav Nosson HaKohen Adler; the write-up adds it as background. Small; worth checking.

Footnote 15's framing of the 9/11 teshuvah is tighter than the transcript. The transcript says the teshuvah is “four hundred pages”, “not printed”, and names “Rebbe V'ramel Branberg” (Rav Shmuel Fuerst?) and “Rebbe Baravach Feigel” (Rav Avrohom Felder?). The footnote fills in standard biographical identifiers (Chicago Rabbinical Council, close talmid of Rav Moshe Feinstein, etc.). Those identifiers are not from the transcript; they are editorial research. Fine for a sefer, but they should be verified rather than assumed.

6.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The shiur's own bein ha-shitin on chezkas kehunah is halachically soft. The “Pinsk kohen” story and the Lakewood pidyon ha-ben anecdote are vivid, but neither is a halachic argument against the chezkah — both are stories of individual fraud or error that would be addressed by case-by-case verification, not by a blanket riyusa on the general chezkas kehunah. The real shitah (whether Rav Efraim Zalman Margolios, as per the transcript, or Reuven Margolios, as per the write-up) is a structural claim about the mesorah, not an aggregation of anecdotes. The write-up could help by distinguishing the shitah's structural argument from the ma'asios that illustrate it, so the reader does not take the Pinsk joke as the ra'ayah.

The Modim de-Rabbanan-is-named-for-the-combination explanation is one shitah, not the shitah. The write-up presents it as the answer; many rishonim (and the Avudraham the Rebbe himself gestured at) read “de-Rabbanan” as marking the obligation on the yachid as a rabbanan-level institution parallel to the takanah of the shaliach tzibbur's Modim. Worth a footnote-level acknowledgement that other explanations exist.

The Rama's parnassah-and-simcha ta'am is explicitly described as a post hoc rationalization — by the write-up itself. The sefer should decide whether to present the Rama's ta'am as the operative halachic reasoning or as the obscure minhag's best-available defense. Right now the write-up does both in adjacent paragraphs (fn 9: “post hoc rationalization of an older minhag whose true origin is obscure”) which reads as having it both ways. The Rebbe himself was clear in the shiur that the ta'am is weak; the write-up should land there with him.

The Mishnah on bnei ma'amados and birkas kohanim is actually a stronger halachic ground for Nesias Kapayim as avodah than the write-up credits. Properly cited (Tamid 7:2), it would do more work than the “like the avodah in the Beis HaMikdash” language the write-up currently uses — it would show Nesias Kapayim as structurally part of the seder ha-yom of the Mikdash, not as an analogy to it. The halachic consequences for shikur, kavvanah, and kedushah flow more cleanly from that.

7.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.Which Margolios? If the teshuvah on lost chezkas kehunah is Rav Efraim Zalman Margolios of Brody (as the transcript indicates), that is a late-18th-century source and settles the shitah's halachic weight differently than if it is Rav Reuven Margolios of the 20th century. Can the Rav confirm which sefer and which teshuvah?
Q2.Which Rishon on “al she-anu modim lach”? Is the teshuvah the Ran's or the Ri Migash's? And does the Rav hold that the two readings — “thanks for the ability to thank” vs. “in addition to what we said in lachash” — are mutually exclusive, or do they combine?
Q3.On the Modim-is-eternal Midrash: given that the Rambam's pshat is that korbanos will return with Mashiach, how does the Rav sequence the Midrash's “later moment” with the Rambam's yemos ha-Mashiach? Is this a yemos ha-Mashiach / olam ha-ba distinction, or is the Rebbe reading the Midrash as a mahalach entirely separate from the Rambam's halachic frame?
Q4.On the three failed attempts: is the pattern (prison, fire, the Ba'al Ha-Tanya's obscure bilbul) itself halachically operative — a kind of “bas kol”-level pesak — or is each ma'aseh standalone and the Aruch Ha-Shulchan's “ka'ilu bas kol yatzta” language is derush, not halachah? What is the halachic status of a minhag that resists restoration b'siyata di-shmaya?
Q5.On the Galil and Rav Kook: the Rebbe reports that Rav Kook duchened as an individual kohen in Haifa but did not try to reform the local minhag. Does the Rav hold that an individual kohen visiting a chutz-la'aretz-style Galil shul today is chayav to duchen (as Rav Kook did), permitted, or constrained by “lo tis'godedu”?
Q6.On the “al nishmoseinu ha-pekudos lach” kavvanah of “every breath”: is this the peshat kavvanah, the mekubbalim's kavvanah, or an hosafah of the Torat Chaim on top of the peshat? If the yachid cannot sustain it, does yetzias ha-chiyuv depend on the attempt or on the completion?

8.Bottom Line

A faithful and well-structured write-up of a two-part shiur whose two headline moments — Rav Elyashiv learning English “thank you” at 102, and the three failed attempts of the Gra, Reb Chaim Volozhin, and the Ba'al Ha-Tanya to restore daily Nesias Kapayim — land with full force. The flags cluster in one area: the write-up is too confident where the shiur was tentative. The Margolios misattribution (Flag 1) is the one error that should not go to print uncorrected; the Ran/Ri Migash question (Flag 2) is the next most important to verify. The Mishnah Tamid and pasuk-for-avodah citations (Flags 3–4) should either be left in the Rebbe's hedged voice or clearly marked as editorial fills. For the sefer, resolve these five flags, restore the Mishnah Berurah and Avudraham hedges, and re-cast the composed “Closing” as an acknowledged editorial coda. The lomdus and the ma'asios are in excellent shape.
