Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

Mitzvat Yishuv Eretz Yisrael

Shiur of 26 May 2025 — Yom Yerushalayim (כ"ח אייר תשפ"ה)
Content review · reviewer read transcript, summary, and finished HTML · 5 October 2026

I. The Shiur in One Paragraph

This is a Yom Yerushalayim shiur framed as a halachic (not ideological) survey of מצות ישוב ארץ ישראל. The Rebbi opens with a methodological disclaimer — "I grew up in Brisk and in Satmar, today is not for ideology" — then anchors the sugya in the pasuk of והורשתם את הארץ וישבתם בה (במדבר ל"ג:נ"ג) and the classic machloikes between Rashi (havtachah) and the Ramban (mitzvah gemurah). He develops the Ramban's position at length — the two chalakim of כיבוש and ישיבה, the Ramban's biography (Girona, Barcelona disputation, Pope Clement IV, flight to עכו 1267, the ראש השנה derashah of 1269), and the aggadic/mystical material on כל הדר בחוץ לארץ כמי שאין לו אלוקה and ישיבת ארץ ישראל שקולה כנגד כל המצוות. He then addresses the Rambam's omission (Megillat Esther, rejected; the "umbrella mitzvah" teretz; the Rambam's own aliyah letter from Sefer Charedim), the opposing shitah of Rabbeinu Chaim HaKohen in Tosafos Kesubos (with the forgery argument of the Mahari"t/Mabi"t/Rashbash/Rashbatz), the Terumas HaDeshen's historical teshuvah, and finally sweeps forward to Rav Kook's letter to the Ridbaz, Rav Moshe's מצוה קיומית formulation, and the Tzitz Eliezer's post-1948 reversal. The Rebbi explicitly cuts the shiur short — "we have to end early, I have a lot more material, we'll have to continue… maybe Shavuos night" — leaving Vayoel Moshe and the שלש שבועות promised but undelivered.

II. What Was Strongest

The spine of the shiur — the Rashi/Ramban machloikes and the Ramban's biographical "action-not-just-torah" treatment — is handled with real warmth and genuine learning. The decision to interleave the Ramban's halachic shitah with his personal mesirus nefesh (leaving wife and children in Spain, the "לשבת בחיק אמי" line from the דרשת ראש השנה) is the right pedagogical move for a Yom Yerushalayim audience and lands cleanly.

Second strongest: the handling of the Rambam's omission. The Rebbi correctly identifies the Megillat Esther as the one shitah that reads the Rambam at face value, correctly identifies the overwhelming rejection of that reading by the Acharonim (with Rav Ovadia's "בחיוב בשתי ידיים" quoted sharply), and offers the standard mehalech — that the Rambam does not separately count umbrella mitzvos — as the mainstream resolution. The appeal to the Rambam's own aliyah letter (via ספר חרדים) as a "ma'aseh rav" ending the discussion is rhetorically effective, even if historically contested (see §IV).

Third: the forgery argument on Tosafos / Rabbeinu Chaim HaKohen. Flagging that the ויואל משה's central Rishon-source is a text that four major Sefardic Acharonim held to be a talmid toeh's interpolation is the kind of lomdish honesty the Rebbi promised in his opening disclaimer, and he delivers on it.

III. Fidelity to the Transcript — What Was Smoothed, Cut, or Added

The shiur is unfinished; the HTML presents it as finished. The transcript is explicit at 00:45:50 onward: "I am sorry, we have to end early because I do have a lot more material. We'll have to continue this at a different time… maybe Shavuos night. There is so much to say." The Rebbi names three sugyos he never got to: (a) the full Rav Kook / Ridbaz exchange around היתר מכירה ("we'll talk about also, but we're not going to have time"), (b) the שלש שבועות ("we'll talk about the shalosh shavu'os. Maybe soon if we have time, which we don't"), and (c) a more developed treatment of Vayoel Moshe. The HTML absorbs the delivered material into a self-contained document, but a reader of the Sefer Edition will have no idea the Rebbi considered the shiur a part-one. Either (i) label this volume "Part I — Rishonim and the Machloikes" with a clear note that Part II (Shalosh Shvuos, Heter Mechira, Vayoel Moshe in full) was delivered on a different occasion and belongs elsewhere, or (ii) build Part II from the shiur the Rebbi almost certainly gave later and merge.
The Rebbi's personal eulogy for his father is in the transcript; the HTML suppresses it. At 00:35:36 — in the middle of the Tanoim-weeping-at-the-border story — the Rebbi breaks into a very raw personal passage: "I did a little bit the other week when I went there to bury my father in Karpas Mishkaboy… I come to the beis hachaim and I bury him, and I sit shiva two hours in my brother's apartment… my heart was ripped to shreds. I wasn't there for Yisrael for 15 years." This is removed entirely from the HTML. For a Sefer Edition this is a judgment call — but it is the most authentic moment in the whole shiur and the strongest personal illustration of כל הדר בחוץ לארץ כמי שאין לו אלוקה. Suggest restoring it, in a footnote or set-off italic, with the Rebbi's permission.
Two historical/political asides in the transcript are flattened in the HTML. (a) The Girgashi aside originally reads as an endorsement of the "Trump plan" to relocate Gazans (00:08:47: "there is such an idea precedent of taking a group of people… and putting them elsewhere because this is our land"). The HTML preserves the Girgashi mahaleich but strips the Trump framing — probably correct for a sefer, but the omission should be a conscious editorial decision, not an accident. (b) The transcript's extended riff on the תשובה-movement vignette about Rav Grossman / Migdal HaEmek (00:41:32) is compressed to one line in the HTML. Fine for brevity, but note that the Rebbi used it as the emotional climax of the Rav Kook section.

IV. Mareh Mekomos Accuracy, Mislabeled or Misattributed Sources

Megillat Esther on Sefer HaMitzvos — author misidentified. The HTML (and the transcript) attribute מגילת אסתר to "an Italian scholar named Moshe d'Leone (fifteenth century)." This is wrong on two counts. The author of מגילת אסתר on the Rambam's ספר המצוות is Rabbi Yitzchak de Leon ibn Tzur, a 16th-century scholar (sefer first printed Venice 1592). The transcript's "Moshe d'Leone" is almost certainly a confusion with Rabbi Moshe de Leon of 13th-century Spain, the compiler/transmitter of the Zohar — a completely different person, different century, different work. The Sefaria verifier agent must correct this; the Book Rewriter must not propagate it. Flag for the Rebbi.
Rambam's source for compelling aliyah — Hilchos Melachim is likely wrong. The HTML footnote 8 (per the מראה מקומות block the summary preserved) lists "Rambam, Mishneh Torah, Hilchos Melachim perek 5" for the halachah of a husband/wife compelling aliyah. The actual Rambam is Hilchos Ishus 13:19-20 (Hilchos Melachim 5:9-12 discusses leaving Eretz Yisrael and reward for dwelling there, not kefiyah on a spouse). Both references are relevant to the sugya, but the specific halachah of kefiyah that the Rebbi cites is in Ishus, not Melachim.
Mezuzah source. The transcript says "there is a gemara in Menachos." Correct citation: Menachos 44a (sh'eil for 30 days in chu"l / immediately in Eretz Yisrael). The HTML's generic "Menachos" footnote should be nailed down.
"כל הדר בחוץ לארץ כמי שאין לו אלוקה." Correctly placed at "the end of Kesubos" in the shiur. The precise daf is Kesubos 110b. Same page carries the Mishnah of hakol ma'alin l'Eretz Yisrael that the Rebbi uses for kefiyah. The Rebbi conflates them a bit (both are "end of Kesubos" but they are separate sugyos on the same amud); the Sefer Edition should cite them separately.
The Rambam's own aliyah letter. The Rebbi cites it via ספר חרדים of R' Elazar Azkari (Tzfas, early 1600s), and treats it as decisive. This is standard yeshivish usage, but a reviewer should flag two things: (i) the Igros HaRambam critical editions (Shilat; Blau) treat the ג' סיון letter as authentic Rambam, but (ii) the Rebbi's claim that the Rambam "moved to Eretz Yisrael" overstates — the letter records a visit (he landed in עכו on ג' סיון, davened at the Makom HaMikdash, then continued to Alexandria and settled in Fustat). The Rambam did not live out his life in Eretz Yisrael; he died in Mitzrayim. The Sefer Edition should soften "moved to" to "made aliyah to visit / set foot in," which still carries the raya but is historically accurate.
"רבינו חיים הכהן, student of Rabbeinu Tam, 1100s." Correct — Tosafos Kesubos 110b s.v. hu omer. The forgery argument is attributed to "Maharit, Mabit, Rashbash, Rashbatz" generically. The classical mekor is Maharit (Rav Yosef Trani), Teshuvos Chelek 2, YD 28, and Mabit (Rav Moshe miTrani), Kiryas Sefer, Hilchos Melachim perek 5. The Rashbash/Rashbatz citations are less famous and the reviewer should verify before the Sefer Edition prints the string of four names as if they are all explicit.
"ריד"ז" vs. "רידב"ז" — inconsistent spelling in the HTML. The HTML sometimes writes ריד"ז and sometimes רידב"ז. The correct acronym for Rav Yaakov Dovid Willowsky is רידב"ז (Rabbi Yaakov Dovid ben Zev). The Rebbi correctly distinguishes him from the רדב"ז (Radbaz, R' Dovid ben Zimra, Egypt). Standardize throughout.

V. Missing Shitos and Sugya Gaps

Shalosh Shvuos (Kesubos 111a) — the elephant. This is the gaping omission. The Rebbi admits he intended to address it ("we'll talk about the shalosh shavu'os. Maybe soon if we have time, which we don't") but did not. For a halachic survey of מצות ישוב ארץ ישראל, the שלש שבועות sugya is non-negotiable — it is the entire basis of the Satmar/Vayoel Moshe position (not Rabbeinu Chaim HaKohen, which is secondary), and the Avnei Nezer's famous teshuvah (YD 454) is built on resolving it. Without this sugya, the Ramban-vs.-Vayoel Moshe debate presented in the shiur is lopsided — the Rebbi lets the forgery argument dispose of the Satmar position far too easily. The Sefer Edition must add a chapter on the Shalosh Shvuos (the three oaths; the gemara in Kesubos 110b-111a; the Ramban's treatment in his Hashmatos; the Avnei Nezer; the Rav Teichtal reversal in Eim HaBanim Semeichah; and the mainstream post-1948 teshuvos that read the oaths as either batel or already fulfilled).
Rishonim and early Acharonim missing from the survey. The Rebbi gives Ramban, Rashi, Rabbeinu Chaim HaKohen, and Terumas HaDeshen. Missing (and expected in a serious survey):
  • The Kuzari (Ma'amar 2 and especially the end of Ma'amar 5) — the Rihal's famous rebuke of Diaspora complacency and his own aliyah. For a Yom Yerushalayim shiur this is a glaring absence.
  • Megillas Taanis Basra / Sefer Charedim's own enumeration — R' Elazar Azkari (whom the Rebbi quotes for the Rambam's letter) has his own ruling on מצות ישוב ארץ ישראל in Sefer Charedim mitzvah 1, perek 15 — a direct psak that should be cited.
  • Chasam Sofer (Yoreh De'ah 234, and the famous letters) — a central 19th-century mekor that bridges to the modern period.
  • Pischei Teshuva, Even HaEzer 75 — the "today the aveirah of leaving outweighs the mitzvah of coming" controversy.
  • Minchas Chinuch mitzvah 425 — explicit sefer hamitzvos-style treatment of the Ramban's addition.
  • Avnei Nezer, Yoreh De'ah 454-456 — the single most important Acharon on the sugya; the Rebbi does not mention him once.
  • Rav Teichtal, Eim HaBanim Semeichah — the Munkatcher-trained posek who reversed himself and wrote the most powerful 20th-century defense of aliyah from inside the Hungarian/Chassidish world. For a Rebbi who opens with "I grew up in Satmar," omitting Rav Teichtal is a real asymmetry — Vayoel Moshe is cited, Eim HaBanim is not.
  • Rav Herzog, Rav Charlap, Rav Yisraeli (religious-Zionist posek strand) — if Tzitz Eliezer is given, these three deserve at least a mention.
  • Chazon Ish — his view (quoted in the name of his talmidim) that there is a mitzvah of ha'achzakah but not of aliyah per se is relevant to the Rav Moshe קיומית/חיובית dichotomy.
  • Rav Soloveitchik's Kol Dodi Dofek — the Rebbi names the Rav as a question ("why didn't he go?") but never gives the Rav's own position. For a shul that likely has YU-trained ba'alei batim listening, this is a felt gap.
The two-chalakim framework needs a third chelek — ירושת הארץ. The Rebbi divides the mitzvah into כיבוש and ישיבה. The Ramban in Sefer HaMitzvos (Mitzvas Ase 4 that was added) actually frames it as שלא נעזבנה ביד זולתנו מן האומות או לשממה — a negative component (not abandoning the land to non-Jews or desolation) which is a third dimension and the operative component in the Avnei Nezer's and Tzitz Eliezer's modern application. Adding this chelek sharpens the lomdus of why "buying an apartment you only use three times a year" (the Rebbi's closing suggestion) is a maaseh mitzvah and not just a hiddur.
The Rav Moshe קיומית/חיובית framing deserves a mekor citation. The Rebbi presents Rav Moshe's position without a tshuvah number. The source is Igros Moshe, Even HaEzer Chelek 1, siman 102 (and the broader discussion in EH 1:102, EH 4:49). The Sefer Edition cannot leave this as "Rav Moshe said" — the whole chiddush of מצוה קיומית on this sugya is attributed to Rav Moshe and the tshuvah must be pinpointed and quoted inside, not just footnoted.

VI. Structural / Organizational Issues

The disclaimer and the delivery are in tension. The Rebbi opens with "today is not for ideology, today is halachah" and then — correctly and inevitably — proceeds to deliver a shiur that is saturated with hashkafah. The soldiers in Gaza as mekaymei והורשתם, the Rav Kook "Zionist as a badge of honor" peroration, the Trump-plan precedent, the swipes at Satmar ("you are going Satmar on me"), the dig at the shul for not saying Hallel — these are hashkafah positions, strong ones, delivered as halachah. For the Sefer Edition this is fine, but the opening disclaimer needs to be rewritten to acknowledge the overlap honestly instead of denying it. Alternatively, keep the hashkafah passages but tag them explicitly as hashkafah so the halachic spine stands clean.
The order jumps. The transcript's order is: Rashi/Ramban → Rambam → Ramban biography → Rambam again → Rabbeinu Chaim HaKohen → Terumas HaDeshen → "three shittos" summary → Ramban aggadic material (Sifri, "שקולה כנגד") → Rav Kook → Rav Moshe → Tzitz Eliezer → alternatives (buying an apartment, financial support). The HTML reorders this into a cleaner "Rishonim → Rambam → opposing Rishonim → Acharonim → modern" sequence, which is correct. But the HTML then drops the Rebbi's own "three shittos" summary (Ramban d'oraisa / Rabbeinu Chaim no / Terumas HaDeshen contextual) that the Rebbi used as his pivot. Restoring that explicit three-way frame would help the reader navigate.
The Rambam section is split across two parts of the HTML (§V and §VIII). This mirrors the transcript (the Rebbi came back to the Rambam twice), but on the page it reads as repetition. Consolidate into a single sustained treatment of the Rambam, with the Megillat Esther / umbrella-mitzvah / aliyah-letter all under one roof.
The closing is weak because the shiur ran out of time. The Rebbi's actual closing — "buy an apartment, support Yidden in Eretz Yisrael, ופדויי ה' ישובון" — is pastoral but not the halachic maskana the shiur promised. The Sefer Edition needs a real conclusion that returns to the opening question (is it a חיוב or a קיום, mi-de'oraisa or mi-derabanan today, and what practically follows), ideally keyed off a nafka minah list.

VII. Follow-Up Questions Worth Asking the Rebbi

1 The שלש שבועות sugya in Kesubos 111a was promised and not delivered. Will the Sefer Edition pull it in from a later shiur, or does the Rebbi want to write it up fresh? Specifically: how does the Rebbi learn the Avnei Nezer's resolution (that the oaths were conditional and were violated first by the umos ha'olam, dissolving them)? And where does the Rebbi stand on Rav Teichtal's reversal in Eim HaBanim Semeichah?
2 The Megillat Esther attribution. The transcript says "Moshe d'Leone, fifteenth century, Italian." The actual author of מגילת אסתר on Sefer HaMitzvos is R' Yitzchak de Leon ibn Tzur (16th c., Italy; sefer first printed Venice 1592). Does the Rebbi want this corrected silently or acknowledged in a footnote?
3 Rav Moshe's מצוה קיומית: the Rebbi presented it as Rav Moshe's own chiddush. The tshuvah is Igros Moshe EH 1:102. Does the Rebbi want the full Rav Moshe quoted inside, or kept at the summary level? And does the Rebbi hold the קיומית/חיובית framework survives after 1948 — i.e., does the Tzitz Eliezer's "everything changed in 1948" argument actually rebut Rav Moshe, or do they talk past each other?
4 Rav Soloveitchik. The Rebbi named him explicitly as a question ("why didn't he go?") but gave no answer. Will the Sefer Edition address Kol Dodi Dofek and the Rav's actual position (that there is a דפיקה but not a straightforward חיוב עלייה for every yachid in galus)?
5 The Rambam's own aliyah. The Rebbi treated the Sefer Charedim letter as proof that "the Rambam moved to Eretz Yisrael." Historically, the Rambam visited (landed ג' סיון at עכו, davened at the Makom HaMikdash) and continued to Mitzrayim, where he lived and died. Does the Rebbi want to tighten this to a "visited / set foot in" formulation, or does he hold the raya from the יום טוב for the family is strong enough regardless?
6 The personal passage about the Rebbi's father's levayah in Eretz Yisrael (transcript 00:35:36) is powerful and is the strongest living illustration of כל הדר בחוץ לארץ כמי שאין לו אלוקה in the shiur. Does the Rebbi want it preserved in the Sefer Edition — as a set-off footnote, or in the body — or struck?

VIII. Bottom Line / Direction for the Sefer Edition Rewrite

A warm, honest, biographically rich halachic survey whose Rashi-vs.-Ramban spine and Ramban-biography material are first-rate, but whose maskana is halachically incomplete: the shiur ran out of time, the שלש שבועות sugya was promised and not delivered, Avnei Nezer and Rav Teichtal and the Rav are missing entirely, Megillat Esther's author is misidentified, and two Rambam citations need to be tightened. The Sefer Edition should (i) frame this as "Part I — Rishonim and the Classical Machloikes" and commission or splice a Part II covering Shalosh Shvuos, the Acharonim, and the modern post-1948 teshuvos, (ii) fix the four source errors flagged in §IV before Sefaria verification, (iii) restore the Rebbi's personal passage about his father's levayah in a footnote with his permission, and (iv) rewrite the closing as a real halachic maskana with a nafka-minah list instead of a pastoral send-off.
