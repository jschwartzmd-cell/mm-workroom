Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

23 November 2025 — סיום תחנון and Opening the ארון קודש: שומר ישראל, קל ארך אפים, and בריך שמיה

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcripts, summary, and finished write-up · 25 October 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

A Sunday-morning shiur in the Seder HaTefillah series, recorded 3 Kislev 5786 (23 November 2025), finishing Tachanun and crossing into Kriyas HaTorah. It opens with Shomer Yisrael (its age, the Siddur Rashi, the stanza that almost no siddur prints, the Shelah's will, R' Nosson Adler, the Gra's practice of saying it only on a public fast), then Mispayyes BeTachanunim and the closing Avinu Malkeinu of Rabbi Akiva. VaAnachnu Lo Neda is read as the end of three postures (sitting, standing, falling), with the Mishnah Berurah's rule: sit for VaAnachnu Lo Neda, stand for Mah Na'aseh. Al Tizkor Lanu Avonos Rishonim is read through the Ach Pri Tevuah of the Liska Rebbe: Rivkah's veil, Dovid HaMelech's day in Berachos 3b, doing business with fellow Jews, and the two first questions asked in heaven (Shabbos 31a). The Chatzi Kaddish is explained by Rabbeinu Manoach. The long middle is the open Aron: the Zohar, the Chida's demand for silence, the Baal Shem Tov in Kesser Shem Tov, then Kel Erech Apayim with its two printed versions, the Roman rite and a Jerusalem museum, three reasons for the prayer (latecomers; days of din; an apology before the reading), and the Avudraham's count of fifty words and his insistence on saying both. The shiur ends with VaYehi BiNsoa HaAron, Brich Shmei (its Zohar source, when to say it, the parenthetical for children, Rav Ram Shor on Ana Avda), the Yesod VeShoresh HaAvodah's kavanah, and a closing mishnah (Makkos 3:16) and Kaddish.

2.Overall Fidelity

The finished HTML follows the shiur closely and is, in several places, better than the summary: it restores Mah Na'aseh (the summary has Zachrenu), gives R' Nosson Adler (the summary has Rav Binyamin Adler), and corrects the printing of the Zohar (Mantua, not Turkey) in a note. Three sources were read: the bilingual Plaud transcript (the clearest, and the only one that gives the actual words of the stanzas of Shomer Yisrael), the Sofer-style transcript (heavily garbled; it ends with a stray non-shiur sentence at about 00:40:41, to be ignored), and the AI summary. The pattern to note is that the write-up inherited several errors from the summary where the summary departs from both transcripts: the missing stanza, the author of the Ach Pri Tevuah, the Tosafos locus for Rabbeinu Manoach, the ground for sitting at Shema, the ending, and the Roman-rite detail. All are localized; they are listed in Sections 3 to 5. None of them touches the central argument of the shiur (the three reasons for Kel Erech Apayim and the fifty words).

3.Content Flags — Misattributions and Overclaims

Flag 1 — The missing stanza is Shomer Goy Rabba, not Shomer Goy Kadosh. The write-up (Section I and fn 2) says the Shelah restored שומר גוי קדוש and quotes שמור שארית עם קדוש. In the Plaud transcript the speaker first recites שומר גוי קדוש שמור שארית עם קדוש ואל יאבד גוי קדוש המשלשים בשלוש קדושות לקדוש as the stanza the Sephardim add, and then, introducing the Shelah's will, says the verse is שומר גוי רבא, שמור שארית עם רבא, ואל יאבד גוי רבא ... האומרים אמן יהא שמיה רבא ("I don't remember that part"). The Sofer transcript agrees ("the guardian of the great nation"). The summary replaced rabba with kadosh; the write-up copied it, and then attached a printing history to the wrong stanza. The sefer should say: Sephardic rites add the goy kadosh stanza; the Shelah's verse is goy rabba; the speaker did not recall all of it.

Flag 2 — "R' Shaya'le" is an artifact. The write-up (Section IV) and fn 7 carry a long note about two names for the author of the Ach Pri Tevuah and a possible R' Yeshaya'le Steiner of Kerestir. The Plaud transcript has only "the Herschel Lisker sefer ... Acher Pri Tevuah ... Rav Hershel Lisker, a gadol tzaddik, a talmid of the Yismach Moshe". "Shaya'le" came from the Sofer garble ("Reb Shailik") and the summary ("רב שעיה'לע's ספר"). The ambiguity does not exist in the clearest source and should be removed from the body and note.

Flag 3 — Tosafos in Shabbos, not Chullin. The speaker says he found Rabbeinu Manoach cited once in Tosafos. Plaud: "in תוספות in שבת ... I Googled it." The summary says חולין; the write-up (Section V and fn 12) follows the summary and adds "the same Rishon." The speaker himself said only "probably." The locus needs correcting, and the identification (a 14th-century commentator on the Rambam cited in Tosafos) deserves a flag.

Flag 4 — The ground for sitting at Shema is al pi Kabbalah, not al pi kavod. Plaud: "לכתחילה, the שולחן ערוך says you're supposed to sit down by קריאת שמע, על פי קבלה." The Sofer text ("al pika bollo") is closer to kabbalah than to kavod. The summary has על פי כבוד, and the write-up repeats it.

Flag 5 — The closing sentence is not in the shiur. The write-up ends: "Next week ... we will continue with the rest of the תפילות before and after לעולם יהא אדם and ה' ה' אל רחום וחנון." In Plaud the speaker says he will discuss next week "all the rest of the תפילות that we say before and after לנינג" (leining), that we may be "זוכה to be מקבל the תורה בשמחה ובאהבה," and then recites Makkos 3:16 (Rabbi Chananya ben Akashya, ה' חפץ למען צדקו יגדיל תורה ויאדיר) and Kaddish. The write-up has neither the mishnah nor the real topic of next week.

Flag 6 — The Roman-rite omission is HaNoten LaYaef Koach, not Ein KeElokeinu. The write-up (Section VII): "they do not say אין כאלוקינו at לעולם כח." Plaud: "They don't say הנותן ליעף כח. They have a different ברכה by ברכות השחר." The summary garbled it into אין כאלקינו לעולם כח. (The Sofer text, "Anoysa Le'ayef Koyach," is the same blessing.)

Flag 7 — The Shabbsai Tzvi remark is recast. In the shiur it is a loose aside: "Some say שבתי צבי added this. He has a big part in adding בריך שמיה because of this line. But now is not the time to discuss it." An audience member then asks about Frankfurt. The write-up turns it into an explanation of why the Yekkes abstain ("concerns about שבתאות") and adds "we will discuss it more next week," which the speaker did not say (his "not now" is not a promise). Say what he said, hedged, or drop it.

Flag 8 — Dates. The summary's header gives כ״ג חשון תשפ״ה (wrong in day, month and year); the write-up's ג׳ כסלו תשפ״ו is correct for Sunday 23 November 2025. Separately, the speaker says the verse of Rivkah's veil is from "last week's parshah"; Chayei Sarah was read on 15 November and Toldos on 22 November, so the phrase is loose (or it points to the parshah of two weeks earlier). The write-up keeps "last week's פרשה"; the sefer should say "the parshah of Chayei Sarah" and drop the temporal claim.

Flag 9 — Two statements of the lecturer left uncorrected in the body. (a) He says the Zohar was revealed "approximately the 1400s ... early 1500s." The write-up keeps "roughly the 1400s" and puts only the printing date in a note. A reviewer's recollection, to be confirmed, is that the Zohar began to circulate in the late thirteenth century (R' Moshe de León); the sefer should not repeat the lecturer's 1400s unqualified. (b) He places Siddur Rashi in "the 1200s." The write-up contradicts this inside the body ("the lecturer said ... but the סידור רש״י ... dates to about 1100"); the correction belongs in the note, with the body saying only "the Rishonim of Ashkenaz."

4.Content Flags — Omissions

The Rambam on sitting after nefilas apayim. In Section III the speaker says (Plaud): "the רמב"ם writes in הלכות תפילה, the רמב"ם writes after you do נפילת אפים you sit." The write-up has no Rambam here. (The Shulchan Aruch, Orach Chaim 131:1, does say that after the fall one lifts the head and supplicates a little while seated; the Rambam's own wording was not checked.) He also says that most siddurim (he names the ArtScroll) print the division sitting/standing at Mah Na'aseh; the write-up omits it.

Running to the Aron. After the Baal Shem Tov, the speaker takes a question: does it matter whether one says Brich Shmei at one's seat or near the Aron? Many in his shul run to the Aron; he saw this minhag in a sefer (he thinks the Seder HaYom: there is a matter of going to the Aron to honor the Torah; "it's not halachah"), and the Mishnah Berurah discusses how far one accompanies the Sefer Torah. This is a real halachic point and is absent from the write-up. The named congregant is not needed.

The stanzas of Shomer Yisrael. The speaker's remark that the stanzas answer what Israel has just said (Shema, the threefold Kadosh, Amen Yehei Shemei Rabba) is absent from the write-up; see Flag 1. The speaker's sentence on this point is itself unclear in all three sources ("that's the nusach that we have today without the Amen Yehei Shmei Rabba"), so the sefer should say less rather than guess.

Small things. That he visited the Jerusalem museum "just last week"; that he had been told about the Roman rite before the shiur and then looked at videos; the audience member who questions Kel Erech Apayim ("Josh must have a source ... it's a big machlokes"). The first is harmless; the others are best left out of a sefer.

5.Added or Editorial Material Not in the Shiur

The apparatus is heavier than the shiur and much of it is the editor's. Most is flagged as such; the following need a closer look:

  • fn 1 (Siddur Rashi, Buber–Freimann, Berlin 1911; c. 1080–1120, Troyes): matches Sefaria. It is fine in a note but is the opposite of what the speaker said ("the 1200s"), so the body must not repeat it as his.
  • fn 2 (Shaar HaShamayim "completed by his son and printed posthumously, Amsterdam 1717"): none of this is in the shiur and none was checked; it also sits on the wrong stanza (Flag 1).
  • fn 11 (Rambam, Hilchos Tefillah 9): the note says the shaliach tzibbur says Kaddish "before Berachos Kri'as Shema ... and after Shemoneh Esrei (full Kaddish Titkabal)." Sefaria shows Kaddish before Shema in the morning (9:1), a second Kaddish after the supplications (9:5), and after Shema only at night (9:9). The note is loose; see Part 3.
  • fn 12 (Rabbeinu Manoach of Narbonne, late 13th century): not in the shiur; the "same Rishon" equation with the Tosafos citation raises a chronology question (Flag 3).
  • fn 16, fn 18, fn 22: biographical and bibliographical detail (Kesser Shem Tov, Zolkiew 1794; Shibbolei HaLeket; Avudraham, Seville and Lisbon 1489) is the editor's. The Shibbolei HaLeket note put the work in "southern Italy"; Sefaria says only "Italian ... 13th-century."
  • fn 23–24 (Rashi on Numbers 10:35): the Tanchuma and the three days' journey are on the words קומה ה', not on ויהי בנסע הארן as the note said. The inverted-nun material and the Shabbos 115b–116a discussion are the editor's.
  • fn 26 (Magen Avraham): the shiur says the Magen Avraham excludes weekday Brich Shmei because of בריך כתרך; the note's "refers to the Zohar on Monday and Thursday" is misleading (the Monday and Thursday reference there concerns pausing in the reading).
  • The body's own parentheticals, including the "(the recording also gives ...)" and "(see the note)" asides, are editorial and belong in the notes.

6.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

Sitting and standing. Mishnah Berurah 131:9 (verified on Sefaria) says it is fitting to say VaAnachnu Lo Neda seated and Mah Na'aseh standing, citing the Shelah and later authorities. The speaker's own explanation (we stood, we sat, we fell) is a derush of the lecturer's, not from the Mishnah Berurah. The Shulchan Aruch 131:1 says, after falling, "lift one's head and supplicate a little while seated" and gives the custom of VaAnachnu Lo Neda, Chatzi Kaddish, Ashrei and LaMenatzeach; it does not itself divide the verse between sitting and standing.

The reasons for Kel Erech Apayim. The three reasons are carefully separated in the shiur and in the write-up, which is a strength. Two checks: (a) Machzor Vitry (Order of Shacharit) does say, for Monday, שב"ד שלמעלה ולמטה שוין מרבים בתפילה ורחמים (verified on Sefaria); the speaker's link to הצילנו מכל רע and to judicial error is, in his words, "I don't see a direct reference ... maybe," and the write-up should keep that hedge. (b) The Gra's attribution to "the sages of Italy" was not found in the Biur HaGra to Orach Chaim 131 or 134 as available on Sefaria; it remains the lecturer's report (he cites Maaseh Rav for the Gra's practice, not for this).

Fifty words. The Avudraham (Sefaria: Weekday Prayers, Torah Reading, 2) writes ותמצא בשתיהן חמשים תיבות כנגד עשרת הדברו' וארבעים יום שעמד משה בהר לקבל התורה. The speaker adds that the Avudraham says it is a mistake to say only one; that was not found in the passage as available. The write-up (in its body) states it as the Avudraham's explicit ruling; it should say "as the lecturer reports."

The Gemara in Berachos 3b. Verified on Sefaria: the lyre, the north wind, "Our master, the king, your nation requires sustenance," David's "go and sustain one another," the sages' reply, and "go and take up arms with the troops." The words כי אם ראש חודש ושבת, which the shiur attaches to the Ach Pri Tevuah's allusion, are not in the passage. The speaker's "Philistines" is his gloss on בגדוד (the Gemara says only "the troops"); he calls it "controversial," and the sefer should not state it flatly as David's advice. On Shabbos 31a the questions are in the order the shiur gives (business first, then fixed times for Torah).

Rivkah and the camel. The speaker says she "fell off the camel" (the Hebrew of Genesis 24:64 is ותפל מעל הגמל; Sefaria's English has "alighted") and then took the veil in verse 65 after asking who the man was. The write-up omits the fall; the sefer can quote ותפל and keep verse 65 for the veil.

7.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.Which stanza did the Shelah ask his children to restore: Shomer Goy Rabba (as the recordings have it) or the Shomer Goy Kadosh of the Sephardic rites? Can he give the whole of the Shelah's verse, and where in the Shaar HaShamayim or the tzava'ah it appears?
Q2.Where is Rabbeinu Manoach cited in Tosafos (the recording says Shabbos), and does the Rav mean the 14th-century commentator on the Rambam?
Q3.What is the source for the Gra's attribution of Kel Erech Apayim to the chachmei Italia (the Rav cites Maaseh Rav for the Gra's Shomer Yisrael practice, not for this)?
Q4.Where does the Ach Pri Tevuah write the allusion, and are the words כי אם ראש חודש ושבת his or the Rav's? Which edition?
Q5.Which sefer (the Seder HaYom?) records going to the Aron during Brich Shmei, and which Mishnah Berurah does the Rav mean on accompanying the Sefer Torah?
Q6.The Jerusalem museum: the 1951 date, the town of the reconstructed Aron, and whether the minyan there still davens nusach Bnei Romi. And the exact blessing omitted in the Roman rite (HaNoten LaYaef Koach)?
Q7.Where does the Avudraham say that one who says only one version errs? And which ArtScroll printing notes yesh noheg to say both?
Q8.The Rambam on sitting after nefilas apayim: which halachah in Hilchos Tefillah does the Rav mean?
Q9.Is the Magen Avraham's exclusion of weekday Brich Shmei in Orach Chaim 282 or elsewhere, and is the reasoning from כתרך his or the Zohar commentators'?

8.Bottom Line

A faithful write-up of a lively, well-organized shiur whose spine (the three reasons for Kel Erech Apayim and the Avudraham's fifty words; the Zohar and the silence at the open Aron) survives intact. As in earlier weeks, most factual slips entered through the summary rather than the speaker: the wrong stanza of Shomer Yisrael, "R' Shaya'le," Chullin, al pi kavod, the Roman-rite blessing, and the ending. Fix those; restore the Rambam on sitting, the running-to-the-Aron point, and the closing mishnah; move the editor's corrections (the 1200s, Turkey, "see the note") out of the body and into the notes; and hedge the Shabbsai Tzvi aside, the Avudraham's "error," and the "Philistines." Then the sefer edition can stand.
