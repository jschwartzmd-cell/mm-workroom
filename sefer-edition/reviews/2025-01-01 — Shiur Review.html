Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

1 January 2025 — מַדּוּעַ אֵין מַסֶּכֶת חֲנוּכָּה — Why No Maseches Chanukah?

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · 23 August 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

Delivered on Erev Zos Chanukah 5785, this shiur is a continuation and follow-up to the previous week's Chanukah shiur, structured as a response to heoros from listeners and a running audit of the classical teirutzim to a famous kushya: why did Rabbeinu HaKadosh not compose a Maseches Chanukah? After two hakdamah-pieces — the Bnei Yissaschar's outstretched hand of teshuvah and the Apter Rav's vort on the secular new year — the Rabbi walks through six teirutzim: the Vilna Gaon (it was lost); the reported Chasam Sofer vendetta against the Chashmonaim (with Sforno's counter on Lo yasur); the Chasam Sofer's l'chadeish principle grounded in the Rambam on Menachos; the pre-existence of Megillas Taanis; and the Rabbi's preferred teretz from Rav Reuven Margaliyos — the political self-censorship of Rabbeinu HaKadosh against the backdrop of the Tumultus Iudaicus, Trajan's massacre of Alexandrian Jewry, and Roman surveillance of Jewish texts. The shiur closes with a beautiful remez from the Bircas HaShalom on Bava Kama's camel-and-storekeeper mishnah: chenvani = Yochanan, and his descendants' ner Chanukah is the tikkun of his own Tzeduki misuse of fire outside the Kodesh HaKodashim. A characteristically wide-ranging shiur that uses one absence in the Mishnah as a lens onto Roman-era Jewish history and the internal politics of Chazal.

2.What Was Strongest

  • The Rav Margaliyos teretz. Historically grounded, textually anchored (Dio Cassius, the Alexandria sugya, the shnei sardiotin of Bava Kama), and it survives its own test: it explains not only why there is no Maseches Chanukah but also why the Chanukah material we do have appears in beraisa form in Shabbos rather than in Mishnah. The Rabbi handles the talmid's pushback (“but Shabbos 21b…”) elegantly by distinguishing the foundational Mishnah from later tana'ic and amora'ic layers. This is the intellectual center of the shiur and it earns its place.
  • The Bircas HaShalom remez. The equation chenvani = Yochanan, tied to the Yoma 53a story of the Tzeduki ketores lit outside and its reversal through the Chashmonaim's outdoor ner Chanukah, is a genuinely luminous piece of learning. It ties together three otherwise unrelated sugyos (Bava Kama, Yoma, Shabbos), it resolves the Bnei Yissaschar's kushya about why Yochanan's name is preserved in Al HaNissim, and it does actual theological work — ner Chanukah as the Chashmonaim's championing of koach d'rabbanan against their grandfather's rejection of it. This is the kind of vort a listener will retain for years.
  • The Sforno on Lo yasur. The krenkel on the Ramban is presented cleanly and it structurally undermines teretz #2 in a satisfying way. The Rabbi does not simply catalog opinions; he lets the Sforno dismantle the premise on which the previous teretz rested. That kind of internal argumentative movement is what elevates a survey-shiur into a real shakla v'tarya.
  • The Rambam's three halachos of history. The observation that Hilchos Chanukah uniquely opens with narrative — and the Rabbi's explanation grounded in the Rambam's own hakdamah (Tanach + Mishneh Torah should suffice) — is a satisfying, well-supported pshat. The comparison with Hilchos Megillah (no history, because Esther is in Tanach) is the kind of internal Rambam-consistency move that carries weight.
  • The Apter Rav's vort on January 1st. Functionally the hakdamah, but it does real thematic work: the retroactive acceptance of tefillos from Rosh HaShanah mirrors the shiur's structural argument that history — both political and spiritual — is read backwards. Coupled with the Rav Yaakov Kamenetsky story, it grounds the shiur in a lived Erev Zos Chanukah moment that a more purely academic treatment would have missed.

3.What Was Weaker or Worth Pushing Back On

The six teirutzim are not weighed against each other. The Rabbi presents each teretz in sequence and eventually declares the Margaliyos approach his preferred one, but the earlier teirutzim are not clearly retired. Are the Chasam Sofer's l'chadeish principle and the Megillas Taanis point still standing alongside the political teretz? Are they cumulative causes, or does the political teretz displace them? A shiur working through six approaches to one kushya owes the listener an explicit accounting of which survive the exercise and how they relate. The current structure leaves the impression of an inventory rather than a resolution.

The Vilna Gaon's position is dismissed too quickly. The Rabbi's kushya — that no cross-reference to “b'maseches Chanukah” appears anywhere in Shas — is real, but the Gaon's tradition is not a casual assertion; it comes with the R' Hai Gaon narrative and a specific mesorah. A one-sentence tzurech iyun would have been fairer than what reads almost like a rejection. If the Gaon believed a Maseches Chanukah once existed, the absence of cross-reference presumably did not trouble him — and understanding why not would enrich the discussion rather than end it.

The Chasam Sofer vendetta teretz is not fully engaged. The Rabbi's own critique — that halachos binding on every Jew still need transmission — is sharp, but it may cut too fast. Rabbeinu HaKadosh could have transmitted the halachos in scattered form (as he in fact does — the mishnayos that mention Chanukah are distributed across seven tractates) without granting the Chashmonaim their own maseches. That distinction — between transmitting the halachos and honoring the yom tov with structural prominence — would let the vendetta teretz stand on its own two feet before being weighed against the political teretz.

The Trajan / Tumultus Iudaicus section, however powerful, expands past the argument it serves. The Alexandria yeshivah and the sea red with Jewish blood are extraordinary as history, but the emotional weight of the section outruns the load-bearing point — that Rabbeinu HaKadosh was writing under Roman surveillance. Tightening this material and moving faster to the sardiotin would have kept the argument's spine visible. The 9/11 personal aside is genuine and moving, but it further stretches an already long historical excursus.

4.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The distinction between Mishnah and beraisa is doing more work than the shiur names. The Rabbi's answer to the Shabbos 21b pushback — that a beraisa is different from a mishnah because it is later — is chronologically defensible but conceptually thin. Beraisos are tana'ic; many are contemporaneous with material the Mishnah included. The real distinction may be one of redaction and authority rather than date: what Rabbeinu HaKadosh chose to codify carries a different political and canonical weight than what the tana'im transmitted alongside. Framing the answer this way sharpens both the teretz and the underlying theory of the Mishnah's canonization.

The l'chadeish principle in Chasam Sofer #3 raises a converse question. If Rabbeinu HaKadosh only recorded what the olam did not already know, why does Maseches Sukkah record obvious material like the four minim, and Maseches Pesachim the fundamentals of bedikas chametz? The principle can be defended, but it needs a mechanism for distinguishing the halachos Chanukah was “well-known enough” to omit from the halachos other yamim tovim apparently were not. Without that mechanism, teretz #3 proves too much.

The b'sha'as ha'sakanah question deserved lomdishe treatment. The Rabbi notes the halachah as historical inheritance — we light inside because of ancient danger — but does not engage the classical machlokes: is our current practice b'di'eved (we should really be lighting outside k'tikuno where we can), or has the hetter hardened into l'chatchilah for diaspora communities? This affects real halachah l'ma'aseh and would have grounded the historical argument in current practice.

The Bircas HaShalom remez hides a serious lomdish claim. That patur in the mishnah means more than “exempt from damages” — that it means Yochanan himself is patur through his descendants' tikkun — is a startling reading of a technical tort term. Is this a drush-only move, or does the Bircas HaShalom ground it in the lashon? A reader wants to know whether the drash is decorative or textually driven.

5.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.Of the six teirutzim presented, which do you think the shiur ultimately leaves standing? Is the Margaliyos political teretz a replacement for the others, or the deepest layer of a cumulative picture in which Megillas Taanis, the l'chadeish principle, and the political concern all contribute?
Q2.The Vilna Gaon's tradition of a lost Maseches Chanukah is presented and set aside on the grounds that no cross-reference appears in Shas. Are there any talmidei haGra or later acharonim who defended the Gaon's position against that kushya? And is it possible the Gaon was speaking of a maseches ketanah-type composition that would not have generated internal cross-references?
Q3.If Rabbeinu HaKadosh self-censored the Mishnah for political reasons, what does that tell us about the halachic status of the Mishnah as a canonical document? Are there other sugyos where we can identify probable political-editorial choices, and does Rav Margaliyos catalog them elsewhere in his writings?
Q4.The Bircas HaShalom's reading of patur as a redemption of Yochanan through the Chashmonaim is stunning. Does the sefer ground this in the lashon of the mishnah, or is it purely a drush? And are there parallel Chassidische treatments — in the Sefas Emes, the Bnei Yissaschar, elsewhere — of the chenvani = Yochanan equation?
Q5.The shiur closes with the Sefas Emes's framing of Chanukah as tarpa balbeis — everything hidden until Mashiach. Does that inner yesod map onto the historical/political teretz as parallel explanations of the same phenomenon, or does one ground the other — e.g., Rabbeinu HaKadosh's political suppression as the gilui in olam ha'zeh of a deeper spiritual necessity of concealment?
Q6.Given the Yerushalmi's and Bavli's willingness to describe Alexandria and record the Trajan ma'aseh, why was that material acceptable when a Maseches Chanukah was not? Is it because those passages are aggadic and Roman censors did not scan aggadta, or is there a more principled distinction?

6.Bottom Line

A dense, wide-ranging shiur that does exactly what a good Erev Zos Chanukah shiur should do — take a famous absence and use it to open up an entire historical world. The Margaliyos political teretz and the Bircas HaShalom remez would each carry a shiur on their own; having them in the same hour is a real gift. The weakness is structural rather than substantive: six teirutzim are laid out but never explicitly ranked or retired, and the emotional weight of the Trajan section outruns the argument it serves. A listener leaves with unforgettable images (the sea red with Jewish blood, the seventy golden thrones, chenvani = Yochanan) and a strong intuition that the political teretz is right — but without a clean answer to which of the other five are still standing beside it, or how the pieces fit together into a single account of the Mishnah's silence on the Maccabees.
