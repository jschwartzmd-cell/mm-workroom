28 September 2025 — The Cholera Epidemic and רבי ישראל סלנטר’s Decision on יום כיפור

Independent review · read fresh, no exposure to the writing process · 12 October 2026

1.Overall Read

A fourteen-section write-up (roughly 5,000 words of body plus a thirteen-note Mareh Mekomos) on the Yom Kippur cholera incident in Vilna. The architecture is a good one for a story that has two competing tellings: biography of Rav Yisrael (I), the sources (II), the epidemic (III), the incident (IV), the rebuke (V), the clash of principles (VI), the summons and the kashya on gittin (VII), two halachic questions (VIII), the eyewitness version (IX), the defence from pikuach nefesh (X), the Chasam Sofer (XI), bein adam lachaveiro (XII), later acharonim (XIII) and a conclusion (XIV). The narrative energy is high and the anecdotes (the cakes in the pocket, the Brisker Rav’s remark, the Shavel man who collapsed, the Brisker joke) carry the piece.

The hard issues are: (a) the elderly dayan appears under two different names and the first is anachronistic; (b) the Hebrew year in the subtitle and several places does not match the Gregorian year beside it; (c) the document is framed as delivered “Erev Yom Kippur” but dated 28 September 2025; (d) the narrative voice is first-person throughout and includes unqualified practical advice; (e) a section that introduces “רבינו ברוך” with no identification; (f) the notes carry five open “verify” instructions and at least three confident claims that go beyond what the body says.

2.What Works

  • The two-version structure. Setting the public-kiddush account (IV) against the person-to-person account (IX), with two sharp questions (VIII) between them, lets the reader feel the problem before the resolution. The device of withholding the second version until the questions have been posed is effective and worth keeping.
  • Section III’s clinical sketch. Short, concrete (water, dehydration, incubation, the crowded quarter) and honest about what the physicians of the time could not have known. It makes the later pikuach nefesh argument credible.
  • The callouts. “The Institutional Picture” (V) is the best sentence-level device in the document: it makes the reader inhabit the young rav’s exposure. “An Incident in Shavel” and the Brisker joke are placed exactly where the argument needs a human weight.
  • Section VI. The two-principles frame is clean and does not overclaim. It states both sides at full strength before the sources speak.
  • Sections X–XII. The chain of Yerushalmi, Beis Yosef, Sotah and Tur is laid out in the order a reader needs it, and the bein adam lachaveiro section (XII) closes the defence on a note that is both new and fitting for the season.
  • The apparatus. The notes follow the short-then-expanded format, with a visible note-academic block where the sources are shaky. The backrefs work and the footnote numbers run 1–13 in order of appearance.

3.Where It’s Weak or Could Be Sharper

Two names for one elder (V and VII). Section V introduces רבי יחזקאל לנדא הכהן, “the elderly dayan of Vilna, eighty years old … author of the sefer Bikurim.” Section VII then opens: “רבי איצל הכהן מווילנא made a great machlokes … he summoned Rav Yisrael to the beis din after Yom Kippur.” The reader assumes two men; the plot requires one. Worse, the first name cannot be right: the best-known Yechezkel Landau, author of the Noda BiYehudah, died in 1793 and was not a kohen, so he cannot be an eighty-year-old dayan of 1848. One name, one identification, one place for the note.

Calendar mismatch. The subtitle reads “Vilna, תר״ח (1848),” the lead and Section III repeat תר״ח, and the text says “In 1848 — תר״ח.” Hebrew year 5608 began in autumn 1847; Elul and Tishrei of 1848 fall in 5609 (תר״ט). The piece also places the episode “during the months of Elul and Tishrei,” so the Hebrew year can be computed; either the year or the Gregorian date is wrong.

“Erev Yom Kippur.” The kicker says “Erev Yom Kippur Drasha” and the colophon says “ערב יום כיפור,” but the header dates the talk to 28 September 2025, a Sunday. Yom Kippur 5786 fell on 2 October 2025; the talk was four days before, not on the eve. The lead’s own words (“the approaching חג”) are correct; the labels are not.

First person and live-room residue. The lead (“I customarily set aside our regular shiur”), the aside on Brisk (“where I learned”), the callout (“I am thirty-eight years old … לבדיל בין החיים”), Section VIII (“I always tell people”) and the “we remember COVID-19” sentence all belong to the room. In a sefer they should go; the garbled idiom in the callout (לבדיל בין החיים — presumably להבדיל …) should be resolved or dropped.

Unqualified practical advice. Section VIII tells the reader how to measure a cheekful, divide it and wait nine minutes. The piece is about a historical dispute; a printed text that appears to instruct a sick person how much to drink on Yom Kippur needs a sentence saying that each case goes to a rav, and the measures should be anchored to the Shulchan Aruch’s own words (note 4 gives different measures from the body without reconciling them).

“רבינו ברוך” is never introduced. Section VIII is titled “רבינו ברוך’s Two Halachic Questions,” and the first sentence begins “רבינו ברוך asks the obvious … question.” Section II had introduced ר׳ ברוך אפשטיין and Section XIII later brings ר׳ ברוך בער ליבוביץ. The reader cannot tell which Baruch is meant, or whether the questions are in Mekor Baruch or are the lecturer’s own. Identify him, or say that the lecturer attributes the questions to him.

The Koidenover Rebbe appears from nowhere. The only mention is in the last paragraph of Section VII: “The chassidim, naturally, maintained that the kefeidah of the Koidenover Rebbe was what later caused his stroke.” The stroke is mentioned once, in a callout in Section I, without a date or any connection to Vilna’s chassidim. Either set up the story (who they were, what the Rebbe said) or cut the sentence.

Section IX attribution. The paragraph that begins “שלום עלמנורי, in his sefer … brings the same version” continues straight into a quotation (“He says: ‘I tell you, this witness … had it right’”) with no marker as to whether he is the eyewitness, Almanori, or the lecturer. The next sentence (“In this telling, the elderly beis din’s anger reduces to …”) shows that the quotation is meant to be Almanori’s own gloss; make that explicit.

The 100% sentence in XI. “When safek pikuach nefesh is established at one hundred percent, there is no she’eilah: everyone should eat” is self-contradictory (a doubt established at certainty). Rephrase as the certain danger versus the doubtful one, and be clear which of the two the Chasam Sofer was addressing. The pregnant-woman paragraph is also unclear on whether it is the Chasam Sofer’s own reasoning or the lecturer’s illustration.

Chronology of XIII. “Fifty to one hundred years afterward … the acharonim discussed this case” is followed by a Jerusalem Rishon LeTzion who knew the story “several years ago in Europe” when the epidemic “later struck Eretz Yisrael.” The reader loses the sequence; reorder so the Jerusalem episode (a few years after) comes before the later acharonim (decades after).

Thin sections. XIII’s first two paragraphs and the whole of XII are short compared with the rest, and Section XIV mostly restates VI. Fold XIII into a single paragraph under XII or give it a heading, and shorten XIV.

4.Factual / Citation Concerns

The Ketzos HaChoshen on kiddush (VIII; note 5). The body states flatly that “the Ketzos HaChoshen holds” a sick person on a Shabbos Yom Kippur makes kiddush. The Ketzos is the classic commentary on Choshen Mishpat; this attribution is not where one would expect it, and the note itself says it “is associated with him in oral tradition but should be verified.” Move the hedge into the body or drop the name.

Note 4 on the measure. The note has the measure for solids as ככותבת הגסה and says the seifim of siman 618 on the patient’s word and on doubt are 2 and 5–7. These should be checked against the text; the body, meanwhile, speaks only of “less than the halachic measure” and of a cheekful. The note also says “(variously calculated as ca. 2, 4, 7, or 9 minutes)” for the interval, while the body says “either two minutes or nine minutes.”

Note 3 on V’nishmartem. It states that the Rambam in Hil. Rotzeach 11 cites this verse as the basis for pikuach nefesh overriding the mitzvos. The overriding of the mitzvos is normally derived from וחי בהם; the verse here is about guarding oneself. Check which verse the Rambam cites.

Note 8 on the Chofetz Chaim. The note says the Mishnah Berurah and Biur Halachah discuss at length the person who must eat on Yom Kippur, that “the סלנטר case stands behind much of his strong language,” and that his rulings “carry firsthand weight.” The body (IX) says only that the story is mentioned there “in passing.” The claims in the note go well beyond the body and are unsourced.

Note 9 and 11 on the Yerushalmi. The note quotes the three clauses in an order (הזריז … והשואל … והנשאל) that should be checked against the text; the body says that the Yerushalmi calls the one who asks מגונה and the Tur calls him שופך דמים, while note 11 gives שופך דמים to the Yerushalmi. The body and notes disagree about who says what.

Note 10. The Rambam reference in Hil. Shabbos 2:3 is cited for “the duty to act swiftly in pikuach nefesh without consultation.” Check that the halachah says that.

Note 1 and note 2 biographical dates. The author of The Making of a Gadol is given as (1930–2018) and first publication as 2002 — the body says “about fifteen years ago.” Note 2 adds a sentence that “later scholarship (e.g., Immanuel Etkes …) has questioned aspects of his narrative.” Nothing in the body prepares the reader for that claim, and it needs a page reference or removal.

The surname in IX and note 6. The eyewitness is שמעון שטרויסון, who “should not be confused with the famous ראש שטרויסון, שמואל שטרויסון.” The famous Vilna figure is the Rashash, שמואל שטראשון; ראש here is the abbreviation רש״ש. The eyewitness’s surname should be spelled the same way.

Age of Rav Yisrael and of the Chofetz Chaim. Section IV says Rav Yisrael was “thirty-six or thirty-seven”; this is worth checking against his birth year. Section X has the Chofetz Chaim as “a young boy” and discusses a ten-year dispute about when he died (“ninety-four or one hundred and four”); the note on him (8) says 1838–1933, which is neither. State his age at the time as a derived figure and say so.

Scale of the congregation. “Fifteen hundred Jews” recurs (IV, twice) as a fact. Mark as the lecturer’s figure or give a range.

The mnemonic in VIII. “תרי״ח — ראש השנה ויום חמישי — רפואת חולי ישראל” does not map letter-for-letter onto ת-ר-י-ח as printed; either spell it out or drop it.

5.Small Polish Suggestions

  • The lead is one long, comma-chained paragraph in italics; break it into two and drop the italics.
  • Transliteration register. Hebrew terms are left in Hebrew script in the body, but headings and phrases mix scripts (“bein adam l’chavero insight” in XIV beside בין אדם לחברו elsewhere; “hashkafic”). Adopt one convention and gloss on first use.
  • Heading style. Section VII’s title (“The Summons to בית דין and the קושיא in גיטין”) and IX’s (a name in the title) differ in kind from the topical headings; settle on one pattern.
  • Typos and phrasing: “had not gone shul to shul” (X) should be “gone from shul to shul”; “pious fool” is introduced twice; “חוצפה!” appears as a quotation inside a quotation; the blockquote in IV ends in an ellipsis (אנו מתירים…) with the sentence completed in a dash-line after it.
  • The verb “nullify”/“abrogate” is used for the fast in several places; one word, defined once.
  • Callout labels: “An Aside,” “The Brisker Rav’s Aside,” “An Incident in Shavel” — use a consistent class of label for stories versus asides.
  • The Brisk paragraph in I says the yeshivah in Jerusalem “where I learned” has no mussar books; drop the personal reference, and say which Brisk.
  • Note 12’s heading states Pressburg 1836 as a fact in the short citation, while the expanded note says “verify exact סימן”; align the two.
  • The colophon (“Hebrew terms preserved in Hebrew script throughout …”) is a production note and should not be printed.

6.Bottom Line

A strong narrative with the right shape for a story told two ways. Before print: (a) resolve the elderly dayan into one named figure and remove the anachronistic first name; (b) reconcile תר״ח with 1848 (the autumn of 1848 is 5609); (c) correct “Erev Yom Kippur” for a talk given four days before; (d) depersonalise the first-person passages and add a rav-caution to the practical measures; (e) identify “רבינו ברוך” and the Koidenover Rebbe; (f) clear the open “verify” instructions in notes 5, 6, 7, 12 and 13 and rework notes 3, 4, 8, 9, 10 and 11 against their sources. Verify two factual anchors — the Ketzos on kiddush and the 1836 Pressburg responsum — and fix the surname שטרויסון. Do these and the document will read as the authoritative English treatment of the incident.
