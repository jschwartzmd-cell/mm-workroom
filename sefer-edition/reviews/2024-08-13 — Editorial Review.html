זוֹכֶה וְרוֹאֶה בְּשִׂמְחָתָהּ — Mourning That Already Tastes of Joy

13 August 2024 · Independent review · read fresh, no exposure to the writing process · 10 August 2026

I. Overall Read

This is a well-structured and moving piece of Torah writing. The argument is architecturally clean — a grammatical puzzle opens the piece, a lomdish yesod from the Chasam Sofer resolves it, and a series of narrative illustrations (Yaakov's refusal, Rabbi Akiva's laugh, the Napoleon legend, Goldhagen, Rav Lau) give it emotional and historical weight. By the final synthesis, the reader understands why "zoche v'roeh" is deliberately present-tense, and the insight feels earned. The writing is at its best in the Makkos section (Section V) and the Rav Lau biographical material (Sections VIII–IX). There are structural and factual concerns that would need resolution before a sefer edition, but the underlying composition is strong.

II. What Works

  • The central argument is presented with discipline. The piece never loses sight of the grammatical puzzle it set out to solve, and the resolution — that mourning Yerushalayim in the present is itself an act of present-tense relationship with her future joy — is stated clearly and returned to in the synthesis.
  • The re-reading of the Makkos dialogue (Section V, "Re-reading the Dialogue") is the intellectual highlight. "Why are you crying?" as answer rather than deflection is a genuine insight, and the explanation — your tears are the proof that Yerushalayim is alive — is both logically tight and emotionally resonant.
  • The Rav Lau biographical material is handled with care. The details are specific (Piotrkow, Naftali, the selection scene, Buchenwald, Rabbi Herschel Schacter, the suitcase), and the emotional pacing of the suitcase-disintegrating-to-handles is well-constructed. The paragraph that begins "The image is itself a mashal" earns its place.
  • The dual-layer footnote system works. The short citation satisfies a quick reader; the expanded commentary rewards close engagement. The backreference arrows are useful. The note-academic divs in fn3 and fn13 correctly flag oral/testimonial sources rather than inflating them into textual citations.
  • Hebrew integration is clean throughout. Block quotes are RTL-correct, the Hebrew type is readable, and the translated lines are accurate and idiomatic.

III. Where It Is Weak

Section IV is the argumentative trough of the piece. "Application to Tziyon — Why Yerushalayim Is Still 'Alive'" (only three short paragraphs) does the most important explanatory work in the piece — applying the Chasam Sofer's yesod directly to Yerushalayim — but it does it in the least developed way. The transition from the Yaakov-Yosef case to the Yerushalayim case is stated rather than argued. A reader who is not already sympathetic will want to know: why does the Chasam Sofer think the gzeiras shikchah principle, which operates at the level of individual bereavement, scales up to a national covenant? The analogy is suggestive but not pressed. Two or three sentences of genuine development here would significantly tighten the argument.
Section VII (Goldhagen) is compelling but unmoored. The exchange between Goldhagen and the German professor is presented as a documented historical incident — "at a conference in Berlin, a respected German academic posed Goldhagen a challenging question" — but no source is given and no footnote is attached to the exchange itself. The rhetorical effect is strong, but a careful reader will notice that there is no citation for this specific conversation. This should either be sourced or introduced with a qualifier ("it is reported that," "Goldhagen is said to have answered"). As written, it reads as confirmed fact.
The footer undermines the sefer-edition ambition. The closing footer reads: "Reworked summary of Rabbi Ephrati's Tisha B'Av afternoon shiur, 13 August 2024. Hebrew terms preserved; personal asides and exchanges with the audience omitted; biographical and source content from the transcript integrated." This is appropriate for the Mareh Mekamos website or archive but incongruous in a sefer edition, where the text should stand on its own without a note explaining that asides were omitted and content integrated. It should be removed or replaced with a colophon appropriate to a book format.
Section VI (Tisha B'Av arc) is underdeveloped relative to its placement. The three bullet points about morning aveilus, post-chatzos easing, and the "moed" designation are laid out but not integrated into the argument at this stage. The insight that "these are not contradictions to the aveilus; they are the aveilus revealing its inner nechamah" is stated as a conclusion without the connecting reasoning that the surrounding sections have built. It feels like a note-form expansion rather than a fully written section.

IV. Factual and Citation Concerns

The identification of "Rav Shmuel 'Shmelke' Rozovsky of Zalachov" (fn3) requires verification. The name "Rozovsky" appears nowhere in the transcript — it was apparently introduced during the rework. Reb Shmuel Rozovsky is universally identified as the Rosh Yeshiva of Ponevezh, not a mashgiach of Chachmei Lublin and not from Zalachov. If the figure in the transcript is "Reb Shemel Lezalachover," he may be an entirely different person. Before this goes into print, the identity needs to be confirmed — and if it cannot be confirmed, the citation must be generalized.
The Rema attribution in fn6 appears imprecise. The footnote references "Rema (R' Moshe Isserles), Toras HaOlah / drashos on the aveilus on Yerushalayim." But the transcript calls the source "Sefer Toras Soilo of the Ramah" — which is likely "Toras HaOlah." These may be the same work, but the footnote should identify a specific chapter or passage rather than a vague "general reference." Without it, the counter-claim that the Napoleon insight predates Napoleon is an assertion rather than a citation.
The Eichah Rabbah citation (fn14) calls it "Pesichta 24," which may need verification. The standard division of Eichah Rabbah has numbered petichtos (proems) before the main commentary. "Pesichta 24" is within the expected range, but the specific petichta that contains the full Avraham-Yitzchak-Yaakov-Moshe-Rachel narrative should be confirmed against a standard edition (Vilna or Albeck). Some editions number differently.
The Goldhagen anecdote (Section VII) has no footnote or citation. Every other named historical claim in the piece carries a footnote. This prominent exchange — presented as a verbatim reply — has none. The omission is conspicuous. Either source it or apply a note-academic style qualifier.

V. Small Polish

  • Section IX heading reads "Rav Lau's Drashah — Let the Letters Testify." If we are aiming for a sefer voice, the heading could be more descriptive of the content: "Torah as Witness: A New Reading of the Midrash" or similar.
  • "The Rebbe relayed a drashah Rav Lau gave…" (Section VIII, first paragraph). This is presenter-framing that must be excised entirely in any book edition. The text should present the drashah as Torah content, not as a retold story.
  • The word "shlita" after Rav Lau's name (Section VIII, first sentence) is appropriate for the archive version but should be evaluated for a sefer, where it may date the text.
  • The boidem aside ("You know what a boydom is. It's like I don't have to say it in English. An attic") is clearly transcribed from the oral shiur and should be tightened in prose to something like "the attic storage room (boidem)". The current phrasing reads as spoken, not written.
  • The parenthetical "not to be confused with Rav Hershel Schachter of YU" (fn13) is useful for an audience note but slightly out of register for a sefer footnote. It could be rewritten as: "not to be confused with Rabbi Hershel Schachter of Yeshiva University."

VI. Bottom Line

A strong piece with one major structural gap (Section IV needs development), two significant factual concerns (the Shmelke identification and the unsourced Goldhagen exchange), and several voice-register issues that would need cleaning before a sefer edition — primarily the footer, the presenter-framing in Section VIII, and the oral-register asides. The core argument and the Makkos re-reading are publication-ready; everything else needs one more pass.
