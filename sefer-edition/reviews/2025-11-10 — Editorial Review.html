10 November 2025 — תחנון, נפילת אפים, and the Origins of והוא רחום

Independent review · written from the finished HTML only (the version before the Sefaria corrections); no transcript or summary material used in the findings · 18 October 2026

1.Overall Read

A thirteen-section write-up (roughly 4,400 words of body plus a nineteen-note Mareh Mekomos apparatus of about 1,800 words) on the structure and history of תחנון, with three distinct parts: the introductory וידוי and Thirteen Attributes, with the status of נפילת אפים (I–VI); a long excursus on one verse in תחנון and the Sabbatean affair of the Kitzur Shelah (VII–IX); and the origin of והוא רחום (X–XIII). The third part is the strongest: the ship story is told cleanly, in a clear sequence, and the two competing explanations of the origin (the three תנאים; חזקיהו) are laid side by side. The second part has the most interesting material (the gematria, Emden’s detection) and the most problems.

The hard editorial issues are seven: (a) Section VIII changes its subject in its last subsection: it begins with the verse ויאמר דוד אל גד and ends discussing ויהי נועם and the words כי פלא פעל ה', which the reader has not been told about; (b) the gematria is asserted but does not compute; (c) internal contradictions on the author of the Kitzur Shelah (first name, century); (d) the Minchah section asserts as fact a practice at odds with standard Ashkenazic practice; (e) the acrostic claims for עזריאל and חזקיהו do not demonstrably work on the page; (f) live-shiur residue and a first-person voice throughout; and (g) citations that are marked “verify,” or contradicted by the body (notes 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 12, 15, 17). Fixing these would make a very readable chapter.

2.What Works

  • The opening structural map (I). Separating וידוי and the Thirteen Attributes from נפילת אפים proper is exactly the right start; the point that the former has no source in the גמרא and rests on the זוהר and the אריז"ל gives the reader the frame for everything that follows.
  • The מהרש"א paragraph (II). The question (“who wore a טלית?”), the verse עוטה אור כשלמה, and the speculation about white טליתות, offered with an honest “I confess I do not know.”
  • The census narrative (VII). The three options, David’s reasoning for the hand of Heaven (men can corner the market), and the point on the grammar of נפלה נא ביד ה' against falling before Him are the clearest teaching in the first half.
  • The Emden episode (VIII). The setup (why a book abridged from a famous one might be suspect), the hawk-eyed reader of prefaces, and the denouement through a gematria hidden under marks over four words make a memorable story with a strong build.
  • The ship story (XI). Clear sequence (ships → king’s test → three days → dream → verse → fire → letter), with a satisfying detail: a man who does not know תנ"ך dreams a verse he cannot name, and the scholars identify it from the count of two כי and three לא. The verse is quoted in Hebrew in a block, which is the right treatment.
  • The two opposed reasons for Monday and Thursday (X) are set out as a genuine dispute, each with its own consequence for how one prays.
  • Note discipline. Nineteen references, numbered in body order with back-links, and most carry a short and an expanded form.
  • The closing סיכום compresses three strands in one paragraph and ties them to the practice.

3.Where It’s Weak or Could Be Sharper

The subject of Section VIII shifts. Section VII ends by asking what the verse ויאמר דוד אל גד is doing in תחנון, and VIII sets out to answer it by tracing the source. The last subsection (“The Impact on Our תפילה — ויהי נועם”) then speaks of a different verse, ויהי נועם ה' אלקינו עלינו, “recited before תחנון — specifically because it contains the words כי פלא פעל ה'.” The reader has not been told that ויהי נועם is said before תחנון or why those words would matter, and the phrase נפלה נא that made ויאמר דוד אל גד remarkable in VII is never connected to it. Either a bridge sentence is missing or the subsection belongs to another verse. The same subsection also says “there is a מקור though that מקור is questionable” and never says which מקור.

The gematria does not compute. The text says the numerical value of משיח האמיתי ימות המשיח “is exactly 814,” the value of שבתי צבי (note 11 calculates only the latter, correctly). Counting by standard values, the four words as printed total 1,643; the two words ימות משיח total 814. A reader who checks will lose confidence in the whole episode. The text must either give the exact words and the calculation or say it is reported. Relatedly, the passage describes “two סימנים, two apostrophes, over those four words,” yet quotes the preface only in an English paraphrase set as a block quotation; since the Hebrew is the evidence, the Hebrew of the preface should be given, or the paraphrase marked as such.

The author of the Kitzur Shelah is inconsistent. He is יחיאל מיכל עפשטיין in the heading and first paragraph, ר' ישראל מיכל עפשטיין in “The Hidden Gematria,” and אפשטיין in note 9. The text places him “in the early seventeenth century,” coming “fifty to seventy years after the שלחן ערוך” (which would be the late sixteenth), while note 9 says “Frankfurt, late 17th century,” “first printed 1683.” Pick one and give the evidence. The text says ר' יעקב עמדן issued a “קול קורא” and note 9 says a “חרם”; these are different acts.

Section VI asserts a custom as fact. “Why does כלל ישראל essentially not say תחנון at מנחה?” is at odds with standard Ashkenazic practice, as far as I know, which says תחנון at מנחה on ordinary weekdays. The section never says whose practice it describes, and its reasoning (it is dangerous at night, so the קהל canceled it even at midday) skips the step from a rule about night to a rule about the afternoon. The unusual expression מקצת בנטיעת is given with no source and is, as far as I know, a corruption of the phrase said of אחר (קיצץ בנטיעות).

The acrostic claims. Section XII says עוזרנו “spells ע-ז-ר-נ-ו; the יוד of ישענו completes it: עזריאל.” The letters listed plus a יוד are ע ז ר נ ו י; the name needs ע ז ר י א ל. Section XIII lists “חשוב ציון · זכור ה' · קום ה' · י־ה־ו...” with a trailing ellipsis, so the reader cannot see the last three letters being spelled. The prayer is not quoted, so neither claim can be checked. Quote the lines.

Oral rhetoric kept as the voice. The text keeps first-person and spoken devices: “I confess,” “I forgot to mention this last week,” “I myself used to say it as a child,” “Last week we began,” “We think Corona was terrible?,” “Today, with Google or ChatGPT, you would find it in seconds,” געוואלדיג, פונקט פערקערט, וועלט, פוגרומען. They are charming on the page but date the document and will not survive a sefer edition.

Unanswered questions. Section IV opens with three questions (what is the connection of the last section of Vayeira to ראש השנה? why not the fourth day? why the קריאה?) and gives the בעל שם טוב’s acronym, which answers none of them. The acronym ואת מעכה is a lovely hint about וידוי and תפילה, but a reader will wonder what it has to do with ראש השנה. The block quotation of the acronym, with each initial in a separate styled span, renders awkwardly.

Section IX is a good story with a sweeping claim. “None of these תפילות existed in כלל ישראל before the תלמיד of the תלמיד of the אריז"ל decided to teach them” is much too strong for something like בריך שמיה (which note 14 itself concedes originates in the זוהר). “Some come instead from the קיצור של"ה... which is why a few people specifically do not say a few of these תפילות” is vague (which?). The numbers differ: the text says 20,000–50,000 killed, note 13 says 20,000–100,000.

The sitting/standing and הפסק remarks (X, XI) are made without a source: “one may not be מפסיק during it” and “so the זוהר teaches” the parallel of דין above carry no note. Note 16 supplies שבת קכ"ט ע"ב but the body does not cite it.

The geography of the ship story. The text has “the king of פורטוגל” greeting תנאים in the first century; Portugal as a kingdom is medieval. The text hedges (“the versions disagree”), but a sentence saying that the story is a legend, and which versions place it where, would protect the reader. The text also says “we even have the names of the three תנאים” without giving them.

4.Factual / Citation Concerns

Note 1 (זוהר חלק ג', פרשת בלק רכ"ב ע"א). The locus looks internally inconsistent: on the standard pagination of the זוהר, folio 222 of volume III lies, as far as I know, in a later פרשה than בלק. Please check. The reference to שער הכוונות, דרושי נפילת אפים also gives no section.

Note 2 (ראש השנה י"ז ע"ב). The Hebrew in italics is presented as a verbatim quotation. It should be checked against the printed גמרא, in particular the wording כשליח צבור against the body’s “wrapped Himself in a טלית.” The body says the גמרא “adds: were it not for the fact that the גמרא states this, we would not be permitted to say so”; the quotation says “it is impossible to say.”

Note 3 (מהרש"א). “Verify by reading the מהרש"א directly” is an unresolved instruction left in the text. The body states that the מהרש"א “says this אור is white,” and the note repeats it as a claim still to be verified.

Note 4 (שו"ת הרשב"ש). The body says רמב"ש; the note says likely רשב"ש and cross-refers to the תשב"ץ, which leaves the author unclear, and gives dates without a source. The note also says the principle is “widely held in the ראשונים (e.g., רמב"ם הלכות תפילה פ"ה ה"י"ג, רא"ש ברכות),” which is not what the רמב"ם there says, as far as I know, and ends with “Verify exact סימן.” The body also says “the שלחן ערוך writes that ultimately כלל ישראל accepted נפילת אפים as a חיוב” with no note; that is not how the שלחן ערוך reads, as far as I know.

Note 5 (בעל שם טוב). The note says the acronym is “widely cited in חסידי literature; verify exact citation,” i.e. the locus is unknown. The verse is also elided: בראשית כ"ב:כ"ד names טבח, גחם, תחש and מעכה, and the note cuts them with ellipses.

Note 6 (לבוש). The note says the לבוש “codifies the various early customs” and refers to לבוש החור, with no quotation. The body says the לבוש “mentions a different קפיטל”; the note says it references the נוסח “linked to פרק כ"ה as well as the פרק ו' alternative”; the two are not the same claim.

Note 7 vs. the body. The body says the ספרדים “do not fall” at all; the note says they recite the קפיטל “seated.” The note also refers to “the סכנת אריכות ימים,” a phrase that reads as the opposite of the danger intended (a shortened life).

Note 8 (שמואל ב' כ"ד; דברי הימים א' כ"א). The body gives seven years of famine; as far as I know the parallel in דברי הימים gives three, which the note should mention. The note’s last sentence (“should be cross-checked against the לבוש, מגן אברהם, and the משנה ברורה”) is an unresolved instruction.

Notes 9–11 (Epstein, Emden, gematria). See Section 3. Note 10 gives Emden’s dates (1697–1776) and Torat HaKenaot “(Altona, 1752)” and adds בית יהונתן הסופר, which is not in the body. Note 9 says Epstein was “a popular halachic and ethical compiler” with no source. The text says Emden obtained a copy and “issued a קול קורא”; no source is given for that act.

Note 12 (ביאור הגר"א; משנה ברורה). The note attributes the omission to the גר"א “in his מעשה רב and ביאור הגר"א” and the משנה ברורה without a סימן beyond או"ח קל"א, and adds that “the סדור בני יששכר also omits it” — the בני יששכר is, as far as I know, a Galician Chassidic work by R. Tzvi Elimelech of Dinov, which does not fit “a famous ספר from an Italian מקובל” in the body. Two reasons are listed in the note, but the body assigns the second reason to the גר"א alone.

Notes 13–14 (Hanover). Note 13 gives the author’s birthplace, death date and manner of death with no source, and a casualty estimate that conflicts with the body. Note 14 lists three prayers as coming from שערי ציון and says the book went through “dozens of editions,” with no sources. The body says the ש"ך “was also there” and wrote Megillat Eifah; no note.

Note 15 (בבא קמא פ"ב ע"א). The note contains an editorial correction of the body (“Note: שיעור cited ע"ב; the principal passage is ע"א”), while the body still says ע"ב. Fix the body and drop the aside. The Hebrew quotation (“שיהו קורין במנחה בשבת... ותקנו שיהו קורין בשני ובחמישי”) reads as a composite and should be checked.

Note 16 (משנה כתובות). The Hebrew quotation of the opening משנה should be checked against the printed text. The note cites שבת קכ"ט ע"ב for the זוהר-connected איסור of bloodletting, which mixes a גמרא and a זוהר.

Note 17 (כל בו, רוקח). “Verify exact citation in printed editions...” is left in the note, and “the story appears in fuller form in the רוקח” is unsupported. The body names the dream-narrator as a ירושלמי without saying whether this means a man of Jerusalem or a student of the תלמוד ירושלמי.

Note 19 (סנהדרין צ"ה ע"ב). The note cites three texts for the siege and the 185,000, none of which, on the face of the note, supports the “two hours” or the acrostic; the note admits this only obliquely (“an alternative מסורה”). The body says חזקיהו “woke up in the middle of the night,” “went to the בית המקדש,” “davened for two hours,” none of which has a note of its own.

5.Small Polish Suggestions

  • Hebrew density. Almost every noun is a Hebrew inline span, so the English spine is hard to read aloud. Adopt the series convention: transliteration first, Hebrew in parentheses on first use, then one form.
  • Hebrew date and header. The header says “Recorded י"א חשון תשפ"ו · 10 November 2025”; by the standard calendar, 10 November 2025 falls on 19 Cheshvan, not 11. Please check. Delete “Reworked from the original שיעור.”
  • Dedication. The dedication names “הרב חיים” without a surname or the sponsor; a sefer reader will want both, or neither.
  • Transliteration drift. יחיאל/ישראל, עפשטיין/אפשטיין, אלוקי/אלקינו: pick one for running text.
  • Section heading style. Headings mix English phrases with Hebrew in different orders (“נפילת אפים — What Is It, Really?”, “והוא רחום — The תפילה of שני וחמישי”). Standardize.
  • The footer repeats the header and a production note; reduce to one line.
  • Terms of art (the parts of תחנון, קל ארך אפים, מקטרגים) are used without gloss; one-time glosses would serve a general reader.

6.Bottom Line

A readable and often vivid chapter whose final third (the ships, the fire, the dream, the two traditions of origin) is close to publishable. The first two thirds need work before print: resolve the shift from ויאמר דוד אל גד to ויהי נועם in Section VIII; make the gematria compute (or say it is reported); settle the author, name and date of the Kitzur Shelah; reconcile Section VI with the standard practice; quote the lines behind the עזריאל and חזקיהו acrostics; and clear the unresolved “verify” instructions and the conflicts between body and notes (notes 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 12, 15, 17). Then strip the spoken voice and the housekeeping lines. With these, it can stand as a lively chapter in the tefillah series.
