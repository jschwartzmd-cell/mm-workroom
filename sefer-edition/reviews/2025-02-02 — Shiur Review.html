2 February 2025 — בִּרְכַת אֱמֶת וְיַצִיב — Origins, Nusach, and Semichat Geulah L'Tefillah

Content-based review · reviewer read the finished write-up and mareh mekomos · 23 August 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

A continuation shiur that lays out four Rishonic accounts of the origin of Birkat Emet V'Yatziv and adds a fifth from the Daas Zekenim. The Rokeach ties the berachah to a one-hundred-letter (or word) prescription composed in Bavel as a refuah for a plague in Eretz Yisrael; Rashi, via the Shibolei HaLeket and Machzor Vitry, counts eighteen kiyumim at the head of the berachah as preparation for the eighteen berachos of Shemoneh Esrei; the Shibolei HaLeket's own peshat, drawn from the Geonim of Rome, reads ha-davar ha-zeh backward to the three themes of Krias Shema; the Seder HaYom preserves an extraordinary masorah that the berachah is the reply of the diaspora communities to Ezra's call to return — lamah nichpol yegonenu; the Daas Zekenim traces the lashon to Moshe Rabbeinu's self-vindication over the vavei ha-amudim. Around this the Rav braids a historical excursus on the communities of Toledo and Worms, the Charlap lineage, R' Meir Shatz's five-Yisrael nusach of Tzur Yisrael, and the underlying midrash on va-ya'aminu ba-Hashem that grounds semichat geulah l'tefillah. The result is dense, historically alive, and unusually generous with biographical color.

2.What Was Strongest

  • The Rokeach biographical set-piece. The 1196 axe-murder of R' Elazar's wife and two daughters, and his resolution to write down all his Torah as zechuyos for teshuvah, is not decoration — it reframes the entire sefer. Every halachah the audience davens tomorrow morning is quietly recontextualized as a private response to churban. This is the shiur at its best: a historical fact used to shift how a text is read.
  • The five-shitah architecture. Presenting ha-davar ha-zeh as pointing forward in Rashi and backward in the Shibolei HaLeket is a genuinely clarifying machlokes b'peshat. The two readings are not contradictions to be smoothed over; they are two disciplined answers to the same textual ambiguity, and the shiur lets them stand in productive tension.
  • The Seder HaYom's diaspora-reply masorah. Lamah nichpol yegonenu is a startling line, and framing the entire berachah as a document of the galus communities' emunah — embedded in every morning's tefillah so that Klal Yisrael never forgets the brothers who stayed — is a reading with real theological reach. The Toledo/Worms follow-up (Shalsheles HaKabbalah, Seder HaDoros, Pope Gregory IX, the blood-libel defense) turns liturgy into lived history.
  • The Charlap digression. A long detour, but earned. Tracing a name in the siddur-history through Chi'ya rosh l'golei Portugal to R' Yaakov Moshe Charlap and his encounter with Rav Kook shortly before Shavuos is exactly the sort of narrative that makes the shiur memorable a year later. The image — a young Litvishe bachur hearing Akdamos and saying “I had never heard anyone daven like this before” — is the payoff.
  • The midrash on va-ya'aminu. Slipping in, beneath the five historical shitos, the theological engine that actually explains semichat geulah l'tefillah — emunah at Krias Yam Suf drawing shirah and then hashra'as ha-Shechinah — is a well-placed structural move. It says quietly: all the Rishonim are working over one midrashic substrate.

3.What Was Weaker or Worth Pushing Back On

The five shitos are never adjudicated or synthesized. The shiur lists them, illuminates each, and stops. But they cannot all be equally true in the plain historical sense — the berachah cannot be simultaneously a Moshe Rabbeinu composition, an Ezra-era diaspora letter, a Yavneh cover-note, a Bavel-authored plague refuah, and a Roman-Geonim peshat. The audience needs the Rav's own judgment: are these layered (each generation adding to a received text), competing, or all really pointing at the same midrashic substrate through different historical windows? Leaving the reconciliation to the audience blunts what could have been a powerful concluding move.

The Rokeach's 100-letter count is asserted but not tested. Two girsaos (letters vs. words) are mentioned, but no one actually counts. A shiur whose central shitah hangs on a numerical mesorah should either verify the count on the page or explicitly note that the girsaos diverge because different nuschaos of the berachah yield different totals. As stands, the claim is inherited on trust when it could have been demonstrated in thirty seconds.

The Seder HaYom's masorah is presented without any critical framing. R' Moshe ben Machir wrote in 1599, more than two millennia after Ezra. The tradition is beautiful and the shiur clearly wants us to take it seriously as mesorah — but the audience deserves at least a word about where the Seder HaYom might have gotten this, and how it interacts with the plainly earlier Rokeach and Rashi accounts that place composition centuries later. Silence on the source-critical question leaves the strongest shitah also the most exposed.

The Toledo/Worms polemic is left half-stated. The Shalsheles HaKabbalah is invoked in both directions — the communities' blood-libel defense and the counter-claim that they suffered more precisely for not ascending — and then the Rav pivots to a contemporary mussar point about aliyah. The pivot is powerful, but the historical claim about Hakadosh Baruch Hu “keeping His own books” on Worms and Toledo is a serious theological assertion presented in passing. Either it deserves development or it deserves flagging as personal hashkafah.

4.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The berachah ha-semuchah la-chaverta argument is underspecified. The Shulchan Aruch's klal is cited for why there is no opening baruch, but the shiur does not confront the standard kashya: Ahavah Rabbah itself opens with baruch despite being “attached” to Yotzer in some accounts. Which precise formulation of semuchah does the shiur rely on? The Rishonim genuinely differ on when the klal applies, and the answer here affects whether the structural argument works.

Rashi's forward-pointing ha-davar ha-zeh vs. the Shibolei HaLeket's backward-pointing reading is a nafka minah, not just a peshat dispute. If Emet V'Yatziv is preparation for Shemoneh Esrei, then a person davening b'yechidus without Amidah might have less obligation to recite it; if it is a restatement of Krias Shema, obligation attaches to Shema itself. The shiur presents the two shitos as historical, but they carry different halachic weight for edge cases (someone who reads Shema without ability to daven Amidah, someone who davens Amidah without prior Shema). Worth pressing.

The five-Yisrael nusach and its non-recitation by Sefardim. The Rav notes the Ashkenazi/Sefardi split on ve-ne'emar go'aleinu but treats it descriptively. What is the halachic status of an Ashkenazi who omits it, or a Sefardi who adds it? The Rokeach's counter-count (60 letters, 14 words) is not merely poetic — it suggests a real early dispute about what belongs in the berachah. That has downstream implications for siddur practice today.

The Daas Zekenim's account raises a lomdish question about the source of liturgy. If the lashon of emet ve-yatziv ve-nachon originates in Moshe Rabbeinu's public accounting rather than in a chachamim-composed berachah, does that change the character of the recitation — from an enactment of praise to a quotation of vindicating truth? This is the kind of chakirah that the shiur invites but does not pose.

5.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.Do the five shitos stand as historical alternatives, or is the Rav's own view that they are layered — a Moshe Rabbeinu core, an Ezra-era diaspora expansion, an Anshei Knesset HaGedolah embedding, a plague-era numerical shaping, a Yavneh cover-note framing — each generation adding to a received text? A synthesis from the Rav would anchor the shiur.
Q2.The Seder HaYom's masorah about Ezra's letter and the diaspora reply is extraordinary. Does the Rav know of any earlier Rishonic or Geonic source for this tradition, or is R' Moshe ben Machir genuinely its first written witness? If the latter, how does the Rav weigh it against the earlier Rokeach and Rashi accounts that would seem to preclude an Ezra-era composition?
Q3.The Rokeach's hundred-letter/word count — can the Rav walk us through the actual counting? Does our current nusach from ve-yatziv to l'olam va'ed yield exactly 100, and if not, does that suggest a different original nusach or a different starting/ending point?
Q4.Given the Shibolei HaLeket's reading of the berachah as a restatement of the three themes of Krias Shema — and given that Rashi in Machzor Vitry reads it forward toward Shemoneh Esrei — is there a practical nafka minah for someone who has said Krias Shema but cannot daven Amidah, or vice versa? Does the reason for the berachah affect its obligation in unusual cases?
Q5.The Shalsheles HaKabbalah's dual claim about Toledo and Worms — that they successfully defended themselves against blood libels by pointing to Bayis Rishon origins, yet suffered some of the worst pera'os in Europe precisely for not ascending with Ezra — is theologically weighty. Is this the Rav's own hashkafah, or is he presenting the Shalsheles's view? How should we relate to the claim that Hakadosh Baruch Hu “keeps books” on that scale?
Q6.The Daas Zekenim's account traces emet ve-yatziv ve-nachon to Moshe Rabbeinu's vindication over the vavei ha-amudim. Is this meant as a drush-level remez or a genuine historical-linguistic claim? And does the Rav see a midrashic connection between the theme of vindicated truth in Pekudei and the theme of vindicated emunah at Krias Yam Suf that the berachah also carries?

6.Bottom Line

A rich, historically ambitious shiur that succeeds most where it goes deepest into biography — the Rokeach's 1196, the Toledo/Worms shalsheles, the young Charlap in Yaffo. Its weakness is structural: five shitos are laid on the table and left for the audience to reconcile. A single closing paragraph from the Rav — are these layered, competing, or all reflections of one midrashic substrate? — would have converted a superb collection of mareh mekomos into a genuine chiddush. The material is here; the synthesis is the sefer's opportunity.
