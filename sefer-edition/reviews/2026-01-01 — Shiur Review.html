Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

Thursday, 1 January 2026 (12 Tevet 5786) — בית חוניו and the Possibility of Bringing קרבנות Today

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcripts, summary, and finished write-up · 1 November 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

A special Thursday-morning shiur at the Harbor View beis medrash, a continuation of the previous Thursday’s talk on the temples that coexisted with the First and Second Temples (Elephantine, then Beis Chonyo). It opens with a recap and two asides on Beis Chonyo: the connection, suggested by the Divrei Shaul (R’ Yosef Shaul Nathanson), between the Alexandrian artisans of Yoma (who failed to learn the lechem hapanim and the ketores from Beis Garmu and Beis Avtinas) and Chonyo’s temple, balanced by the Targum Sheni on Achashveirosh’s attempt to copy Shlomo’s throne, and the length of time the temple stood (343, 250 or 200 years). It then gives the halachic problem (shechutei chutz), the three accounts of Chonyo (avodah zarah and the problem of the yetzer hara; the Rambam’s Copts; Tosafos: only gentile offerings) and the chiddush of the Toras Chaim (a training facility), with the bamos objection from the room. The second half is the modern question: Rabbi Tzvi Hirsch Kalischer’s Drishas Tzion and the choice of korban Pesach; the Rambam/Ra’avad dispute over kedushah rishonah and its consequence for going up to Har HaBayis; Kalischer’s “either way” argument (a bamah), Rav Kook’s reply in Mishpat Kohen, Rabbi Akiva Eiger’s refusal of his student; the precedent of Rabbeinu Yechiel of Paris (Disputation of Paris, the burning of the Talmud, aliyah, and the report in Kaftor VaFerach); and the practical obstacles (tumah and the tzitz, no Sanhedrin or Kohen Gadol, the priestly garments and techeiles, argaman, the exact place of the altar, the zerikah in the portions of Binyamin and Yehudah, and yichus of kohanim, with the Djerba DNA aside). It ends with Rav Kook to Rav Ovadia Hadaya: only with Mashiach.

2.Overall Fidelity

The finished write-up follows the order of the shiur and keeps most of its content. Three sources were read: the bilingual Plaud transcript (clearest; it glosses each Hebrew word and is authoritative on every point below), the Sofer-style transcript (“Speaker 1”; garbled, e.g. “Kiveiger” for Akiva Eiger), and the AI summary. No Sofer .docx exists for this date, so the Sofer .txt was treated as secondary to Plaud. The pattern is the same as in previous weeks: the write-up inherited its misstatements from the summary. The summary swaps the teacher of Kalischer (Vizhnitz for Akiva Eiger), puts the letter of refusal in the mouth of the Kovno Rav (R’ Yitzchak Elchanan, who was not Kalischer’s teacher), turns Greiditz into Grodno, misnames the Toras Chaim, garbles three speakers in the mizbeach passage, and names three people (R’ Tendler, “Rav Shira Shuler,” R’ Avraham David Auerbach) that the recording does not name. Those are listed below; all were corrected in the final-MM HTML in the same pass, except where marked.

3.Content Flags — Misstatements and Overclaims

Flag 1 — Kalischer’s teacher. The write-up has Kalischer as “a תלמיד of the Vizhnitzer rebbe” (and fn 11 noted the likely error). Plaud: “a תלמיד of רבי עקיבא איגר,” repeated three more times. Corrected. (Reference works also name R’ Yaakov of Lissa.)

Flag 2 — The refusal came from Rabbi Akiva Eiger, not Rabbi Yitzchak Elchanan. Section X and fn 16 had the letter and the “I beg you, do not do it” from רבי יצחק אלחנן ספקטור מקובנא. Plaud: “he was a student of Rav Akiva Eiger… He wrote a letter to Rav Akiva Eiger… I have the teshuvah right here, a famous letter of Rav Akiva Eiger to his student.” The Sofer transcript has “Kiveiger.” Chronologically the summary’s version cannot stand either (R’ Yitzchak Elchanan was born in 1817, Kalischer in 1795). Corrected in the heading, body and fn 16.

Flag 3 — Gutmacher of Greiditz, not Grodno. Plaud: “Rav Eliyahu Gredetzar… Rav Eliyahu Gutmacher” (the Tzaddik of Greiditz), also a student of Rabbi Akiva Eiger, and recognizable “from the הגה in the back of the גמרא.” Corrected to Greiditz, and to a student of Akiva Eiger.

Flag 4 — The author of the Toras Chaim. The write-up has “רב חיים שור” and fn 7 “ר׳ חיים בן שמואל שור, 1572–1632, Lublin/Belz.” Plaud: “ר׳ אברהם חיים שור… תורת חיים, one of the most important ספרים on סנהדרין, בבא קמא and other מסכתות.” Corrected (name; the Jewish Encyclopedia gives his death at Belz in 1632; the birth year was not confirmed and was dropped).

Flag 5 — Who wrote what in the mizbeach passage. The write-up: “רבי חיים זנוויל wrote that the Arabs would never permit… רבי שמעון סופר writes to his son-in-law (the כבוד): ‘you are an old man living in Europe…’” Plaud: Rav Chaim Sonnenfeld, then in Jerusalem, wrote “to one of his friends, Rav Shimon Sofer… an אייניקל of the חתם סופר”; and it was the Chasam Sofer himself, writing to his father-in-law Rabbi Akiva Eiger, who said “you’re an old man living in Europe, you don’t know what’s going on in Eretz Yisrael; I get letters every day.” Corrected. (R’ Shimon Sofer’s title is unclear in both transcripts: “Leyerer Rav,” “L’Cham Soifer.”)

Flag 6 — “Show me תכלת” is Rabbi Akiva Eiger, and the reply is Kalischer’s. The write-up has “רבי אברהם דוד אוערבאך writes: show me תכלת” and “הרב שירא שולר rejects his רבי’s words.” Plaud: “Rav Akiva Eiger right away writes… to his תלמיד, ‘Show me techeiles,’” and “Rav Tzvi Hirsch Kalischer rejects his rebbe’s טענה.” Neither Auerbach nor Shira Shuler appears. Corrected.

Flag 7 — Hutrah is Kalischer’s, not Ishtori’s. Section XII: “רבי אשתורי writes ‘הותרה’ but according to the Rambam that is not correct.” Plaud: “Rav Tzvi Hirsch Kalischer writes: Na-na, it’s הותרה בציבור, it’s fine… It’s not really true according to the Rambam.” (In the earlier Ishtori passage the speaker who “jumps” to טומאה הותרה בציבור is ambiguous in the recording; left as is. Fn 20’s sentence that Ishtori’s reading “presupposes הותרה” therefore rests on the writer’s inference.)

Flag 8 — Ishtori Haparchi is early 14th century, not “the end of the 1200s.” The summary says the end of the 1200s; Plaud has “the late 1300s” (a slip, since the lecturer also says Ishtori wrote about 100 years after Rabbeinu Yechiel, who died c. 1268). Standard references: 1280–1355, Kaftor VaFerach written in 1322. Corrected to “early 1300s.” fn 17 (“a student of the Rosh”) hedged.

Flag 9 — “רבי טנדלר also held that one could go.” Not in Plaud (“Often those held you could go there”) or in the Sofer transcript. Replaced with “Others of this type also hold that one may go.”

Flag 10 — The Rambam “at the beginning of הלכות בית הבחירה.” The lecturer says it (twice); the passage on offering without a Temple and on kedushah rishonah is Hilchos Beis HaBechirah 6:14–16 (checked on Sefaria). The place of the altar is indeed 2:1. The wording was changed to “in הלכות בית הבחירה.”

Flag 11 — Smaller notes. “Lyssa, 1862” (fn 11) is Lyck, 1862 (the lecturer said 1861). The “Parah Adumah” in Section V (“without a פרה אדומה there is no purification”) is a gloss: the lecturer says only that טומאה וטהרה is the issue “until Mashiach comes.” True as halachah; not his words. Left, and noted in the Sefer Edition as the editor’s.

4.Content Flags — Omissions

Said in the shiur and left out of the write-up. The first two are substantive.

  • The questions of the room on Beis Chonyo. R’ Dovid: the fact that a kohen served there is not proof that the offering was by a gentile (the lecturer: “correct, this is only one shitah”); the Sfas Emes on this, citing Kiddushin. R’ Yitzchak: why was it so important to Chonyo? — the lecturer says he would love a historian to answer. Also Josephus: Chonyo as a grandson of Shimon HaTzaddik, where tradition says son. The lecturer on the Toras Chaim: “he goes back and forth, he is not sure.”
  • The Brisker Rov and Kodshim. The Chofetz Chaim’s urging to learn Kodshim in the write-up is one clause; the shiur has the story of Rav Chaim Brisker bringing his son R’ Yitzchak Zev to the Chofetz Chaim (“you are the future of Kodshim”), the yeshivos that learn only Kodshim (now fifteen or twenty), and Machon HaMikdash and Ateret Kohanim (Rav Aviner), who reenact the service.
  • The Ra’avad’s “כך נגלה סוד ה’ ליראיו” and the dispute whether it means ruach hakodesh (the Rim of Levin: siyata dishmaya), with Torah lo bashamayim hi; and that the Ra’avad says the same in Hilchos Lulav.
  • The Temple Mount asides: the Briskers who stand at the back of the plaza; Satmar going only at night; the grandfather’s joke on a person learning Daf Yomi in Hebrew at the Kosel; those who go up have protocols of purification which are problematic according to the Rambam (partly in the write-up).
  • The “either way” argument in full: Rabbi Akiva Eiger’s mimah nafshach; whether the bamah would be a bamas yachid or bamas tzibbur; the need for a keren yesod.
  • The foundation of the wall: Al naharos Bavel — “ערו ערו עד היסוד בה” for the First Temple, but the Second left a wall; the machlokes Tannaim on the exact spot; and the position of the Ark.
  • Ishtori’s forgotten questions: he forgot to ask Reb Boruch about tumah and bigdei kehunah (the write-up has only tumah).
  • Rabbeinu Yechiel: the Mélenchon aside; where he settled and whether he returned to France (“a whole question”); Rashi’s karsi/yarok on techeiles; Rav Elyashiv’s techeiles (raised by a congregant).
  • The end: Rav Tzvi Yehudah Kook (and the Nazir, Rav David Cohen) and the disagreement among Rav Kook’s students; the young men with a shofar on the Mount (“nothing is going to happen until Mashiach”). The Djerba note: the terror attack a few years ago.

5.Added or Editorial Material Not in the Shiur

Several footnotes carry background that the lecturer did not say. Most is plausible; some conflicted with what he said or could not be verified.

  • fn 7: dates and place for the Toras Chaim (see Flag 4). fn 17: “a student of the Rosh” (hedged). fn 9: “1810–1875” (not checked).
  • fn 11: the Rothschild and Montefiore correspondence and the “Chasam Sofer’s family” (not in the shiur; not confirmed). fn 12: the Chasam Sofer “generally cautious but supportive in principle” (the lecturer: he is unsure whether we may give on behalf of the lost brothers). fn 20: “the Rosh and others הותרה” (not in the shiur; not verified). fn 21: the Ptil Tekhelet note. fn 22: the author’s full name (Dembitzer), with a working “verify” note. fn 23, 24: biographical detail and a citation (Lechem Shamayim) not in the shiur.
  • fn 8: the claim that the Mishnah “records this as a שבח” is not checked; the Gemara (Yoma 38a, confirmed) has the Alexandrians failing in both crafts.

6.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

  • The Rambam on Chonyo. Checked on Sefaria (Commentary on the Mishnah, Menachos 13:10): Chonyo dressed his older brother Shimi in women’s garments, falsely accused him, and fled to Alexandria; the Egyptians who joined him, called Kavtzar (Copts), offered sacrifices there; the Rambam says it was not idolatry but a violation of the one chosen place, and that it stood 200 years. This fits the lecturer, who adds that it was a cult of personality.
  • Rambam and Ra’avad. Beis HaBechirah 6:14–16 (confirmed): sanctified “for its time and for the future”; offering without a house; Lev. 26:31. The Shechinah rationale (shechinah einah beteilah) is the standard commentary on 6:16, not the text of 6:14. The Ra’avad’s disagreement is as the lecturer states; the consequence for entry in tumah is his own summary of the Ra’avad’s view.
  • Hutrah or dechuyah. Pesachim 77a (confirmed): tumah hutrah is held by R’ Yehudah; R’ Shimon holds dechuyah (not hutrah). The lecturer’s statement that the Rambam rules dechuyah was not retrieved.
  • The Targum Sheni. The retrieved text of Esther 1:2 on Sefaria has Shlomo’s letter, the bird and the Queen of Sheba, and not the artisans; the throne story the lecturer cites was not located (the lecturer himself said it is a story “told to children in cheder”).
  • Altar location and kohanim. Beis HaBechirah 2:1 (confirmed: a very precise place that may never change). That Kalischer relies on chazakah for kohanim and the Chasam Sofer agrees, while the Yam shel Shlomo and others require yichus, is the lecturer’s report; the sources were not retrieved.

8.Bottom Line

A faithful, well-ordered write-up of a discursive and very good shiur. As before, its errors came mostly from the summary, and this week they clustered in one place: who said what to whom among Kalischer, Rabbi Akiva Eiger, the Chasam Sofer, Sonnenfeld and Shimon Sofer, and the techeiles exchange. With those fixed, the Ishtori century corrected, and the room’s questions, the Brisker Rov story and the Ra’avad’s “secret of the Lord” restored, the sefer edition can stand. The weakest footnotes (7, 11, 12, 20, 22–24) state more than the shiur or the sources support and should be hedged.
