Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

1 September 2024 — The End of Pesukei d'Zimra — Maharam of Rothenburg, Vayivarech David, and Shiras HaYam

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · 23 August 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

This shiur takes the question “where does Pesukei d'Zimra actually end?” and uses it to walk the audience through four historical layers piled on top of the six closing Halleluyos of Tehillim. First come two structural halachos that govern the boundary — the prohibition against manufacturing a new pasuk (the chained Halleluyah problem, with the Yom HaShishi parallel), and the anti-shtei rishuyos caution about repetition that constrains chazzanim (the Yossele Rosenblatt / Belz anecdote). Then the biographical set-piece: the Maharam of Rothenburg's seven years in the Ensisheim Fortress, the Tashbatz dictated through the prison window, his refusal to be ransomed at any price, and his fourteen-year post-mortem — framing the takanah of Baruch Hashem L'olam as an emergency structural seal that became a permanent siddur fixture, arriving in Sephardic practice through the Arizal. From there to Vayivarech David, tied both to David HaMelech's foundation-dig against the tehom and to his fundraising hodaah, doubled with Ezra HaSofer's mass-teshuvah shirah from Nechemiah 9 (the two-pillar reading: building and boundary). Finally Shiras HaYam, absent from the Geonic siddurim, positioned after Yishtabach by the Rambam, mandated daily and pre-Yishtabach by the Zohar and the Arizal — unlocked practically through Rabbeinu Avraham ben HaRambam's teaching of koach ha'dimyon, the disciplined imagination that stands one on the dry seabed each morning.

2.What Was Strongest

  • The Maharam of Rothenburg biographical arc. This is the emotional and structural spine of the shiur, and it is told with unusual completeness — the burning of the Talmud in Paris and Sha'ali Serufah Ba'eish, the aborted aliyah, the informer, Rudolf I, seven years of dictation through a window, the migdal poreach ba'avir line from Oholos, the refusal of ransom as a long-war halachic victory, the fourteen years above ground, R' Alexander Wimpen's condition. Every element is loaded, and the takanah lands with proportionate weight because of it.
  • The two-pillar reading of Vayivarech David. The observation that the unit is a composite — Divrei HaYamim for David's building, Nechemiah for Ezra's boundary — is the kind of structural insight that makes an audience notice something in a text they have said thousands of times. Framing them as “building” and “boundary,” both preconditions for geulah, gives the daily recitation a durable interpretive handle.
  • Rabbeinu Avraham ben HaRambam on koach ha'dimyon. A genuinely uncommon teaching brought into practical service: the kavanah of Shiras HaYam is not linguistic mastery but sanctified visualization. The three examples (falling asleep with the Beis HaMikdash, Mashiach's arrival, standing on the seabed) are concrete enough to actually try. The pivot to the modern radio-and-podcast diagnosis lands because the underlying chiddush is real.
  • The Rashi on gomrei Hallel. A small but load-bearing move: anchoring the structural endpoint of Pesukei d'Zimra in a specific Rashi that most daveners have never had pointed out to them. Everything that follows in the shiur depends on this anchor being solid.
  • The chained-Halleluyah / Yom HaShishi pairing. Using the Kiddush pause on Friday night to illuminate the Hassidic chained-Halleluyah practice — both governed by the same pasuk d'lo paskei Moshe principle — is a clean piece of cross-domain teaching that makes both practices intelligible at once.

3.What Was Weaker or Worth Pushing Back On

The mechanism of the “quasi-brachah” seal is asserted, not explained. The shiur states that a cluster of pesukim beginning with Baruch formulations can “function as a structural seal” when a real brachah cannot be invented. But why? What halachic mechanism converts four scattered Tehillim verses into a functional replacement for Yishtabach? Is this a me'ein brachah concept, a hazkaras Sheim Shamayim substitution, a symbolic bracketing without formal halachic weight? The Maharam's genius is precisely this move, and the shiur does not open the hood on it. Without the mechanism, the takanah reads as clever improvisation rather than principled halachah.

The Shiras HaYam section leaves the central halachic tension unresolved. The Rambam's position — recite it, but after Yishtabach — is presented as respectful but historically defeated. Yet the Rambam's structural concern (that Shiras HaYam is a hefsek inside the brachah-frame) is not answered on its own terms. The Zohar and Arizal “overrode” it; but on what halachic ground? Is Shiras HaYam reclassified as part of the shevach of Pesukei d'Zimra? Is it a tzorech ha'tefillah that by definition cannot be a hefsek? Simply saying the sod won here leaves the nigleh problem hanging.

The composite-text observation invites but does not deliver a deeper question. Once the audience sees that Vayivarech David stitches Divrei HaYamim to Nechemiah, the natural question is: who did the stitching, and when? The Geonim? The Anshei Knesses HaGedolah? Ashkenaz? The shiur's “building and boundary” reading is beautiful, but it is a drash on the finished product, not an account of the editorial act. Even a provisional historical answer would strengthen the interpretive one.

The four-pesukim-symbolizing-five-sefarim anomaly is flagged and dropped. The shiur admits that only two of the four extra pesukim are actually the closing pesukim they are supposed to be, then moves on with a general remark about mesorah's reluctance to “fix” received text. The problem deserves more: which two are wrong, which Rishonim flagged it, is there a manuscript trail, and does any current siddur restore the intended verses? This is exactly the kind of textual puzzle that the audience would remember if resolved and will forget if left dangling.

4.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The two Ashkenazi kulos on hefsek operate on different levels. Kavod ha'melech / moreh malchus is a dechuyah concern (danger, honor of monarchy suspends the norm); hefsed meruba is a hutrah-style economic heter. The shiur groups them as parallel permissions triggering the same Maharam-takanah, but the takanah's function may differ: for sakanah cases the seal is post-facto damage control; for hefsed cases it may be a precondition for permitting the interruption at all. Worth teasing apart.

Why does the seal function bidi'avad rather than lechatchilah? If the Maharam's cluster is a valid me'ein brachah, one would expect it to be recited routinely at Kol ha'neshamah as a permanent second frame. Instead it operates only when the interruption actually occurs. This asymmetry — permanent in the printed siddur, functional only at hefsek — is a fascinating halachic anomaly that the shiur passes over.

The Rambam vs. Zohar dispute on Shiras HaYam is a paradigm case. A structural Rishon-level halachic objection is displaced by a Kabbalistic imperative absorbed through liturgical practice rather than through direct halachic argument. This is precisely the kind of tension that lomdish analysis would want to characterize: does the Zohar's ruling function halachically here, or does it operate through a parallel minhag channel that never engages the Rambam's svara at all? Two very different pictures of how Kabbalah and halachah interact.

The Mishnah Berurah's “run to Baruch Hashem L'olam” ruling exposes a scope question. If a permitted hefsek arises mid-Halleluyos, the Mishnah Berurah tells the person to reach Baruch Hashem L'olam first. But how far along in Pesukei d'Zimra must one already be for this to be practical? Does the same rule apply if the hefsek arrives just after Baruch She'amar? The scope of the Maharam's seal is unclarified.

The Shelah's tzedakah-at-Vayivarech David is action-word coordination in a specific technical sense. Not merely giving tzedakah during a tefillah about giving, but ritually re-performing David's mobilization — word and deed together, in the mode of a ma'aseh mitzvah that reinforces its own kavanah. There is a small but real lomdish question here about whether the tzedakah is tefillah-adjacent or tefillah-constitutive.

5.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.What is the halachic mechanism by which the Maharam's Baruch Hashem L'olam cluster functions as a structural seal? Is it a me'ein brachah, a hazkaras Sheim substitution, or something else entirely? And does the Beis Yosef or the Bach engage this mechanism directly, or do they simply record the takanah?
Q2.How does the Rambam's structural objection to Shiras HaYam-before-Yishtabach actually get answered? Do the Kabbalistic poskim reclassify Shiras HaYam as part of the shevach unit (so that it is no hefsek at all), or do they concede the hefsek and hold that the sod requirement overrides? The distinction matters for other Zohar-driven insertions.
Q3.Who actually composed the composite Vayivarech David unit — welding Divrei HaYamim to Nechemiah? Is there a Geonic siddur that already shows the join, or is it earlier? And does the “building and boundary” reading appear in any of the classical peirushei ha'tefillah, or is it a fresh drash?
Q4.Which two of the four extra pesukim after Kol ha'neshamah are the “wrong” ones, which Rishonim flagged the anomaly, and are there siddurim — historical or contemporary — that print the correct closing pesukim of Sefarim 1 and 3?
Q5.Rabbeinu Avraham ben HaRambam's koach ha'dimyon teaching sounds tantalizingly close to what modern people call “visualization” or “guided imagery.” Are there earlier mekoros in Chazal or in the Geonim for a similar discipline, or is this genuinely a chiddush of Rabbeinu Avraham (perhaps informed by the Sufi environment of his generation)? And how do later Rishonim and Acharonim receive it?
Q6.The Maharam's refusal to be ransomed is treated as normative psak for future generations. But the Mishnah in Gittin already forbids paying above market value for captives — so what did the Maharam add? Was his chiddush that even the gadol ha'dor is included in the rule, or that ta'ama of the Mishnah applies specifically to strategic Jewish leadership targets?

6.Bottom Line

One of the strongest shiurim in the series — a small liturgical seam (three lines the Geonim didn't say) opened into a tour through medieval German persecution, the foundations of the Beis HaMikdash, Ezra's mass teshuvah, and a rare Rabbeinu Avraham teaching on the imagination. The biographical spine (Maharam, seven years, refused ransom, fourteen years above ground) does what only good biographical drush can do: it makes a halachic takanah feel humanly earned. Where the shiur retreats is at the two structural halachic seams — the mechanism of the Maharam's seal and the answer to the Rambam's Shiras HaYam objection — both of which are asserted rather than argued. Fix those, tighten the four-pesukim anomaly, and the shiur graduates from “memorable” to “definitive.”
