Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

23 February 2025 — The Three-Part Structure of שמונה עשרה and ברכת אבות

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · 30 August 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

This shiur takes a single architectural question — why Shemoneh Esrei is built as three brachos of shevach, twelve of bakashah, and three of hoda'ah — and uses it as the doorway into the opening of the amidah. The Rav develops a sharpened chanufah problem (why does praise-before-request not turn tefillah into flattery?) and answers it with two complementary terutzim: the Mabit's in Beis Elokim (the praise reshapes the mispallel) and the Terumas HaDeshen's (praise bypasses the mekatregim). Grounded in Moshe Rabbeinu's petition at the opening of Vaeschanan and codified through the lone accepted psak of the otherwise universally rejected Nachum HaModi. The second half turns to Birkas Avos: the Tosfos in Berachos on the missing malchus, the twofold source of Jewish emunah in zeh Keili / Elokei avi, the Bava Metzia narrative of Eliyahu HaNavi waking each av separately and Rabbi Chiya's sons shaking the graves at mechayeh meisim, and the Mishnah in Yoma on hafnei mizrach me'irin ad Chevron. A shiur that ties the words of the opening bracha to their kabbalistic, halachic, and historical scaffolding.

2.What Was Strongest

  • The chanufah framing. The Rav does not soften the question — he sharpens it deliberately (“imagine asking a wealthy friend for a hundred dollars and opening with praise of his middos”). Once the question is that sharp, the two terutzim land with real force. This is the kind of pedagogic move that makes the audience understand why a two-rishon answer was ever needed.
  • The Mabit's inversion. The claim that the praise is for the mispallel's own benefit, not the Ribono Shel Olam's, is a genuinely re-orienting chiddush for most listeners. It reframes the first three brachos from decorative preamble into structural preparation — a functional part of the request itself. The biographical callout on the Mabit (fifty-four years as dayan, the matzeivah in Tzfas that spells out dayan in gematria) is exactly the kind of detail that anchors the source in memory.
  • The Nachum HaModi excursus. A genuinely striking piece of Shas most listeners will not have encountered: an entire tanna whose positions are almost universally rejected, and the one psak the gemara retains from him is precisely the structural rule under discussion. The Bar Minyomi-to-Abaye exchange, the parallel to Rabbi Eliezer ben Hurkanos, and the possible identification with Nachum Ish Gamzu add historiographic texture without derailing.
  • The Bava Metzia narrative on Eliyahu HaNavi. Told with the timing of a good storyteller — the daily learning session, the late arrival, the reason, Rebbi's challenge, the wind at mashiv haruach, the graves at mechayeh meisim, the sixty lashes from a pulsa d'nura. And its integration with the structural question (why the three Avos are named separately) is exactly right: the story is not decoration but the deep ta'am. Excellent shiur-craft.
  • The hafnei mizrach ad Chevron convergence. The Sadigura Rebbe's teaching that a single word (Berditchev) suffices to activate a tzaddik's zechus — used to explain why the tamid opens with Chevron and why Shemoneh Esrei opens with Elokei Avraham — is the kind of synthetic chiddush that ties disparate sources into a single insight.

3.What Was Weaker or Worth Pushing Back On

The two terutzim are presented as complementary but never actually reconciled. The Rav says they are “complementary rather than competing,” but the shiur does not do the work of showing how. The Mabit's answer is psychological / internal: praise recalibrates the mispallel. The Terumas HaDeshen's is metaphysical / external: praise pierces a spiritual barrier. These are genuinely different mechanisms — do they both operate simultaneously? Does the Mabit think there are no mekatregim? Does the Terumas HaDeshen think the mispallel is already properly aligned? A minute of synthesis would have paid off.

The Tosfos answer on malchus is left as an assertion. The claim that mentioning Avraham counts as malchus because Avraham revealed malchus shamayim to the world is presented, but not defended against the obvious counter: if this substitution works, why is it not available in any other bracha? Tosfos presumably has a principled reason (this bracha is specifically about the Avos, so the Avos-connected malchus is intrinsic here in a way it would not be elsewhere), but the shiur states the uniqueness without unpacking it. The alternate tirutz from magen David is mentioned even more briefly and never adjudicated.

The Rabbi Chiya narrative is left theologically bare. The ma'aseh is powerful, but a serious question sits on top of it and is not touched: if Klal Yisrael needs Moshiach, why is the gemara portraying it as a danger that he might come before his time? The obvious answer — that a premature geulah would not carry the redemptive tikkun that Klal Yisrael needs to have earned — is real Torah worth an extra minute. Leaving it as pure narrative dilutes what is actually a substantial theological point about the mechanism of bi'as haMoshiach.

The Rebbe's standing puzzle on Yiddishe kinderlach is mentioned but not developed. That there is no explicit bracha for one's children in Shemoneh Esrei is genuinely striking; the passing gesture toward l'dor va'dor as a possible reading and the practical redirection to shema koleinu are useful, but the theological question — why the anshei knesses hagedolah omitted so central a request — is left hanging. This is not a criticism of the answer, only of stopping short of engaging why the question is a question.

4.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The mechanism of the “first three vs. last three” rule needs sharpening. The Berachos 34a rule bars personal bakashos in the opening and closing three. But is the prohibition an intrinsic din in the nature of shevach / hoda'ah (they are inherently incompatible with request), or is it a structural rule imposed by Chazal that these brachos not be repurposed? The two framings differ on borderline cases — e.g. zochreinu l'chaim, or whether an individual whose specific need happens to match the theme of shevach (say, gevuros for a person facing physical incapacity) may nonetheless not intend request there.

The tzorech tzibbur exception in the Rav Hai Gaon teshuvah is doing more work than acknowledged. If zochreinu l'chaim is permissible because it is a communal need rather than a private one, the principle generalizes: what about communal refuah in a plague year? Communal parnasah in a famine? The Tur's siman 613 discussion leaves room here that the shiur does not walk into — and the practical nafka minah on additions during communal emergencies is real.

The two locations for personal bakashos (shema koleinu vs. after Shemoneh Esrei) have different halachic character. Additions inside shema koleinu are integrated into the formal tefillah, are subject to the gemara's specific allowances (adding b'ein bracha) and to hefsek concerns; standing at length after yihyu l'ratzon is a separate tefillas yachid and is not bounded by the same rules. Practically identical for most mispallelim; halachically not the same instrument.

The zeh Keili / Elokei avi dichotomy has a lomdish payoff the shiur does not press. If Birkas Avos is built to model both the inherited mesorah component and the personally forged zeh Keili component, then a mispallel whose kavvanah is only in one register (say, davening on his father's zechus without a personal relationship, or the reverse) has arguably said the words but not fulfilled the ma'aseh the bracha is architected to accomplish. Whether that is a halachic defect (kavvanah in Birkas Avos as me'akev) or only a diminishment is a real question — especially given that the Shulchan Aruch classifies Avos as the one bracha where kavvanah is me'akev and requires repetition.

5.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.Are the Mabit and the Terumas HaDeshen really giving complementary answers, or are they answering different questions? On the Mabit's framing, the first three brachos would still be needed even for a person without a single aveirah on his record; on the Terumas HaDeshen's, one might expect that a tzaddik gamur in perfect standing has no mekatregim to bypass. Do the two rishonim agree that both mechanisms operate for every mispallel, or is there a real underlying machlokes?
Q2.Why is Nachum HaModi almost universally rejected? The gemara's language — tishtakach divrei ma'ayah — is remarkable, and the only close parallel is Rabbi Eliezer ben Hurkanos after tanur shel achnai, which had a very specific institutional cause. Was there an analogous institutional break with Nachum HaModi, or is his rejection more a matter of the geographic tension between Eretz Yisrael and Modi that Bar Minyomi alludes to? And is there a reason precisely the shevach-bakashah-hoda'ah structure was the one halacha the gemara could not reject?
Q3.The Bava Metzia narrative implies that Rabbi Chiya and his sons possessed a ko'ach ha'tefillah comparable to the Avos. Is this a claim about their personal madreigah, or about a specific power that runs in a family unit — father with sons davening together? And is there any suggestion elsewhere in Shas that such power exists in other family lines, or is Rabbi Chiya uniquely singled out?
Q4.The Rav mentioned in passing that the Yid has no explicit bracha in Shemoneh Esrei for Yiddishe kinderlach. Given how central that bakashah is in every siddur's extra-nusach tefillos and in the tehillim repertoire, why did the anshei knesses hagedolah omit it? Is there a mefarshim tradition that reads it into a specific bracha (beyond the l'dor va'dor gesture), or is the omission deliberate — and if deliberate, what does it teach about the intended function of Shemoneh Esrei?
Q5.The Tosfos in Berachos 40a treats the mention of Avraham as functionally equivalent to malchus in Birkas Avos. Why is this substitution unique to this bracha? Would the same logic (a tzaddik who revealed malchus shamayim) work for Moshe Rabbeinu, or David HaMelech more directly, in other brachos? And how does Tosfos' account relate to the alternate rishonim who read the malchus at magen David?
Q6.The story of Eliyahu HaNavi receiving sixty lashes from a pulsa d'nura for revealing the secret raises the question of what governs disclosure of hidden Torah. What is the gemara's working principle here — are certain sodos forbidden to reveal because their revelation itself would trigger the outcome (as with Rabbi Chiya's sons), or is the punishment about a general prohibition on gilui sodos that Eliyahu transgressed? And how does the shiur's own framework of open disclosure of kabbalah-adjacent material fit within that principle?

6.Bottom Line

A tightly constructed shiur that uses a single architectural question — the three-part structure of Shemoneh Esrei — as a spine on which to hang the Mabit, the Terumas HaDeshen, Nachum HaModi, Tosfos on malchus, and the Bava Metzia narrative of Rabbi Chiya's sons. The pedagogic craft is high: the chanufah question is sharpened before it is answered, the biographical calluts on the Mabit and the Ketzos-Terumas HaDeshen brothers are placed where they anchor rather than distract, and the hafnei mizrach ad Chevron ending returns the whole shiur to a single word (Elokei Avraham) that now carries the full weight of what preceded. The main weakness is theological: several deep questions — how the two terutzim actually cohere, why premature Moshiach is a danger, why the anshei knesses hagedolah omitted an explicit bracha for children — are surfaced and then left as open surface. A shiur that succeeds beautifully as architecture and needs one more pass to close the theological loops it opens.
