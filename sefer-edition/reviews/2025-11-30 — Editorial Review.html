30 November 2025 — קריאת התורה: Sacred Protocols and the Legacy of the יקעס

Independent review · written from the finished HTML only (the version before the Sefaria corrections); no transcript or summary material used in the findings · 18 October 2026

1.Overall Read

A sixteen-section write-up (roughly 4,800 words of body plus a twenty-note Mareh Mekomos apparatus of about 1,700 words) in three movements: a personal preface (I); a long historical answer to the question why Frankfurt did not say בריך שמיה, running through the Geiger brothers, Hirsch, שבתי צבי and the Ashkenazic suspicion of קבלה (II–VIII); and a walk through the Torah service, from גדלו לה' אתי to the division of honors (IX–XVI). The first movement is moving, the second has the most vivid material (the brothers, the secession, the Amsterdam חרם) and the most problems, and the third is clear and practical but loosely connected to the first two.

The hard editorial issues are seven: (a) chronology and dating conflict between body and notes (the first printing of the זוהר; how and when בריך שמיה entered the סידורים; the dates of Rav Shlomo Zalman Geiger); (b) unsourced narrative claims presented as settled (the Amsterdam חרם, the מהרש"א, the חפץ חיים's private practice, the "known מעשה" of a defective scroll); (c) several note–body contradictions and unresolved "verify" instructions (notes 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 16, 17, 18); (d) Section XV conflates two calls and two songs; (e) a source title in Section XIII that the note itself doubts; (f) a mismatch between a name and its note in Section XIV; and (g) live-shiur residue and first-person voice throughout. Fixing these would make a very readable chapter.

2.What Works

  • The reframing in Section V. "The question is not why Frankfurt did not say בריך שמיה. The real question is: why did we start saying it?" is the best moment of the write-up; it turns a curiosity about one community into a question about the whole Ashkenazic world and justifies the long history that follows.
  • The two brothers (III). The contrast between Rav Shlomo Zalman, who stayed with the מנהגים, and Abraham Geiger, who removed ירושלים from the liturgy, and the thread through Hirsch's חברותא at Bonn, gives the reader a human way into the story.
  • The sequence of opponents of קבלה (V). שבתי צבי → Amsterdam → חתם סופר → מהרש"א → נודע ביהודה → the רמ"א's principle is built in an order that supports the claim, each step with a distinct point (a ban, a silence, a ruling, a rule).
  • The balance of Section VI. The turn to the בעל שם טוב and the חפץ חיים's advice to read even without understanding gives the opposing view fair weight, and the closing line about Frankfurt's steadfastness is earned.
  • The six steps (IX). The story of דוד המלך, the Ark, מיכל, and the stop every six steps is told cleanly and ends exactly where the question started (why this verse, why six words). It is the strongest teaching in the third movement.
  • The practical sections (X–XIII) are short, discrete and each carries a single point (escort; gaze; why after; an uncommon honor; a custom for an expectant father), which suits a book.
  • The personal preface (I) is well controlled, with an arc (forgetting, the reminder, the dedication) and the "Between Two Worlds" callout adding depth without sermonizing.
  • Note discipline. Twenty notes, numbered in body order with back-links, and most carry a short and an expanded form.

3.Where It's Weak or Could Be Sharper

The printing of the זוהר and the arrival of בריך שמיה contradict the notes. Section II says the זוהר "was first printed in 1557 in קושטא," that "within a few decades … בריך שמיה appeared in nearly every סידור," and that "by the mid-1600s it was standard practice." Note 1 says the standard attribution of the first printing is Mantua 1558–60 and Cremona 1559–60, and that the passage came into mainstream סדורים "especially via שערי ציון … (Prague 1662)." A reader cannot hold both: if the passage entered through a 1662 book, it cannot have been universal "by the mid-1600s." Either reconcile (say what "standard" means and for which editions) or present the shiur's timeline as reported and put the correction in the note's first sentence.

The dates for Rav Shlomo Zalman Geiger do not fit the body. The body says he served as a דיין in Frankfurt "in the late 1700s and early 1800s … for many years." Note 2 gives (1792–1878), publication of דברי קהלת in 1862, and calls him the older brother of Abraham Geiger (1810–1874). A man born in 1792 cannot have been serving as a דיין in the late 1700s. Choose the period (probably the mid-nineteenth century if the note is right) and make the body agree. The same sentence places the whole discussion before the 1862 date of his own book, which a reader will notice.

Narrative claims without sources. (i) The Amsterdam חרם (a decree to surrender the של"ה within three days) is stated with specifics, a פנקס הקהילה as the source, no date and no note. (ii) "The מהרש"א … writes: I do not want to hear from זוהר" is given as a quotation; note 5 says the quotation "should be cross-checked." (iii) The חתם סופר's statement is given as a תשובה with no סימן; the note says it is "from his own צוואה and biographical sources," which is not the same thing. (iv) "There is a known מעשה of a פסול discovered after וזאת התורה had been said" is unreferenced. (v) The Hirsch material (eighty families; הקפות "something that had simply not been done in Germany before") is asserted without a note; the latter is, as far as I know, overstated, since dancing on שמחת תורה was known in German communities.

Section VII is the hardest to follow. It carries three separate arguments (the phrase בר אלהים and fear of the authorities; the Yerushalmi on Nebuchadnezzar; the phrase אנא עבדא) and a fourth, the יעב"ץ, that depends on a source the reader is not given. "If the section is a piece from רשב"י, and רשב"י himself elsewhere says you may not say בר אלהים": where does רשב"י say it? Without that, the קושיא cannot be evaluated. The Yerushalmi paragraph also says "at the end of בראשית, when the פסוק says בני אלהים, the תרגום renders it דיינא"; בני האלהים is not at the end of בראשית as far as I know, and "we are forbidden to call them אלהא" is unclear (who are "them"?). The line "These are dangerous claims to repeat publicly" is followed by repeating them; a book should say what it means to say, with the appropriate caution, or say less.

The חפץ חיים paragraph (VIII) is confusing about people. "His grandson, Hillel Zaks — the זאקס family is well known; one zaks is a son-in-law of the חפץ חיים, and מענדל זאקס was a ראש ישיבה in YU": it is not clear whether Hillel is the grandson, or son or son-in-law of someone, or whether Mendel is the son-in-law, and note 10 adds a different arrangement. The anecdote itself (the חפץ חיים did not say בריך שמיה because he did not wish to say אנא עבדא) is a striking claim resting on a single, unsourced testimony; present it as reported, name the witness correctly, and say so.

The quotation of the verse in Section IX. The text quotes "גדלו ה' אתי ונרוממה שמו יחדיו" and counts six words. As far as I know the verse (תהלים ל"ד:ד', as note 11 says) reads גדלו לה' אתי, with a לשון prefix on the divine name; the count of six is right for the verse as it stands but the text as printed has dropped the ל. The heading uses איתי, the body אתי; pick the verse's form.

Section XII has a likely slip. "The גואל who performs גלילה" appears to be גולל (the one who rolls the scroll), which the context supports ("rolling and binding"). The first-person lines ("I have never seen any shul sell it … I have never seen it") are the lecturer's, and a sefer should either attribute them or drop the first person.

Sections XV and XVI. Section XV says the gabbai "announces ברכו," that this is a call for silence, and that משה רבינו "before he sang the שירה of אז ישיר, called out האזינו." אז ישיר is the Song at the Sea (שמות ט"ו) and האזינו opens the Song of דברים ל"ב; they are two different songs, and, as far as I know, the call that the gabbai makes is הכל הבו גדל לאלהינו while ברכו is called by the עולה or the חזן. The section may conflate two calls; note 19 gives דברים ל"ב:א'. Section XVI says the מרדכי rules that each action may be split "and assigned to different people," then lists three persons, while note 20 says the principle underlies "the standard contemporary practice of selling each item separately": assigning and selling are different things, and the note's list (replacing, closing) goes beyond the body's three.

4.Factual / Citation Concerns

Note 1 (זוהר חלק ב', פרשת ויקהל ר"ו ע"א). The note opens by correcting the body ("the standard scholarly attribution … Mantua 1558–60 …"), then states how the passage entered the סדורים; the correction should be in the text, not only here. The locus (ויקהל, vol. II) is, as far as I know, the standard place of בריך שמיה, but the folio was not checked.

Note 2 (דברי קהלת). Dates (1792–1878; "Frankfurt, 1862") conflict with the body's "late 1700s and early 1800s." The note calls him "the leading codifier" and "the older brother of Abraham Geiger (1810–1874)"; the two are not both in the body.

Note 3 (משך חכמה, בחקתי). The reference to "the famous concluding פרשה" is general. Give the verse (probably ויקרא כ"ו:מ"ד) and a short quotation of the Hebrew; the body's "Berlin was their ירושלים" is the reader's whole evidence.

Note 4 (שו"ת חתם סופר). A flagged unresolved "verify exact סימן/biographical citation"; the body calls it a תשובה, the note says צוואה. Move to endnote with an honest caveat or supply the reference.

Note 5 (מהרש"א). "The shiur's specific quotation should be cross-checked." The note adds a genealogical remark ("their shared lineage runs through the Heller family") that is not in the body and is stated without a source; "Lublin (his קבר) was a major center" is a non-sequitur, and I am not sure the מהרש"א is buried in Lublin.

Note 6 (נודע ביהודה). "Verify exact סימן"; the short form gives two responsa (יו"ד מהדורה תניינא צ"ג; או"ח קמ"ז) while the expanded form says the principal one is צ"ג. The body says "in two תשובות"; reconcile.

Note 7 (דרכי משה). The cross-reference to "משנה ברורה כ"ה ס"ק מ"ב" is unexplained; no סימן is given for the דרכי משה.

Note 8 (ירושלמי שבת פ"ו ה"ט; פסחים קי"ח ע"א). "Verify exact location" is left in the note, along with a second cross-reference to פסחים that the note does not explain. The quoted Aramaic ("דָּמֵה לְבַר אֱלָהִין," ג':כ"ה) should be checked against the printed text. The body's "כבר אלהים" is not the verse's wording.

Note 9 (יעב"ץ). The note describes the argument and says it "uses passages such as the בר אלהים language" as evidence, which the body does not support with a citation; "Cited cautiously by the shiur" is a production note.

Note 10 (משנה ברורה קל"ד). Ends with "Verify the biographical citation in printed Chofetz Chaim biographies." The family detail in the note (R' Mendel Zaks, son-in-law) differs from the body's phrasing (see above).

Note 11 (אבודרהם; מחזור ויטרי). The verse is quoted without the ל (see Section 3). The attribution to the מחזור ויטרי is given only in outline.

Note 12 (שמואל ב' פרק ו'; ברכות ס"ג ע"ב – ס"ד ע"א). The note says "sextuplets every birth"; the body says "his daughters had sextuplets." Check against the printed Gemara. The body's "the גמרא discusses different שיטות regarding what was brought" is not referenced; give the page.

Note 13 (מסכת סופרים פרק י"ד; עץ חיים). No halachah number; עץ חיים without a שער. The body's "The שלחן ערוך says one walks the ספר תורה to the בימה" is unreferenced.

Note 14 (מגן אברהם, או"ח קל"ד). No סעיף קטן; the body's "known מעשה" has no note.

Note 15 (אור זרוע (1180–1250)). Gives dates and a character sketch but not the סימן; the claim that the אור זרוע "treats the various כיבודים … including … handing the מעיל and גארטל" is the very point of Section XII and needs a reference.

Note 16 (מחזיק ברכה / מורה באצבע). The short form names two works; the body names a third (מחזה כהונה תמרי תקניין), which the note says to "verify." The body bolds a title the note doubts, and I am not aware of a work by the חיד"א with that name.

Note 17 (כף החיים … ר' חיים פלאג'י). The short form combines two authors of works with the same name (כף החיים of ר' יעקב חיים סופר and of ר' חיים פלאג'י) and ends with "likely רוח חיים." Unresolved.

Note 18 (שו"ת מהרש"ג (שם אריה)). The body names the מהרש"ג as "the שם אריה"; the note says the שם אריה is another author's work and asks the reader to "verify which work is intended." This is a contradiction left in the text.

Note 19 (אבודרהם; סדור הרוקח; מחזור ויטרי on ברכו). Quotes האזינו השמים ואדברה (דברים ל"ב:א'); see Section 3 on the conflation.

Note 20 (אור זרוע; מרדכי). See Section 3. No מסכת or סימן for the מרדכי.

Unannotated claims. The Amsterdam חרם; the חפץ חיים in ראדין; the מיכל childlessness (שמואל ב' ו':כ"ג) and any Gemara on it; משנה ברורה on בריך שמיה (named in the body, in note 10 only as "קל"ד"); the חיד"א's מוסר on talking during the removal of the scroll (the body gives no source); the חיד"א's view that בריך שמיה is to be said before the removal.

5.Small Polish Suggestions

  • Hebrew density. Almost every noun is a Hebrew inline span, so the English spine is hard to read aloud. Adopt the series convention: transliteration first, Hebrew in parentheses on first use, then one form.
  • Live-shiur residue. "Last week we began," "Next week בעזרת השם," "whose קבר we visited in Lublin," "I am sponsoring today's breakfast," "this is the gabbai's domain," and "Last night I discovered" are spoken. Mark them or replace with a neutral voice.
  • Voice of the callout. The "Between Two Worlds" callout shifts into first-person memoir and an aside ("I once attended יום העצמאות services …"). It works, but name the grandfather (and the father) once with their Hebrew names and drop the rest of the first person.
  • Dedication. "לעילוי נשמת ר' יעקב אליהו בן ר' אשר אנשיל הי״ד" can be read as saying that the first man was killed; a book reader needs a sentence of explanation, or the הי״ד should be attached unambiguously.
  • Transliteration drift. בריך שמיה is the form used; Hebrew forms vary (פראנקפורט/פרענקפורט; זאקס/זקס; גייגער/גייגר). Pick one for running text.
  • Terms of art (יקעס, פנקס הקהילה, פארברענטער, הגבהה, פתיחה, KAJ) are used without gloss; one-time glosses would serve a general reader.
  • The header and footer contain production notes ("Reworked from the original שיעור"; "Hebrew terminology preserved in Hebrew script throughout"); reduce to a dateline.
  • Section order. Section IV (Hirsch and the future of German Jewry) interrupts the answer; a bridging sentence at the opening of V ("we return to the question") would help. Section XIV's last paragraph (the מהרש"ג) belongs more naturally after IX.

6.Bottom Line

A readable and often vivid chapter whose centre (the Geiger brothers, Hirsch, the long Ashkenazic suspicion of קבלה, and the six steps of the Ark) is close to publishable. It needs work before print: reconcile the chronology of the זוהר and of בריך שמיה with note 1, and the dates of Rav Shlomo Zalman Geiger with note 2; supply or soften the sources for the Amsterdam חרם, the חתם סופר, the מהרש"א and the חפץ חיים story; clear the notes that still read "verify" and the contradictions between body and note (notes 16, 18); restore the verse in Section IX to its form; untangle the two calls and two songs in Section XV; and strip the spoken voice. With these, it can stand as a lively chapter in the tefillah series.
