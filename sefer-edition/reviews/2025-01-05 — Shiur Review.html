Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

5 January 2025 — יְדִיד נֶפֶש — Origin and Journey of the Piyut

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · Hei Teves 5785 · yahrtzeit of Rav Shlomo Molcho

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

A one-off detour from the regular seder ha'siddur, hung on the peg of Hei Teves as the yahrtzeit of Rav Shlomo Molcho. Beginning with a modest-sounding question — why the Rav skips Yedid Nefesh in the morning — the shiur opens outward into one of the most extraordinary historical narratives in post-Expulsion Sephardic history. The Rav traces Yedid Nefesh through five overlapping layers: (1) the mechaber, Rav Elazar Azkari, the “simple” gabbai of the Arizal whose Sefer Charedim theorizes shirei ahavah to the Beloved; (2) the biography of Diego Pires / Shlomo Molcho — self-circumcised Anus, protégé of David HaReuveni, talmid of Rabi Yosef Taitatzik, source of the Beis Yosef's kshorim of tzitzis, prophesier before Pope Clement VII, killed al kiddush Hashem on Yud-Gimmel Teves 1532; (3) the Beis Yosef's Maggid's threefold unfulfilled promise that he would die like Shlomo b'chiri; (4) the resolution — Rav Elazar Azkari and his chevra composed Yedid Nefesh as a coded tefillah that the Beis Yosef not die young, so that Klal Yisrael would receive the Shulchan Aruch; and (5) the piyut's centuries-long journey into the siddur beginning with the Yaavetz at Krias Shema Al Ha'Mitah. The JTS ksav yad's reading “bincha yechidcha” is the linchpin of the whole thesis.

2.What Was Strongest

  • The overall narrative arc. Very few shiurim on a familiar piyut manage to reframe it as a coded petition for the survival of a specific gadol. The Rav's structure — opening with a mundane liturgical question and closing with “next time you say Yedid Nefesh, you are davening for the survival of the man who gave us the Shulchan Aruch” — is exceptionally satisfying, and the audience will never say the piyut casually again.
  • The Molcho biography as historical set piece. The self-circumcision, the 30 days at the gates of Rome as a leper-Mashiach figure, the Pope, the Tiber flood, the cotton stuffed into his mouth before the pyre — these details are told with genuine narrative force and, critically, tied back to halachic anchors (the Sefer HaMefoar, the Sefer Chayas Kinah, the Beis Yosef's tzitzis kshorim). This is Jewish history done properly: not decoration around the halachah, but the water in which the halachah swims.
  • The tzitzis kshorim anchor. The single most memorable detail of the shiur is that our tzitzis today are tied based in part on how Molcho tied his — testified to by the Beis Yosef, confirmed by the Tosfos Yom Tov's personal visit to Prague, brought down by Magen Avraham and Mishnah Berurah. This is a haunting, tangible link from an Anus-turned-talmid chacham's daily practice into every observant Jew's morning. Placing this halachic fact inside the biography — rather than as a dry aside — is a masterstroke.
  • The JTS manuscript and the “bincha yechidcha” reading. The textual proof is what elevates the thesis from beautiful legend to serious historical claim. Grounding the coded-tefillah theory in a specific extant manuscript variant — and explaining Rav Elazar Azkari's later self-edit to “amcha yechidcha” once the danger passed — is exactly the kind of concrete evidence a claim of this size requires.
  • The historiographic honesty. The Rav's insistence that we cannot judge the Jews of 1500 by our post-facto ideological categories — illustrated by the surprisingly effective parallel to Reb Shalom Shvadron's recollection of heimishe Yidden dancing before IDF trucks in 1948 — is a serious methodological point that most maggidei shiur avoid. It gives the audience permission to admire Molcho without needing to sanitize the messianic elements of his career.
  • The Reuveni / Molcho paradox as closing frame. Asking why we venerate one partner and consign the other to the “dustbins of history” — and answering that the difference is limud and halachah — is a real hashkafah point about what confers legitimacy in our mesorah.

3.What Was Weaker or Worth Pushing Back On

The central thesis is not sourced with the rigor its size demands. The claim — that Yedid Nefesh was composed as a coded petition for the Beis Yosef's survival — is spectacular. But nowhere in the shiur is a text quoted from Rav Elazar Azkari, or from a talmid, or from a later mekubal, that says this. The JTS manuscript proves the variant reading “bincha yechidcha” existed — it does not, on its own, prove the reading refers to the Beis Yosef. The identification of “Your only son” with the Beis Yosef specifically (as opposed to Klal Yisrael as God's ben yachid, the standard reading of “beni bechori Yisrael”) needs an internal source. Where does this mesorah live? Which sefer first records it? Without a name, the thesis floats.

Chronology needs a second pass. Rav Elazar Azkari (~1533-1600) was born after Molcho's death (1532), which the shiur acknowledges. But the Beis Yosef was niftar in 1575; the Shulchan Aruch was already largely complete and had circulated by 1565. If Yedid Nefesh was composed in Rav Elazar's mature Tzfas years (say, 1560s onward), the “we cannot let the Beis Yosef die before he writes the Shulchan Aruch” framing is chronologically strained. The Beit Yosef commentary on the Tur was completed in 1542 and printed in the 1550s. When exactly is the tefillah being composed, and what remained to be written when it was?

The Sefer Charedim reading of the piyut’s purpose is left unreconciled with the thesis. The Rav elegantly quotes Sefer Charedim as saying that ahavas Hashem must be expressed through shirei ahavah, and that this is the framework for Yedid Nefesh. But this framing — a general love-song to HaKadosh Baruch Hu, sung with the chevra shel chaverim — is in obvious tension with the reading of the piyut as a targeted, coded petition. Is the coded reading a sod layer underneath the shir ahavah layer? Both? The shiur presents both readings but does not integrate them.

The Hadras Zekenim narrative is loosely cited. The Lag B'Omer story is one of the most striking pieces of the shiur, but Hadras Zekenim is a Rishonim-era commentary on Chumash, not a nosei keilim on Shulchan Aruch, and its Lag B'Omer / Meron content would postdate the mechabrim by centuries. The citation as it stands is either misspoken or refers to a different sefer (perhaps a later hanhagos compilation). A reader wanting to check the source will not find it.

4.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

Bris milah b'atzmo is a real halachic issue not touched. Molcho performed his own bris. There is a substantial machlokes (see Avodah Zarah 27a and its nosei keilim) on whether one can be mohel himself — the Rambam and Shulchan Aruch YD 264:1 hold he can. Since Anusim as a class were required to mohel themselves in secret, the Molcho story is not just a dramatic biographical detail; it is a halachic maaseh that likely fed the later pesak. Worth raising.

The keitz-calculating issur is invoked but not resolved. The Rav notes the Gemara's “tipach atzman shel mechashvei kitzin” and observes that Molcho did exactly this. If Molcho's messianic activities violated a stated issur, why is he still counted as a talmid chacham whose halachah we follow? The answer presumably lives in the various heteirim the Acharonim extend for tekufos of severe tzar, or in a distinction between chishuv and public activism — but the tension is left standing.

The kabbalas moser nefesh = kiddush Hashem teretz has strong lomdishe consequences. Rav Yaakov Chaim Sofer's teretz — grounded in Sefer Chasidim — that acceptance alone equals the deed is presented as an answer to the Beis Yosef's Maggid's promise. But taken seriously it reshapes an entire body of halachah: the daily “bechol nafshecha” commitment at Shema, the classical machlokes Rambam v'Ramban on whether the mitzvah of kiddush Hashem is fulfilled in machashavah or requires maaseh, and the entire framework of “lo naaseh v'lo asah”. If accepting equals doing, is the Beis Yosef's Shema itself — recited twice daily for eighty-seven years — a fulfillment of the Maggid's promise? The shiur does not press.

Piyutim in the tefillah proper. The Rav notes that inserting extra piyutim into tefillah is “a long, much-debated parshah.” This is understatement: the Rambam's well-known opposition (Teshuvos HaRambam, and the language in Hilchos Tefillah) versus the Ashkenazi mesorah of the Kalir piyutim; the Vilna Gaon's pruning of tefillah additions; the Chabad nusach's selective embrace. A shiur whose whole point is how a piyut migrated into the siddur could have said more about the general jurisprudence of such migrations.

5.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.What is the earliest textual source that identifies the “bincha yechidcha” of the original piyut with the Beis Yosef specifically, rather than with Klal Yisrael as God's ben yachid? Is this from a talmid of Rav Elazar Azkari, a later mekubal, or a modern scholarly reconstruction from the JTS manuscript?
Q2.Chronologically, when was Yedid Nefesh composed relative to the completion of the Beit Yosef on the Tur (~1542) and the printed Shulchan Aruch (~1565)? If both were already in circulation before Rav Elazar Azkari reached his mature Tzfas years, how do we understand the “we needed him to survive to write it” framing?
Q3.The Beis Yosef's Maggid promises kiddush Hashem in Parshas Noach and Lech Lecha. Are there other Maggid Meisharim entries in later parshiyos that rescind or reinterpret the promise once it becomes clear it will not be fulfilled in deed? If so, that internal evidence is worth quoting.
Q4.Given that Molcho engaged in chishuv keitzin in violation of the Gemara in Sanhedrin 97b, why is he counted b'kohol kadosh while Reuveni is not? Is limud alone dispositive, or is there a specific mesorah that Molcho received heter for his messianic activism (e.g., from Rabi Yosef Taitatzik or the pre-Arizal mekubalim)?
Q5.The Beis Yosef records Molcho's tzitzis kshorim as halachah l'maaseh. On what authority? Molcho was not a posek in the Beis Yosef's generation; he was a martyr and a mekubal. Does the Beis Yosef anywhere explain why a hanhagah of a single tzaddik — not rooted in sugya or in earlier Rishonim — enters the Tur?
Q6.The Rav mentions three placements of Yedid Nefesh in modern siddurim (morning, Kabbalas Shabbos, Shalosh Seudos), plus the Yaavetz's original Krias Shema Al Ha'Mitah placement. Which of these does the Rav himself say — and if he skips the morning recitation, does he say it at any of the other times, or has the piyut effectively left his personal seder?

6.Bottom Line

One of the richest shiurim in the collection — a virtuoso weaving of biography, halachah, hashkafah, and liturgical archaeology into a single sustained arc, with a payoff (“you are davening for the survival of the man who gave us the Shulchan Aruch”) that will permanently change how the audience relates to a piyut most of them have said thousands of times. The historical narrative is the strongest element; the halachic anchors (Molcho's tzitzis kshorim, the Sefer Charedim's theory of shirei ahavah, the Sefer Chasidim's kabbalas moser nefesh) are well-chosen and well-placed. The one significant weakness is the sourcing of the central thesis: the “coded tefillah” reading is presented as if the identification of “bincha yechidcha” with the Beis Yosef were as well-attested as the manuscript variant itself, when in fact the identification requires its own mesorah that the shiur never quite names. Tightening that one link would transform a magnificent shiur into an unimpeachable one.
