Mareh Mekamos · Editorial Review

15 June 2025 — Teka B'Shofar in a Time of War

HTML-only editorial pass · craft, prose, footnote discipline, typography · 5 October 2026

1.Overall Read

The writeup is unusually well-shaped for a shiur delivered under crisis conditions. The nine-section arc moves cleanly from the matzav (Sections I–II) into the structural sugya (Megillah), the midrashic origin of “mekabetz nidchei”, the Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer two-horns reading, and the Rav Schwab metaphor supported by the two Brisker stories; the closing (Sections VIII–IX) returns the metaphor to the ground and sends the reader back to the war. The Rabbi Kozak appeal is kept inside Section I rather than exiled to a footer, which is the right call — the appeal is the shiur's mussar. Hebrew-in-script is handled with a light hand, and the mareh mekomos apparatus runs with the correct two-tier (short / expanded) and academic-note distinction established in the series.

What the document needs before it can go to type is almost entirely at the apparatus-and-plumbing level: a footnote-numbering collapse (notes appear in the body in the order 1,2,3,4,11,5,6,13,7,8,12,9,10), a half-dozen Hebrew/English mixed-direction fixes, two biographical slips in the footnotes, one nested-italic callout, and a lead paragraph that is doing too much work for a lead. The Rebbe-steps-back voice-shift that marred the 20 July writeup is not present here — the voice is first-person throughout, with the callout box used (correctly) to carry the one piece of meta-framing.

2.Structural Flow

  • The nine-section architecture is sound, but the Section headings do not fully report the chiddush inside them. Section II is titled “ברכת תקע בשופר — The Hinge” but the hinge framing (personal → communal, with refuah → parnassah → kibbutz as the ascending ladder) is the single most quotable yesod of the opening half. Promote it into the running head: “ברכת תקע בשופר — From Personal to Communal”, or similar. The current heading reads as a preface to a section it is in fact the of.
  • Sections II and III are structurally mismatched. Section II (The Hinge) contains exactly one h3 (“The סדר in מגילה י"ז”) — a sub-head under a parent that has no other sub-heads is editorially orphaned. Either (a) drop the h3 and let the paragraph run inside Section II, or (b) add a second h3 (“Why This Order”) and give the refuah → parnassah → kibbutz ladder its own paragraph. The current one-child h3 pattern repeats also in Section IV (one h3, “The Connection to חצוצרות”, under a parent with only one earlier h3). Decide the pattern once.
  • The Rabbi Kozak appeal sits under Section I as a sibling to “The Hour”, “Trust and השתדלות”, “Seriousness”, “For Israel's Leadership”, “Reb Kozak's Organization”. That is correct in principle, but the five h3's inside a single Section I is denser than any other section in the document; the eye reads it as a mini-TOC. Consider promoting “Reb Kozak's Organization” to its own Section I-bis (or appending it as a short Section X after the Closing), so Section I itself stays focused on the matzav.
  • Section V opens with the biographical cluster (Popack, Meir Weiss, Yisrael Schwab) before introducing Rav Schwab's reading. On the page this reads as footnote material intruding on the body. Lift the mechutan-and-inikl chain into fn13 (which already covers the acknowledgement) and let Section V open with the chiddush: “Rav Shimshon Schwab זצ"ל proposes a different reading of the שופר גדול: it need not be physical…”. The biographical thread earns its line in a footnote; it costs the section's lead when it runs first.
  • Section VI (Rav Chaim of Brisk) and Section VII (Different שיטות) are inverted. As paginated now, the two Brisker stories (VI) land on top of the “Different שיטות” paragraph (VII) and then Section VIII returns to Rav Schwab. The reader's eye expects Rav Schwab → chizuk stories → Rav Schwab again — i.e. Sections V → VI → VIII — with Section VII (Different שיטות) either moved before the Brisker stories (so the Lubavitch/Brisk tension is set up before the Reb Chaim material lands) or dropped into the Section V callout as a continuation. Right now Section VII is a transition that interrupts rather than connects.
  • The lead paragraph tries to telegraph the entire shiur. It names Bnei Brak, Bat Yam, Avinu Malkeinu, the hinge, the midrash on the shevatim, the midrash on the akeidah horns, and Rav Schwab's metaphor — in one paragraph. For sefer-grade pacing this should break into two: one sentence of matzav, one of structure. The current lead reads as an abstract rather than an invitation.

3.Footnote Discipline

Footnote numbers are out of body order. The references in the body appear in the sequence 1, 2, 3, 4, 11, 5, 6, 13, 7, 8, 12, 9, 10. This is the single largest editorial problem in the document. Numbered footnotes must appear in ascending order of first reference, otherwise the apparatus reads as composition scaffolding left behind. The mareh mekomos list at the bottom is already ordered 1–13 ascending, which means the body refs are what needs re-sequencing. Agent 4 should renumber all thirteen refs so the body sequence runs 1–13, and reorder the mareh mekomos list to match. (A quick scan: current fn11 should become fn5; current fn5 → fn6; current fn6 → fn7; current fn13 → fn8; current fn7 → fn9; current fn8 → fn10; current fn12 → fn11; current fn9 → fn12; current fn10 → fn13. Verify before renumbering.)

fn10 has a markup error. The short-form opens “ה' מלך, ה' מלך, ה' ימלוך לעולם ועד” followed by a stray double-close: </em></em> after the closing quotation mark. This renders as text in some browsers and as a floating italic fragment in others. Collapse to a single </em>.

fn3 has a broken Hebrew/English fused clause. The text reads “one of the גדולי הדור of דור the previous generation”. The “of דור” is residue from an aborted phrasing. Delete the “of דור” and let the clause read “one of the גדולי הדור of the previous generation”.

fn7 (Rav Schwab biography) has a factual slip. The note identifies Rav Schwab as a “תלמיד of the חזון איש, רב ברוך בער, and the סבא of Slabodka”. The Chazon Ish is almost certainly wrong here — Rav Schwab's principal rebbeim were the Chofetz Chaim (at Mir), Rav Baruch Ber Leibowitz (Kaminetz), and the Alter of Slabodka. Agent 3 should verify against Rav Schwab's published biographical material and correct the Chazon Ish to Chofetz Chaim.

fn12 (Beis HaLevi / Rav Chaim) attribution of the חזן story. The chazzan's requested brachah for singing in Moshiach's choir is widely transmitted in two forms — one attributing it to Rav Chaim of Brisk, the other to the Brisker Rav (Rav Yitzchak Zev, Rav Chaim's grandson). The current footnote correctly flags that the “precise published source [is] not specified in the שיעור”; Agent 3 should check whether the version in oral mesorah around Rav Chaim specifically is attested in a published collection (Peninei Rabbeinu HaGriz? Uvdos v'Hanhagos?) and either pin the citation or soften the attribution to “a Brisker story told in both the name of Rav Chaim and the Brisker Rav”.

fn5 (Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer). The footnote cites פל"א (chapter 31). The two-horns-of-the-akeidah teaching is in fact in Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer chapter 31 in the standard printed editions, so the citation is correct, but Agent 3 should confirm the daf or os within the chapter for sefer-grade precision — the Radal's edition numbering drifts from the earlier printed editions.

fn4 (Megillah 17b). The citation “מגילה י"ז ע"ב” is correct for the seder of Shemoneh Esrei sugya, which runs Megillah 17b into 18a. Pin the specific sugya line (“ומה ראו לומר…”) within the daf; for the parnassah → kibbutz ladder the key move is on 17b toward the bottom and continues on 18a.

fn6 (Tur). The Tur's connection of Teka B'Shofar to chatzotzros is noted but the siman is not pinned. This is Tur Orach Chaim siman קי"ח (118), which the Beis Yosef expands. Agent 3 to pin.

fn11 (midrash on shevatim's reunion). The note references “משנה ברכות on the סדר of the ברכות; מדרש on the שבטים's reunion”. The Mishnah Berachos citation is loose — the structural sugya is in Megillah (already fn4), not Mishnah Berachos. The midrash on “Baruch mekabetz nidchei” being first uttered at Yaakov and the shevatim's reunion needs a specific pointer — Midrash Tanchuma? Bereishis Rabbah פרשה צ"ד–צ"ה? Agent 3 to locate and pin.

4.Hebrew / Transliteration / Punctuation

  • Mixed-script punctuation inside the lead. The lead paragraph runs seven Hebrew spans in two sentences; the inline-RTL embedding (unicode-bidi: embed on .heb-inline) produces reliable rendering only when the Hebrew is a sealed unit. In the sentence “Then into ברכת תקע בשופר as the hinge of שמונה עשרה — where the personal בקשות close and the communal בקשות begin,” the em-dash sits on the English side correctly, but in several other sentences the Hebrew span is followed immediately by an apostrophe-s or an English possessive (“יוסף's years in גלות מצרים and שמעון's imprisonment”), and the apostrophe migrates visually. Switch unicode-bidi to isolate on both .heb and .heb-inline for sefer-grade typesetting (matches the 20 July editorial-review stylesheet).
  • Transliteration drift. The document commits to preserving Hebrew in script almost everywhere — which is the right policy — but one word is transliterated italic: “aza'akah” (Section I, “Seriousness”). Everywhere else in the series this appears as צעקה / אזעקה. Standardize to Hebrew-in-script. Similarly “פיקוד העורף” is italicized (<em>) in one place and plain in another — pick one.
  • “בעקישע” (Section VII). The Yiddish term appears in Hebrew letters but with English gloss “a frock coat”. The spelling בעקישע is a common transliteration of the Russian/Polish bekishe; the more traditional spelling in sefer-grade print is בעקעשע. Agent 3 to decide the house form.
  • Nikud. The title bar renders תקע בשופר unpointed; the Shiur Review title uses full nikud (תְּקַע בְּשׁוֹפָר). For the sefer-edition series, decide: all pointed, all unpointed, or pointed only for liturgical/pasuk quotations. The 20 July writeup also drifted here, so the issue is series-wide.
  • Hebrew in the italic lead. The .lead rule sets the whole paragraph to font-style:italic. Hebrew glyphs inside an italic span render as roman in most Hebrew fonts (SBL Hebrew, Ezra SIL) and as oblique in a few; the rendering is unpredictable. Explicitly reset font-style: normal inside .lead .heb-inline.
  • “Rav of KAJ”. fn7 names Rav Schwab as “רב of קהל עדת ישורון in Washington Heights for over forty-five years”. He arrived at KAJ in 1958 and served until his petirah in 1995 — approximately thirty-seven years, not forty-five. Agent 3 to correct the figure or drop it (“for several decades”).
  • The “Reb Meir” / “Meir Weiss” loop (Section V, body and fn13). The body sentence reads “Meir Weiss, the מחותן of Reb Meir” — “Reb Meir” has no antecedent. If “Reb Meir” is the Rebbe himself, say so; if it is a third party, name him; if the sentence is corrupted, cut it. Right now the clause reads as a shiur-room aside that was captured without its context.

5.Typography and CSS

  • The Section V callout box wraps its entire paragraph in <em>. Nested <em> inside the already-italic callout renders ambiguously (same issue flagged in the 20 July editorial review). Flatten: either drop the outer <em> and italicize only the quoted phrases, or set the callout italic by class and strip the inline <em>s.
  • The .callout border and the .mareh .note-academic border use the same gold. On the page the two look identical and the reader cannot tell a body-callout (meta-framing) from a footnote academic-note. Give the academic-note border a slightly different weight or color (match the 20 July .factcheck flag-orange).
  • The divider ✦   ✦   ✦ between the main body and the mareh mekomos section is doubled by the .mareh top border. Pick one; the double rule above the mareh list is enough.
  • The .lead paragraph has a left border and italic styling, matching the blockquote rule. On the page the lead reads as a pulled quotation rather than as the shiur's own introduction. Consider dropping the left border on .lead (keep the italic and the muted color).
  • Footer summary. The footer repeats what the lead and the mareh mekomos list already give the reader. For sefer-grade print the footer can be dropped; for the HTML edition keep it short (“Reworked from the shiur of 15 June 2025.”).

6.Attribution Precision

Schwab vs. Hirsch. No conflation present — the writeup cleanly identifies “Rav Shimshon Schwab” and attaches him correctly to KAJ Washington Heights and to Iyun Tefillah / Rav Schwab on Prayer. (The series-wide risk is that KAJ was Rav Hirsch's posthumous spiritual lineage and Rav Schwab's actual pulpit, so a careless editor can slide one into the other; here the writeup gets it right.)

Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer chapter. Chapter 31 is correct for the two horns of the akeidah-ram. Confirm against the Radal edition the os within the chapter.

Megillah 17b vs 18a. The sugya starts at the bottom of 17b (“ומה ראו…”) and continues on 18a. The citation “מגילה י"ז ע"ב” is defensible but incomplete; the fuller form “מגילה י"ז ע"ב–י"ח ע"א” is more honest. Agent 3 to decide.

Lubavitcher Rebbe. The callout attaches זצ"ל to the Lubavitcher Rebbe. Series-wide check: Chabad sources use זי"ע or נבג"מ; זצ"ל is Litvish/Yeshivish-register. If the house style is to adopt the honorific the subject's own community uses, change to זי"ע. If the house style is uniform זצ"ל, keep as is — but decide once.

Rav Chaim Kanievsky quote (fn3). The “kol til yesh lo ktoves” teaching is widely attributed to Rav Chaim during the first Gulf War but also appears in the name of Rav Shach and (earlier) of the Chazon Ish during the War of Independence. The current footnote says “reportedly responded”, which is honest — keep the hedge.

Yisrael Schwab (fn13 / Section V body). The bar-mitzvah connection and the einikl-of-Rav-Shimshon chain is given in the body but not sourced; this is a personal acknowledgement, so no outside citation is needed, but the name spelling and relationship should be confirmed with Rabbi Yossi Popack before print.

Rabbi Kozak (Section I). The organization is named only as “Rabbi Kozak”'s; for the sefer edition the formal name of the organization and (ideally) a reference URL should be added in a footnote, so readers who want to support it can find it. The Rebbe explicitly endorsed it after personal birur; a note that preserves that endorsement serves the mussar of the shiur.

7.Prose Polish

  • Section I, second paragraph: “I just heard: three children killed in Bat Yam. Missiles falling everywhere. Devastation.” The three-fragment telegraphic cadence works — preserve.
  • Section I, “Trust and השתדלות”: “We believe that absolutely. And the חיוב of השתדלות…” — the bold “And” is an oral-register device. In print, drop the bold and let the sentence structure carry the “and-yet” weight.
  • Section I, “Seriousness”: “Tell him: when there is an aza'akah…”. The direct-address second person breaks the register. Rewrite as reported speech: “I asked that he be told: when there is an צעקה…”, or similar.
  • Section I, “Reb Kozak's Organization”: the parenthetical list of co-hosting rabbanim (“Rabbi Asher Stern, Rabbi Aharon, Rabbi Masri, Rabbi Orlian”) uses first-name-only for “Rabbi Aharon”; the other three carry a surname. Either add the surname or drop the first name and standardize.
  • Section IV, “What Is the שופר?”: the opening “Is the שופר physical or metaphor?” is correctly rhetorical but syntactically asymmetric (physical / metaphor is noun/noun with one adjective); rewrite to “Is the שופר physical or metaphorical?”.
  • Section V, “The Misconception”: the paragraph on “beepers… WhatsApp and the rest” is strong but the “the rest” dates immediately. Prefer “beepers… and later WhatsApp and global messaging”, or similar.
  • Section VI, Beis HaLevi quote: “It is not הלכה to say הלל now. If it is not the proper זמן הלכתית…”. The Hebrew phrase should be זמן הלכתי (masculine) agreeing with זמן, not הלכתית. Fix the agreement.
  • Section VI, Rav Chaim's rebuke: “Who do you think you are? Even the greatest צדיקים will be hiding their faces.” This is the shiur's hinge sentence — set it as its own paragraph or callout so it carries the visual weight of the chiddush.
  • Section VIII, the “Imagine when the President of the United States…” paragraph is excellent, but the writeup softens the Shiur Review's two concrete examples (Trump's “if not for the grace of God” after the assassination attempt; Milei crying at the Kotel days before the war). The abstract “World leaders sometimes acknowledge divine providence” in the “Glimpses” sub-section is pale next to the two names. For the sefer, restore at least one of them — Rav Schwab's metaphor becomes concrete only when the “seeds of awareness” are seeds the reader can point to.
  • Section IX (“Closing”): the closing paragraph is strong (“physical or metaphorical, however הקדוש ברוך הוא chooses”) — a nicely humble resolution of the chakirah. Preserve the sentence as written.

8.Opportunities for the Sefer

  • Give the Rabbi Kozak appeal its own closing section. The shiur ends with “May we hear בשורות טובות”; the sefer can end with a short “Postscript” naming the organization, the chayalim it serves, and a line for how to give. The Rebbe said he “checked the organization carefully before lending my name” — the sefer should let that endorsement work.
  • Expand the midrash on the shevatim's reunion. Section III is three sentences; the chiddush — that “mekabetz nidchei” was first uttered before the galus began, by angels watching the family stand complete — deserves a full paragraph. The reunion in Mitzrayim as the prototype of kibbutz galuyos is counter-intuitive (why Mitzrayim? because that was the first re-unification after separation) and the writeup leaves the reader to infer it.
  • Bracket the opening and closing. The lead names Bnei Brak empty; the closing closes on the shofar gadol. A one-sentence return to Bnei Brak at the end — “And then those streets will fill again”, or similar — would close the architecture the lead opens.
  • Add an epigraph. “ותקע בשופר גדול לחירותנו” as the sefer-page epigraph would do real work. The shiur circles this phrase without ever setting it alone.

9.Bottom Line — Prioritized Punch-List for Agent 4

A warm and well-shaped writeup whose structural bones hold and whose chiddush-handling (both midrashim and the Rav Schwab metaphor) is sound. The editorial priorities before type are:

  1. Renumber the footnotes so the body refs run 1–13 in ascending order (currently 1,2,3,4,11,5,6,13,7,8,12,9,10); reorder the mareh mekomos list to match.
  2. Fix fn7: Rav Schwab's rebbeim are the Chofetz Chaim, Rav Baruch Ber, and the Alter of Slabodka — not the Chazon Ish. Also correct the “forty-five years” KAJ tenure (actual ~37).
  3. Fix fn10 (stray </em></em>) and fn3 (“of דור the previous generation”), and clarify the “Reb Meir” antecedent in Section V's biographical cluster (or lift the whole cluster into fn13).
  4. Flatten the Section V callout nested-italics and switch unicode-bidi to isolate on .heb / .heb-inline; reset font-style: normal inside .lead .heb-inline.
  5. Restore the two concrete examples (Trump's “grace of God”, Milei at the Kotel) in Section VIII's “Glimpses”, and reconsider the Section VI↔VII ordering so the Brisker stories land after the Different שיטות framing rather than before it.

Secondary: pin the specific daf/siman citations flagged in §3 (Megillah 17b–18a; Tur סימן קי"ח; Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer os; the mekabetz nidchei midrash); standardize nikud and Yiddish-term spelling across the series; fix the זמן הלכתית→זמן הלכתי agreement in Section VI; give the Rabbi Kozak appeal a proper closing-section postscript.
