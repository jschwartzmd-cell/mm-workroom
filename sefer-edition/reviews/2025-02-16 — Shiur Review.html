Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

16 February 2025 — The Origins of שמונה עשרה — Words of the מלאכים

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · 30 August 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

The second installment in the Shemoneh Esrei series takes as its center of gravity a startling midrash — brought by the Levush, the Beis Yosef, the Kol Bo, the Orchos Chaim, and the Machzor Vitri — that assigns each of the nineteen berachos to a specific moment in Tanach when the malachei hashareis first proclaimed it. To ground the midrash's halachic force, the Rav pivots into a full biography of R' Mordechai Yaffe — birth in Prague, exile, ten years in Mantua absorbing science and Kabbalah, his sharp break with the Maharshal, the cherem for missing an amen, the failed attempts to write his own Shulchan Aruch, and above all the Chofetz Chaim's account (in Nidchei Yisrael) of the noblewoman's trap and the escape through the sewer that gave rise to the ten Levushim. The midrash is then walked through beracha by beracha, with halachic detours on kavvanah in Magen Avraham (Rav Chaim Brisker's two-kavvanos chiddush) and on Birkas HaMinim (the historical mosrim, R' Reuven Margaliyos on Lemberg). The shiur closes on "H' Sefasai Tiftach" — the Gemara's tefillah arichta resolution, and Talmidei Rabbeinu Yonah's stunning derivation that David HaMelech chose tefillah over korban because his pegam with Bas Sheva was not shogeg. A shiur that uses a single opening pasuk and a nineteen-item midrash as scaffolding for a much broader argument about what standing in Shemoneh Esrei actually is.

2.What Was Strongest

  • The Levush biography as halachic argument, not decoration. The Rav does not treat R' Mordechai Yaffe's life as a warm-up act. The biography is the argument: the Levush's halachic kavvanah-reading of the midrash carries weight precisely because we know what kind of posek is speaking. The Mantua years, the confrontation with the Maharshal, the willingness to absorb chachmos chitzonios in a period when his own rebbe polemicized against them — all of this establishes the Levush as an independent halachic mind, which is the reason his gloss on the midrash should be taken as ruling rather than drush. This is genuinely good pedagogy: the framing licenses the conclusion.
  • The Nidchei Yisrael episode. The Chofetz Chaim's account of the noblewoman, the sewer escape, and the kabbalah to write ten sifrei Levush as “clean clothing against the yetzer” is the shiur's emotional peak. The detail that one amidah b'nisayon generated the halachic literature all of Klal Yisrael learns is landed cleanly — theology through biography, exactly what a Sunday-morning tefillah shiur ought to do.
  • Rav Chaim Brisker's two kavvanos. Slipped in as a “detour” on Magen Avraham, but this is the halachic center of the shiur. The distinction between omeid lifnei ha-Melech and peirush ha-milos reframes the whole question of kavvanah in Shemoneh Esrei: not word-by-word focus (which almost no one sustains) but a durable consciousness fixed at the three steps forward. This is a tool the listener can actually use tomorrow morning, and it is the shiur's most portable takeaway.
  • The Rabbeinu Yonah closing. The derivation of H' Sefasai Tiftach from David HaMelech's teshuvah after Bas Sheva — and the reading that David chose tefillah precisely because no korban chatas covers meizid — is the sort of chiddush that permanently changes how one enters Shemoneh Esrei. The structural payoff is elegant: the entire tefillah-korban theme of the series is compressed into a single verse said three times a day.
  • The topical anchor to Sasha Trupanov. Framing mekabetz nidchei amo Yisrael around a freed hostage putting on tefillin for the first time — on Parshas Yisro, in the week of the Aseres HaDibros — is not mere current-events window-dressing; it makes the midrash's ninth beracha land as living halacha.

3.What Was Weaker or Worth Pushing Back On

The midrash itself is under-scrutinized. The midrash is the structural spine of the shiur, but its provenance is left vague — a “midrash brought by five rishonim,” possibly rooted in Pirkei d'Rabbi Eliezer. Given that the Tur pointedly does not bring it and the Beis Yosef does, and that some of the assignments feel more drush than pasuk-tight (e.g., chonein hadaas for Gavriel teaching Yosef the seventy languages; hashiva shofteinu anchored to both Mattan Torah and Krias Yam Suf), the audience needs to know what genre of midrash this is and how tightly its individual attributions bind. The Rav treats the whole package as a monolith; a serious critical eye would ask whether all nineteen entries carry equal weight.

The chronology problem is raised and then dropped. The midrash is not in chronological order — Yaakov's beracha precedes Avraham's refuah; bonei Yerushalayim from Shlomo appears before matzmiach keren yeshuah from Krias Yam Sof. A single sentence points to Megilah 17b and observes that Chazal arranged along a “different axis.” But which axis? If we are being told the words come from the malachim at specific moments, the seam between the moment-sequence and Chazal's arrangement is exactly where the interpretive interest lies, and the shiur walks past it.

The Levush biography, magnificent as it is, crowds out the halachic payoff. The Levush's actual lashon in his gloss on Hilchos Shemoneh Esrei is summarized in a single paraphrased blockquote. Given that this is the halachic linchpin of the entire shiur — the ruling that when we say these berachos we ascend to the madreigah of malachim — more time on the Levush's precise language and on how later poskim received (or ignored) his kavvanah-ruling would have strengthened the argument. Right now the biography does the persuading; the halacha arrives already believed.

The Birkas HaMinim excursus, though gripping, is disproportionate. The Lemberg mosser-in-the-mikveh story is unforgettable, but structurally it is a long tangent inside a single beracha in a nineteen-beracha list. The reader/listener is momentarily unclear whether the shiur is about the midrash, about the halachic history of morideen v'lo maaleen, or about something else. A tighter version would either commit to Birkas HaMinim as its own shiur or trim the anecdote.

4.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

Rav Chaim's kavvanah chiddush deserves the follow-up it did not get. If omeid lifnei ha-Melech is a distinct chiyuv from peirush ha-milos, then the Rema's ruling that Magen Avraham said without kavvanah must be repeated — and the contemporary poskim's retreat from that ruling l'maaseh — needs to be re-analyzed. Are the contemporary poskim saying that peirush ha-milos is what the Rema required and it is now unattainable? Or that the underlying omeid lifnei ha-Melech is presumed present as long as one is standing in Shemoneh Esrei at all? These are two very different positions with two very different practical implications.

The Levush's kavvanah-ruling is a third kavvanah. If we take his gloss seriously — that one must consciously identify with the malachim at the specific historical moment when each beracha was first proclaimed — that is a far more demanding kavvanah than either of Rav Chaim's two. The shiur presents the Levush's teaching as inspiring; the lomdishe question of whether it is l'chatchilah, b'diavad, or supererogatory is not raised. The Chofetz Chaim's five-minutes-on-the-ketores-ingredients model (from the earlier Ketores shiur) would suggest a similar conditional structure here.

Tefillah arichta as a halachic category is more interesting than the shiur allows. The Gemara's answer — that once Chazal instituted H' Sefasai Tiftach it counts as part of Shemoneh Esrei — is treated as a technical patch. But the concept has serious downstream implications: does tefillah arichta mean the pasuk has the din of an Amidah-verse (no talking, no interruption, said standing with feet together)? If it is part of the tefillah, why does one not begin the three steps forward before it? The shiur asserts the category without exploring its edges.

Rabbeinu Yonah's chiddush has a latent lomdishe problem. If H' Sefasai Tiftach is David's declaration that tefillah stands where korbanos stand for a meizid, that maps Shemoneh Esrei onto a korban chatas-substitute framework specifically for meizid — which is odd, because the mainstream k'neged temidim framework maps it onto the daily Tamid, which is not a sin-offering at all. Are these two different accounts of what tefillah does? Do they operate on different berachos? The tension is beautiful and unaddressed.

5.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.What is the actual textual home of this midrash? The shiur names Beis Yosef, Kol Bo, Orchos Chaim, Shibolei HaLeket, and Machzor Vitri as citing it, with a possible root in Pirkei d'Rabbi Eliezer. Has anyone traced whether these five rishonim are quoting a single earlier source or reconstructing independently? And why does the Tur pointedly omit it?
Q2.The Levush's halachic kavvanah-ruling — that when saying each beracha one ascends to the madreigah of malachim at that specific historical moment — is that a chiyuv, a hiddur, or something else? Do the Magen Avraham, the Mishnah Berurah, or later poskim receive it and rule on it, or does it stay as the Levush's solo view?
Q3.Rabbeinu Yonah's derivation of H' Sefasai Tiftach from David HaMelech's teshuvah is stunning — but does it conflict with the k'neged temidim framework? If Shemoneh Esrei is fundamentally a substitute for the daily Tamid, why is the opening pasuk keyed to a korban chatas-style teshuvah scenario, and one that specifically fails as a korban substitute at that? Are we running two theological accounts of tefillah in parallel?
Q4.How does the Levush's emendation on hamachzir shechinaso l'Tzion — that the midrash's original girsa may have placed the proclamation at the second Beis HaMikdash in yemei Ezra — interact with the fact that the Shechina famously did not return to the second Beis HaMikdash (the Gemara's list of five items missing)? If the malachim proclaimed hamachzir shechinaso l'Tzion at a moment when the Shechina was not actually returning, what were they proclaiming?
Q5.The Birkas HaMinim excursus raises a live halachic question that the shiur leaves suspended: today's practice of saying the beracha without directing it at contemporary mosrim. Is the beracha still halachically targeting the historical Christian minim, or has its object shifted, or is it now understood as a general prayer against reshaim? R' Reuven Margaliyos's Lemberg episode implies the halachic category was still operative in the early twentieth century — when did it functionally lapse, and by whose ruling?
Q6.Practically: given Rav Chaim Brisker's two-kavvanos framework, if a person realizes mid-Shemoneh Esrei that his omeid lifnei ha-Melech consciousness lapsed a few berachos back, does he need to do anything? Restart? Re-focus and continue? The Rema's rule about Magen Avraham is discussed; the general question — loss of the omeid lifnei ha-Melech consciousness mid-tefillah — is not.

6.Bottom Line

A rich, structurally ambitious shiur that succeeds at two things simultaneously: it delivers a piece of Torah that permanently changes how the listener enters Shemoneh Esrei (the malachim-midrash, Rav Chaim's two kavvanos, Rabbeinu Yonah on H' Sefasai Tiftach), and it embeds that Torah in the biography of a posek most listeners had never seriously met. The weakness is one of proportion: the biographical and historical set-pieces — the Levush's nisayon, the Lemberg mosser, Sasha Trupanov's tefillin — are so vivid that the actual halachic core (what the Levush's kavvanah-ruling requires, how the two kavvanos of Rav Chaim interact with the Rema, what genre of midrash is bearing all this halachic weight) gets less pressure than it deserves. This is a shiur that a listener leaves inspired; a slightly denser version would leave a listener both inspired and equipped to answer the halachic questions the shiur raises but does not press.
