Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

27 July 2025 — תְּפִלַת נַחֵם and מוֹדִים

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · 5 October 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

A two-movement shiur delivered one week before Tisha B'Av. The first movement opens with the Rebbe's grandfather's riddle — which brachos do we say only once a year? — and lands on the once-a-year chasimah of Nachem: “מְנַחֵם צִיוֹן וּבוֹנֵה יְרוּשָׁלַיִם” The Rabbi Yonatan Eibschutz mashal of the bear licking honey through the siddur frames the once-a-year bracha as a corrective to automatic tefillah. The shiur then walks through the sources: absence from Bavli, presence in Yerushalmi, Rav Saadiah Gaon's “ein hefsed b'amiraso”, the Yerushalmi's me'ein ha-me'ora principle, Rav Muna's past-in-Modim/future-in-Retzeh rule, and the Rif's override on the basis of Avoda Zara 8's me'ein inyan ha-bracha. Siman 557 is unpacked: forgetting in Yerushalayim and recovery in Shema Koleinu (without the chasimah), the non-repetition rule tied to the absence of Mussaf, and a sharp denunciation of the Israeli rabbi who wanted to re-write the nusach toward praise. Then: why only at Mincha (the Rosh's puzzlement, the Mechaber/Rama split, the Rama's Beis Yosef-based reason that the burning began only late in the day), the three-way date problem (seventh in Melachim, tenth in Yirmeyahu, ninth in Chazal), the Karaim's dual fast, and Rebbi Yochanan's shitta that the tenth should have been fixed — tied back to his shitta l'shitaso on eish mishum chitzav in Bava Kamma. The second movement turns to Modim: the four kriyos in Shemoneh Esrei, the frightening Gemara in Bava Kamma about the spine turning into a snake, Tosafos and the luz bone, the nachash as archetype of kafui tov, the halachic depth of the bow (“yispakeku kol chuliyos she-ba-shidrah”) with sensitivity to those with back trouble, and the double sense of modim as both “thank” and “acknowledge/admit.” It closes with the chiddush of an unnamed acharon that the missing bracha for children is hidden inside “l'dor va-dor,” anchored by two personal ma'asim — the all-night tefillah at Meron before leaving Eretz Yisrael, and the three-year-old post-upsherin saying brachos on the magna-tiles.

2.What Was Strongest

  • The Rabbi Yonatan Eibschutz framing is the shiur's structural keystone and the write-up captures it well. The mashal of the bear licking honey through the siddur is not decorative — it provides the shiur's organizing claim: Nachem's power is not primarily its content but its unfamiliarity. The once-a-year chasimah is halachically available precisely so that the mispallel cannot auto-pilot through it. The write-up carries this across cleanly in Sections I-II, and the nimshal paragraph (“Suddenly we wake up. We start thinking about what we are saying. The whole year is implicitly contained in that moment of attention”) is a crisp rendering of the Rebbe's point without over-writing it.
  • The Rav Muna rule and the Rif override. This is the shiur's most elegant piece of structural lomdus and the write-up handles the three-step sequence with discipline: (a) the Yerushalmi's past/future rule applied to Al ha-Nissim and Ya'aleh v'Yavo; (b) the machlokes rishonim on which side of the rule Nachem falls under; (c) the Rif's structural pivot — neither past nor future carries the day; me'ein inyan ha-bracha from Avoda Zara 8 puts Nachem in Birkas Yerushalayim. The write-up correctly notes that our pesak follows neither limb of the Yerushalmi's rule — a nuance the shiur emphasized and that is easy to blur.
  • The non-repetition rule tied to Mussaf. The klal — a me'ein ha-me'ora addition is repeated when forgotten only on a day with a korban Mussaf — is one of the shiur's cleanest pieces of halachic structure, and the write-up presents the Ya'aleh v'Yavo/Nachem contrast with the right economy. This is the sort of principle a reader can carry out of the piece and apply.
  • The lo tasur on altering the nusach. The write-up renders the Rebbe's objection to the Israeli rabbi who proposed rewriting Nachem as praise with the correct force — “without מַלְכוּת בֵית דָוִד, without the בֵּית הַמִּקְדָּשׁ, without קָרְבָּנוֹת, without the כֹּהֲנִים at their stations בְּשִׁירָה וּבְֶזְמְְְָָָָָתרָה — it is destroyed.” The acknowledgement of the real Yishuv, the real Torah u-mitzvos, is in the writeup and matches the shiur. It would have been easy to soften this; the write-up does not.
  • The Rebbi Yochanan l'shitaso tzeiruf. This is the shiur's single most geshmak piece of lomdus: linking Rebbi Yochanan's shitta on the fast date to his shitta that eish is mishum chitzav. If fire is the mazik's continuing arrow, then the burning that peaked on the tenth is one ma'aseh whose severity landed later — and the fast should mark the moment of greatest damage. We hold the fast marks techilas ha-pur'anus, the moment the arrow was launched. The write-up delivers this in Section VII with the right compactness: the Bava Kamma mehalach is sketched, Rebbi Yochanan l'shitaso is named, and the Chazal maskana is given. One of the cleanest paragraphs in the piece.
  • The nachash as kafui tov is the shiur's deepest conceptual pivot and the write-up protects it. The move — Modim's structural posture is the body's refusal to be a kafui tov; the nachash was the original kafui tov; therefore the one who will not bow becomes, in his atzamos, the nachash — is the kind of yesod that transforms a halachic chiyuv into a mehalach. The write-up carries it with the Tosafos's luz-bone discussion flanking it on one side and the “kol atzmosai tomarnah” pasuk on the other — so the chiddush lands with both halachic and philosophical weight.
  • The “l'dor va-dor” chiddush with the two personal ma'asim. This is the shiur's warmest stretch and the write-up gets the balance exactly right — the halachic chiddush is stated plainly (“One אַחֲרוֹן: the בְּרָכָה for children is hidden in l'dor va-dor here in Modim”) and then the two ma'asim — the all-night tefillah at Meron, the three-year-old on the magna-tiles — are presented without over-explanation. The three-year-old ma'aseh in particular is placed where it belongs: as the live demonstration that l'dor va-dor is already being davened in the Rebbe's own house as he prepares the shiur upstairs. The write-up's closing paragraph on this section (“Have כַּוָּנָה at ‘l'dor va-dor’...”) is the one takeaway of the shiur that the reader will carry into tomorrow's Shemoneh Esrei.

3.What Was Weaker or Worth Pushing Back On

The Ritz Geyos digression is dropped from the write-up entirely. The transcript (and the summary's mareh mekomos) preserve a real halachic back-and-forth in response to an audience question about whether one may add a chasimah at the end of Shemoneh Esrei for a forgotten Nachem. The Rebbe raised the shitta of Ritz Geyos (Rav Yitzchak ibn Giyat, eleventh-century contemporary of the Rif) — who holds that since Chazal added a chasimah to the bracha that became part of Krias Shema, forgetting requires repetition — and used it to float the possibility that one might say the Nachem chasimah at the end of Shemoneh Esrei. The Rebbe explicitly flags that he does not know how we paskin (“I am not sure how we paskin actually”). The write-up strips the entire exchange. For a halachic piece on Siman 557 this is a real loss — the question is nogeia l'ma'aseh for a mispallel who realizes mid-Shema Koleinu that he never said Nachem and wants to know whether anything can be done at Elokai Netzor. A footnote at the end of Section III would have cost almost nothing and preserved the shiur's halachic completeness.

The grandfather's riddle is de-personalized. The shiur opens with “My grandfather, when I was a kid, used to ask us a riddle” — a personal frame that locates the once-a-year-brachos list in a specific family mesorah. The write-up flattens this to “An opening riddle.” The content survives; the atmosphere does not. For a Rebbe who returns to mesorah and dor va-dor as the shiur's closing theme, the opening frame — a riddle received mi-saba l'nechdo — is thematically load-bearing. Restoring one sentence is cheap.

The Al Achilas Matzah once-a-year claim is slightly off. The shiur was careful here — the transcript shows the Rebbe explicitly distinguishing Eretz Yisrael (once a year) from Chutz La'aretz (twice, both sedarim) and praising a questioner for noticing the distinction. The write-up lists “Al Achilas Matzah” flat, without the eretz/chutz qualification, so a Chutz La'aretz reader who is told Al Achilas Matzah is said once a year will be puzzled, since he said it twice. Insert “(in Eretz Yisrael)” after the item.

The Avos “lo bakasha” sub-discussion is dropped. In the transcript, when the Rebbe asks why there is no bracha for children in Shemoneh Esrei, an audience member answers that Avos contains “u-l'vnei v'neihem” as a zecher. The Rebbe takes the point and refines it: Avos is shevach, not bakasha; it is an expression of faith that Hashem will bring geulah to the grandchildren, not a request. The write-up jumps straight from “there is no formal bracha” to “One acharon: the bracha for children is hidden in l'dor va-dor.” The Avos candidate — and its disqualification as a bakasha — is the move that sets up the chiddush. Without it, the chiddush arrives slightly unmotivated: the reader has not yet felt the pressure of “where could it be?”

The Mechaber/Rama split carries a small reporting slip. The write-up presents the Mechaber as ruling Nachem at every tefillah and the Rama as ruling only at Mincha. That matches the transcript. But the transcript also records a moment of audience push-back — “Are you one hundred percent sure?” — where the Rebbe himself notes he had been “under the impression that Sfardim did not say it at every tefillah” before confirming from the Mechaber. This hedging — and the real-world fact that current Sephardic minhag varies by community and by nusach (many Sephardim do say at all tefillos of the day; some say only in some tefillos) — is flattened in the write-up to a confident “many Sfardim follow.” This is close enough for a general piece but less sharp than the shiur itself.

The “v'avanu esh atah asid li-vnosah” paragraph has a small text-quotation issue. Section VIII quotes “ובוֹ אֵשׁ אַתָּה עָתִיד לִבְנוֹתהָּ” — u-vo esh. The received nusach is “ובָּאֵשׁ אַתָּה עָתִיד לִבְנוֹתָּה” (u-va-esh). The transcript itself has “u-vo esh”, probably a transcription artifact, so the write-up is faithfully reproducing the audio. For a sefer, the quoted text should be corrected to the standard nusach — otherwise a reader using the piece as a siddur reference will be puzzled.

Footnotes 15 and 16 (Shlomo Simchi and Rabbi Yonatan Eibschutz) read as reviewer additions, not shiur content. The Shlomo Simchi footnote supplies a plausible identification and the Eibschutz footnote supplies a short biographical note — both useful for a reader, both appropriate to a sefer-level annotation, and neither claimed in the shiur. These are fine as editorial notes but should be visually distinguished (as the note-academic styling does — good instinct), and the Eibschutz story's historicity — which the Rebbe himself called out in the shiur (“Don't let the truth get in the way of a good story”) — is cleanly handled in the write-up's own body text (“The famous ma'aseh”) without over-committing. The academic footnote is proportionate.

The write-up's closing paragraph is beautiful but slightly over-written for the shiur. Section XII (“From tefillat Nachem at Mincha of Tisha B'Av...through Modim...into l'dor va-dor...the entire arc of tefillah is the same arc: churban answered by nechamah; kafui tov answered by hakaras ha-tov; broken generation answered by faithful generation”) is a sefer-worthy coda but is not something the Rebbe himself said. The shiur ends more informally with the Rebbe turning to Bernie about seeing the boys. The editorial coda is defensible — a published shiur needs a close — but should be marked as the mesader's synthesis rather than reading as the Rebbe's own words. This is a sefer-editorial question, not a bedikah-of-content question.

4.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The me'ein inyan ha-bracha override deserves one more sentence of pressure. The Rif's move — override the Yerushalmi's past/future rule because Nachem is me'ein Birkas Yerushalayim — is elegant, but it is in some tension with Rav Muna's structural claim. If Rav Muna's rule is really a klal of mivneh ha-tefillah, then why does a me'ein-ha-bracha consideration override it? The answer presumably is that Rav Muna's rule is a default that applies when no specific bracha on-point exists — Al ha-Nissim has no bracha-on-point for Chanukah/Purim in Shemoneh Esrei, so the past/future shape decides; Nachem does have an on-point bracha (Birkas Yerushalayim), so specificity trumps shape. That reading rescues both halachos, but the shiur does not quite work it out, and the write-up does not either. One sentence in Section II would stabilize the entire halachic architecture.

The Rama's “end of day” reasoning is halachically asymmetric with the Mussaf rule invoked earlier. The Rebbe gives the Rama's reason — the burning began only in the late afternoon, so Nachem belongs only at Mincha. But by the same halachic logic, we should also expect Nachem at Ma'ariv of the end of Tisha B'Av (into the tenth, when the fire was actually peaking) — and we do not. The real halachic shape is that Nachem is anchored to the day of the fast itself, said at its latest tefillah. The write-up follows the Rebbe's framing without flagging this — a reader who presses on the Rama's reason will discover it does not quite cover the data. A short “see Mishnah Berurah there” would close this.

The luz-bone/nachash connection could be sharpened on the Tosafos itself. The write-up gives Tosafos's suggestion (luz becomes nachash → no techiyas ha-meisim), the rejection (too harsh), and the maskana (“some lesser punishment, with snake-like characteristics”). What the write-up does not quite surface is why the Tosafos reaches for the luz-bone in the first place — the Gemara's “seven years” is itself an echo of the luz literature's chronology, which gives Tosafos the textual hook. Flagging that one connection would turn Tosafos's otherwise strange leap into a tight exegetical move.

The “kol atzmosai tomarnah” pasuk is doing more work than the write-up credits. The pasuk is cited as the halachic ground for the full bow (“yispakeku kol chuliyos”) — the body's structural participation in the hoda'ah. But the pasuk is also the source for the “mi kamocha matzil ani mi-chazak mimenu” frame, which is specifically about hatzalah — Hashem rescuing the weak from the strong. If Modim's bow is “kol atzmosai”, then the Modim we are davening is specifically a hoda'ah for being the weak-rescued-from-the-strong — which fits the Modim de-Rabbanan language of “mi-dor va-dor nekaveh lach” as the ongoing hatzalah of the klal. The Rebbe did not quite work this out, and the write-up does not either; it is a mehalach waiting in the footnotes.

The Rebbi Yochanan l'shitaso construction deserves a cross-check against the pshat of the Gemara in Ta'anis itself. The tzeiruf is beautiful — the eish mishum chitzav shitta explains why Rebbi Yochanan's takanah would have been the tenth. But the Gemara in Ta'anis gives its own reason in Rebbi Yochanan's mouth: “mipnei she-rubo shel heichal bo nisraf” — the majority of the heichal burned on the tenth. That is a “rov”-based consideration, not a chitzav-based consideration. The lomdish link is still available — perhaps Rebbi Yochanan cares about rov as the moment of damage specifically because he reads the fire as chitzav rather than mamono, so damage-location rather than cause-location governs — but the move is not transparent, and both the shiur and the write-up present the tzeiruf as if it were. Worth marking as a lomdish suggestion rather than a settled connection.

The “no Mussaf → no repetition” rule is more subtle than Section III admits. The write-up gives the klal cleanly, but the Shulchan Aruch's own treatment carries an exception for cases where the forgotten addition is itself part of the bracha's ikar (contrast with insertions that are purely me'ein ha-me'ora). The write-up presents the Mussaf-based rule as self-sufficient; it is better described as the primary rule with Mishnah Berurah working out edge cases. For a shiur-level piece, the current treatment is fine. For a sefer-level piece on Siman 557, a Mishnah Berurah reference would be worth adding.

5.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.On the Ritz Geyos shitta dropped from the write-up: the Rebbe raised, mid-shiur, the possibility that one who forgot Nachem entirely and did not say it in Shema Koleinu might say the Nachem chasimah at the end of Shemoneh Esrei on the Ritz Geyos's model, and flagged that he does not know how we paskin. Does the Rebbe have a maskana? If the Aruch Ha-Shulchan addresses it, which siman, and what is the pesak?
Q2.On the Rif's me'ein inyan ha-bracha override: does the Rebbe hold that Rav Muna's past/future rule applies only in the absence of a bracha-on-point, so that Al ha-Nissim is governed by the Yerushalmi's rule (no on-point bracha in Shemoneh Esrei for Chanukah/Purim) while Nachem is governed by the me'ein-rule because Birkas Yerushalayim is on-point? If so, is this an explicit yesod in the Rif or a reconstruction?
Q3.On the Rama's “end of day” reason: if the halachic principle is “say Nachem when the burning was happening,” why do we not also say Nachem at Ma'ariv of Motza'ei Tisha B'Av (the fire peaking into the tenth)? And conversely, if the principle is “the fast's last tefillah,” why is the Rama's reason given in terms of the burning rather than in terms of the structure of the fast day?
Q4.On the Israeli rabbi who proposed rewriting the nusach: is the halachic objection purely a “shinui me-matbe'a she-tav'u Chazal” point, or is there a deeper claim that the nusach's description of Yerushalayim as “chareivah” is itself halachically accurate — so that any mesader tefillah who tried to update it would have failed on both mesorah grounds and on halachic-description grounds? The Rebbe's language (“Yerushalayim is still destroyed”) suggests the latter; is that a halachic claim or a theological one?
Q5.On the luz-bone and Tosafos: is there a reason the Gemara specifies seven years rather than any other number? And does the “snake-like characteristics” maskana have halachic consequences — for example, in a question like whether such a person is a pasul l'eidus or whether he discharges other chiyuvei kriyos — or is it purely in the realm of sachar ve-onesh?
Q6.On the “l'dor va-dor” chiddush: which acharon is the source? The write-up says “one acharon” without identifying him, and the transcript is the same. If the Rebbe can name the source, the chiddush would gain real halachic weight — it would become a position in the acharonim, not an unattributed vort. Also: if the bracha for children is in l'dor va-dor, is there a corresponding chiyuv at that word (similar to the chiyuv at “modim” itself), or is it purely a kavvanah enhancement with no formal halachic status?
Q7.On the “Mashiach born on Tisha B'Av at midday” mesorah cited for the kulah of sitting on a chair after chatzos: the write-up presents this as the halachic basis for the post-chatzos relief. But Tur and Shulchan Aruch ground the post-chatzos leniencies on “temples burned into the tenth” grounds (Rebbi Yochanan's own point), not on the Mashiach-born mesorah. Is the Mashiach-born point an additional svara that stacks on top, or is it the ikar and the burning-into-tenth consideration secondary?
Q8.On Modim's double sense (“thank” and “acknowledge/admit”): if the two senses genuinely fold together, does the Rebbe hold that one who davens Modim with kavvanah only of “thanks” (and no explicit kavvanah of “acknowledgment”) is yotzei? Or is Modim specifically a bracha whose kavvanah requires both senses — which would make it a far more demanding chiyuv than most mispallelim treat it as?

7.Structural and Editorial Notes

The two-part structure is a chessed to the reader. The write-up's I-XII numbering tracks the shiur's live progression without pretending to a tighter architecture than the shiur itself had. The “Returning to our regular סֵדֶר” hinge at Section IX is exactly how the Rebbe himself re-oriented the shiur, and it reads naturally.

The Hebrew-in-script styling across the piece is consistent and tasteful. The heb-inline treatment keeps the piece readable for an English-primary reader while preserving the Hebrew terminology integrity — a real improvement over all-transliteration or all-Hebrew. The mareh mekomos footnote apparatus with back-reference arrows is well-engineered.

The callout boxes earn their keep. The post-chatzos-chair box (Section VI) and the two “l'dor va-dor” boxes (Section XI) are precisely the sort of warm personal content that should be visually distinguished from the halachic spine — a reader scanning for halachah can skip them; a reader looking for the shiur's neshamah can find them. Good editorial instinct.

The le-ilui nishmas line in the lead is well-placed. The dedication to “Rabbeinu R' Nachman Mendel ben R' Nachman Mendel ha-Levi zt"l" — which the transcript records as the sponsor's opening — is given its own styled lead rather than buried in a footer. For a sefer that will likely function partly as a yahrzeit keepsake, this placement matters.

Section VIII's placement is slightly awkward. The emotional reading of the nusach arrives after Section VII's Rebbi Yochanan l'shitaso lomdus. Logically it belongs between the nusach-not-to-be-altered discussion (Section IV) and the halachic details of when (Section V) — so the emotional charge of the nusach is live before the halachic machinery that governs its recitation. As it stands, the halachic sequence builds, the lomdus peaks, and then the nusach emotion arrives almost as a coda — which is not how the Rebbe said it live. In the live shiur, the “ir ka-almanah” emotion sat inside the nusach-not-to-alter section, as the reason why altering it is unthinkable. Consider moving.

Footnote 10's “chof-beis — chof-gimel” daf range is slightly more precise than the shiur. The transcript cites “Bava Kamma daf chof-beis”; the write-up footnote gives “22a — 23a”. The sugya does span that range, so the write-up is correct and useful — a reader can go look it up. No complaint; a note that the write-up is doing editorial work here.

8.Bottom Line

A faithful and warm rendering of a pre-Tisha-B'Av shiur that carries three durable takeaways — the Rav Muna rule and the Rif's elegant override; the Rebbi Yochanan l'shitaso tzeiruf linking Bava Kamma's eish mishum chitzav to the fast-date machlokes; and the hidden bracha for children in l'dor va-dor, bracketed by the Meron all-nighter and the three-year-old on the magna-tiles. The write-up is almost entirely faithful on content and strong on atmosphere; its one real omission is the Ritz Geyos digression on whether a forgotten Nachem can be rescued at the end of Shemoneh Esrei, which should be restored even as a single footnote. Smaller losses — the grandfather framing of the opening riddle, the Eretz/Chutz qualification on Al Achilas Matzah, the Avos/“u-l'vnei v'neihem” candidate that sets up the l'dor va-dor chiddush — are easily patched with one sentence each. The “u-vo esh”/“u-va-esh” quotation needs a nusach correction. Section VIII is in the wrong place structurally. The closing coda is beautiful but is the mesader's, not the Rebbe's — defensible, but worth marking. Overall: a sefer-worthy chapter after a modest second pass. Worth reading before Mincha of Tisha B'Av next year.
