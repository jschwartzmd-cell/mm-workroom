Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

6 July 2025 — בִּרְכַת יְרוּשָׁלַיִם and צֶמַח דָּוִד

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · 5 October 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

A pair-of-brachos walk-through of Ulirushalayim and Es Tzemach Dovid. The Rav opens with the structural vav-connector between Al HaTzadikim and Ulirushalayim, moves to the Shibbolei HaLeket's midrash of the malachim at Shlomo's chanukas ha-bayis, and then presents the Tur's gematria of 1,800 (opening letters of the nineteen brachos matching the Zohar's 1,800 malachim). The Arizal's preference for “Tishkon” opens the Edus HaMizrach/Sfard-vs.-Ashkenaz split. The mein ha-chasimah kushya is framed around Reb Shabsai Sofer Sagi Nahor of Przemyśl (with a long biographical excursus on his blind-dictated siddur, the haskamos of the Maharsha, Bach, Rama, and Shelah, and the Vaad Arba Aratzos' kol koreh), resolved by the Bach's identification of Yerushalayim with malchus beis Dovid. The Yerushalmi-Tosefta source for the originally merged bracha, the Kalir's krovetz-proof for Purim, and the Taz's definition of krovos follow. The centerpiece is the Arizal's revelation from Shmaya ve-Avtalion in the Gush Chalav me'arah — the kavanah that Armilos ha-Rasha not kill Mashiach ben Yosef — opening into a long eschatology section (Sefer Zerubavel, Rav Saadia, Targum Yonasan, Ibn Ezra's dismissal, the Paras-vs.-Edom war, Har Megiddo). The second half opens Tzemach Dovid: the four names in Sanhedrin 98b, the Chida's “Mashiach Hashem shmo Chaim,” the Baal Shem Tov story about the sod of netilas yadayim for Shalosh Seudos, Rav Schwab on tears watering the tzemach, the “u-metzapim li-yeshuah” printer's-error story, and a sustained Rav S. R. Hirsch quotation on hashpa'as ha-goyim, pre- and post-WWI German mores, and the Jewish-mafia descent (Lansky, Bugsy Siegel).

2.What Was Strongest

  • The Reb Shabsai Sofer biographical excursus. The write-up captures the material well. The combination of blindness, dictated siddur, 600-page hakdamah, the Maharsha/Bach/Rama/Shelah haskamos, and the Vaad Arba Aratzos' kol koreh landed hard in the shiur and lands hard on the page. The detail that the nusach was painted on wooden shul walls so the kahal could compare is a zinger that survives transcription.
  • The Bach's identification of Yerushalayim with malchus beis Dovid. A clean, portable yesod — Yerushalayim is not buildings but malchus — and the write-up ties it correctly to the Yerushalmi/Tosefta single-bracha tradition and the Kalir's krovetz-proof. The architecture here is tighter than most sections.
  • The Shmaya ve-Avtalion cave revelation. The eschatological frame — Armilos, Mashiach ben Yosef, Har Megiddo, Paras-vs.-Edom — is where the shiur earns its title. The write-up handles the Sefer Zerubavel material responsibly, flagging the Ibn Ezra vs. Rav Saadia/Targum Yonasan split, and the mapping of Edom=Western civilization / Paras=Islamic world is preserved as the Rav framed it.
  • The Rav Schwab “tears water the tzemach” passage. Preserved essentially intact, and the Arizal's overlay on “malachei shalom mar yivkayun” and nichum of Rochel Imeinu comes through.
  • The “u-metzapim li-yeshuah” printer's error. A classic maggid shiur set-piece and the write-up preserves the mechanism, the Shabbos 31a hook (“tzafisa li-yeshuah?”), and the Rav's moral riff (“Mashiach is not a bumper sticker”).

3.Omissions — What the Transcript Has That the Write-up Dropped

Critical omission — the Bentching question. In the middle of the mein ha-chasimah section (transcript ~13:36), Reb Dovre interrupts and asks: “Why is this different than Bentching?” — i.e., why does Bentching's Boneh Yerushalayim close without any reference to kisei Dovid, if (per the Bach) Yerushalayim and malchus beis Dovid are inseparable? The Rav explicitly calls it an “excellent question,” says plainly “I don't know — I never even thought of it,” and commits to exploring it. The write-up drops this entirely. For a shiur whose central yesod is Yerushalayim = malchus beis Dovid, the Bentching counter-example is a live kushya on the Bach's own answer and belongs in the sefer — either as a flagged tzarich iyun or with a proposed yishuv.

Critical omission — Bugsy Siegel and Las Vegas. After the Lansky anecdote the Rav continues: “There was another guy — Bugsy Siegel — he started Las Vegas. So not only Israel is ours, Las Vegas is ours.” The write-up keeps only Lansky. The Bugsy Siegel line is the pointe of the aside — the sardonic Jewish-ownership-of-Vegas joke — and its absence flattens the Rav S. R. Hirsch stretch.

Omission — the Rashi in Ta'anis on the Bayis Shlishi. Near the opening (transcript ~04:00) another talmid asks, “Can we build one?” The Rav responds with a brief but substantive aside — there is a machlokes on how the Beis HaMikdash will come, and Rashi in Ta'anis holds the bayis shlishi is already built in Shamayim and will descend. The write-up folds the bayis shlishi point into the Shibbolei HaLeket paragraph as “bnyan olam” but drops the Rashi and the machlokes. For a Birkas Yerushalayim chapter this is a worthwhile mareh makom.

Omission — the Rav's self-awareness about the Baal-Shem-Tov-and-Chida story circulating in Chabad. The transcript records: “I thought I heard the story in Chabad. They say this story also, but everybody has this story.” The write-up presents the story as a single Baal Shem Tov tradition. The Rav's hedging — and the fact that the story circulates in multiple chassidishe traditions — is worth preserving as a mesorah-note rather than smoothing out.

Omission — the identity of the Chida in the shiur: the Rav actually said “Rav Chaim ben Attar.” The transcript (~29:50) is explicit: “He had a nitzotz of the neshama of Mashiach — Reb Chaim ben Attar.” Rav Chaim ben Attar is the Ohr HaChaim HaKadosh, not the Chida (Chaim Yosef Dovid Azulai). The two are different people. The Baal Shem Tov / Shalosh Seudos / nitzotz Mashiach story is classically told about the Ohr HaChaim, and the chronology works (Ohr HaChaim 1696–1743; Baal Shem Tov 1698–1760 — genuine contemporaries, and the Baal Shem Tov outlived him). The Chida (1724–1806) outlived the Baal Shem Tov by nearly fifty years, so the story cannot be about him. See §4 below for how the write-up handled this.

Omission — the Rav's Satmarer vort about his Hungarian shul-trip. Minor, not for the sefer, but noted for completeness: the Rav plugs the upcoming Mitzvah Shem shul-trip to Hungary as a contrast to the Arizal's kivrei tzaddikim circuit (transcript ~18:00). Correctly omitted.

Omission — the Rav's actual framing of the Chida's remez. The transcript's roshei teivos are given as “Mashiach Hashem shmo Chaim” — mem-shin-ches — but the four-letter name Mashiach is spelled mem-shin-yud-ches, and the transcript records the Rav saying “mem shin yud ches — Mashiach Hashem Shmo Chaim.” The acronym is really M-SH-Y-CH = Mashiach Hashem Shmo Yihyeh Chaim (or similar four-word construction). The write-up collapses the yud and gives only three words — the fourth-word slot is dropped silently. Worth a return to the Chida's original piece in Nachal Kedumim / Chomas Anach on Re'eh to recover the full acronym the Rav had in mind.

Minor omission — Berachos context for the opening sugya. The gemara on the seder of the brachos is actually Megillah 17b; the write-up cites Megillah 17b–18a correctly in the footnote but the shiur also leans heavily on the pasuk “sha'alu shalom Yerushalayim yishlayu ohavayich” (Tehillim 122:6) — preserved. Good.

Minor omission — the Rav's hedged sheva on Hananya/Chanina. The transcript records the Rav uncertain on the fourth name: “Hananya, Chanino, something like that.” The write-up renders it crisply as Chanina. In fact the gemara gives it as Chaninah (school of Rav Chaninah) with textual variants — worth a footnote on the variant readings in Sanhedrin 98b.

4.Mischaracterizations and Drift — Where the Write-up Adds Material Not in the Shiur

The Chida/Ohr HaChaim conflation. This is the single most consequential editorial move in the write-up. The shiur, as transcribed, names Reb Chaim ben Attar — i.e., the Ohr HaChaim. The write-up silently substitutes the Chida (Chaim Yosef Dovid Azulai) throughout — renaming the figure, re-sourcing the remez to “close of פָּרָשַׁת רְאוֵה,” and then flagging in footnote 17 that “the historical chronology of the story may reflect a different tzadik ha-dor.” Two problems: (a) the Chida's commentary on parshas Re'eh is Nachal Kedumim/Pnei Dovid; the Rav did not name a sefer, and the sourcing in the footnote may be a reconstruction, not a transmission. (b) The traditional attribution of the Shalosh Seudos/sod story is to the Ohr HaChaim, not the Chida — and the Rav's actual words preserve the correct attribution. The sofer should restore Rav Chaim ben Attar / Ohr HaChaim as the subject of the story, and — if the “Mashiach Hashem shmo Chaim” remez is in fact the Chida's and not the Ohr HaChaim's — name the Chida for the remez and the Ohr HaChaim for the Baal Shem Tov story, as two separate Chaims who each carried a nitzotz.

Footnote 12 mis-cites the sugya. The transcript has the Rav quote (loosely) “asid malchus Paras shiyavo al malchus Edom.” The write-up's footnote cites Yoma 10a with the formulation “asidah Paras she-tipol beyad Romi.” Those are two different gemaras: Yoma 10a's “Paras shetipol beyad Romi” describes Paras falling before Rome, while the “shiyavo al” language — Paras coming against Edom — is Yoma 10a's own continuation (both are there, but the Rav's quotation is the one about war, not the one about Paras's fall). The footnote correctly identifies the sugya but gives the wrong lemma. Sofer should verify in the sugya.

The Gra's “parallel kavanah” is enriched beyond what the Rav said. Transcript: “The Vilna Gaon said something that also needs to be mechaven.” Write-up (fn10): “Rabi Eliyahu mi-Vilna, in his siddur commentary and his kabbalistic writings on tefillah, develops a kavanah on the bracha of 'Es Tzemach Dovid' that parallels the Arizal's.” This is editorial inference — the Rav did not cite the Siddur HaGra or the Gra's kabbalistic writings specifically, and the Gra's kavanos on Es Tzemach Dovid may or may not match. The sofer should verify before leaving the sourcing in the footnote.

Footnote 21 (Vaad Arba Aratzos) and Footnote 22 (Cairo Geniza) are editorial expansions. The Rav mentioned the Vaad and the Geniza in passing; the footnotes supply detailed dating, personnel (Solomon Schechter, Menahem Zulay, Ezra Fleischer), and dissolution dates (1764). Appropriate enrichment for a sefer, but the sofer should mark these as reviewer-supplied rather than attributed to the Rav.

Footnote 11 on Sefer Zerubavel is also an editorial expansion. The Rav did not place the composition in “the early Geonic period (c. 7th century CE)”; the footnote supplies the dating. Fine for a sefer-level apparatus, but again: reviewer-supplied, not from the Rav.

Shift of register in §XI. The Rav's Rav Hirsch stretch is more associative and more jagged in the transcript than in the write-up. The transcript carries real heat on tznius, busha, and mixed swimming; the write-up smooths it into a measured essay. For a sefer this is the right trade, but the sofer should know that the shiur's actual texture is sharper, and the “we have to remember who we are” moment is more charged on the audio than on the page.

5.Transcript Gems Worth Lifting Into the Sefer

  • “Thank God for the Arizal; he provided a lot of parnassah for Yiddishe kinder.” — the kivrei tzaddikim aside about the Tzfat tourism industry. The write-up preserves this; good.
  • The Rav's gentle chavrusa-dynamic with the Sefardi visitor (“Rav Dan”) throughout the Tur / 1,800 / Arizal-nusach section — the “you have more malachim” banter — is edited out. For a sefer this is right. But the live-shiur energy around “you have the tav, we have the vav; so you have four hundred, we have six — you have more malachim, bracha” is a nice shaas ha-cheder moment that could be footnoted as oral mesorah.
  • The Rav's framing that the Vaad Arba Aratzos' authority was “like, I don't want to say Sanhedrin, but a very similar idea” — the hesitation is characteristic and worth preserving in some form.
  • The “mamish ha-roshei teivos of every single bracha” emphasis on the Tur's gematria — the Rav's wonder at the exactness is understated in the write-up and could be restored.

6.Halachic / Lomdish Observations the Shiur Raises but Does Not Close

The Bentching question (see §3 above) is the live kushya on the Bach. If Yerushalayim is incomplete without malchus beis Dovid, then Boneh Yerushalayim at the end of birkas boneh Yerushalayim in Bentching should carry the same thematic burden — and it does not. The pashtus: either (a) the Bentching bracha is halachic-shape only (requesting rebuilding of the ir) while the Shemoneh Esrei bracha is a bakashah for geulah shleimah (requesting the ir-plus-malchus composite), or (b) the Bach's pshat operates at the level of mein ha-chasimah as a technical halachah of bracha-construction, and the Bentching bracha's self-contained closing (with no pre-chasimah kisei Dovid line to explain) simply doesn't trigger the question. Worth developing in the sefer as a tzarich iyun.

The Yerushalmi-Tosefta merged-bracha claim depends on a textual question the shiur does not surface. The Yerushalmi Berachos and Tosefta Berachos cited both have textual variants — some manuscripts list eighteen brachos, some list seventeen, and the inference that Ulirushalayim and Tzemach Dovid were specifically the two that were merged (as against, say, Avodah and Hoda'ah) is one of several possibilities. The Kalir's krovetz-proof is strong but not dispositive — the krovetz's absence of a Tzemach Dovid piece could reflect a liturgical rather than a halachic merger, or an unrelated reason the paytan had.

The Arizal's “Tishkon” as against the Tur's 1,800 is not quite the machlokes the write-up suggests. The Tur's gematria presupposes a count of exactly nineteen opening letters including the vav; drop the vav and you get a different number entirely. The Arizal's stated reason (“we are not concerned with that gematria”) is thin; a sefer-level treatment would want to engage why the Arizal's kavanos-system (opening letters as sheimos ha-malachim) actually prefers “Tishkon”, not just how it is unconcerned with the Tur's calculation. See Pri Etz Chaim on Shemoneh Esrei.

The Mashiach ben Yosef kavanah raises the question whether we daven for his survival or for his eventual victory through death-and-vengeance. Sefer Zerubavel, Pesikta Rabbasi, and the Zohar are not uniform. If Mashiach ben Yosef's death is a necessary step in the eschatological drama (atoning for the chet of the asarah shevatim, say), davening for his survival may be in tension with the received mesorah. The Arizal's chiddush — that the death itself is not mechuyav and klal Yisrael's tefillah can avert it — is a chiddush worth naming as such.

7.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.The Bentching question (Reb Dovre's live kushya): if the Bach's yesod is that Yerushalayim and malchus beis Dovid are a single theme, why does Boneh Yerushalayim in Bentching carry no reference to kisei Dovid? Does the Rav have a yishuv on return?
Q2.On the Chida / Ohr HaChaim question: did the Rav intend Rav Chaim ben Attar (Ohr HaChaim) — whose chronology fits the Baal Shem Tov story cleanly — or Chaim Yosef Dovid Azulai (Chida)? And whose remez on “Mashiach Hashem shmo Chaim” is it in fact?
Q3.On the Arizal's kavanah at “Es Tzemach Dovid”: is the kavanah that Mashiach ben Yosef survive, or that klal Yisrael be spared the churban of his death — even if the death itself remains part of the eschatological script? And how does the Gra's “parallel” kavanah read on the question?
Q4.On the merged bracha in Eretz Yisrael: is the Kalir's silence on Tzemach Dovid in his krovetz sufficient proof, or do we have independent textual evidence from the Geniza siddurim that the merged form was dominant in Eretz Yisrael at the time of the paytan?
Q5.On the Rashi in Ta'anis about the bayis shlishi descending from Shamayim: how does the Rav read this against the Rambam's pesak that Mashiach will build the Beis HaMikdash — and does the bracha of “Boneh Yerushalayim” favor one side of the machlokes?

8.Bottom Line

A rich two-bracha shiur carrying three memorable zingers: the Bach's identification of Yerushalayim with malchus beis Dovid (with the Reb Shabsai Sofer biographical frame), the Arizal's cave-revelation of the Mashiach ben Yosef/Armilos kavanah, and the Rav Schwab “tears water the tzemach” mehalach. The write-up is competent and well-sourced, but misses one critical live kushya (Reb Dovre's Bentching question, which the Rav himself called excellent and left open) and silently substitutes the Chida for the Ohr HaChaim in the Baal Shem Tov / Shalosh Seudos story — the Rav said “Reb Chaim ben Attar.” The Bugsy Siegel punch-line and the Rashi in Ta'anis on the bayis shlishi are also dropped. Several footnotes (fn10, fn11, fn12, fn21, fn22) are editorial expansions beyond what the Rav sourced on the audio and should be marked as such for the sofer. For the sefer: restore the Bentching kushya, resolve the Chida/Ohr HaChaim identification against the recorded audio, re-check the Yoma 10a lemma, and verify the Chida's acronym (mem-shin-yud-ches, four words, not three).
