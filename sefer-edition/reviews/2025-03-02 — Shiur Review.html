Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

2 March 2025 — הַלְכוֹת תְפִלָה — Repetition of Words in חַזְנוּת

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · 30 August 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

Prompted by a virtuoso guest chazan the previous Shabbos, the Rav interrupts the run-through of Shemoneh Esrei for a self-contained halachic essay on whether a shaliach tzibbur may repeat words for the sake of the niggun. The build is orderly: the Mishnah's rule on “Modim Modim” and the gemara's extension to “Shema Shema”; the machlokes rishonim between the Ritva (broad prohibition), Rabbeinu Yonah (with the Ne'ilah-kushia and its resolution from Har HaKarmel), and the Bach (narrow); the Maharam Shik's five reasons; the Machane Chaim's father physically expelling a chazan; Rav Moshe Feinstein's sharp psak in America against Kwartin-era chazanus. The shiur then does something structurally daring: it names, at length, the one posek the entire chazanus world quietly rests on — R' Yehuda Aryeh of Modena — and disqualifies him biographically (gambling addiction, church-melody-borrowing, self-confessed aveiros). The Aruch HaShulchan's parenthetical lament supplies the realist coda: the poskim are screaming and the oilam is not listening. The shiur closes with the practical heter the Rav gave the same chazan for Hallel — grounded in chatzi Hallel's structure and the Mishnah's minhag nevi'im of internal doubling.

2.What Was Strongest

  • The five reasons of the Maharam Shik. Assembled as an actual halachic architecture rather than a list — bal tosif, public embarrassment, hefsek, issur shirah b'tefillah from Sanhedrin, and kavod malchei ha-melachim. Each ground is independent; each carries a different mechanism of prohibition. The Rav's ordering also implicitly ranks them: hefsek is called the “central” reason, which is the honest structural read.
  • Naming R' Yehuda Aryeh of Modena and refusing to sanitize him. Most maggidei shiur would cite the heter and move on; a few would name the mattir and stop there. The Rav does something rarer — he uses the biography as an argument. Whether one accepts the move or not (see below), the intellectual courage is real: pointing out that the sole meikil a whole industry rests on was a self-described gambler who attended church for musical research is the kind of observation that reshapes how one hears the sugya.
  • The Aruch HaShulchan's parenthetical. Anchoring the entire modern situation in a single realist passage — the chazan's tuning fork, then the almost throwaway concession that repetition too cannot be won — is a beautiful pedagogic move. It captures a whole sociological pattern of halacha-vs-minhag ha-oilam in three lines.
  • The Rabbeinu Yonah resolution from Har HaKarmel. Elegant and load-bearing. Turning the “H' Hu HaElokim” problem into a positive category — communal, biblically-modeled, structurally-closing repetitions are simply a different din — supplies the framework that later justifies the Hallel heter at the end. The two bookends talk to each other.
  • The self-check in the opening. “Is this kanaus l'sheim Shamayim, or am I upset that he is undermining my authority?” A single sentence, and it earns the rest of the shiur the standing to be sharp. Without it the entire piece would read as turf-defense.

3.What Was Weaker or Worth Pushing Back On

The ad-hominem disqualification of Modena is rhetorically powerful but halachically underspecified. The Rav essentially argues: this heter should not be relied on because its author was a ba'al aveirah. But halacha does not generally invalidate a psak because of the posek's personal failings — the Chida, cited within the shiur itself, brings Modena as a chashuv talmid chacham. If the argument is that Modena's music-first framework is itself methodologically flawed — treating a musicological convenience as sufficient to override a psak of the Bais Yosef — that is a substantive argument that should be made on its own terms. Grounding the rejection in the biography leaves it vulnerable to the reply that halacha is halacha. The Aruch HaShulchan's faint invocation of Modena, notably, is not made because he trusts Modena but because the oilam has already made the argument for him.

The Bach is treated too briefly. He is a first-tier posek whose lenient reading is the actual halachic counterweight — not Modena. If the Bais Yosef's extension of the Mishnah is genuinely a derabbanan extended by hekesh, the Bach's reluctance to extend is methodologically serious, not merely idiosyncratic. Treating him as a colorful counter-mainstream voice and moving on lets the shiur avoid the harder question: what is the actual halachic status of the issur beyond Modim and Shema — a genuine issur, a gezeirah, or a hiddur?

The Rav Moshe treatment is compressed. A one-paragraph paraphrase of a landmark Igros Moshe teshuvah on a topic that shaped a generation of American chazanus deserves more. In particular, does Rav Moshe engage the Modena heter directly, or does he simply paskin with the Bais Yosef and treat the heter as non-existent? The difference matters for the shiur's own strategy.

4.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The categorization of repetition. The shiur assumes repetition is a single halachic category, but the sources actually distinguish at least four: (1) Modim Modim and Shema Shema — theological problem, meshatkin oso; (2) mid-berachah word-repetition — primarily hefsek; (3) structurally-closing doubled pesukim — permitted lechatchilah per Rabbeinu Yonah; (4) minhag nevi'im repetition inside Hallel — positively obligatory. Each category has a different mechanism (mar'is ayin, hefsek, structural marker, takanas nevi'im). The Maharam Shik's five reasons only cleanly attach to category (2). Sorting this out would tighten the whole sugya.

Bal tosif on a derabbanan. The Maharam Shik's first ground treats repetition inside the fixed nusach as bal tosif, on the reasoning that chazal's takanos are k'ein deoraisa. Rav Ovadia's Yabia Omer is invoked as questioning whether bare repetition (without new content) counts as an “addition” at all. This is a real lomdishe question — is bal tosif defined by content or by act? — that the shiur raises and then leaves. The Rav's own psak (a yachid who missed kavvanah may repeat “Baruch”; a shatz may not) suggests the operative distinction is not bal tosif at all but public vs. private hefsek — worth making explicit.

The Hallel heter's mechanism. Two grounds are given — chatzi Hallel's reduced hefsek concern, and the minhag nevi'im of internal doubling. But these operate differently. The first says the issur hefsek is diminished; the second says the tefillah itself already contains repetition, so further repetition is qualitatively similar. The heter is stronger if grounded in the second: once a tefillah is structurally a doubled-pesukim form, the entire theological concern of “two entities” is off the table by construction. Whether that then licenses any word-repetition inside Hallel or only in the doubled pesukim themselves is a further question.

The removal-from-the-amud psak. The Maharam Shik and the Machane Chaim's father both physically remove the chazan mid-tefillah. This is an unusually forceful halachic remedy that itself requires a source. Is it because the chazan's chazaras hashatz is halachically invalid once he repeats (so the tzibbur is missing chazaras hashatz and must restart with a valid shatz), or is it a kenas? These have different downstream implications — e.g., whether a tzibbur that let the chazan finish has failed chazaras hashatz.

5.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.Does the Rav's rejection of the Modena heter rest on the biography, or on a substantive claim that a musicological sevara cannot override a psak of the Bais Yosef? If the latter, would he reject the same heter if it had come from an unimpeachable posek — and if so, what does Modena's biography actually add to the argument?
Q2.How does the Rav categorize the different types of repetition in tefillah — Modim Modim, mid-berachah word-repetition, structural doubled pesukim, and Hallel's minhag nevi'im? Are these one issur with different applications, or genuinely separate dinim?
Q3.Where does the Rav draw the line on chazan-removal? A chazan who repeats one word once in Aleinu vs. one who repeats systematically in Kedushah — is the halachic response the same, and if not, what is the operative threshold? And is the removal a kenas or a statement that chazaras hashatz is halachically invalid?
Q4.Does Rav Moshe in the Igros Moshe engage the Modena heter directly, or does he paskin with the Bais Yosef as if the heter does not exist? And is there any modern posek — even a lonely one — who defends Modena on the merits rather than dismissing him?
Q5.The Rav gave the chazan a heter for “Ha-E-l” in Avos but not for “Mekimi”. What is the operative principle distinguishing them — is it a shem Hashem factor, a structural position in the berachah, the specific word's semantic self-containment, or something else?
Q6.The Aruch HaShulchan concludes with the meta-klal that one does not raise a great commotion against an entrenched practice. Where does the Rav think that klal stops? If the oilam's attachment to a practice is itself the criterion for halachic tolerance, does that not effectively transfer psak authority from the poskim to the chazanim — the very inversion the Maharam Shik was fighting?

6.Bottom Line

A tightly-built halachic shiur that does something unusual and valuable — it does not just paskin the sugya, it explains sociologically why the psak has not held. The Maharam Shik's five reasons, the Machane Chaim's expulsion, Rav Moshe's sharpness, and the Aruch HaShulchan's resigned parenthetical form a coherent arc. The shiur's central rhetorical move — disqualifying the Modena heter through biography — is powerful but analytically incomplete; the harder and more useful argument is that a musicological sevara is not a halachic counterweight to the Bais Yosef, and that argument is available on the merits without leaning on Modena's gambling. The Hallel heter at the end is a small structural gem that shows the Rav's own halachic seriousness in real time: same chazan, same davening, different tefillah, different psak, and each grounded in a specific mekor.
