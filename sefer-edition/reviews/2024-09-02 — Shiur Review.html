Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

2 September 2024 — אֱמוּנָה בְּבִיאַת הַמָּשִׁיחַ — Belief, Anticipation, and Their Limits

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · 23 August 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

Delivered the morning after the murder of six chatufim in captivity — roughly day 330 of the war — this unplanned shiur takes up the twelfth ikar: emunah b'Bias HaMashiach. It opens with the Rambam's Hakdamah to Perek Cheilek as the source of the thirteen ikrim, notes the Portuguese-1492 provenance of the popular Ani Ma'amin text, and unpacks the twelfth ikar's dual demand — belief in a personal Mashiach of zera Beis David and active chikui for his coming, grounded in Yeshayahu 11, Malachi 3:1, Chavakuk 2:3, and Bilam's nevuah. From there the shiur moves through the plurality of Rishonim frameworks (Saadia's ten, Albo's three, the Maharshal's principled objection, the Arizal not saying Yigdal), addresses Marc Shapiro's Limits of Orthodox Theology, works through the difficult Sanhedrin 99a of Rabbi Hillel, and lands on the central machloikes between the Brisker camp (Rav Moshe, the Brisker Rav — positive daily certainty, kol ha'yom) and the Chofetz Chaim (absence of yeush as the operative baseline), with the pashkevil against Rav Aharon Leib Shteinman as the flashpoint. The halachic ripples — kohanim and wine, haircuts erev Yom Tov, pregnant women and the beis ha'chaim, the nazir vow — show that even the Rambam does not push the emunah to maximal operational conclusion. Closes with a practical rubric for daily chikui and the theological refusal to despair that binds this morning's yeshiva ketana drop-off in America to shiva-houses in Eretz Yisrael.

2.What Was Strongest

  • The occasion frames the material without hijacking it. The opening juxtaposition — six burials yesterday, yeshiva ketana drop-offs this morning — gives the shiur its charge without becoming maudlin. The Rebbe holds the emotional and the analytic in the same frame; by the closing paragraph the connection is earned rather than asserted.
  • The Brisker Rav vs. Chofetz Chaim exposition. This is the shiur's real spine. Presenting both positions with their maximum plausibility — including Rav Moshe's tzertikeit tzertikeit today-tomorrow formulation, the Brisker Rav's kol ha'yom extension, and the Chofetz Chaim's redefinition of chikui as the absence of yeush — gives the audience the full spectrum rather than a partisan retelling. The Reb Chaim distinction between the am ha'aretz and the koifer (intent vs. content) is the key lomdishe move that makes the disagreement live.
  • The Rambam-against-himself point on kohanim and wine. The observation that the very Rambam who requires daily chikui as doctrine paskens that kohanim need not abstain from wine is a genuine chiddush in framing — it internally undermines the Brisker maximalism by showing that the Rambam himself distinguishes doctrinal emunah from operational halachah. This is the sharpest analytic point in the shiur.
  • The Shteinman pashkevil episode. Naming this by name, and calling out the misuse of the label koifer, is a courageous piece of contemporary hashkafah. It makes the abstract machloikes concrete and shows how the theoretical question of what chikui requires plays out in real communal politics.
  • The parah adumah / walkway detail. The image of the raised walkways with gaps against tumas meis from below, and the children raised in absolute taharah from birth for the mei chatas, brings the abstract emunas chikui down to a specific archaeological-ritual practice that most listeners will not have heard framed this way. Ties a widespread minhag (pregnant women avoiding the beis ha'chaim) to the twelfth ikar with real force.

3.What Was Weaker or Worth Pushing Back On

The Marc Shapiro section is under-argued. The Rebbe grants that the academic mapping is real, then falls back on "our rabbeim have cleaved to the Rambam's framework" — which is true but is exactly the move Shapiro anticipated and answered. The shiur would be stronger if it engaged Shapiro's actual method — that he is not asking whether the beliefs are true but whether the Rishonim universally endorsed calling their denial kefirah — and distinguished between (a) the content of belief (unchanged) and (b) the meta-halachic question of what one becomes for denying it (contested). As it stands the section concedes the ground and then reasserts the position without actually re-taking the ground.

The Rabbi Hillel resolution is smoother than the Gemara warrants. "He pre-dates the psak; we live after it" is elegant, but it does not answer the harder version of the question: how could a recognized Amora be uncertain about something the Rambam a millennium later classifies as bedrock kefirah? Either the ikar's status was itself the product of later crystallization (which supports Shapiro), or Rabbi Hillel was operating with a defective mesorah (which is itself a difficulty). The shiur brushes past this — a fuller treatment would locate the tension rather than dissolve it.

The "1492 coincidence" is teased without being weighed. The Portugal-1492 printing of Ani Ma'amin is presented as historically striking, and the historians' speculation is mentioned — but the Rebbe declines to say whether the coincidence matters. If the point is that Klal Yisrael reached for a formulaic emunah b'Mashiach precisely at the moment of catastrophic exile, that is worth saying out loud; it would in fact tie back to yesterday's murders as the frame for this very shiur. Leaving it as a raised eyebrow costs the shiur a natural thematic bridge.

The daily-practice rubric in section X is thin relative to the analytic buildup. After nine sections of sharp lomdishe and historical work, the closing "what does this look like in practice" list is a set of familiar exhortations (daily consciousness, kavanah in the brachos, avoiding yeush). Given that the shiur just spent significant time showing the Brisker Rav's kol ha'yom and the Chofetz Chaim's ein yeush as different madreigos, the practical section would land harder if it named the reader's target madreigah honestly and traced what movement from one to the next would actually require.

4.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The doctrinal / operational split is the shiur's real thesis, but it is not named as such. The kohanim-wine ruling, the nazir case, the Erev Shabbos exclusion, the Nisan/Tishrei machloikes — every one of these operational rulings presupposes that chikui does not translate straightforwardly into "act as if Mashiach comes at every moment." The Brisker Rav's "when Mashiach comes, I'll answer that question too" is not just wit — it is a principled refusal to let the operational halachah back-propagate into the doctrinal emunah. The shiur circles this repeatedly but never names it as the underlying yesod; doing so would make the whole architecture click.

The Rambam's own halacha on kohanim and wine is a serious kashya on Rav Moshe. If Rav Moshe reads the Rambam as requiring positive daily certainty, how does he understand the Rambam's own ruling that kohanim need not abstain? Either (a) the Rambam distinguishes between emunah and tzipiah l'ma'aseh (doctrinal certainty compatible with statistical operational leniency), or (b) Rav Moshe is more Brisker than the Rambam. The shiur presents the tension but does not report whether Rav Moshe himself addresses it in the teshuvah.

The Shaagas Aryeh's kashya deserves a real answer, not a punchline. If Mashiach cannot come on Shabbos, Yom Tov, or eleven months of the year on some opinions, then the standard chikui-of-every-moment cannot be literal. The Brisker Rav's "we'll answer that later" is spiritually defensible but analytically unsatisfying. A stronger reading might be that the daily chikui operates on the level of nefesh and disposition, not on the level of metzius — which is essentially where the Chofetz Chaim ends up. Framing this as an am dinei question (is chikui a chiyuv of belief-content or of dispositional orientation?) would make the disagreement lomdishe rather than merely rhetorical.

The safek-kefirah classification is worth its own shiur. The Rambam's ruling that safek alone is kefirah is unusually harsh — most of the ikrim tolerate safek before requiring active denial. If Bias HaMashiach is uniquely intolerant of doubt, why? Is it because it is the ikar most vulnerable to erosion by lived experience, and therefore requires the strongest doctrinal fence? The shiur cites the ruling without asking why this ikar.

5.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.Is the doctrinal / operational split a formal yesod one can attribute to the Rambam, or is it a modern reading of him? Does the Rambam anywhere in Hilchos Melachim or Hilchos Teshuvah distinguish explicitly between what one must believe about Mashiach and how one must act in anticipation of him?
Q2.How does Rav Moshe in the Igros Moshe teshuvah handle the Rambam's own ruling on kohanim and wine? If the Rambam himself allows kohanim to drink, is the Rambam not implicitly conceding that "certainly today" cannot be operational? Where does Rav Moshe locate the release valve?
Q3.Why is Bias HaMashiach uniquely intolerant of safek in the Rambam's classification? Most ikrim tolerate uncertainty short of denial. Is there a structural reason this ikar requires a stricter fence — because it is the one most exposed to erosion by galus, or because it structures every tefillah, or something else?
Q4.The Marc Shapiro challenge can be steelmanned: he is not disputing the beliefs themselves but the meta-halachic claim that denial is kefirah. Do we have any Acharon — perhaps the Chazon Ish or Rav Elchonon — who has taken the meta-halachic question head-on and defended it on its own terms rather than by falling back on mesorah?
Q5.Is the Ani Ma'amin's Portugal-1492 provenance historically established, or is it more accurately the earliest surviving printing? And if it was in fact a Marrano-era formulation, does that shape how we should read it — a text produced under duress, meant to hold the community together under conditions of forced conversion?
Q6.On the Chofetz Chaim's baseline: is "absence of yeush" a redefinition of chikui, or is it a floor beneath which one fails the ikar with the Brisker kol ha'yom as the ceiling? If it is a floor-and-ceiling picture, then the two positions are complementary rather than contradictory — is that reconciliation available in the Acharonim, or does someone insist the two are genuinely incompatible?

6.Bottom Line

A weighty shiur delivered in a weighty moment, and the two match. The historical survey (thirteen ikrim, the plurality of frameworks, Shapiro) is competent; the Rabbi Hillel Gemara is well-handled; but the shiur earns its keep in the Brisker-vs.-Chofetz-Chaim machloikes and in the kohanim-and-wine observation that quietly undoes Brisker maximalism from inside the Rambam himself. Where the shiur leaves work on the table is in refusing to name the doctrinal/operational split it keeps circling — that split is the actual yesod, and stating it would consolidate everything the shiur has assembled. The closing move — that the same emunah that carried Klal Yisrael through nineteen hundred years of tzarah is what lets American parents send their children to yeshiva ketana this morning while six families sit shiva in Eretz Yisrael — earns its emotional weight because the analytic architecture beneath it is solid. A shiur that gives the reader both the historical map and a live sense of what is at stake in the disagreement.
