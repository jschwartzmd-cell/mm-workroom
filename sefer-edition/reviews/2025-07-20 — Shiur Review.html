Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

20 July 2025 — שְׁמַע קוֹלֵנוּ: The Culmination of Our Petitions

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · 5 October 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

A warm, discursive shiur on Shema Koleinu as the chasimah of the twelve bakashos peratiyos, delivered on the yartzeit of Rabbi Yaakov Yosef — the first and last Chief Rabbi of New York — with the Satmar Rebbe's tribute that every good machshavah a Jew has in New York is bizchus Reb Yankev Yosef. The architectural piece: the central twelve split six-and-six, Shema Koleinu closes the arc, the amen on it elevates every amen and bakashah of the entire section. The historical anchor: the Midrash that the malachei ha-shareis coined “Baruch Atah Hashem Shomea Tefillah” at the moment Hakadosh Baruch Hu heard vayi'anchu vayiz'aku. The nusach question of “Av Harachaman” turns on the Tur's gematria — the roshei teivos of the eighteen brachos sum to 1,800 to parallel the Zohar's 1,800 malachim, requiring the ש' of Shema rather than the א' of Av Harachaman; the Arizal was not makpid on it. The centerpiece of the first half is the chilling Gemara in Ta'anis kaf-heh on Shmuel ha-Katan's two ta'aniyos for rain — the rain that came before the fast (the master who cannot bear the servant's knock) and the rain that came at shkiah (the master who waits for the servant's exhaustion) — both answering the pshat of “shema”: we want to be heard with chibbah, not out of fatigue. Then the long halachic excursus on personal additions: Shulchan Aruch permits, Taz cautions against b'kviyus, Aruch HaShulchan escalates into a full polemic against the printers (“they are guilty”, chutzpah, “I mourn the corruption of our siddurim”) — and the Rebbe's counter-move with the Yavetz/Rabbi Yonah Landsofer's teaching to forget the printed additions and add in your own words, backed by the Brisker Rav on a one-line bakashah for a business deal and the Chida on brevity before the chasimah, with the Mishnah Berurah's caution not to miss kedushah. The Zohar's Rav Yeiva Saba — who never began a meal without davening for the next one — stands as the model. The emotional climax is the double pshat on “reikam al teshiveinu”: the plain reading (“don't send me away empty — make a deal, give me a third, half”) and the deeper reading of Rav Shimshon Schwab from Iyun Tefillah — the WWI memory of his mother, the Kommissar-Brot loaf of black bread, her tear falling on it as she refused him a second slice — grounded in a fresh pshat on “lo ra'isi tzaddik ne'ezav” as never seen a tzaddik who felt abandoned. The Rebbe closes with the maaseh of his own father a"h, the only person in the shul who cried on a regular Tuesday Shemoneh Esrei, and Rav Avraham Schorr's public testimony to that fact. A coda on the Bach's warning about communal she'eilas geshamim in Shema Koleinu — the two gedolim who died within the year — closes the bracha. Time ran out; the Rebbe added a brief Retzei vort on Michoel sar ha-gadol's daily korbanos of nishmos tzaddikim that properly belongs to next week.

2.What Was Strongest

  • The Shmuel ha-Katan double-maaseh. This is the shiur's structural masterstroke. Two years, two rains, two meshalim — the master who gives to be rid of the knocking, and the master who waits for exhaustion before granting — and both are forms of “shomea” that are not the “shema” we want. The move turns Shema Koleinu from a request for an outcome into a request about the quality of the hearing. Once heard, the kavvanah of the bracha changes permanently.
  • The Tur's 1,800 and the ש' of Shema. A rare moment where a nusach-dispute is adjudicated by arithmetic, not minhag. The 1,800-malachim count in the Zohar, mirrored in the roshei teivos of the eighteen brachos, is the sort of structural signature that justifies otherwise opaque wording choices — and the Arizal's indifference to that counting is a clean, honest machlokes rather than an evasion.
  • The Aruch HaShulchan polemic as a "window" rather than a pesak. The Rebbe sets up the Aruch HaShulchan's fury with full force — “they are guilty!”, chutzpah, “the world has been handed to fools”, printers blamed, siddurim corrupted — and then lands the halachah l'maaseh: “no one else holds this way; please continue saying the viduy every day.” The sharp, deliberate asymmetry between the Aruch HaShulchan's shouting and the Rebbe's calm instruction is pedagogically excellent: it teaches respect for structural seriousness without turning it into practice.
  • The Yavetz/R' Yonah Landsofer pivot. After thirty minutes building the case against daily b'kviyus additions, the Rebbe pivots to the Yavetz's chiddush: forget the printed insertions — add your own words. This is the shiur's practical takeaway, and the shidduch-crisis quote from 350 years ago lands with perfect period-feeling (“we suffer greatly in finding shidduchim for our children or grandchildren”). The Brisker Rav's one-line business-deal bakashah and the Chida's brevity-before-chasimah round it out l'maaseh.
  • The Rav Schwab WWI memory. The “Kommissar-Brot” story — the black loaf, the mother's tear falling on the bread as she refused the child a second slice, the six-year-old understanding at that moment that the sharpness was love — is as powerful a vort as the shiur contains. The pairing with “lo ra'isi tzaddik ne'ezav” read as never seen one who felt abandoned is the kind of pshat that resolves a pasuk every bentcher has stumbled over and leaves the hearer with a fresh mesorah.
  • The personal note on the Rebbe's father a"h. The maaseh of Rav Avraham Schorr testifying publicly that the Rebbe's father was the only person in the shul who cried during a weekday Shemoneh Esrei, and the Rebbe's own childhood embarrassment at that crying, is unusually self-revealing and does real work: it validates the preceding “shed a tear and your tefillah is accepted” with lived testimony rather than exhortation.
  • The Bach coda. The maaseh of two unnamed gedolim who instituted a communal she'eilas geshamim in Shema Koleinu outside a formal ta'anis, and both died within the year, is the shiur's final mussar on the structural weight of Shemoneh Esrei — and it does so without doctrine, by a sippur that forces the point.

3.What the Writeup Omitted or Softened

The Rabbi Yaakov Yosef opening was flattened in the write-up. The shiur-opening carries real color the writeup trimmed to a lead-paragraph vignette: ten thousand people visit the kever on the yartzeit even in the rain, the mention that Reb Yankev Yosef faced an actual rebellion (“they threw rocks on the people down there”), the surviving shul on Ocean Parkway, the cheder “Mevakshei Hashem” on Ditmas, the fact that he ultimately moved to the Lower East Side. The audience back-and-forth about the kever — “someone made a big deal about his kever that you get big yeshuos over there... someone's laughing all the way to the bank” — is cut (reasonably). The reconstructed “his tzaros were great” paragraph is tasteful but noticeably thinner than the live telling.

The contemporary Eretz Yisrael hook on Anenu is missing. The Rebbe mentions — in setting up the she'eilas geshamim theme — that “a couple of years ago the batei din in Eretz Yisrael put out a letter that everyone should add the anenu” during drought. This is the single contemporary application point in the entire shiur and would land the Bach's warning more precisely (because the batei din's letter is the right way: communal addition under rabbinic direction, not improvisation). The writeup collapses the Rebbe's “probably never again relevant for us” into a flat “a tefillah for geshamim when there is a drought”, losing the hook.

The Bernie Nagelberg siddur vignette is cut. At the very end, when checking whether the inserts appear in minchah, the Rebbe handles an audience member's siddur and discovers it was Bernie's father's siddur — which Bernie himself inherited and now davens from. The Rebbe stops, visibly moved, and tells him: “It's a very big zechus to daven in the siddur of your ancestors, of your father. It's a very, very choshuveh zach.” This small moment belongs structurally with the “Power of Tears” section about the Rebbe's own father: both are about yerushah through tefillah, and together they would frame the shiur's close more organically than the current “Closing” paragraph does.

The “Toiv Hashem le-kol” framing anecdote is cut. The Rebbe sets up his personal kavvanah on “reikam al teshiveinu” by first saying: “someone came over to me yesterday — he says when he says 'toiv Hashem le-kol v'rachamav al kol maasav' he has more kavana there than in any other pasuk in the entire tefillah.” It's a conversational hook that frames “everyone has their own pasuk” — and then the Rebbe shares his own. Without it, the writeup's “An expression in this bracha the Rebbe shares his personal kavvanah on” is cleaner but less human.

The audience interaction with Chaskel is understandably trimmed, but one line is worth recovering. Chaskel is the person who does say the viduy every day; the Rebbe lightly teases him (“That's why you daven such a long Shemoneh Esrei”) and then, when the Aruch HaShulchan gets most heated, reassures him by name: “Chaskel, I'm sorry, don't get offended by his words — many Jews do like you.” The writeup understandably strips the name but with it goes the key pedagogical point that the Rebbe is explicitly addressing the person in the room who holds the minority practice. For the sefer, a de-personalized version (“to anyone in this room who says the viduy daily — please continue; this is shared l'yediah, not l'maaseh”) would preserve the tone.

The Wikisource / sforim-and-wife aside is correctly cut. This one belongs out of the sefer.

The Retzei appendix is correctly cut from this shiur. The Rebbe, with ten minutes to spare, pivoted to a bonus vort on Michoel sar ha-gadol bringing korbanos daily in shamayim (nishmos tzaddikim, per the Gemara Menachos at the very end, with the Tosafos that ends the mesechta, and the “ishei Yisrael” לעא two-reading chakirah). This belongs with the Retzei write-up, not with Shema Koleinu. The decision to cut it from this document is correct, provided it is captured in the next.

4.Where the Writeup Added or Framed Beyond the Shiur

The Rav Schwab attribution is correctly handled — and this is important. The summary document mis-attributes the WWI bread story to Rav Shimshon Raphael Hirsch (both in the body text and footnote 11). This is a transcription-era conflation of names. The transcript is clear: “I saw Reb Shimshon Schwab in a sefer Iyun Tefillah...” — and Iyun Tefillah / Rav Schwab on Prayer is Rav Schwab's work, published posthumously. The writeup correctly identifies the author as Rav Schwab throughout and in footnote 11; the editorial note that he is the rav of Khal Adath Jeshurun in Washington Heights is accurate biographical context. Flag for the editorial pass: the summary document should be corrected to prevent the Hirsch-mistake from propagating into future shiurim that cite this story.

The lead-paragraph attribution of reikam to a “deeper reading” already in the lead is editorial foreshadowing. In the shiur the double-pshat emerges organically only after the whole personal-additions excursus. The writeup's lead signals the structure in advance, which is appropriate for a sefer-reader but changes the dramatic shape.

Footnote 18 identifies Rav Avraham Schorr's shul and his authorship of HaLekach VeHaLibuv; footnote 15 adds Rabbi Yaakov Yosef's full dates, stroke in 1895, and Union Field Cemetery; footnote 1 cites Yeshaya 56:7 on bais tefillasi. None of these are in the shiur. All are reasonable editorial enrichments.

The lead's “from da'as to refuah, from parnasah to geulah” is a cleanup of the transcript's “from death to refuah to parnasah”. “Death” here is almost certainly a mistranscription of da'as (the first bakashah); the writeup's reconstruction is the right call, and da'as-through-geulah correctly spans the arc of the twelve.

The writeup's “Do not be a bear licking honey through the page” in the closing paragraph is a strong image that is not in the shiur. It is a fine editorial flourish that captures the Rebbe's spirit, but it is not something the Rebbe said; any sefer-edition pass should either flag it as editorial or replace it with a line the Rebbe actually used.

5.Nuances and Chiddushim Worth Pulling Into the Sefer Edition

  • The amen-after-Shema-Koleinu as the chasimah of the whole central section. The Rebbe makes this point crisply — “just as the amen after Kedushas Hashem seals the opening unit, this amen seals the twelve.” The writeup has it but might foreground it: it is the structural yesod on which the entire “culmination” framing rests, and worth an opening-paragraph callout.
  • The chassidishe nusach that places “Av Harachaman” mid-bracha. The Rebbe mentions this as a “compromise” preserving the ש' at the opening. The writeup records it. A footnote identifying which chassidishe nusach (Nussach Ha-Ari of Zhidachov? Sephard-Chabad?) would elevate the point from folklore to mesorah.
  • The batei din of Eretz Yisrael drought letter. As above — this is the live halachic point that would ground the Bach coda. Even a one-sentence editorial footnote would help.
  • The Bernie Nagelberg siddur moment. Short, concrete, warm — worth a sentence in the “Power of Tears” section as a second lived vignette after the Rebbe's father.
  • The “Toiv Hashem le-kol” framing — everyone has their pasuk. One line reinstated before the “reikam” section captures what the Rebbe is actually modeling: not just this is my kavvanah, but you should find yours.
  • The Mesechta Ta'anis frame. The Rebbe mentions, in setting up the Shmuel ha-Katan sugya, that “the whole Mesechta Ta'anis revolves around the gezeiros of ta'aniyos they used to have when they needed rain.” A one-line footnote locating the sugya in the broader mesechta's drought-focus would help a reader unfamiliar with Ta'anis.
  • Framing of “b'kviyus” vs “b'arai”. The Taz/Aruch HaShulchan axis turns entirely on this distinction. The writeup has it but a one-line explicit halachic definition would help a general reader.

6.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The Aruch HaShulchan's position deserves more precise framing. The Rebbe lands the practical pesak correctly — no one holds this l'maaseh — but the Aruch HaShulchan's underlying reasoning is sharper than “chutzpah”: he reads the heter of Avodah Zarah as a rishyus to “add a bakashah”, not a rishyus to “install a bakashah into one's permanent nusach.” That is a real conceptual chiddush in the mechanics of “adding to Shemoneh Esrei”, and it is what makes the position coherent rather than merely stringent. Worth a footnote.

The Yavetz's chiddush dovetails with the Aruch HaShulchan's rather than contradicting it. Both agree the printed inserts are a problem; they disagree about what to put in their place. The Aruch HaShulchan says nothing; the Yavetz says your own words. The shiur sets them up as rivals (“however, let's not forget the Yavetz”) but they are closer to allies — both resisting the normalization of someone else's bakashah as your bakashah. The sefer-edition could draw out this convergence.

The Shmuel ha-Katan sugya has a kashya the shiur doesn't address. If both stories model “hearing without chibbah”, what did Shmuel ha-Katan think the proper outcome of a ta'anis would look like? Rain neither before, nor at shkiah, but mid-day? Is there a Gemara or Rishon that identifies the right timing? The “we don't want to be like that congregation” framing is strong, but it leaves open what the alternative would have been. (One possibility: the mishnah's own description of rain coming b'eis tefillah.)

The Rav Schwab pshat on “ne'ezav” — felt abandoned rather than objectively abandoned — is powerful but linguistically a stretch. Nifal-forms in Tanach are not typically experiential. The pshat's force is mussar-level, not strictly peshat-level, and it is worth noting that this is a chassidishe pshat on a pasuk that has multiple Rishonim reading it more literally (including the Ibn Ezra's discomfort with the pasuk itself). The shiur presents it as the pshat; the sefer-edition could present it as Rav Schwab's pshat among several.

The Bach's maaseh of two gedolim dying within the year carries weight only if we trust the Bach's source. The Bach doesn't name them, and the story has an evident homiletical shape (two gedolim, same mistake, same consequence, same year). For a sefer, flagging this as “the Bach records a tradition” rather than reporting it as fact would be more honest — without weakening its mussar-force.

7.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.On the Tur's 1,800: does the count include the opening letters of v'la-malshinim and re'tzei as they currently stand, or does it rest on an earlier nusach? If the latter, is the ש'-of-Shema argument itself a reconstruction of an earlier state of the brachos that is now partly obscured, and if so does that change its halachic weight for us?
Q2.On the Shmuel ha-Katan sugya: what would have been the ideal outcome — rain mid-day, rain at the moment of tefillah, or something else? Is there a mekor for the alternative, or is the sugya purely a shalilah?
Q3.On the Yavetz/Chida/Brisker Rav line: if one adds a one-line bakashah in one's own words daily — the Brisker Rav's business-deal case, say, for a person who davens for the same deal every day — does that cross back into the Taz/Aruch HaShulchan's objection to b'kviyus? Or does the in-your-own-words frame fundamentally change the halachic character of the addition even when the subject is the same each day?
Q4.On Rav Schwab's pshat on “lo ra'isi tzaddik ne'ezav”: is this specifically Rav Schwab's pshat, or does it have earlier mekoros (perhaps in chassidishe sources on the bentcher-closing)? The pshat is powerful enough to want a longer mesorah behind it than a single sefer.
Q5.On the Bach's two gedolim: does the Bach name them anywhere, or do later Acharonim? If not, how should we weight the maaseh — as mussar or as evidentiary halachah?
Q6.On “Av Harachaman”: does the Sephardic community preserve a mesorah on which the Tur's 1,800-count is answered differently? (The Rebbe mentioned in the shiur that Dan was not there to ask — the Sephardi voice in the room.) If Sephardic nusach opens with א', how do they handle the Tur's arithmetic?
Q7.On the Michoel sar ha-gadol appendix: the Rebbe landed on a Tosafos at the very end of Menachos reading “v'ishei Yisrael u-tefilasam” two ways. Is the Tosafos's preferred reading the “today's korbanos in shamayim” reading or the “restore the ancient korbanos” reading? And does the Rebbe hold one over the other, or is the tzvei dinim reading itself the chiddush?

8.Bottom Line

A warm, generous shiur that moves through Shema Koleinu's architectural, nusach, halachic, and emotional registers in that order, with three stretches a hearer will not forget: the Shmuel ha-Katan double-rain sugya as the shadow side of “shomea”, the Aruch HaShulchan → Yavetz pivot from printed b'kviyus to one's own words, and Rav Schwab's WWI bread story anchoring “reikam al teshiveinu”. The Mareh Mekamos writeup is faithful to the shiur's structure and clean of the summary document's one real error (the mis-attribution of the bread story to Rav Shimshon Raphael Hirsch rather than Rav Schwab). The sefer-edition pass should recover a handful of lived moments the writeup trimmed: the Rabbi Yaakov Yosef opening in fuller texture, the Eretz Yisrael batei din drought-letter as the live anchor for the Bach coda, the Bernie Nagelberg-father's-siddur moment to pair with the Rebbe's own father vignette, and the “Toiv Hashem le-kol” framing before the Rebbe's personal kavvanah. One editorial flourish — “do not be a bear licking honey through the page” — should be either flagged or replaced with a Rebbe-line. The Retzei appendix the Rebbe added at the end was correctly deferred and belongs with next week's shiur.
