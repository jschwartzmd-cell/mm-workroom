Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

6 April 2025 — Preparing for פֶּסַח — בְּדִיקַת חָמֵץ and בִּיעוּר חָמֵץ

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · 7 September 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

An erev Pesach departure from the Monday morning tefillah track — a six-days-before-Pesach preparation shiur on bedikat and bi'ur chametz. The Rav stacks the classical material on the structural uniqueness of bal yeraeh u'bal yimatzei, moves through the Rambam's reading that “tashbisu” is fulfilled mideoraysa through bittul alone, sets the Tosafos and the Ran against one another on why chazal layered bedikah and bi'ur on top, and then delivers the shiur's lomdish centerpiece: the Minchas Chinuch's chakirah whether tashbisu is kum va-aseh or shev v'al ta'aseh, with three sharp nafka minos (the gluten-free Jew, eating found chametz on Shabbos as bi'ur, and the interloper who throws your bag into the fire). The second half turns from lomdus to avodah — the Radbaz's peshat that chametz represents the yetzer hara, the Chasam Sofer's chiddush on why matzah must be made specifically from grains that could become chametz, the yachatz/wholeness thread, and a closing historical excursion on mechiras chametz as an eighteenth-century Ashkenazi response to the tavern-keeper economy. Concluding chizuk to go find one's own chametz in the corners of the heart.

2.What Was Strongest

  • The Minchas Chinuch's three nafka minos presented as a coherent structure. Many shiurim on tashbisu pick one nafka mina and run. The Rav lays out all three — the gluten-free Jew, eating chametz on Shabbos, and the chotef at the fire — and lets each one press the chakirah from a different angle: existence-of-chametz, mode-of-removal, and ownership-of-the-ma'aseh. Presenting all three together is what turns the chakirah from an abstract question into something the listener can feel.
  • The move from lomdus to avodah is genuinely earned. The Rav does not simply pivot to the Chasam Sofer as a vort for the end. He grounds the avodah reading — chametz as yetzer hara, tashbisu as the act of finding-and-burning — back into the Minchas Chinuch's first nafka mina. The gluten-free Jew who must buy chametz in order to burn it makes philosophical sense once you accept the Radbaz: absence is not the point; confrontation is. This is a real synthesis and the shiur's structural high point.
  • The Chasam Sofer on matzah-from-chametz-grain is beautifully staged. The rhetorical build-up — why did the Torah tell us to make our matzah from the single most dangerous ingredient on Pesach? — is disciplined and lands. The siman of rice's sirchon rather than chimutz makes the question airtight, and the answer (tzaddikim don't need this mitzvah because they have no chametz to arrest) is genuinely moving.
  • The historical excursion on mechiras chametz. The tavern-keeper / distillery-lessee background is a piece of real economic history and the Rav delivers it in his signature register: “history shaping halachah” is the point, not a footnote. Anchoring the modern heter in a specific seventeenth–eighteenth-century parnassah reality — and explaining why some poskim hold the heter was only ever for the schnapps, not the cookies — is exactly the kind of context the ba'al ha-bayis almost never hears in a shiur.
  • The yachatz observation is elegant. Everywhere else in the Torah the object of the mitzvah must be whole — shofar, esrog, the square of tefillin — and then on the seder we deliberately break the matzah. The peshat that Pesach begins with an admission of not-being-whole is the kind of small observation that reframes a familiar ma'aseh.
  • The Reb Itzele Charif opening story is well-chosen and self-aware. A miltza d'bedichusa at the top of the shiur, with the immediate self-check (“let us not speak to our wives this way”) and the chazal-flavored aside that “goy gamur” in lashon chazal always means a Jew. Warmly delivered without becoming the point.

3.What Was Weaker or Worth Pushing Back On

The Tosafos–Ran machlokes is presented as parallel when it is actually a machlokes in the halachic geometry. The Rav sets the two rishonim side by side and lets the listener choose. But the difference is consequential: on the Tosafos, the tekanas bedikah targets the risk that the bittul itself failed (mental unreliability); on the Ran, the bittul is fine and the tekanah targets the downstream issur achilah. That difference should generate nafka minos: for instance, whether someone who is trustworthy in his kavvanah could rely on bittul alone (permitted on the Ran's framing? plausibly forbidden on Tosafos'?). The shiur presents the two ta'amim and moves on.

The second nafka mina of the Minchas Chinuch is stronger than the shiur admits. The Rav flags mitzvah ha-ba'ah b'aveirah as the natural objection and lets the chakirah resolve it. But the deeper problem is that on the shev v'al ta'aseh side, the person is still chayav kares for the achilah even though he is yotzei tashbisu. That is a startling result — simultaneous kiyum mitzvah and chiyuv misah shel malkos in one ma'aseh. Worth naming rather than glossing.

The Radbaz-as-peshat-not-kabbalah claim needs unpacking. The Rav is insistent that chametz-as-yetzer hara is peshat in the issur, not chassidus or kabbalah. That is a striking claim — the more common framing is that this is a beautiful remez layered on top of a gezeiras ha-kasuv. If the Rav means that the extreme chumros of chametz can only be explained if the Torah's target is the yetzer hara and not the substance, that is a real halachic-philosophical argument and deserves to be made explicit. As delivered it lands as an assertion.

The historical mechiras chametz discussion is teased and then deferred. The Rav names the topic as one of the most fascinating in all of hilchos Pesach and then explicitly parks it for another shiur. Fair — but he has already given the setup (tavern-keeper economy, korte'shmer Jews, the heter for schnapps versus for cookies). Enough context is on the table that either committing to the discussion or cutting the tease would serve the listener better than leaving him hanging on “this is a great machlokes we will discuss another time.”

4.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The Rambam's formulation of bittul is doing more work than the shiur credits. The Rambam writes “she-yevatel be-libo ve-yachshov oso ke-afar” — the bittul is be-libo, in the heart, not on the lips. That framing already anticipates the Tosafos's concern: if the bittul is essentially be-lev, then mouthing the Aramaic is not the mitzvah. This is why the Mishnah Berurah's pesak to translate for one's wife and children is not a stringency but the logical extension of the Rambam's own language. The shiur presents the Mishnah Berurah as a corollary; it is closer to being necessary.

The Chasam Sofer's chiddush has a hidden halachic tail. If matzah is avodah-from-chametz, then the eighteen-minute clock is not merely a siman that no chimutz has occurred — it is the actual halachic geometry of the mitzvah. This may push against the standard framing that chimutz is a gezeirah and eighteen minutes is a rabbinic parameter. If the Chasam Sofer is right, then the entire eighteen-minute rush is the ma'aseh mitzvah of arresting potential chametz. Worth flagging.

The Minchas Chinuch's first nafka mina may prove too much. If the gluten-free Jew must purchase chametz in order to burn it, then by parity of reasoning any Jew whose chametz is only marginal should buy more. That is not the minhag. Either the first nafka mina is a lomdish hypothetical that no one actually paskens (in which case the Minchas Chinuch's chakirah is a chakirah in the theoretical structure of the mitzvah only), or it should reshape our actual practice. The shiur leaves the practical status ambiguous.

The Ran's framing has implications for mechiras chametz. On the Ran, the tekanah targets the risk of achilah. But mechiras chametz leaves the chametz physically in the sold cupboard, taped over — the very situation the Ran says chazal came to prevent. The Ran's reasoning arguably weakens the heter of mechiras chametz in a way the Tosafos's reasoning does not. This connects the two halves of the shiur (the machlokes rishonim and the mechiras chametz excursion) and would have made the closing feel less like an appendix.

5.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.On the Ran's framing that bedikah is a gezeirah shema yochal — how does this coexist with contemporary mechiras chametz, where the chametz gamur literally sits in a locked cupboard in the house? Doesn't the Ran arguably read against the modern practice?
Q2.The Rav paskens not to sell chametz gamur, following his father and grandfather. Does he hold this as a personal chumra/family minhag, or does he think the ikkar ha-din is that mechiras chametz for chametz gamur is on shaky ground even for others?
Q3.On the Minchas Chinuch's first nafka mina — the gluten-free Jew who must buy chametz to burn — is this a purely lomdish hypothetical, or would the Rav actually direct such a person l'halachah to acquire chametz before Pesach in order to have something to be mekayem tashbisu on?
Q4.The Radbaz-Chasam Sofer reading treats chametz as yetzer hara as the peshat of the issur, not chassidus. If that is correct, what work does the issur's halachic structure — bal yeraeh, mashehu, kares — do that a straightforward gezeiras ha-kasuv framing does not? What is the halachic argument for reading chametz substantively rather than formally?
Q5.On Tosafos' framing that bittul is unreliable because it is purely be-lev: if chazal's tekanah was aimed at the person who might not achieve authentic kavvanah, shouldn't a person who does achieve authentic bittul (a talmid chacham, a ba'al kavvanah) be able to rely on bittul alone b'shas ha-dechak? Or did chazal intentionally impose a uniform standard?
Q6.The Chasam Sofer reads the eighteen-minute rush as the ma'aseh of arresting potential chametz. Does this reshape the halachic status of the eighteen-minute clock — is it a rabbinic parameter, or is it built into the d'oraysa geometry of matzah itself?

6.Bottom Line

A well-shaped erev Pesach shiur that does two things well: it delivers the Minchas Chinuch's chakirah with all three nafka minos and lets the chakirah breathe, and then it earns the pivot from lomdus to avodah by looping the Radbaz/Chasam Sofer back onto the first nafka mina — the gluten-free Jew becomes the paradigm of what Pesach demands rather than a curiosity. Weak spots: the Tosafos–Ran machlokes is presented as parallel rather than mined for nafka minos; the Radbaz-as-peshat claim is asserted without a real argument for reading the issur substantively; and the mechiras chametz historical excursion is teased and deferred rather than either committed to or cut. Worth returning to before bedikah tonight; worth pressing the Rav on Q1 and Q3 before the sefer version.
