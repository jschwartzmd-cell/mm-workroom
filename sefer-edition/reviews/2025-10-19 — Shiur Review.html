Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

19 October 2025 — Siyum שמונה עשרה: יהיו לרצון, אלוקי נצור, and the מחלוקת of Pressburg

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcripts, summary, and finished write-up · 12 October 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

A Sunday-morning shiur in the Shemoneh Esrei series, the first after the Yom Tov break, on the close of the Amidah. After a dedication le’ilui nishmas R’ Chaim Yehuda ben Meir Willinger (a survivor of the death march, from Selish, whose rav wrote Tzeror HaChaim), the shiur returns to Oseh HaShalom against HaMevarech es Amo Yisrael BaShalom: the gematria (HaShalom = oseh = Safriel); the book Chut HaMeshulash by a grandchild of the Chasam Sofer (Rav Akiva Eiger, Chasam Sofer, Ksav Sofer; lo yinatek; the “Sofer” chain ending in Da’as Sofer); the Pressburg split into Oiseh-niks and Hamevarech-niks; and the Chassidic revolution (posture, the Vilna Gaon’s cherem, nusach, the Chofetz Chaim’s remark, Nusach HaAri of Chabad). It then takes up Yihyu Leratzon: the Gemara in Berachos 9, Dovid HaMelech’s eighteen chapters, the Rashba against the Vilna Gaon, the Mishnah Berurah’s “both,” and the Yom Kippur Minchah question; the history of Elokai Netzor (the Avudraham: only for yechidim) and of Mar bereih d’Ravina (the Worms legend of Rashi’s mother, the camel at Mechoza, the glass at his son’s wedding, his prayer); kavanah and the Radak; and the advice to lengthen after Yihyu Leratzon. The long second half is the segulah of the verse of one’s name (the Shelah; a parenthetical addition to Rashi on Michah; Shem Ephraim; Amsterdam 1699; the Yaavetz), the soul identified by its name (the Arizal, the Bnei Yissaschar, Nimrod), the severity of lashon hara (Bava Basra 165a), and sechar ve’onesh. It closes with Mishnah Makkos 3:16. The shiur stops short of treating Elokai Netzor itself, deferred to the following week.

2.Overall Fidelity

The write-up follows the shiur closely and keeps its best moments: the Worms legend leading back to the Gemara, the glass at the wedding, the Hong Kong joke, and the progression from the Gemara to the Rishonim to the Mishnah Berurah. The reordering into fourteen sections is a real improvement on a live flow that doubled back several times (the name material arrives in three separate passes). Three source texts were read: the bilingual Plaud transcript (the clearest), the Sofer-style transcript (whose garbling is heavy and which contains a stray non-shiur sentence about “JSON format” at about 00:17:20, to be ignored), and the AI summary. The pattern to note is that the write-up inherited several errors from the summary, which in places departs from both transcripts (Sandalfon for Safriel; Zohar for Or HaChaim; Remah of Fano for the Rema; “tradition” for “legend”; “until all their thoughts”). Those are localized and are listed below; all but the last two were corrected in the finished HTML in the same pass.

3.Content Flags — Misattributions and Overclaims

Flag 1 — The angel is wrong: Safriel, not Sandalfon. The write-up says the gematria of השלום equals סנדלפון. Both transcripts say Safriel, and the arithmetic settles it: השלום = 381, עושה = 381, ספריאל = 381, but סנדלפון = 280. The summary introduced Sandalfon; the write-up copied it. The point of the shiur (that oseh also yields Safriel) works only with the corrected name. Corrected in the HTML.

Flag 2 — “The Rema, Shulchan Aruch” became “Rema MiPano.” The Sofer transcript has “the Ramon Shechonarach,” and the clearer transcript has “the רמ"א, שלחן ערוך also says it.” The summary expanded it to רמ"ע מפאנו, and the write-up adopted that, with a footnote dating him (1548–1620) and citing “liturgical writings” that no one named. The Rema’s gloss on Orach Chaim 122:1 does record the custom of saying Elokai Netzor before Yihyu Leratzon (verified on Sefaria), so the plain reading is supported. Corrected.

Flag 3 — Or HaChaim, not Zohar. The summary renders the speaker’s “the אור החיים הקדוש… in פרשת האזינו I think” as the זוהר הקדוש; the write-up followed and then built a Zohar locus (a footnote that places פרשת תזריע in volume II). The speaker hedged (“I think”); the Or HaChaim on דברים ל"ב as available on Sefaria shows no such passage. Corrected to Or HaChaim, flagged not located.

Flag 4 — The earliest source of the name-verse was mis-heard as two books. The write-up has “ספר מכלול from one of the ראשונים, and תריסר אסר.” The clearer transcript is unambiguous: after the digression on parenthetical additions in Rashi, the speaker says “this addition that was added to רש"י in ספר מיכה, מיכה, one of the נביאים in תרי עשר,” and that it says only that the Torah will save one from Gehinnom, not that one says it in prayer. Verified on Sefaria: a parenthetical passage after Rashi on מיכה ו:ט says exactly this. The write-up separated the Rashi-parentheses digression from the source it was introducing, so the logic of the passage was lost. Corrected.

Flag 5 — “Until all their thoughts” is not what Mar b. Ravina said. The summary and write-up give עד כל מחשבותם. The clearer transcript has “אלקי נצור until דקלקל מחשבתם,” and the Gemara (Berachos 17a, verified) reads וכל החושבים עלי רעה מהרה הפר עצתם וקלקל מחשבתם. The speaker also remarked that the Gemara’s text differs from the siddur (the Sefardi text adds an introductory yehi ratzon) and includes “a bad woman,” which the siddur lacks (said as a joke, not carried into the sefer edition). Corrected.

Flag 6 — “Two questions raised by רבינו ברוך” (Section VIII) does not occur in either transcript. The speaker deferred “why we chose that amora’s tefillah” (“we’ll talk about it in a moment,” then “next week”) and addressed lashon hara briefly at the end. The invented attribution, and the framing of both as questions “left off,” should go. Corrected in the HTML.

Flag 7 — “Long account” of Pressburg in Chut HaMeshulash. The speaker says the Pressburg split is something “you’ll find nowhere; that’s the only place where I found it.” The write-up makes it a “long account.” The single-source status matters for a sefer. Hedged in the HTML.

Flag 8 — Genealogy in Section II is self-contradictory. The write-up says a grandchild of the Chasam Sofer wrote of “his grandfather R’ Akiva Eger, his father the Chasam Sofer, and his own father the Ksav Sofer.” The speaker is audibly working it out (“I think his brother or his father the Ksav Sofer, maybe it was the grandson of the Chasam Sofer”). The footnote then names the author and dates (a name not in either transcript). Fixed in the HTML to a neutral wording; the author’s identity is recorded as unverified.

Flag 9 — “Legend” softened to “tradition,” and the Shelah attribution presented as settled. The Sofer transcript has the speaker explicitly calling the account of judgment “a legend … because we don’t know the exact source for it in Chazal,” and on the soul forgetting its name: “says the Shelah, again, I don’t know where it comes from … I don’t know exactly.” The summary says “tradition” and the write-up follows. The sefer should carry the hedge.

Flag 10 — Whether the name-verse practice “comes from the Shelah.” The two transcripts disagree (“it’s not a minhag that comes from the Shlo” against “it’s a minhag that comes from the Shelah”). The write-up follows the first, which fits the speaker’s later “Where does it come from? No one knows” and “it’s not the Shelah, might be someone else.” Right call; the discrepancy should be recorded.

Flag 11 — The Rashba is credited with a position that Sefaria gives to the Rabad. Both transcripts say Rashba. The text of the Rashba on Berachos 17a cites the Rabad as saying that Yihyu Leratzon belongs before these supplications, right after the eighteen berachos, as David after eighteen psalms. This is a flag against the shiur, not the write-up, but the sefer should say “the Rabad, cited by the Rashba.”

Flag 12 — The blessing on a personal miracle. The shiur has Mar b. Ravina saying ברוך שעשה לי נס במקום הזה and being מלומד בנסים. On Sefaria (Berachos 54a) that formula is Rava’s, to a man saved from a lion; Mar’s two blessings name both miracles (the spring in the Aravos, the camel and the wall); and the phrase מלומד בנסים was not located in the passage. Flagged in the Sefaria pass and carried into the sefer edition as a caveat.

Flag 13 — Tosafos and zecher lechurban. “That’s what Tosafos over there says, zecher lechurban.” On Sefaria Tosafos on 31a says only “from here they took the custom of breaking glass at weddings”; the zecher lechurban rationale is in the Rema (Orach Chaim 560:2). Corrected in the notes.

4.Content Flags — Omissions

  • The dedication detail. The speaker says the rav of Selish wrote a sefer called Tzeror HaChaim and plays on nishmaso tehei tzerurah bitzror hachayim. The write-up has only “survivor from Seilish.” The sponsor’s surname is “Paley” in the clearer transcript and “Pele” in the summary; unresolved.
  • Why Pressburg shifted nusach, left open. An audience question (“why actually did they do that shift of nusach?”) is acknowledged, not answered (“good question … I don’t have an answer”); a follow-on about the Chasam Sofer’s teacher, R’ Nosson Adler, ends with “very interesting … I have to find out the source.” The write-up drops both and has the Chasam Sofer “adjusting.” That Pressburg moved toward Nusach Sefard, and that much of the misnagdim/Chassidim conflict was about nusach, is in the transcript and had been omitted.
  • The author of Da’as Sofer. “He was the Pressburger rav in Yerushalayim, already moved to Eretz Yisrael.” The write-up says only “the first one to make aliyah.” The speaker’s further joke on the next generation (ta’us sofer, a scribal error), which he himself called not nice, is rightly absent.
  • “We still need real misnagdim.” A short aside after the Chofetz Chaim remark, omitted. Also the comparison of the Pressburg split to later divisions (Bobov 48 and 45, the two Satmars, Ponovezh), and the remark that most disputes begin in shul. The sefer edition keeps a generic sentence.
  • The three steps back. The shiur makes a clean point that, were it not for Mar b. Ravina’s prayer, one would step back right after Yihyu Leratzon; since we adopted his prayer we keep our feet together, say it, then step back. The write-up has only that Yihyu Leratzon “seals” the Amidah. Restored in the HTML.
  • The shaliach tzibbur question. An audience member asks why the chazan says Yihyu Leratzon at the end of chazaras hashatz (and why Elokai Netzor is omitted on Tishah B’Av, with a remark attributed to the Netziv). The speaker has no answer (“I have to look at Minchah of Erev Yom Kippur”). Omitted; recorded now as an open question.
  • The principle behind the personal additions. The speaker cites the Gemara as allowing one to add “even like the order of Yom Kippur” (the confession); the transcript is garbled and the write-up replaces it with a bare statement. Not located.
  • The name-verse practice, encouraged. “If you do it, good … the Shelah says to do it … it’s a beautiful thing to do.” The write-up says only that many continue the practice.
  • Attribution of the names discussion. The Nimrod and Yishmael exchange is prompted by R’ Uziel David; the speaker answers the Yishmael question with Yishmael Kohen Gadol. The write-up has only that Yishmael is a Tanach name.
  • The deferral of Sim Shalom against Shalom Rav, and of the remazim of the two endings, to Maariv and to a previous shiur respectively, are omitted. Reasonable; a sentence of cross-reference is worth having.
  • The closing. The shiur closes with Mishnah Makkos 3:16 (Rabbi Chananya ben Akashya). The write-up closes on a composed paragraph (below). Restored in the sefer edition.

5.Added or Editorial Material Not in the Shiur

The final paragraph of Section XIV and the Siyum are composed. “This is the deeper backdrop to the closing of our Shemoneh Esrei…” ties together kavanah, the name and lashon hara in a way the speaker never does, and the Siyum’s “may our names be called in shamayim with honor” is not in the shiur, which closes with a prayer for acceptance and for guarding one’s speech. Fine as a coda if marked; in the sefer edition it is dropped and the shiur’s own ending is used.

Bibliographic particulars in the notes are editorial, not from the shiur, and some are doubtful: the author of Chut HaMeshulash with dates and a generation that contradicts the body; the Shem Ephraim author’s birth year; “Seville, mid-14th century” for the Avudraham; the Radak’s Michlol as the source of the name-verse. Removed or hedged in the HTML.

Section VIII’s framing (“We left off with two questions”) is invented (Flag 6).

6.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The placement debate is cleaner than the shiur makes it, and more complicated. The Rabad (as cited by the Rashba on 17a) places Yihyu Leratzon before the supplications because David said it after eighteen chapters; the Rema (Orach Chaim 122:1) records a place where Elokai Netzor comes first; the Mishnah Berurah (122, se’if katan 3) says it is good to say it before the supplications and after them. Worth noting for the reader: the Gemara’s own text of Mar b. Ravina’s prayer ends with Yihyu Leratzon (Berachos 17a, verified), which is the point of the Rashba’s remark that the books record it so. The write-up, like the shiur, speaks as if Elokai Netzor came only after Yihyu Leratzon.

The Vilna Gaon’s position is reported, not quoted. The speaker prefaces it with “he doesn’t say these words.” The Biur HaGra on Orach Chaim 122 as available on Sefaria discusses only Yihyu Leratzon. The write-up’s “the Gra says:” reads as a quotation; the sefer should say “the position of the Gra, as received.”

Section X’s halachic advice. The shiur says one may answer Kaddish and Kedushah after Yihyu Leratzon. The Shulchan Aruch (122:1) says one does not interrupt for them between the eighteen berachos and Yihyu Leratzon, with the Rema’s exception for the custom of saying Elokai Netzor first. The two statements are consistent but the write-up’s “exactly how the Shulchan Aruch frames it” overstates.

The glass. The Gemara says the guests “became sad” (ve’atzivu), not that they fell silent or that “the hillulus dissipated.” The speaker’s gloss is fair but is his.

Geography and chronology. Mechoza “near Mosul” and Mar b. Ravina “before” the Ravina of Ravina and Rav Ashi are the speaker’s; neither was verified, and both are likely to need correction.

7.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.Which Rishon said Yihyu Leratzon before Elokai Netzor: the Rashba himself, or the Rabad whom the Rashba cites on 17a? And where is the Vilna Gaon’s ruling on Elokai Netzor (Maaseh Rav?) found?
Q2.Who wrote Chut HaMeshulash and where does it tell the Pressburg Oseh/Hamevarech story? Is the nickname “Hamevarech-niks” in the book or the Rav’s coinage? Why did the Chasam Sofer’s circle shift nusach (the question left unanswered), and was the role of R’ Nosson Adler the point the Rav meant to make?
Q3.Which Or HaChaim in Parshas Haazinu is meant for the name and the soul? Or was it the Zohar, as the summary had it?
Q4.Why does the shaliach tzibbur say Yihyu Leratzon at the end of chazaras hashatz, and what is the Netziv’s remark about omitting Elokai Netzor on Tishah B’Av?
Q5.What is the source for the account that a soul forgets its name and is asked for it? The Rav called it a legend and said he did not know where the Shelah gets it.
Q6.The sponsor: “Paley” or “Pele,” and the town (“Selish”), for the dedication.
Q7.The Gemara permitting additions “even like the order of Yom Kippur”: which sugya did the Rav mean?

8.Bottom Line

A faithful and well-shaped write-up of a discursive shiur whose best material (the Worms legend becoming the Gemara of Mar b. Ravina, the glass, the Hong Kong joke, the progression from Gemara to Rishonim to Mishnah Berurah) survives intact. The weaknesses are of two kinds. Most of the factual errors came in through the summary rather than the shiur: the angel (Safriel, not Sandalfon), the Rema, the Or HaChaim, and the Rashi-on-Michah source that was mis-heard as two books. Second, the write-up converts hedged remarks into confident ones: the single-source Pressburg story, the “legend,” the unanswered questions, the invented “two questions.” Fix those, restore the three steps back point and the closing mishnah, replace the composed ending with the shiur’s own, and the sefer edition can stand. The Rashba/Rabad point is the one substantive correction to the shiur itself and should be said plainly in a note.
