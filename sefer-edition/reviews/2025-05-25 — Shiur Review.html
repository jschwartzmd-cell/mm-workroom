Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

25 May 2025 — רְפָאֵנוּ and בָּרֵךְ עָלֵינוּ: Healing, Sustenance, and Rain

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · 5 October 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

Two brachos — the eighth (Refaeinu) and the ninth (Barech Aleinu) — followed by the halachah of v'sein tal u-matar. On Refaeinu: the Gemara in Megillah 17a tying the eighth bracha to bris milah, the Midrash that “baruch rofei cholim” was first uttered by the malachim after Avraham's bris (with the maggid shiur's own self-correction mid-shiur — initially said “rofei cholei amo Yisrael,” then thanked Reb Berel for the correction to “rofei cholim”); the unique license to insert a yehi ratzon inside the bracha; the Rav's open kashya on why siddurim print the yehi ratzon for a choleh but not for parnasah; the Gemara Berachos 34 on Moshe's tefillah for Miriam; the Pele Yo'etz's chiddush on using the mother's name to avoid kitrug on talmud Torah; the Rambam's derashos that choli ha-guf is rooted in choli ha-nefesh; the pasuk from Yirmiyahu 17 noted as the haftorah read the day before (Shabbos, 24 May 2025); the doctor as shaliach, grounded in Bava Kama, with the Rebbe's extended personal account of Dr. Frank Hanley at Stanford; the nisayon of bikkur cholim with the bachur-at-Hadassah vignette; the Gemara on ben gilo taking one-sixtieth. On Barech Aleinu: the agricultural idiom and the Chabad kavanah for matzah, arba minim, and kiddush wine; the Baal Shem Tov's kashya-and-answer on mezonos ketzuvim (quantity decreed, quality not); the Skverer Rebbe / Yankel Shalom Unger “last five minutes of the year”; the Yaaros Devash's “b'heter v'lo b'issur” kavanah; the Bnei Yissaschar on koach ha-po'el ba-nif'al and toxic parnasah; the Smag and Tosafos on gezel akum, followed by the sharp contemporary application to government programs and HUD; and finally the Rosh's lifelong attempt to align tal u-matar with local climate — defeated by mesorah and agudah achas.

2.What Was Strongest in the Shiur

  • The Dr. Frank Hanley story. The maggid shiur's personal account of a nineteen-to-twenty-hour open-heart surgery on his own child, and the surgeon's quiet “this is my job; my hands are guided by God,” is the shiur's emotional center. The frame is unusually honest — “I was clinging to him like he was the savior” — which is what gives the mussar its force. The write-up captures this well in its own callout.
  • The Pele Yo'etz's kitrug framework for the mother's name. The move from the pashut reason (certainty of maternity) to the deeper reason (chiyuv talmud Torah as the opening kitrug on a man's name) carries real conceptual weight, and Dovid HaMelech's “ben amasecha” seals it textually. Both the sheet and the shiur handle this cleanly.
  • The Skverer Rebbe / Reb Yankel Shalom Unger vignette. “The last five minutes of the year!” — weeping at mincha of Erev Rosh Hashanah over “ha-shanah ha-zos” because the whole year still rests on those five minutes — is the most memorable line of the Barech Aleinu half.
  • The Rosh's teshuvah. The arc — birth in Ashkenaz, the Maharam's refusal to be redeemed, the Rindfleisch pogroms, the flight through France, the Rashba's intervention in Barcelona, the first and last Ashkenazi rav of Spain, the drought, the pesach-night attempt, the shul's eruption, the teshuvah written on chol ha-mo'ed for chilul Hashem/davar ha-aved, the final “I lift my hands — agudah achas” — is the shiur's finest piece of narrative mesirah, and the sheet reproduces it with real care.
  • The Baal Shem Tov's quantity-vs-quality chiddush. A short and useful piece of maggid shiur work — the shiur frames it with disarming humility (“I don't want to go into it too much because it's a very controversial topic and the rishonim didn't exactly understand it fully”) — which the sheet quietly sands off but preserves in substance.

3.Topics Covered in the Shiur but Missing / Underdeveloped in the Sheet

The Rav's own mid-shiur self-correction on the Midrash's lashon. In the live shiur the Rav first said that the malachim uttered “baruch rofei cholei amo Yisrael,” was challenged from the floor (“Rebbi, there was no Am Yisrael yet”), paused, acknowledged Reb Berel, and corrected to “baruch rofei cholim.” This live exchange is the whole reason the chasimah question is memorable. The sheet silently reports only the corrected version and loses the exchange. Suggested restoration: a brief footnote or in-text aside — “the Rav initially presented the midrash as ‘rofei cholei amo Yisrael,’ then corrected to ‘rofei cholim’ — precisely the kushya that generates the discussion below.”

The chavrusa-floor attributions. The question about why refuas ha-nefesh precedes refuas ha-guf was explicitly credited by the Rav to Rabbi Pinny (“Rabbi Pinny had asked me an excellent question”); the question on the pshat of “refaeinu Hashem v'nerafei” was addressed to Rabbi Yitzchak; the Dr. Frank Hanley reference was addressed to Rabbi Yossi (“you know Dr. Frank Hanley”). The sheet strips every one of these attributions. For the sefer edition that may be the right editorial call — but at minimum, the fact that the refuas ha-nefesh/ha-guf move was a floor-raised question rather than the Rav's own pre-prepared move is lomdish-structurally significant and should be acknowledged somewhere.

The Rav's own aside — “there's always a choleh in klal Yisrael; I come out every single Shemoneh Esrei and say yehi ratzon for someone.” This is a modeling comment that the oilam can act on. The sheet drops it entirely. For the sefer, this is worth a one-line restoration in the halachic-practice section: “the maggid shiur's own practice is to insert a yehi ratzon at every Shemoneh Esrei, since there is always a choleh in klal Yisrael.”

The sharpness of the cholei amo Yisrael answer. The transcript is unusually blunt: “the goyim don't have cholei ha-nefesh, or if they do, it's not going to get better.” The sheet reports the chiddush but softens the second half. For an audience of readers who will not hear the delivery, the sheet's softening is defensible — but the reader loses the register. If retained, it should be in the Rav's own voice (block-quoted) rather than paraphrased.

The identification of Yankel Shalom Unger. The Rav in the shiur identifies him as “the father of the Ungers — Motch's father” and places the Rav's own tenure in Miami at “nine years.” Both details are stripped from the sheet; the fn15 footnote preserves “settled in Miami in his later years and davened at the small shul on 37th and Prairie” but loses the familial anchor that lets the oilam know who the man was. Suggested fix: restore “father of the Unger family” in the body or the footnote.

The “koach ha-po'el ba-nif'al” material detail. The Rav gives the concrete chain — “pizza, ice cream, chicken” — to make the Bnei Yissaschar's chiddush visceral. The sheet reports the mechanism in abstract (“you are feeding them the residue of the gezel embedded in the bills”), which is cleaner but loses the mussar's bite. Judgment call for the sefer; worth considering restoring one concrete example.

The halachah-practice detail on yehi ratzon for parnasah. The Rav explicitly instructs the oilam: “in your own words, say ‘Ribbono shel Olam, give me parnasah, let the deal go through, let the market go up, let the rates go down’” — followed by the Bitcoin joke. The sheet captures the heter abstractly but does not reproduce the practical script. For a sefer on Shemoneh Esrei, a one-sentence model of what such an insertion sounds like would help the reader actually do it.

The Rav's framing of the bachur-at-Hadassah story as “a tefillah zach” — a tefillah-matter. In the shiur the self-analysis (“I was nineteen, twenty years old; I analyzed my feelings; why did I feel better?”) is explicitly offered as a tefillah-relevant self-awareness, not just a mussar aside. The sheet treats it as a general mussar point; the specific framing as tefillah-work is lost.

4.Points in the Sheet Not Emphasized (or Not Said) in the Shiur

Fn 7 names Dovid HaMelech's mother as “Nitzeves bas Adael.” The Rav never said this. The traditional identification (from Bava Basra 91a) is correct scholarship, but the write-up should flag it as a reviewer's expansion rather than present it as something the Rav said.

Fn 15 identifies the Skverer Rebbe as “likely Rebbe Yaakov Yosef Twersky ha-zaken (1899–1968), founder of New Square.” The Rav said only “the old Skverer Rebbe.” The identification is reasonable but speculative, and the “likely” hedge in the footnote is correct and should be preserved in any downstream edit.

Fn 21 contains a quiet but important tziun-correction: the Rav said Maharam sat in prison “fourteen years,” and the footnote correctly notes the standard reckoning is seven years (1286–1293), with the body redeemed fourteen years later by Rebbi Alexander Zuskind Wimpfen. This is exactly the kind of catch an editorial pass should make and the reviewer should endorse. Consider promoting the correction to a brief in-body footnote marker so the reader does not miss it.

Fn 22 (Arizal, Sha'ar ha-Kavanos via Rebbi Chaim Vital) is a scholarly expansion of the Rav's one-liner (“the Arizal says beg Hashem for the seichel”). The expansion is good; it is not something the Rav said. Fine as a footnote; should not read as direct mesorah from the shiur.

Fn 12 locates the yehi ratzon-insertion halachah in Shulchan Aruch OC 119. The Rav referred to Shulchan Aruch generically but did not cite the siman; the attribution is correct editorial work. Same for fn 19's citation of OC 117 for v'sein tal u-matar.

The sheet's chronology on the Rosh. The body (section XIII) reports “born in Ashkenaz in 1215.” The Rav said this live — and corrected himself mid-sentence (“I think the Rosh was born in 1215. It had to be later, 1293 maybe. The Rosh was born in 1215”). Historians generally give c. 1250–1327, and fn 20 correctly reports “c. 1250–1327.” The sheet thus contains an internal contradiction: 1215 in the body, c. 1250 in the footnote. Fix in body: change “Born in Ashkenaz in 1215” to “Born in Ashkenaz c. 1250.”

The sheet reports the Rav's attribution to the Ran as a confident citation. The transcript says “The Ran, I believe, writes that one should say it in Refaeinu.” The “I believe” hedge is maggid shiur-honesty and should probably be preserved. Fn 11's “R"N on Berachos 34” is a scholarly placement; verify before setting in print.

Fn 8's attribution of cholei ha-guf / cholei ha-nefesh to Shemoneh Perakim and Hilchos De'os. The Rav said “the Rambam writes in his derashos.” The footnote broadens the citation across the Rambam's corpus. The broadening is defensible but should be flagged as reviewer-expansion; the Rav's own citation was specifically “derashos.”

5.Factual / Attribution Issues to Verify

  • Rosh's dates. See above — internal contradiction in the sheet (1215 vs c. 1250). The footnote is right.
  • Maharam's years in prison. Rav said fourteen; the footnote correctly says seven. Keep the correction visible.
  • Rindfleisch pogroms. Rav said late 1290s; the sheet's main body says “late 1290s.” Standard dating is 1298. Fine as written.
  • Mordechai ben Hillel's death and family. Rav and sheet both say “wife Zelda and five children.” Zelda bat Shlomo is the standard identification; children's number is traditionally reported as five. Consistent.
  • Dovid HaMelech's mother. Fn 7 names Nitzeves bas Adael. The Rav did not say this; the identification is from Bava Basra 91a — correct scholarship, but not from the shiur.
  • “Rebbi Yitzchak Kohen” / Minhagei Chabad. The Rav said he saw it “a couple of years ago in minhagei Chabad, Reb Yitzchak Kohen,” and specifically asked the oilam (“you'll tell me and I'm chaim mayim if this is correct”) to verify. The write-up presents it with confidence; worth preserving a trace of the Rav's own invitation to verify, especially since the specific sefer attribution to Reb Yitzchak Kohen is not standard.
  • Shulchan Aruch siman citations for yehi ratzon (OC 119) and tal u-matar (OC 117) are editorial fills, not from the shiur. Both are correct; worth checking the mishnah berurah / biur halachah cross-reference before print.
  • Yankel Shalom Unger. Rav identifies him as “Motch's father” / “father of the Ungers” from Skver. The sheet omits the identification. Worth confirming the exact name (Yankel Shalom vs. Yaakov Shalom — the Rav uses both interchangeably in the transcript) before fixing in print.
  • The maggid shiur's own tenure in Miami. Rav said “I've been here for nine years every Erev Rosh Hashanah.” Dropped by the sheet. If this was the shiur in which the Rav established his Miami yichus to the oilam, it is worth a line.

6.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The “ba'alei mumin”-shaped question that the Rav flagged two weeks ago on Atah Chonen does not resurface here, but it should. If cholei ha-nefesh is the structural root of cholei ha-guf (Rambam), and da'as-deficit is a structural mum (two weeks ago), then Refaeinu is doing double work — healing the cholei ha-nefesh whose absence of da'as was prayed for in the eighth-bracha frame of Atah Chonen. The chaburah-arc is sitting right there and neither the shiur nor the sheet closes the loop. Worth a cross-reference footnote in the sefer.

The mezonos ketzuvim sugya is reported from the Rav as a Baal Shem Tov-attribution, but the Gemara's own list (Beitzah 16a: Shabbos, Yom Tov, talmud Torah of the children) is given in passing. The Baal Shem Tov's chiddush on quality-vs-quantity is one of several rishon- and acharon-level responses to the problem; the sheet reports it as “a chiddush the rishonim circle around,” which is a defensible paraphrase of the Rav's own “the rishonim didn't exactly understand it fully.” But the reader will want to know who else is in the chaburah on this — the Nefesh ha-Chaim, Chasam Sofer, and others have treatments worth cross-referencing in a footnote.

The yehi ratzon-asymmetry between Refaeinu and Birkas ha-Shanim is a genuine kashya the Rav raised and left open. The sheet preserves the open status (“I do not know the answer. If anyone has one, please share”) — good. One sevara worth noting: perhaps the asymmetry reflects not a halachic gap but an emotional-practical one — a choleh has a specific identifiable individual who must be named and timed to the moment of tefillah, whereas dochak parnasah is a diffuse state that the mispallel himself carries without needing a scripted insertion. The siddur-printers may have calibrated to the typical use case rather than the halachic permission. If the Rav wants to engage this when he returns, worth flagging.

The Rosh's “agudah achas” reasoning should be contrasted with his pesak elsewhere that mesorah yields to chiyuvei de-oraisa. If tal u-matar is me'akev (which is the entire reason for the drought-emergency context), the Rosh's backing down looks like a lifnim mi-shuras ha-din yielding of a halachically defensible chiddush to communal shalom — not a concession on the merits. The sheet reports the yielding as if it settled the halachah; a line distinguishing halachic yielding from halachic error would sharpen it.

The Smag's pesak on gezel akum as chilul Hashem, and its contemporary application to government programs, deserves at least one anchor in a contemporary posek. The Rav is correct that the Smag's yesod is sharp, and he is honest that “all my friends in Lakewood are on government programs.” But the move from Smag to a sharp indictment of HUD applications is a gadol-level pesak-shaped claim, and the sefer edition should either sharpen the halachic argument (what, exactly, makes a given program gezel akum?) or soften the conclusion. As published, the sheet carries the mussar without the halachic infrastructure.

7.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.On the yehi ratzon-asymmetry: does the Rav hold that the halachic permission to insert in Birkas ha-Shanim is equally operative, or is there a chiluk — perhaps because parnasah-dochak is diffuse and cholah is identified — that explains the siddur-printers' practice? And would the Rav endorse a minhag of regularly inserting “Ribbono shel Olam, give me parnasah” on the model he described in-shiur, or reserve it for actual dochak?
Q2.On cholei amo Yisrael: the Rav explained the chasimah's particularism through cholei ha-nefesh (goyim do not have cholei ha-nefesh in our sense). But the opening “baruch rofei cholim” of the midrash is universal. Does the Rav hold that the bracha contains a “universalist-opening / particularist-close” architecture, parallel to the “sim shalom” opening on Yisrael and closing on “ha-mevarech es amo Yisrael ba-shalom”? And if so, is the opening “refaeinu” itself a tefillah on behalf of all cholei ha-olam?
Q3.On the Pele Yo'etz's kitrug-mechanism: the yesod that mentioning the father triggers a “where is the Torah he learned today?” kitrug implies that the beis din shel ma'alah processes a name-mention as an open-file event. Is this a specific kabbalistic-mechanic, or a mussar-shaped idiom? And how does it square with the halachah of “ein mazkirin maaseh” — the general principle that in tefillah one does not invite scrutiny of the mispallel?
Q4.On koach ha-po'el ba-nif'al and toxic parnasah: the Bnei Yissaschar's mussar is powerful but the mechanism is strong — a re'si'ah-level claim about how food, tahara, and children's ne'emanus la-Torah connect. Does the Rav read the Bnei Yissaschar's claim as literally causal (physical particles of gezel transmitted through food), as middas-ha-din-mediated, or as something like a pedagogic parable? And is teshuvah on past gezel sufficient to break the koach ha-po'el ba-nif'al-chain going forward?
Q5.On government programs: does the Rav want to name the exact halachic criterion that distinguishes a legitimate claim from a gezel akum-shaped one? Is it “does the applicant actually meet the stated income/need threshold?”, or something broader — “does the applicant could support himself without the program?”? The sefer-reader will want to know how to think about his own case.
Q6.On the Rosh's yielding: does the Rav hold that the Rosh's “agudah achas” is a one-time hora'as sha'ah, or a standing yesod that liturgical mesorah trumps halachic argumentation about climate even today? If a beis din today in Argentina or Australia wanted to re-raise the question, would the Rosh's own teshuvah permit the attempt, or does it foreclose it?

8.Structural Recommendations

  • Collapse the two lead paragraphs or merge the sponsorship note into a dedicated short header. The sheet currently stacks a long topical lead and a long sponsorship lead; the second one is doing memorial / simchah work that reads naturally as a header-level note rather than a lead paragraph.
  • The Refaeinu half (sections I–VI) and the Barech Aleinu half (VII–XI) and the tal u-matar half (XII–XIV) should be presented as three Parts rather than fourteen flat H2 sections. The shiur's own architecture is tripartite; the sheet's flat sectioning obscures it.
  • Section XI's long government-programs callout is the sheet's most eis la'asos paragraph. Consider whether it belongs in the main text, in a “contemporary application” appendix, or in a footnote. As it stands it shifts the register of the sefer toward mussar-exhortation at the one place readers may most want careful halachic treatment.
  • The transition from the Chabad kavanah (VII) to the Baal Shem Tov's kashya (VIII) to the Skverer story (end of VIII) to Yaaros Devash (IX) is well-done. The transition from Bnei Yissaschar (X) into gezel akum (XI) is clean. The transition from gezel akum (XI) into tal u-matar (XII) is not — the shiur itself jumped (“let's continue”), but the sefer should supply a bridging sentence: perhaps anchoring on the fact that both halves are embedded in Birkas ha-Shanim.
  • Restore the Rav's live chaburah-correction on the midrash's lashon. The exchange with Reb Berel is the pedagogical fulcrum of the chasimah-question and the cholei amo Yisrael answer; stripping it loses the “question drives answer” architecture.
  • Reconcile the Rosh's dates in the body with the footnote. See Section 5 above.

9.Bottom Line

A clean and well-paced shiur that pairs two brachos with a long Rosh-teshuvah narrative at the close, and that carries three unforgettable set-pieces — the Dr. Frank Hanley story, the Skverer's weeping over the last five minutes of the year, and the Rosh's lifelong campaign ending in “I lift my hands.” The sheet is faithful to the shiur's substance and in several places does the editorial work the shiur does not (dates, siman-citations, chronology of the Maharam). It also makes a handful of silent expansions — Dovid HaMelech's mother's name, the identification of the Skverer Rebbe, the attribution of Rambam's cholei ha-nefesh-yesod to specific sefarim — that are good scholarship but should be flagged as reviewer-expansions, not reported as if the Rav said them. The main textual repair needed for the sefer is reconciling the Rosh's birth date (1215 in the body vs. c. 1250 in fn 20) and restoring the Rav's own mid-shiur self-correction on the midrash's “rofei cholim” / “rofei cholei amo Yisrael” — the exchange that generates the whole chasimah discussion. The gezel akum / government-programs stretch is the shiur's riskiest piece and should be either sharpened with a halachic criterion or softened; as written in the sheet it carries the Rav's mussar-charge without the supporting infrastructure a reader will need.
