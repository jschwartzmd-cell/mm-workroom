29 June 2025 — בִּרכַת הַמִינִים and בִּרכַת עַל הַצַּדִּיקִים

Transcript-vs-write-up comparison · reviewer read the source HTML, the transcript, and the summary · 5 October 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

Continuing from the prior week, the Rav picks up בִּרכַת הַמִינִים with the Rambam's framing in Hilchos Tefillah, the identification of the minim as Yoshke's talmidim within Klal Yisrael's own community, a long back-and-forth with Reb Faivy on the trinity (sourced, in the shiur, to Rav Shimon Schwab's sefer, which the Rav concedes he needs to re-check), the mal'achim's original utterance at kri'as yam suf, Shmuel HaKatan's composition of the bracha, the Nicholas Donin / Paris disputation / 1242 burning of the Shas as the source of the minim-to-akum substitution, the Aruch's peirush with Benjamin Mussafia's striking aleph-removal chiddush and the Gershom Scholem exposé of Mussafia's Sabbatean affiliation, the Avodah Zarah 27b ma'aseh in Tzippori as homage to Rav Kalish zt"l, and the lashon of the bracha through the Frierdiker Rebbe's ma'aseh. Then Birkas Al HaTzaddikim: the Megillah-derived connection via Tehillim 75, the six categories, the ma'aseh of Reb Yitzchak Charif and the Yesod HaAvodah, Rav Shimon Schwab (not Rav Hirsch, as the write-up has it) on “peleitah,” the Yaaros Devash on geri hatzedek, Rav Yisrael Salanter to Reb Naftali Amsterdam, and finally the Arizal's pshat on “chelkenu”, closing with Rabbeinu Avraham ben HaRambam on bitachon and the Yaakov-Yosef midrash.

2.Content from the Shiur Missing from the Write-up

  • The Rav Shimon Schwab attribution for the trinity point is dropped. In the transcript the Rav is explicit — “I was looking at Schwab's sefer. … What Schwab writes in a sefer, that the Christians, one of the main things they wanted the Jews to believe in is the trinity” — and he openly concedes he needs to check Schwab's sources again. The write-up turns this into a flat assertion by the Rav plus a bracketed editorial note on Reb Faivy, erasing Schwab entirely.
  • The Paul / Yochanan aside is cut. The transcript carries a lengthy and striking digression — “Some say it was Paul. Some call him Yoichanan, a very mysterious figure who we still talk about in hushed tones … one of our great sages infiltrated the Christians and changed Shabbos … some say he wrote Nishmas. Rashi writes that whoever says that should do tshuva” — a theologically live piece of the shiur. The write-up compresses this to a single sober clause: “Paul (some identify him as Shaul) was the figure who carried the message to the Romans”, deleting both the Yochanan identification and the Nishmas-authorship controversy with its Rashi.
  • The Yaaros Devash attribution for the geri hatzedek teaching is omitted. The transcript explicitly names the source: “yaris, basha, bionus and dibshits” — i.e. Rav Yonasan Eibeschutz's Ya'aros Devash, who gave the derashah in his Prague shul. The write-up strips the attribution to “a teaching he saw long ago in a derashah from a Prague beis knesses”, which is both vaguer and anonymizing.
  • The Oruch's print-history fun fact is dropped. The transcript includes the aside that the Oruch was “one of the first Hebrew books to be printed after Tanakh and Talmud and Rambam”. The write-up loses this.
  • The Rubashkin “aleph beis gimel of emunah/bitachon” aside is cut. The transcript has it in the lead-in to Rabbeinu Avraham ben HaRambam; the write-up removes it. Minor, but it was in the shiur.
  • The Rebbe's open admission on “temager” is softened. Transcript: “I don't know … I wonder what the artscroll … I don't know exactly what each one is referring to.” The write-up tightens the vignette considerably and provides a cleaner answer (“to fragment, to crumble”) than the Rav actually offered at the live moment.
  • The Shmuel HaKatan hespedim material is dropped. Transcript: “The hespedim that were said on Shmuel Hakatan were full of weeping and crying by the chachamim. They said a king dies; he leaves his crown for the next generation. But Shmuel Hakatan … chamudo shel olam v'halach lo.” The write-up jumps straight to “was niftar without children”, losing the chachamim's framing.

3.Content in the Write-up Not Said in the Shiur (or Embellished)

Wrong attribution: Rav Shamshon Raphael Hirsch. The write-up (Section on “peleitas beis sofreihem”) says: “Rav Shamshon Raphael Hirsch in his sefer hatefillah explains the word 'peleitah' — survivors, remnants.” Footnote 19 even quotes The Hirsch Siddur. But the transcript is unambiguous: “Rabbi Shimon Schwab explains in the safer why they called 'plateh' basically 'frame.' 'Plateh' means the remnants.” The write-up has substituted Hirsch for Schwab — two different Frankfurt-tradition ba'alei siddur, but not interchangeable. This is a clear mischaracterization.

Wrong attribution (likely): Rav Aharon Schechter. The transcript says “I daven for Avdon Segal”. The summary and write-up both render this as “Rav Aharon Schechter shlit"a”. Even allowing for mistranscription (Avdon → Aharon), the surnames do not align. The write-up should either preserve what was said or flag that the editor has corrected what he takes to be an audio error.

The Paris Disputation footnote is heavily built out beyond anything the Rav said. The transcript gives: “the famous Talmud disputation, the vikuach about the Talmud in Paris. And in twelve forty two, I think that's what it was. The Talmud was burned.” Footnote 17 adds Donin's thirty-five accusations to Pope Gregory IX in 1238, the 1240 trial under Louis IX, the four named defenders (Rabbeinu Yechiel, Moshe miCoucy, Yehuda ben David, Shmuel ben Shlomo mi-Falaise), “twenty-four cartloads”, and the “La Rochelle” provenance. None of this is in the shiur; it is editorial research presented as transcription.

The Mussafia biography is also built out. Transcript: “an Italian scholar that lived in the sixteen hundreds. He actually lived most of his life in Holland.” Footnote 15 adds “c. 1606–1675, born in Spain, raised in Hamburg, lived most of his life in Amsterdam; physician and linguist.” Fine as a footnote — but it is editorial, not the Rav's.

The Frierdiker Rebbe identification is a (likely correct) editorial fix. The transcript says “the freeke bubba varebbe Reb Shleimel” and the Hebrew summary says “רבי שלמה.” Both would point to Rav Shalom DovBer (the Rashab), not the Frierdiker Rebbe. The write-up renders it as “Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn”. This is probably the right Rebbe for this ma'aseh in standard Chabad oral tradition — but the write-up presents it as what was said, without noting the audio ambiguity.

The Reb Faivy callout re-engineers the exchange. The write-up frames Reb Faivy's kushya as resolved by independent research (“Looking into this — Reb Faivy is correct…”) and immediately moves to a theological save (“This does not affect the takanah…”). In the transcript, the Rav's actual response is less tidy — he keeps toggling between Reb Faivy's point, Schwab's unverified source, and a tangent on Paul/Yochanan. The write-up's clean resolution does not match the shiur's live shape.

Expanded verb-by-verb gloss in the Frierdiker Rebbe story. The write-up offers the full six-verb exchange in neat parallel; the transcript's rendering is choppier and the bachur's answers are briefer (“Sachali means get rid of them”, etc.). Not a distortion, but noticeably polished.

Section XII (“Bitachon — Real and Pretended”) adds a reflective sentence — “The bushah of having spoken these words a thousand times and not actually having held them is the embarrassment we daven not to face” — that is a graceful editorial amplification. The Rav's transcript simply says: “we shouldn't have the busha. We shouldn't have the cherpah and busha when we come to that.”

4.Mischaracterizations, Wrong Attributions, Sequence/Emphasis Issues

Attribution of the shiur itself. The write-up's header reads “Shiur of Rabbi Ephrati.” This file is from the Rabbi Taub set per your task framing (the transcript opens with a greeting to “Rav Yisroel” in the audience, not as the maggid shiur). This should be verified; if the shiur is Rabbi Taub's, the header attribution is wrong.

“Rav Natan” is cited without identification. The write-up carries over the transcript's “Rav Natan asks” on “ve'aleinu” without naming the source. In context the most likely referent is Rav Natan of Breslov in Likutei Halachos; the write-up should either confirm the identification in a footnote or flag the attribution as uncertain. As it stands, a reader cannot tell which Rav Natan.

Sequence shift on the Avodah Zarah ma'aseh. In the transcript, the Rav introduces the Tzippori ma'aseh briefly, then uses it as a springboard to the Rav Kalish appreciation, then (in the next paragraph) returns to the Rishonim's expansion of the bracha's scope and the oivim/zedim distinction. The write-up's Section VI makes the Rav Kalish tribute carry the whole ma'aseh, with the actual sugya content reduced to a one-line summary. The emphasis is inverted: in the shiur the ma'aseh was the primary content and the Rav Kalish tribute was the human aside.

“Rav Shimon Schwab” is used twice in the transcript — once for the trinity, once for “peleitah.” The write-up erases the first (Section II callout) and substitutes Rav Hirsch for the second (Section on peleitas beis sofreihem). The net effect: a maggid shiur who was audibly leaning on Rav Schwab in two different places appears in the written edition to lean on him in neither.

The oivim/zedim distinction is presented as fresh. The write-up correctly notes “as we discussed last week”, matching the transcript. Good.

The Chofetz Chaim parenthetical on lashon hara is presented as the Rav's own confident citation. The transcript shows the Rav uncertain: “I don't know what the origin for it is. I don't know where he gets it from.” The write-up's footnote 23 presents a confident doctrinal exposition from Chofetz Chaim and Shemiras HaLashon. A note that the Rav himself was uncertain about the source would preserve the honesty of the live moment.

5.Specific Sources — Discrepancies

S1.Missing from write-up, present in shiur: Rav Shimon Schwab (on the trinity); Rashi's view that one who says a Jewish sage wrote Nishmas “should do teshuvah”; Yaaros Devash (R' Yonasan Eibeschutz) as named author of the Prague derashah on geri hatzedek.
S2.Present in write-up, not said in shiur: Rav Shamshon Raphael Hirsch's Siddur (substituted for Rav Shimon Schwab); specific defender-names at the Paris Disputation (Moshe miCoucy, Yehuda ben David, Shmuel ben Shlomo mi-Falaise); the “twenty-four cartloads” and the 1238/1240/1242 dating sequence; Mussafia's dates and “Hamburg” origin; Rav Aharon Schechter (where the transcript has “Avdon Segal”); the identification of the Rebbe in the ma'aseh as the Frierdiker Rebbe rather than Rav Shalom DovBer; a “Shaar HaKavanos” citation for the Arizal (the transcript says “V'Chaim V'Tal, this talmid writes in the Kavonos” — close but not literally the same).
S3.Attribution mismatch worth confirming: The transcript has the “Rebbe shares a teaching he saw long ago in a drasha from a Prague beis knesses” as the Yaaros Devash; the write-up anonymizes to “a drasha from a Prague beis knesses.” Restoring R' Yonasan Eibeschutz would strengthen the write-up and faithfully reflect what the Rav said.
S4.Mussafia's second reason — “minim because there are so many kinds of kofrim” — is correctly carried in the write-up (Section V), matching the transcript.
S5.The Gershom Scholem material is faithful. The transcript's warning (“Don't read too much Gershom Shalom, but a little, limited … he could turn you to koifah very easily”) is softened in the write-up to a neutral citation. The warning itself is worth preserving in the footnote as a flavor of the Rav's live register.

6.Bottom Line

The write-up is a careful, polished reconstruction — but it is a reconstruction in a stronger sense than its presentation admits. Two live source-citations by the Rav (Rav Shimon Schwab on the trinity, Rav Shimon Schwab on “peleitah” — the latter silently converted to Rav Hirsch) have been displaced. One identified source (Yaaros Devash) has been anonymized. One memorable aside (“Yochanan” and the Nishmas controversy) has been cut entirely. The Paris Disputation footnote is built out of standard reference-work material rather than from what was said. The Frierdiker Rebbe identification corrects what sounds, in the audio, like Rav Shalom DovBer — probably the right correction, but one the write-up should flag. For the sefer: restore Schwab in both places, restore Yaaros Devash, restore the Yochanan aside (even in a footnote), and distinguish the Rav's live citations from editorial research built out around them. The sefer's credibility depends on the reader being able to trust that when it says “the Rav says”, the Rav actually said it.
