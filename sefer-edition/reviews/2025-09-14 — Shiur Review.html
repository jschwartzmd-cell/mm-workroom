Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

14 September 2025 — מכניסי רחמים and the Question of Intermediaries

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, the AI summary, the enhanced transcript text, and the finished write-up (no Sofer.ai document exists for this date) · 12 October 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

An Elul shiur, delivered the morning after the first night of Selichos and cut short so the room could attend a breakfast fundraiser. It opens by picking up Sim Shalom: the old Ashkenazi ending “oseh ha-shalom” kept only in the Aseres Yemei Teshuvah, and the sefarim’s gematria (השלום = 381 = ספריאל), which raises the question whether we are in fact invoking an angel. The lecturer then turns to the previous night’s Machnisei Rachamim, to a practical question put to him that week (a traditional Selichos or a musical one), and to the Baal Shem Tov’s story of the orphan shepherd boy whose flute annulled a decree. He asks whether Machnisei Rachamim (and Barchuni LeShalom, Malachei Rachamim, Midas HaRachamim and the “Zevadyah yishmereini” after Hallel) is prayer to angels, and sets out the problem: the Rambam (kefirah / avodah zarah to petition anyone but Hashem), the Yerushalmi (a king of flesh and blood needs intermediaries, the King of Kings does not), and the Ramban’s drashah Toras Hashem Temimah (“take it out of your machzor”). The Chasam Sofer (asked by his student, the Maharam Ash) brings the Maharal (one letter, yachnisu) and himself lengthened his tachanun in silence; the Maharit Asad rules against public denunciation; the Gra and Reb Chaim Volozhiner skipped Barchuni; Rav Moshe Feinstein reports that his father (Rav Dovid, rav of the chassidim in Uzda, later Starobin) never said any of these. A challenge from the room (what of going to a rebbe or a rav for a blessing?) leads to the Gemara and the Nimukei Yosef on going to a sage, and then to three answers: R. Yaakov bar Yakar (Rashi’s teacher) reads machnisei as living tzaddikim; tzaddikim be-mitasam keruyim chayim and the absence of any risk of ta’us (no one thinks the rebbe is God); and the Mahari Bruna’s reading of shiflus (turning to a minister because we are too ashamed to approach the King). The shiur ends abruptly, unfinished, to be continued the next week.

2.Overall Fidelity — What the Write-Up Does Well

The write-up is the AI summary almost line for line, with a footnote apparatus added. For the argument it does retain, it is accurate and well proportioned: the problem (Rambam, Yerushalmi, Ramban), the responses (Maharal, Chasam Sofer, Maharit Asad, the Litvish abstention), the pivot to tzaddikim, and the three answers all appear in the order in which the lecturer built them, and the cleaned-up structure (twelve sections) is a real improvement on a live discussion that doubled back repeatedly. Hebrew terms are consistently in Hebrew letters. The note about Rav Dovid Feinstein being a misnaged who served as the chassidic rav is correctly preserved, as is the Chasam Sofer’s silent nefilas apayim practice, which is the most memorable single detail of the shiur. The footnote apparatus is conscientious and hedges honestly where it could not pin a siman.

The write-up’s weakness is not infidelity but omission and compression: the whole first third of the shiur (the practical question, the Besht story, the other liturgical examples) is gone, several biographical details are dropped, and a few specifics have been stated more firmly than the lecturer stated them.

3.Content Flags — What Is Wrong, Misattributed, Omitted, or Added

A. Misattributions and overclaims

Flag 1 — “The Sephardim explain” should read “the sefarim say.” The write-up (Section I) attributes the השלום = 381 = ספריאל gematria to ספרדים. The recording says “the sefarim say” (the transcript renders it “the Sfaram”; the enhanced text reads ספרים). A single mis-hearing turned a general reference to books into a Sephardi source. The arithmetic itself checks (ה 5 + ש 300 + ל 30 + ו 6 + ם 40 = 381; ס 60 + פ 80 + ר 200 + י 10 + א 1 + ל 30 = 381). The lecturer gave no named source.

Flag 2 — Shabbos 12b is presented as the ground of the Ramban’s prohibition; in the shiur it is a kashya. The write-up (Section V, fn 5) says “the Gemara in Shabbos 12b forbids asking malachim.” The lecturer said only that the Ramban writes “because of this Gemara” (the Yerushalmi just cited), and it was later, in the discussion, that he raised the Aramaic Gemara (“the malachei ha-sharet don’t understand Aramaic”) as “another kashya that all the acharonim ask”, because it implies that angels carry our tefillos. The text of Shabbos 12b (and Sotah 33a) does not forbid anything. A short passing remark about Tosafos and angels as “pasqanim” is too garbled to use.

Flag 3 — The Chasam Sofer is said to cite the Ramban; the lecturer said he does not. The write-up (fn 7, and Section VI) has the Chasam Sofer “cite the Ramban and Maharal.” In the recording: “he quotes a Maharal; he doesn’t even bring down the Ramban. I don’t know if he had this Ramban.” The lecturer then adds, as a separate point, that the Chasam Sofer often quoted the Ramban and that great tzaddikim said he was the shoresh neshamah of the Ramban (omitted from the write-up; see B). The Chasam Sofer’s own words, quoted in the lecturer’s paraphrase (“it is a shtikel kefirah, the Ramban, the Maharal, I don’t want to say it”), are the lecturer’s retelling and should not be framed as a located quotation. [Sefaria check: Chasam Sofer OC 166 does treat Machnisei Rachamim, relies on the Maharal, and records מכניסי רחמים דרכי להאריך בנפילת אפים; it cites the Ramban on the Torah only for another point.]

Flag 4 — The Maharit Asad “operative ruling” (fn 11) is embellished. The lecturer reports only: someone banged on the bimah and declared that anyone who says Machnisei Rachamim is an idolater; the Maharit Asad wrote “chas ve-shalom to say such a thing,” that gedolim ve-tzaddikim said the nusach, that the Maharal and the Ramban have a difficulty, but that one may not change the nusach and call everyone a kofer. The footnote adds that doing so is “a chillul Hashem against the holiness of minhag Yisrael”; that is not in the shiur. The attribution of the responsum to OC and the biography (“Dunaszerdahely, 1794–1866”) are also the write-up’s own research and unverified.

Flag 5 — Quoted “speech” in block quotations. The Ramban (“Do not say Machnisei Rachamim. Remove it from your machzor”), the Chasam Sofer (“I did not want to change the nusach…”), Rav Moshe’s father, R. Yaakov bar Yakar and the Mahari Bruna are all set in indented block quotations, as though they were located texts. Every one is the lecturer’s paraphrase delivered in the first person. The Sefer Edition should present them in substance (“the Ramban is reported to have urged…”) and not as quotations.

Flag 6 — The Maharal’s change is described differently in the write-up’s notes and body. The body says the Maharal turned a request into a command (יַכְנִיסוּ); fn 8 calls it “a declaration of how Hashem uses these angels.” The lecturer said “command.” [Sefaria check: the Maharal (Netiv HaAvodah 12) objects to הכניסו רחמינו as seeming to address the angels, permits reading it as an instruction to the appointed angels, and prefers the future form so that the request is clearly addressed to Hashem.] The Sefer Edition should report the lecturer’s version and the Maharal’s own purpose side by side.

Flag 7 — “Answer 1” is slightly misframed. The write-up has R. Yaakov bar Yakar teach that we are asking the tzaddikim to bring in our tefillos. The lecturer says the phrase is not addressed to the angels but to the living tzaddikim of the generation, who protect the generation and carry its prayers in with their own; in the same breath he remarks that “they don’t hear us when we say it” (the recording is unclear on whom this refers to), and that we are really “davening to Hashem” (“Machnisei Rachamim” being a description of who brings in the mercy). Whether the line is addressed to them or only names them is left open by the recording and should be left open in the Sefer Edition.

Flag 8 — Date of the Nimukei Yosef. The write-up says “the late 1300s”; the transcript has the lecturer saying both “fourteen hundreds” and “late thirteen hundreds”, the enhanced text “the 1400s, in the later 1400s.” The lecturer never gave a precise date. [Sefaria check: Yosef ibn Habib / Habiba, c. 1330–1410.]

B. Omissions

  • The practical question of the week. The lecturer was asked whether to attend a traditional Selichos with one’s father or a musical one. He presents his answer in the Baal Shem Tov’s story: at a Selichos where everyone was screaming, an orphan shepherd boy who knew no prayer took out his flute and played; his flute, from the deepest place in his heart, annulled a decree against Klal Yisrael that all the prayers could not. He then turns it around: in a shul where everyone brings a guitar and a flute, there may be one Jew who knows only the words, and those words may annul the decree. He is explicit that he is not criticizing the music (“chas ve-shalom”; hundreds of thousands of Jews rose early for Selichos, and Hashem has great nachas). None of this is in the write-up.
  • The other liturgical examples. Malachei Rachamim mesharsei elyon; Barchuni LeShalom malachei ha-shalom on Friday night; the Midas HaRachamim (an attribute, not an angel, said at the shalosh regalim); and the “Zevadyah yishmereini ve-yechayeini” after Hallel, which the Chafetz Chaim was reported to dislike seeing in the siddurim (the lecturer notes it is not even a pasuk; the room offers the “yishmereini not tishmereini” and “it is about the angel, not to him” distinctions, which, as the discussion records, do not rescue Barchuni or Machnisei). All omitted.
  • The sources said aloud. The recitation of the fifth of the Thirteen Principles (לו לבדו ראוי להתפלל ואין לזולתו); the remark that Machnisei Rachamim “comes from the Geonim already” (which the write-up turns into Rav Amram); that an unspecified Tosafos or Aramaic passage is relevant; and the Gemara’s “mi she-yesh lo cholah be-veiso yelech etzel chacham”.
  • The previous Shabbos’s shiur. The lecturer says that the question of speaking to the dead at kivrei tzaddikim (a week spent davening at graves) was the subject of last Shabbos’s shiur, “connected but different.” Worth one cross-reference line.
  • The Ramban’s drashah on Rosh Hashanah: the lecturer’s aside on how a four-to-five-hour drashah fit in (people went home, made kiddush, and came back). The write-up keeps “four to five hours” but drops the colour and the lecturer’s explicit mention that the Ramban was a gifted orator who took part in the disputation (a biographical detail the write-up sharpens in one clause only).
  • The Chasam Sofer as shoresh neshamah of the Ramban. Omitted (see Flag 3); also the remark that the Chasam Sofer quotes the Ramban “every second or third line.” Per the project rule (more detail on the biographical narrative), this belongs in the Sefer Edition, attributed.
  • The Chasam Sofer’s reasoning for not changing the nusach: “we don’t change nuschaos so easily”, and that a ten-minute tachnun would have been needed had everyone sung at length. Compressed in the write-up.
  • Rav Moshe’s father: details. Rav Moshe wrote “my father… I was a kid”: the testimony is a child’s memory of his father’s synagogue practice. The lecturer says the father held it to be assur gamur. He was rav in Uzda (near Minsk) and later in Starobin. The lecturer adds the parallel of Dvinsk, where the Ohr Somayach was the rav of the misnagdim and the Rogatchover of the chassidim, and a mild joke about hiring a Litvish rav as well. The write-up gives only Uzda.
  • The challenge from the room about rebbes. Someone objects: “by this logic you shouldn’t go to rebbes.” The lecturer qualifies (“not even rebbes, a rav or whoever”), and the interlocutor reframes the question as whether the angels would be acceptable if they are only a conduit. The challenge is the pivot of the shiur and is flattened in the write-up to “an obvious question.”
  • Unexplained distinction: tzaddik gozer ve-HaKadosh Baruch Hu mekayem. The lecturer offers it as the chiluk and then breaks off (“what’s the pshat?”). The write-up drops it. It should appear in the Sefer Edition as a stated but undeveloped line of answer.
  • The Rambam’s thirteen principles and the practical challenge: “go give the pidyon and don’t ask for a blessing”; the remark that Chazal’s test of danger is asi le-mitei (אתי למטעי, “it may lead to error”).
  • Closing. After the Mahari Bruna, the lecturer says a similar view is held by some acharonim and that someone in the room mentioned it, but he cannot recall who; then a remark that the best time for tefillah is Elul (melech ba-sadeh), which may be offered as a further consideration (the recording does not show how). He announces that he has not yet given all the sources (he begins “Rabbi Chananyah ben Akashyah omer…” but breaks off) and promises to continue next week.
  • Asides. A mention that he gave the shiur briefly because of a breakfast fundraiser; a long aside about the 95-year-old Rav Dov Landau of Slabodka (Bnei Brak) flying that morning to raise funds for lomdei Torah; a joke about “PJ” (private jet); references to a political acquaintance and the Hungary trip. None belongs in the body of a sefer; the Landau remark is the only biographical content and may be preserved in the notes.

C. Added or editorial material not in the shiur

Sections XI (“Synthesis”) and XII (“Closing”) are composed. The lecturer ended abruptly. The “Synthesis” (the Rambam’s principle stands, the Ramban’s warning stands, … “what does not stand: branding those who recite the nusach as kofrim”) and the closing prayer for the Yamim Noraim are the editor’s voice. Mark them as an editorial coda or drop them. The final line of the footer (“Hebrew terms preserved in the Hebrew alphabet”) and “Reworked 3 May 2026” in the header are also production notes.

Resolved from the enhanced text. “בתחפיסה” in the Mahari Bruna answer is a mis-transcription of בדווקא (“we go on purpose to a minister”; transcript: “bitafkigo”; enhanced text: בדוקא). “Majerosa” and “Budapest” are an aside on where the group was on the two previous Sundays. “Kvasim / kibbutz anikim” is kivrei tzaddikim. “Rebbe Bar Yoker” is R. The names of the rebbes the lecturer lists (“the Tosh Rebbe”, the Vizhnitzer or “B’nai Yisroel” Rebbe, “Rav Kook in Tveria”, the Skverer Rebbe); the Tosafos remark about angels as pasqanim; the identity of the speaker who mentions the Elul melech ba-sadeh; the exact wording of the question about concerts.

Sefaria pass. The footnote apparatus was checked against Sefaria. Verified: Yerushalmi Berachos 9:1 (patron; “not to Michael nor Gabriel”); Rambam, Hil. 5.Recommended Corrections for the Sefer Edition

  1. Change “the Sephardim explain” to “the sefarim say” (Flag 1).
  2. Present the Shabbos 12b Gemara as a difficulty raised in discussion, not as the Ramban’s source (Flag 2).
  3. Say that the Chasam Sofer brings the Maharal; give the lecturer’s doubt about the Ramban; add the shoresh neshamah remark as the lecturer’s report; cite OC 166 (Flag 3).
  4. Remove the chillul Hashem gloss and present the Maharit Asad in the lecturer’s terms (Flag 4).
  5. Convert all block quotations of oral paraphrase into reported speech (Flag 5).
  6. Report the lecturer’s “command” and the Maharal’s own purpose together (Flag 6).
  7. Keep the addressee of the R. Yaakov bar Yakar reading open (Flag 7).
  8. Restore the opening question (traditional versus musical Selichos) and the Baal Shem Tov’s flute story, in the Besht’s own terms, as the lecturer told it.
  9. Restore the parallel liturgical examples (Malachei Rachamim, Barchuni, Midas HaRachamim, Zevadyah).
  10. Restore the biographical detail: Rav Dovid Feinstein (Uzda, later Starobin; testimony as a child; assur gamur), the Dvinsk parallel, the Ramban as orator, the Chasam Sofer as shoresh neshamah.
  11. Restore the challenge from the room, the tzaddik gozer line, and the lecturer’s closing remarks, marking the unfinished state. Drop the invented “Synthesis” and mark any closing as an editorial coda.
  12. Drop live cues (breakfast, the Landau flight, PJ, “last night”, “next week”); keep Landau only if a biographical note is wanted.

6.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav, and Bottom Line

Q1.Where does the Ramban say “take it out of your machzor”? Is it in Toras Hashem Temimah itself, and is the Ramban speaking of Machnisei Rachamim by name or of petitioning angels in general?
Q2.The Chasam Sofer’s remark about Rav Amram: which source did the Rav have in mind? (It is not in OC 166.) Does Machnisei Rachamim actually appear in Seder Rav Amram?
Q3.Which teshuvah of the Maharit Asad, and which volume of Igros Moshe for Rav Moshe’s father?
Q4.Where does the Mahari Bruna (or the acharonim mentioned afterward) say that Machnisei Rachamim expresses shiflus? And who is the later authority the room recalled?
Q5.What is the Nimukei Yosef’s own wording of the French custom, and on which daf?
Q6.What was the point of the tzaddik gozer ve-HaKadosh Baruch Hu mekayem line as an answer to the angels question? And what is the further consideration drawn from Elul (melech ba-sadeh) with which the shiur ends?
A faithful rendering of the part of the shiur it keeps, which is the argument from the Rambam to the three answers. But it is missing the first third of the shiur (the Selichos question, the Besht’s flute, the other liturgical examples) and several biographical details, has promoted the Shabbos 12b passage to a role it did not have, has the Chasam Sofer citing the Ramban against the lecturer’s own doubt, adds a fabricated gloss to the Maharit Asad, and closes with a composed synthesis the lecturer did not give. Fix the eight flags, restore the omitted material, mark all oral quotations as reported, and keep the open items open. The Sefaria pass confirms the main frame (Yerushalmi, Rambam, Chasam Sofer OC 166, Maharal) and corrects the secondary details.
