22 June 2025 — הָשִׁיבָה שׁוֹפְטֵינוּ and בִּרְכַּת הַמִּינִים

Independent review · read fresh, no exposure to the writing process · 5 October 2026

1.Overall Read

A compact entry in the series — roughly 2,000 words of body plus a seventeen-note Mareh Mekomos apparatus — covering two brachos. The first half (Hashivah Shofteinu) is the stronger: the Tur's kushya, the Rabbenu Yechiel answer from Mishlei, and Rav Schwab on “וְהָסֵר מִמֶּנוּ יָגוֹן וַאֲנָחָה” as the tefillah for the end of אֲגּודוֹת אֲגּודוֹת together form a clean three-step argument. The Birkat HaMinim half is also well-structured: Shimon HaPakuli, the crisis of indistinguishability, Shmuel HaKatan, and the two readings of “בִּנְפוֹל אוֹיִבְךָ אַל־תִּשְׂמָח” (standard pshat and Rav Kook's inversion) land well.

The document's main editorial problems are at the seams. The lead paragraph is a 180-word table-of-contents in italics that fatigues before the reader has begun. Section II contains a garbled parenthetical about “מֶלֶך” in the body versus the chasimah that reverses its own sense mid-sentence. Section V bundles two Rav Schwab teachings — yagon/anachah and the brachos on chachamim and melachim — under one roof, and the second is a long digression that pulls the bracha's center of gravity sideways. The closing (Section IX) is three sentences that only announce next week. And the footnote numbering runs 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 17, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16 in body-order — fn6 beats fn5 by a paragraph, and fn17 appears between fn11 and fn12 — which needs renumbering before print.

2.What Works

  • Sections II–III (the Tur's kushya and the Rabbenu Yechiel answer) are the document's cleanest sustained argument. The chain moves without wasted motion: Rav Saadia Gaon's observation that only this bracha has “מֶלֶך” in the chasimah, the halachic background on bracha ha-smuchah la-chavertah, the kushya, the Mishlei pasuk. The Rabbenu Yechiel biographical frame — the Chida's mesorah that the Rosh stood for his son — is proportionate and genuinely moving.
  • Section V's first teaching (yagon/anachah = agudot agudot) is the document's quotable stretch. Binding “וְהָסֵר” to the Yevamot sugya on “לֹא תִתְגֹּדְדוּ” — absence of central authority produces grief by definition — is the kind of structural reading that reorganizes a reader's kavvanah permanently. The “before Shammai and Hillel, no major machlokos” footnote-worthy observation lands.
  • The “crisis of indistinguishability” framing in Section VI is editorially excellent. The three-line paragraph — minim wore tzitzis, attended our minyanim, were called נוֹצְרִים יהוּדִים — delivers the historical stakes compactly and makes Rabban Gamliel's takkanah feel inevitable. The next subsection (“How the Bracha Worked”) closes the loop with equal economy.
  • Section VIII's two pshatim on “בִּנְפוֹל אוֹיִבְךָ” are set up as a clean dialectical pair — the warning reading and Rav Kook's inversion — and the Rav Kook move (only the ish anavah may compose the bracha against enemies) is the document's single most memorable sevara.
  • The dedication paragraph — the Tach commemoration, the two neshamos from Auschwitz, the frame of “זְמַן תְּשׁוּבָה for us, יהוּדֵי הונְגַּרְיָה” — is weighty and well-placed, and sets a tone that the Birkat HaMinim half of the shiur quietly honors.
  • The Mareh Mekomos apparatus is strong across the board. The academic-flag notes on fn1 (Mahari Beirav, with proper historical texture on 1538 and the Ralbach's opposition), fn10 (Rav Schwab's Iyyun Tefillah), fn16 (Rav Kook on Shmuel HaKatan), and fn17 (Einstein/Hawking framing) are the right register.

3.Where It's Weak or Could Be Sharper

Section V bundles two unrelated Rav Schwab teachings and loses its spine. The yagon/anachah-as-agudot-agudot reading (the first teaching) is the structural heart of the bracha's kavvanah; the brachos-on-chachamim-and-melachim aside (the second) is an interesting adjacent chiddush on sidrei kavod in our generation, but it is not the same point and does not deepen the first. The current section runs the two together as if they continued one argument; the Stephen Hawking/Einstein paragraph and the Satmar/Queen of England parenthetical drift the reader well away from “הָשִׁיבָה שׁוֹפְטֵינוּ”. Consider either splitting the second teaching into a short Section V-bis titled “Rav Schwab on the Diminished Sidrei Kavod” — preserving the link to “הָשִׁיבָה” in a transitional sentence — or compressing to a single footnote off the first teaching's close.

Section IX (Closing) does not do work. Three sentences that announce next week's continuation and conclude with a formulaic “may we soon say the original eighteen”. Given that Rav Schwab's agudot agudot frame (V) and the Birkat HaMinim arc (VI–VIII) both end at the same destination — the dissolution of fragmentation and the return to a unified Klal Yisrael — the closing could tie the two halves together: the daily Shemoneh Esrei itself is a tefillah for its own return to eighteen, mirroring the Hashivah Shofteinu yearning for restored authority. A single paragraph connecting the two would elevate the whole piece.

The lead paragraph is a 180-word table of contents in italics. It names every chiddush before the reader has read any, including the Shmuel HaKatan close (“בִּנְפוֹל אוֹיִבְךָ אַל־תִּשְׂמָח”) that lands as a quiet surprise in Section VIII. Spoiling it in the lead flattens the reveal. Consider a 40-word abstract (two brachos; one unique chasimah, one later addition) or a bulleted section-list.

Section II contains a broken sentence. The parenthetical “('מֶלֶך אוֹהֵב צְדָקָה וּמִשְׁפָּט' contains 'מֶלֶך' in the body, but in the חֲתִימָה.)” is incoherent as it stands — the second half appears to negate the first but is missing its negation. Based on the summary and the surrounding argument, the intended sentence is roughly: “The phrase 'מֶלֶך אוֹהֵב צְדָקָה וּמִשְׁפָּט' contains 'מֶלֶך' within the body of the chasimah; the Tur's point is that no other bracha contains 'מֶלֶך' in its chasimah at all.” Fix.

The “ואשיבה” → “השיבה” transformation is skipped. Section IV introduces the Yeshaya pasuk “וְאָשִׁיבָה שׁוֹפְטֶיךָ” as the bracha's textual ground — but Chazal transformed Hashem's future-tense promise (“I will return”) into our present-tense bakashah (“return”). The transcript flags this (“They changed it slightly”); the write-up elides it. A single sentence noting the shift would recover a lovely yesod: the bracha turns a prophetic havtachah into a daily tefillah that participates in its own fulfillment.

The Rabbenu Yechiel callout in Section III ends with a personal frame from the Rebbe (“his family was 'old school,' his own children saw him stand for his father, now they stand for him”). It is warm and shiur-appropriate — and genuinely moving on kibbud av. But it is tacked onto a biographical callout about Rabbenu Yechiel, and in a sefer edition it will read as a personal aside interrupting the historical frame. Consider moving to a footnote on the Chida's mesorah, or lifting into a short standalone paragraph outside the callout box.

Section VI's heading “בִּרְכַּת וְלַמַּלְשִׁינִים” is grammatically awkward — “בִּרְכַּת” in semichut followed by the vav-prefixed opening word of the bracha. Standard is “בִּרְכַּת הַמִּינִים” (as the file title has it) or simply “וְלַמַּלְשִׁינִים”. Standardize to one form across section heading, lead, and footer.

4.Factual / Citation Concerns

Footnote numbering runs out of body-order: the reader encounters notes in the sequence 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 17, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16. Fn6 (Chida, Birkei Yosef) is referenced inside the Rabbenu Yechiel callout, which sits before the Mishlei pasuk (fn5). Fn17 (Einstein/Hawking) sits in Section V well before fn12–fn16. In a shiur transcript this is unremarkable; in a printed sefer it is jarring. Renumber in body-order before print.

The Rosh's sons as listed in Section III. The body gives: “the Tur; Rabbi Yehudah who wrote Zichron Yehudah; and his oldest son Rabbenu Yechiel.” The transcript, by contrast, identifies Rabbi Hillel as the oldest son and does not clearly rank Rabbenu Yechiel. The historical record names at least four sons — Yechiel, Yaakov (the Tur), Yehudah (author of Zichron Yehudah), and Hillel — and Rabbenu Yechiel is in fact traditionally identified as the eldest (succeeding the Rosh in Toledo). The write-up's reading is probably correct; the transcript's “Rabbi Hillel was the oldest son” appears to be a shiur-moment slip. Worth confirming against a standard biographical source and footnoting the resolution.

The Mahari Beirav account of semichah recipients. The body gives: “the Beit Yosef, the Mabit, and three or four others.” Fn1 is more specific: four named recipients — the Beit Yosef, the Mabit, Rabbi Yosef Sagis, and Rabbi Moshe Cordovero. The historical consensus is that the Mahari Beirav gave semichah to four named talmidim, but the identification of the fourth is contested — some sources list Rabbi Yisrael de Curiel rather than the Ramak, and the Ramak's inclusion is doubtful on chronological grounds (he was quite young in 1538). Verify fn1's list against a standard history (e.g., Katz, Halakhah ve-Kabbalah, or Dimitrovsky's studies on the semichah controversy) and either firm up or hedge.

Fn4 attributes the Tur's kushya to Siman 118. The Tur's discussion of Hashivah Shofteinu is generally located in Orach Chaim Siman 118 (the sequence of brachos in Shemoneh Esrei) — this is correct. The parallel discussion of the chasimah and the Rama's pesak about not repeating in Aseres Yemei Teshuvah is sometimes located in 118 and sometimes in 582; fn7 places the Rama's pesak in 118. Verify — the standard citation for the Aseres Yemei Teshuvah pesak is in Siman 582 (hilchos Aseres Yemei Teshuvah), with 118 holding the year-round discussion.

Rav Schwab's “only Einstein” claim (Section V, fn17). The body states that “Einstein's foundations” are the single exception in which successive generations have confirmed rather than overturned the earlier chacham's science. This is Rav Schwab's rhetorical point and is faithfully represented, but the scientific record is more complicated than the body implies (quantum mechanics has qualified several of Einstein's intuitions). The footnote (fn17) handles this well with the hedge “arguably”; consider aligning the body with the footnote's register (“which Rav Schwab identifies as the rare exception”) rather than stating it as settled fact.

Fn13 asserts that Shmuel HaKatan “forgot the bracha himself the following year — only to recover it through extraordinary effort.” This is the Gemara's account in Berachos 28b-29a (“שִׁכְחָה וּבִקְשׁ לְהָבִיאָהּ”) — well attested. The body of the write-up itself does not raise this detail, so the footnote adds information the body does not use. Consider either introducing the detail in Section VII (it reinforces Shmuel HaKatan's unique relationship to this bracha, and the Tosafos discussion of why he was not removed from the amud is itself a famous chiddush) or trimming the footnote.

The “head of Hamas is killed” aside in Section VIII — “people may dance in Yerushalayim; Chazal permit rejoicing in the downfall of certain enemies” — is live and shiur-appropriate but will date fast in a sefer. The underlying principle (the exception for “בַּאֲבֹד רְשָׁעִים רִנָּה” and the Mishnah in Avos' own hedges) could be made in a generic form, with the contemporary illustration moved to a footnote.

Dedication paragraph — “Reb Mulch Yitzchak Akiva (Reb Tommy Herman)”. “Mulch” appears in the transcript as well, so it is not a reworking error, but it reads as a probable transliteration slip from the audio (Yiddish Melech or Mulyeh; or possibly a Hungarian diminutive). The parenthetical English name “Reb Tommy Herman” suggests the family will know; confirm the Hebrew spelling with Reb Yankel Yosef before print.

5.Small Polish Suggestions

  • “Judeo-Christians” / נוֹצְרִים יהוּדִים: the English gloss “Judeo-Christians” is anachronistic (the term in contemporary usage refers to a cultural-ethical heritage, not the first-century nozrim). Consider “Jewish Christians” or simply retaining the Hebrew “נוֹצְרִים יהוּדִים” without English gloss.
  • “The censorship of our printed texts has hidden the truth; one must look at ancient manuscripts” is a strong unsourced claim at the open of the Minim subsection. Soften to the specific: “Printed editions of the siddur from the sixteenth century onward routinely replaced the original 'לַמִּינִים' with 'לַמַּלְשִׁינִים', and manuscript evidence (including the Cairo Geniza) preserves the original.”
  • “Stephen Hawking before he died” is loose. Either cut (the point does not require the specific name) or standardize with Einstein's full treatment (name, dates, discipline). Fn17 handles this well; the body is looser than the footnote.
  • The “agony” ↔ “יָגוֹן” etymology aside is well-hedged (“possibly, possibly not”). Shiur-appropriate; for the sefer consider moving to a footnote so the Rav Schwab argument is not interrupted mid-flow.
  • “Chuck Schumer,” “Trump,” and the inauguration-bracha material have (correctly) been pruned from the body. The Satmar/Queen of England parenthetical remains and reads as a shiur-moment aside; consider moving to a footnote on fn17 or cutting.
  • Section I's heading — “I. הָשִׁיבָה שׁוֹפְטֵינוּ כְּבָּרִאשׁוֹנָה” — duplicates the article title. Consider “I. Structural Place and the Restoration of סַנְהֶדְרִין” or similar to signal content.
  • Repetition of “Why?” — the rhetorical single-word question appears five times across Sections II, III, VI, VII. Shiur-cadence; vary in print.
  • Spelling of “Hashivah” — the filename uses “Hashivah”; the body is almost all Hebrew; the meta subtitle uses tefilah. Standardize the one or two English transliterations (Hashivah Shofteinu) consistently throughout.
  • The “חֵלֶק”-style Rambam citation in Section IV — “the Rambam in his Pirush HaMishnah” — could name the mishnah (Sanhedrin perek 10, perek Chelek) in the body as fn9 does; one phrase saves the reader a trip to the apparatus.

6.Bottom Line

A tight, sefer-worthy double-bracha entry with two genuine peaks — the Rabbenu Yechiel-Mishlei answer (II–III) and the Rav Kook inversion on Shmuel HaKatan's “בִּנְפוֹל אוֹיִבְךָ” (VIII) — and a quietly strong central reading of yagon/anachah as agudot agudot (V, first teaching). The main editorial issues are structural: Section V bundles two unrelated Rav Schwab teachings and the second (brachos on chachamim/melachim) pulls focus; the lead paragraph spoils the Shmuel HaKatan close; the closing (IX) does no work and should tie the two halves together. Fix the broken parenthetical in Section II, renumber the footnotes in body-order (fn6 currently precedes fn5, fn17 sits between fn11 and fn12), verify the Siman citation for the Rama's pesak (fn7) and the fourth Mahari Beirav recipient (fn1), and recover the lost “וְאָשִׁיבָה” → “הָשִׁיבָה” observation in Section IV. Fix these and this is a solid mid-tier entry — B+.
