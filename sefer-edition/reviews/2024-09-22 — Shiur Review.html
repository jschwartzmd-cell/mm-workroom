22 September 2024 — בָּרְכוּ — Origins, Power, and Practice

Content-based review · reviewer read the finished write-up and mareh mekomos · 23 August 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

This shiur takes a single call-and-response — the two lines of Barchu that every Jew has said thousands of times — and unpacks it along four axes: textual origin (why Barchu anchors Birchos Kriyas Shema despite the Gemara framing it around Kriyas HaTorah; the sole grounding in Maseches Sofrim), spiritual force (the Zohar's five levels of the neshamah; the Tanna D'Bei Eliyahu story of R' Yochanan ben Zakkai and the meis gathering wood, teaching that Barchu carries Kaddish-like power to release a soul from Gehinnom), halachic mechanics (the Devarim source for communal davar shebikedushah; the Sefer Chasidim's brutal “gozel kedushah” charge against mumblers; the requirement to hear the initiator, not just the crowd; the R' Akiva / R' Yishmael machloikis resolved in the story of Rav Acha bar Papa at Abu Jobar; the Bach's grammatical kashya forcing the chazzan to repeat), and historical texture (the shul of Abu Jobar and its Eliyahu HaNavi tradition; the surprising Damascene origin of the Abu Chatzeira dynasty; the Maharam of Rothenburg's institution of Baruch Hu u'Baruch Shemo and the Ritva / Rema dispute on post-Maariv Barchu). The closing synthesis binds the recurring Maharam thread across three shiurim into a single arc: much of our siddur was formulated in the Ensisheim prison cell.

2.What Was Strongest

  • The Abu Jobar arc. This is the standout of the shiur. Beginning with a Gemara dispute over two words, the Rav walks the audience out of the Bavli, into a physical shul near Damascus, back into Tannaitic tradition (R' Elazar ben Aroch), further back into Sefer Melachim (Eliyahu's cave), and forward again into the Chida's Shem HaGedolim for the Abu Chatzeira etymology. Very few maggidei shiur can move that fluidly across two millennia around a single geographic point. The “pas yochmo” etymology — the talmid chacham blackened by Torah like a pot left on the fire — is the kind of image that reshapes how one reads a familiar Gemara.
  • The Tanna D'Bei Eliyahu story, well-deployed. The meis-gathering-wood narrative is not merely told; it is placed structurally, precisely where the shiur needs to lift Barchu from the halachic register into the aggadic. The comparison to Kaddish — that Barchu carries analogous power to elevate a neshamah but is culturally under-appreciated for it — is a genuine chiddush for most listeners and is likely to change behavior.
  • The Maharam synthesis in Section X. Pulling together three separate previous shiurim — Baruch Hashem L'olam, Baruch Hu u'Baruch Shemo, and the Maharam as the central conduit of Ashkenazic halachah — into a single paragraph gives the year's arc a spine. The image of the Tashbatz at the prison window, receiving the siddur we now use, is exactly the kind of consolidating moment a sefer edition needs.
  • The Sefer Chasidim / Mishnah Berurah pairing. The blunt language of “gozel kedushah” plus the M.B.'s ruling that one should correct quiet bar mitzvah boys even at the cost of embarrassment lands hard. The follow-up point — that shomea k'oneh requires hearing the initiator, so latecomers cannot “jump on the train” via the crowd's response — is a practical takeaway that many careful daveners have never thought through.
  • The Bach's grammatical kashya. A small point, but one of those moves that permanently changes how one hears the chazzan's repetition. Explaining why the chazzan says Baruch Hashem ha'mevorach a second time — because passive-tense ha'mevorach could read as past tense — is the kind of quiet halachic insight the shiur excels at.

3.What Was Weaker or Worth Pushing Back On

The Zohar's five-level structure is asserted, not developed. Section II lists Nefesh / Ruach / Neshamah / Chayah / Yechidah and states that each corresponds to a stage of tefillah, culminating in Barchu as the moment of full integration. But which stage corresponds to which part of davening? Why do the five words of the response map to the five levels? The mapping is presented as a fact and moved past. Given that this is the Zohar's central contribution to the topic, more time on the mapping — even one worked example — would carry the section.

The dual-function claim is asserted rather than argued. The shiur says Barchu serves both as the Kriyas HaTorah call-and-response and as the preface to Yotzer Or, sharing “the same logic”. But this glides past the real puzzle: if the Gemara treats them as one institution, why does it discuss only one context? And if they are two separate institutions that happen to look the same, why is Maseches Sofrim alone sufficient to establish the pre-Yotzer Or use across all of Klal Yisrael? The tension between Talmudic silence and universal practice is where the interesting halachic work lives.

The Ritva / Rema dispute is presented as a draw. The Ritva's objection is characterized as “halachically incoherent” and the Rema's defense as a claim to Barchu's independent kedushah. But the Rema's position needs more grounding: where does he get that independent kedushah from, and how does he answer the Ritva's structural claim that Barchu is by nature an invitation? The shiur's closer — “follow your mesorah” — is the practical answer, but the machloikes deserves a fuller resolution before that.

The Abu Jobar / Eliyahu HaNavi tradition is presented uncritically. The Rav is careful with Talmudic sources; he is markedly less careful with the local Syrian traditions grafted onto the site (the cave, the R' Elazar ben Aroch attribution, the stone inscription). These are worth telling but worth flagging as later local tradition rather than settled history. Framing it as “Syrian Jewish tradition holds” is done briefly — but the material then proceeds as if established.

4.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The exact scope of the shomea k'oneh requirement is not fully drawn out. The shiur rules that hearing the tzibbur's response is not enough — one must hear the initiator. But what about a case where one hears the initiator but not clearly (e.g., a soft-voiced chazzan)? Is there a shiur of audibility, or a majority-of-words standard, or is any hearing at all sufficient? The Sefer Chasidim's gozel kedushah framing implies a threshold, but the shiur does not identify it.

The Rava intervention with Rav Acha bar Papa raises a broader question of machloikis-resolution. If R' Akiva's nusach was halachically legitimate — and Rav Acha bar Papa was a serious enough talmid chacham to hold by it — on what grounds does Rava override him? Is this al tifrosh min ha'tzibbur? A ruling that R' Yishmael's view is normatively preferred? A social-order concern about disruption? The Rishonim presumably differ on which reading of Rava's move is correct, and that difference has implications for parallel cases.

The Bach's move creates a lomdish question of its own. If the chazzan's repetition of Baruch Hashem ha'mevorach is required so that he personally participates in the blessing, what is his status before he repeats? Has he been motzi the tzibbur without being yotzei himself? And how does this interact with the general principle that one who is motzi others must himself be obligated?

The Maharam's mechanism for Baruch Hu u'Baruch Shemo is elegant but has quiet halachic consequences. If the phrase originated as an acknowledgment “we are ready to be yotzei” via shomea k'oneh, then in an era when nobody is being motzi through the chazzan's Birchos Kriyas Shema, the phrase is a survival. The shiur notes this but does not draw the natural inference: is there a substantive hefsek concern in interjecting a phrase whose original function no longer applies? The poskim address this; the shiur does not.

The interaction between Barchu-post-Maariv and tefillah be'tzibbur requirements. If the minhag exists so that latecomers can be yotzei, and if being yotzei requires that they daven Maariv in the same shul, there is a technical question about what constitutes tefillah be'tzibbur when the tzibbur has already finished. The shiur mentions the practice but does not engage the substantive halachic frame.

5.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.The Zohar maps the five words of Baruch Hashem ha'mevorach l'olam va'ed to the five levels of the neshamah. Which word corresponds to which level, and is there a source in the Zohar or the Arizal that spells out the mapping? The claim is striking; a worked example would let a listener actually daven with it.
Q2.The Tanna D'Bei Eliyahu teaches that Barchu carries the same koach as Kaddish to release a neshamah. Is there a practical halachah or minhag that follows from this — e.g., should one davening for the iluy nishmas of a parent make an extra effort to be the one who answers Barchu aloud, or to lead Barchu, in the way that some prioritize saying Kaddish? Or does the Rav view the parallel as a spiritual truth without a formal halachic consequence?
Q3.The Sefer Chasidim / Mishnah Berurah ruling authorizes actively correcting a mumbler even at the cost of embarrassment. In practice, how far does the Rav think this extends — a bar mitzvah boy on the bimah? An adult guest? A weekly regular whose voice is simply soft? Is there a middah line before which the halachic license does not apply?
Q4.Given the Ritva's structural objection to Barchu-post-Maariv — that a Barchu without a following brachah is halachically incoherent — does the Rav hold that the Rema's defense fully answers it? Or is the Rema really rescuing a widespread minhag whose halachic underpinning remains contested? And in a shul whose mesorah did not include the practice, is there any ground for adopting it?
Q5.The Maharam's explanation of Baruch Hu u'Baruch Shemo was rooted in a specific historical need (universal illiteracy in tefillah, shomea k'oneh). Now that the underlying need is largely gone, are there poskim who have questioned whether the phrase should still be inserted mid-brachah — particularly given standard concerns about hefsek? And how does the Rav address the Vilna Gaon's well-known position that one should not answer Baruch Hu u'Baruch Shemo for brachos one is being yotzei with?
Q6.The Chida records that the Abu Chatzeira family descends from R' Shmuel Elbaz of the Abu Jobar courtyard — not from the Abir Yaakov. Is this widely known within the Abu Chatzeira family itself today, or has the popular attribution to R' Yaakov effectively displaced the earlier tradition? And are there other gedolim whose family name similarly encodes a specific shul or chatzer?

6.Bottom Line

One of the strongest shiurim in the series — the kind that takes a two-line liturgical fragment and returns it enriched by history, halachah, aggadah, and Kabbalah without any of the four registers pulling against the others. The Abu Jobar arc alone is worth the hour; the Maharam synthesis at the end gives the year's tefillah sequence its spine. Where the shiur underdelivers is on the Zohar's five-level mapping (asserted rather than developed) and on the Ritva / Rema machloikes (drawn but not resolved). The central lesson — that Barchu is not a procedural throat-clearing but a communal davar shebikedushah whose koach rivals Kaddish and whose mumbling is halachic robbery — is delivered with force and will change how a careful listener davens. A model of what the sefer edition should aim to preserve.
