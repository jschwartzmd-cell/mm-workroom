Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

25 August 2024 — אַשְׁרֵי — A Gateway to Olam HaBa

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · 23 August 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

Delivered on the yahrtzeit of Reb Chaim Brisker — framed by a charming opening vignette of the child Reb Chaim and the Rogatchover as chavrusas in Slutsk — the shiur unpacks the Gemara's promise in Berachos 4b that one who says Ashrei three times daily is ben olam ha'ba. The Rav moves through the double criterion (aleph-beis acrostic plus Posei'ach es yadecha), the distinction between cheilek and ben olam ha'ba, the kavanah requirement of the Mishnah Berurah, and the puzzle of why we say Ashrei twice in Shacharis and never in Maariv (the Rishonim's answer: because Maariv was originally reshus). From there the shiur turns structural: the two prepended pesukim, the missing nun answered by the samech of somech Hashem, and the closing petition to continue praising Hashem in olam ha'ba. A second movement follows the trailing Halleluyah into Rav Amram Gaon's shalsheles, the Hassidic double-Halleluyah practice, and R' Shlomo Kluger's sharp objection to it. The shiur closes with the Tur's Sephardic minhag of repeating closing pesukim, and the striking stira between our siddur and our Sefer Torah at Ki va sus Paroh — illustrated by the grave Maharil Diskin story of the senior talmid chacham who tried to “fix” every sefer Torah in Brisk and drowned in the mikveh that Erev Shabbos. A shiur whose real subject, underneath the liturgical mechanics, is the reverence mesorah demands.

2.What Was Strongest

  • The missing nun / answering samech. The Rav's framing — that David HaMelech's structural omission does not erase nefilah but reframes it, with the very next letter announcing somech Hashem l'chol ha'noflim — is the single most theologically satisfying moment of the shiur. It turns a familiar “fun fact” about the acrostic into a teaching about the shape of Jewish life: falls are real, and Hashem catches. This is exactly the register at which a shiur on tefillah most needs to land.
  • The Maharil Diskin story. Told with real narrative discipline — young Rav, senior antagonist, the escalating chutzpah of going shul-to-shul with the sofrim too intimidated to refuse, the Rav running behind him pleading, and the terrible resolution at the mikveh. What lifts it above a good story is the lomdish setup that precedes it: the reader arrives at the drowning already gripped by the Rambam/Ramban machloikis, so the punishment reads as a statement about the seriousness of a kasha-driven emendation to mesorah, not as folklore.
  • The reframing of Pesukei d'Zimra as institutionalized shohin sha'ah achas. The line connecting chassidim ha'rishonim's hour of preparation to the mesorah's decision to give Klal Yisrael a structured preparatory avodah — and then to grounding the whole enterprise in the opening pasuk of Ashrei (“first sit and settle, then praise”) — is a genuinely illuminating unification. It turns a set of kapitlach people mumble through into a coherent liturgical institution with a clear purpose.
  • The Maariv puzzle and its resolution. The obvious question — two in Shacharis, one in Minchah, none in Maariv — is asked cleanly, and the Rishonim's answer (Maariv was reshus; the third recitation was moved forward and the arrangement fossilized) is the kind of historical-liturgical explanation that satisfies once heard. That the Rav flags the alternative girsa of the Rosh and Rav Amram Gaon's softer “three-as-a-margin” reading gives the section real depth.
  • The closing note on desire itself as merit. The reading of Va'anachnu nevareich Kah me'atah v'ad olam as a petition to continue davening in olam ha'ba — and the pivot that wanting to keep davening forever is itself part of the merit — is elegant and quotable. It closes the circle on the ben olam ha'ba promise that opened the shiur.

3.What Was Weaker or Worth Pushing Back On

The ben olam ha'ba / cheilek distinction is asserted rather than explained. The Rav cites the Rishonim's move — every Jew has cheilek, but aveiros can erode it, whereas ben olam ha'ba is a stronger, erosion-resistant status — but the mechanism is left blank. Why should Ashrei with kavanah confer an erosion-resistant status when countless other mitzvos apparently do not? The most natural reading — that internalizing Posei'ach es yadecha permanently reorients the nefesh around emunah, which is the root midah that all aveiros presuppose the absence of — is worth stating. Without it, the promise sounds mechanical.

The Shlomo Kluger objection is presented but not adjudicated. The klal that “any pasuk Moshe Rabbeinu did not say we do not say” is stated as an axiom, but the shiur does not test it. We in fact do stitch parts of pesukim together constantly — every kri'as haTorah break, every piyyut, every yehi ratzon composed from fragments. The Kabbalists' sod-based response is invoked, but a nigleh-level answer would have been more satisfying: perhaps the klal applies only to reciting a full pasuk-shaped unit, not to two pesukim joined at natural liturgical seams.

The Rabbi Akiva Eiger / Satmar Rav aside on eilav tishma'un is charming but does not belong. It shares nothing with the liturgical themes of the shiur except the accident of appearing in the same biography as the Maharil Diskin story. Included as it is at the end, it dissipates the moral weight built up by the drowning story. Either it should have been developed into its own theme (the halachic status of pesukim applied by maspidim beyond their pshat) or omitted.

4.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The Mishnah Berurah's chazarah ruling is more radical than the shiur signals. Being obligated to return to Posei'ach es yadecha even after davening has concluded is a serious chumra: normally, missing kavanah in mid-Pesukei d'Zimra does not trigger re-reading. It suggests that Posei'ach es yadecha is not a pasuk of shevach at all but a discrete mitzvah kiyum embedded in Ashrei — effectively a mini-Shema for emunah b'hashgachah. The chiyuv chazarah is best understood in that framework, and the shiur could have named it.

Rav Amram Gaon vs. the plain reading is a real nafka minah. If three recitations are a margin to ensure one, then someone confident he will hit one with kavanah arguably need not stress the other two. If they are strictly required per the plain pshat, each carries independent weight. The shiur's practical pesak (“don't skip even one”) glosses over this — the question of whether an Ashrei recited without kavanah is a kiyum at all or merely a placeholder deserves engagement.

The Rambam/Ramban machloikis at Ki va sus Paroh has downstream halachic consequences the shiur does not draw out. If Ki va sus Paroh is part of the shirah, it presumably shares its din of being sung with shirah cantillation, of being read on the seventh day of Pesach as part of the shirah unit, and of being included in whatever count of pesukim the shirah generates for aliyah divisions. The two mesoros in siddur and sefer Torah are, in practice, harmonized by everyone; identifying how they are harmonized — and whether any poskim flag the tension for kri'as haTorah — would have been valuable.

The chained-Halleluyah practice raises a brachah l'vatalah-adjacent question. If R' Shlomo Kluger is right that saying Halleluyah, Halleluyah back-to-back manufactures a non-existent pasuk, does one who does so violate lo sosif? Or is it merely a chumra not to do it? The Kabbalists' sod justification presumably answers this at some level, but the nigleh weight of the objection is not tested.

5.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.What is the actual mechanism by which Ashrei with kavanah confers ben olam ha'ba as opposed to the ordinary cheilek? Is the reading that internalized emunah b'hashgachah is the root midah underlying all aveiros, so it inoculates the nefesh against erosion — or is there a more technical Kabbalistic account (perhaps in the Nefesh HaChaim or the Leshem) that the Rav has in mind?
Q2.The Mishnah Berurah's ruling that one returns to Posei'ach es yadecha even after concluding davening is unusually strong. Is Posei'ach es yadecha functionally a discrete mitzvah kiyum embedded in Ashrei — effectively a second kabbalas ol malchus shamayim at the level of hashgachah — or does the chiyuv chazarah flow from a different yesod?
Q3.On the Rambam/Ramban machloikis at Ki va sus Paroh: does the difference have any practical consequence for kri'as haTorah on the seventh day of Pesach — the aliyah divisions, the nigun of the last pasuk, whether the reader repeats it aloud? Or is the stira the Rav describes purely a matter of formatting on the page?
Q4.How seriously should we take R' Shlomo Kluger's klal that we do not say any pasuk Moshe Rabbeinu did not say? On its face, much of the siddur and piyyut corpus stitches pesukim together at seams. Is the klal limited to reciting a full pasuk-shaped unit, and if so, does the Halleluyah, Halleluyah chain even fall within its scope?
Q5.The Maharil Diskin story implies a strong principle: a kasha, however well-founded, is not grounds for altering mesorah. But sofrim and poskim do sometimes correct sifrei Torah based on textual questions — the Minchas Shai's work, for example, or occasional corrections based on the Chumash-Masorah parallels. Where is the line between legitimate scholarly correction and the kind of tampering the story condemns?
Q6.The Rav mentioned the Sephardic minhag of physically opening the hands at Posei'ach es yadecha. Is there any source in Rishonim or early Acharonim for this — a Ben Ish Chai or Kaf HaChaim reference — and would there be any chashash for an Ashkenazi adopting it as a personal kavanah-aid?

6.Bottom Line

A structurally beautiful shiur — three movements (the promise, the architecture, the boundary questions) tied together by a single unifying theme of mesorah-as-received. The high points are exceptional: the missing nun answered by somech Hashem, the framing of Pesukei d'Zimra as institutionalized preparation, and above all the Maharil Diskin story, which lands with the full moral weight the earlier lomdish setup built up. Its weakness is a recurring pattern of presenting a machloikis or objection (Shlomo Kluger, the cheilek/ben distinction, the Rambam/Ramban stira) without pushing to the underlying svara that would let a listener know how to weigh the sides. The eilav tishma'un tail is genuinely extraneous and dilutes the ending. But those are notes on a shiur that is otherwise a model of how to build a coherent piece of avodas Hashem-oriented Torah out of the fine print of the siddur.
