Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

18 May 2025 — אַתָּה חוֹנֵן, הָשִׁיבֵנוּ, and the Path of תְּשׁוּבָה

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · 7 September 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

A wide-canvas walk-through of the first six bakashos peratiyos of Shemoneh Esrei — from Atah Chonen through Ga'aleinu. The Rav opens with the three-part architecture (three brachos of shevach, twelve of bakashah, three of hoda'ah), sub-divides the middle twelve into six personal and six communal, and then focuses on why da'as must come first — grounded in the Rambam's k'hen shoteh as pasul for avodah. The centerpiece of the first half is Rabbi Efraim Zalman Margolios's chiddush (in the name of his uncle R' Alexander) that the anomalous opening shevach of Atah Chonen was calibrated for Motzai Shabbos — Havdalah not yet made, personal bakashos not yet permitted — and preserved for the rest of the week. The second half opens the question of Hashiveinu for the ba'al teshuvah who never learned, answered from the Gemara in Niddah on tinok b'me'ei imo and from Har Sinai, then pivots into Rav Noach Weinberg's celebrated discovery at Aish HaTorah that kiruv cohorts survive only through learning black gemara. From there: the Avudraham's principle on Avinu, the Rambam's twenty-four me'akvei ha-teshuvah, the Gra's klal on lashon Tanach in Shemoneh Esrei with the Cairo Geniza recovery of “מְחֵה”, the Magen Avraham's three-tier selach/mechal/kaper, and ga'aleinu as ge'ulah peratis — anchored by three unforgettable pre-Holocaust not-knowing vignettes.

2.What Was Strongest

  • The Rabbi Efraim Zalman Margolios chiddush. The move is genuinely beautiful and structurally satisfying: the halachah that no personal bakashos may be davened before Havdalah (Yerushalmi) combines with the fact that Havdalah in tefillah sits inside Atah Chonen as “Atah Chonantanu”, yielding an exact three-step scaffold — shevach, then Havdalah, then bakashah — that Chazal then preserved as the weekday nusach. Once seen, the reader cannot unsee it. That the entire week's Atah Chonen carries a Motzai Shabbos shape is the shiur's zinger.
  • The kohen shoteh anchor for da'as. Grounding the primacy of da'as in the Rambam's pesak that absence of da'as is a mum disqualifying from avodah — and then hooking it to tefillos ke-neged temidin — is exactly the kind of halachic architecture that turns a vort into a mehalach. It is also freshly connected to the recent parshios the chaburah was learning on ba'alei mumin, which shows a careful maggid shiur integrating the year's cycle.
  • The Avudraham's klal on Avinu. A quiet but powerful piece of siddur-mapping: Avinu appears only in the brachos bound to a father's chiyuvim to his son — ll'lamdo Torah for Hashiveinu, rachamei ha-av for Selach lanu. The principle both explains the presence of Avinu where it appears and its absence where it does not. The riddle of Chabad — grandfather and grandson — is a lovely shabbos-table takeaway that also does real conceptual work.
  • The Rav Noach Weinberg discovery. This is the shiur's most quotable stretch. The empirical claim — that the Aish HaTorah cohorts that stuck were the cohorts grounded in black gemara, not the cohorts fed inspirational programming — is unforgettable, and the Rav frames it as a live methodological principle for our own generation of inspiration. The candor about the mechanism (“I don't know exactly how it works”) is disarming and correct.
  • The Cairo Geniza / Gra stretch on “mechal.” A textbook example of good maggid shiur work: raise a real kushya from the Gra's klal, present the philological recovery of “מְחֵה” from the ancient siddurim, and then discipline the chiddush with an explicit warning — “don't change your nusach” — that keeps mesorah intact while enriching the kavvanah.
  • The three pre-Holocaust vignettes to close Ga'aleinu. German intellectuals dismissing Hitler as a gezeirah; the Mir bachur's Terach diary trusting Poland; the Bobover Rebbe's story of the disheveled Galician Yid laughed out of a Hungarian shtiebel. Together they land the shiur's most theologically difficult point — that the galus we most need to be redeemed from is the one we do not see — with the specificity that makes it impossible to shrug off.

3.What Was Weaker or Worth Pushing Back On

The shiur is architecturally loose in the middle. The transitions between Hashiveinu, the Rav Noach Weinberg segment, and the Avudraham's Avinu-mapping are not stitched. The reader arrives at Rav Noach by association — why Torah is the specific vehicle of teshuvah — but then Avinu comes in without a bridge, and the discussion of chochmah/binah/da'as arrives as a further association off the Chabad riddle. The Rambam's twenty-four me'akvim then re-opens Hashiveinu a second time, after the bracha has already been left behind. A tighter maggid shiur would have finished Hashiveinu once — question, both answers, Torah-as-vehicle, Avinu, Rambam's obstacles — before moving on to Selach lanu.

The bein ha-shorashim claim on “mechal” is stated more absolutely than the evidence supports. The Rav says the root mem-ches-lamed as “forgive” does not exist in Tanach. In fact the root does appear in later sifrei Tanach in adjacent senses, and Chazal's deployment of “mechilah” as a legal-financial term of waiver may itself explain the semantic drift more efficiently than the Geniza's shift from “מְחֵה”. The Geniza recovery is beautiful; it should be presented as one explanation among several rather than as the answer.

The bein ha-shitin on the tinok b'me'ei imo story is asked to carry more than it can bear. The Belzer Rebbe-and-the-philtrum ma'aseh is presented with a light hand — “hard to believe, but the core is what Chazal teach” — but the maggid shiur also insists that “later I discovered it was true” on the strength of a second-hand account from a Satmarer chassid in Miami. A cleaner move would be to detach the Gemara's yesod from the ma'aseh altogether: the Chazal stands independently; the ma'aseh is a lovely aside but does not add force. As presented, the ma'aseh's epistemic status wobbles.

The three-“shleimah” observation is a nice piece of siddur-craft but arrives without integration. Sandwiched between Ga'aleinu proper and the three Holocaust vignettes, the observation that teshuvah shleimah / refuah shleimah / ge'ulah shleimah mark the three brachos that specifically demand completeness is asserted, restated, and dropped. This is a shiur-in-itself worth developing on its own terms, or a footnote — not a middle-of-ga'aleinu pit stop.

The shiur ends softly. The three pre-Holocaust vignettes are the shiur's strongest close, but the maggid shiur's framing — “Israel is out of the picture right now; it's Qatar, it's Saudi Arabia” — is a contemporary-politics gesture that will date fast, does not carry the lomdish weight of what preceded it, and shifts the register from tefillah to op-ed. The ga'aleinu tefillah itself — “take us out of this galus” — would have landed harder without the geopolitical framing.

4.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The Yerushalmi-plus-Havdalah scaffolding of the Rabbi Efraim Zalman chiddush raises a natural question the shiur does not ask. If the entire structure of Atah Chonen is a Motzai Shabbos-calibrated scaffold, we should expect the same shape in every bracha where Chazal foresaw a Motzai Shabbos configuration. Yet no other bracha carries Havdalah-inside, and no other bracha opens with shevach. That is either the chiddush's strongest confirmation (only Atah Chonen carries the Havdalah, so only Atah Chonen got the scaffold) or its weakest link (why did Chazal not calibrate the rest of the twelve for the same Motzai Shabbos problem?). The Rav should own the question either way.

The Rambam's k'hen shoteh as mum is doing more halachic work than the shiur credits. The move — that da'as-deficit is a structural mum though not enumerated in the mishnah of ba'alei mumin — is a chiddush of the Rambam itself, not universally accepted; it deserves a note on how Raavad and Ramban handle it, especially since the entire tefillah hava amina hinges on it.

The Avudraham's klal is under-tested against the bracha of shalom. The Rav notes that “בָּרְכֵנוּ אָבִינוּ” in nusach Sefard's Sim Shalom also carries Avinu, and offers a quick account that this too concerns talmud Torah. But the Nusach Ashkenaz Sim Shalom’s dropping of Avinu is not neutral — either the Avudraham's klal is being read differently by Ashkenazim, or the klal itself only holds for the two brachos of Hashiveinu and Selach lanu. The Nusach Sefard question should have been noted as a live machlokes rather than folded in as a corroboration.

The Rambam's twenty-four obstacles reading of Hashiveinu is halachically elegant but philosophically expensive. If tefillah for teshuvah is really a tefillah for removal of obstacles — not for the teshuvah itself — then the bracha's language should read as such. “Hashiveinu” is not “haseir mimenu es ha-me'akvim.” The reading rescues the Rambam's ikkar of bechirah, but at the cost of a somewhat forced fit with the nusach ha-bracha — a tension worth naming rather than smoothing.

5.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.On the Rabbi Efraim Zalman chiddush: if the entire ma'arachah of Atah Chonen is Motzai Shabbos-calibrated, why is only this bracha so calibrated, when the halachah of ain sho'alin tzerachim b'Shabbos would seem to apply to every bakashah in the twelve? Does the chiddush require the presence of Havdalah inside the bracha, or is the shevach-before-bakashah shape independent of that?
Q2.On Rav Noach Weinberg's discovery: what is the halachic-conceptual status of the claim? Is this a chiddush in the chinuch of ba'alei teshuvah, or does it reflect a yesod about Torah itself — that Torah's segulah as “talmudah mevi'ah lidei ma'aseh” is the mechanism? And is there a source in Chazal or Rishonim for the specific empirical claim — that learning, not hisorerus, is the durable engine of teshuvah?
Q3.On the Cairo Geniza recovery of “מְחֵה”: the Rav is clear we do not change the nusach. But does the recovery affect kavvanah even b'dieved? That is, when I say “מְחַל”, should my inner kavvanah track “forgive” (the received meaning) or “erase” (the original meaning)? Is there a pesak on which kavvanah discharges the chiyuv?
Q4.On the Rambam's twenty-four me'akvim: the shiur reads “Hashiveinu” as a request to remove obstacles rather than for teshuvah itself. But the nusach ha-bracha asks for return. Is the Rav prepared to say the two are equivalent, or does the tefillah carry a separate chiyuv to daven for siyata di-Shmaya in the act of teshuvah itself, alongside the removal of obstacles?
Q5.On the Avudraham's klal: if Avinu is bound to the father's chiyuvim, and one of those chiyuvim is parnasah, why is Avinu absent from Barech aleinu? Is this the hava amina the Rav flagged in the shiur (“the point deserves consideration”) — and does the Rav have a maskana?
Q6.On the three-“shleimah” observation: is there a reason the “shleimah”-language attaches specifically to these three brachos and not, say, to chochmah shleimah in Atah Chonen or to selichah gemurah in Selach lanu? Is “shleimah” a technical marker of a bracha whose partial fulfillment is worthless, and if so, what is the halachic status of a bracha whose fulfillment is partial?

6.Bottom Line

A generous, wide-canvas shiur that carries three zingers anyone will remember — the Rabbi Efraim Zalman Margolios Motzai Shabbos-calibration of Atah Chonen, the Rav Noach Weinberg empirical finding about gemara-based kiruv, and the Cairo Geniza recovery of “מְחֵה” — and closes with three pre-Holocaust vignettes that make the abstract of ge'ulah peratis concrete. The shiur's architectural weakness is the middle: Hashiveinu is opened, closed, and re-opened; Rav Noach comes in by association rather than by transition; the twenty-four me'akvim re-enter the bracha after the reader has moved on. For the sefer, tighten the middle and let each bracha finish before the next begins. Worth returning to before davening the six bakashos peratiyos next weekday morning.
