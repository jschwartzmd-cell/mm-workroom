Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

4 May 2025 — אתָה קָדוֹשׁ, the כּוזִָרי, and אתָה חוֹנֵן

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · 7 September 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

The Rav returns to the running track of Shemoneh Esrei after last year's detour through kedushah, and takes up the third berachah of Atah Kadosh. The architecture is built off the Gemara in Megillah 17b anchoring the first three berachos in “הָבוּ לַה’ בְּנֵי אֵלִים”. From there the Rav moves through: the doubled “אתָה קָדוֹשׁ וְשִׁמְךָ קָדוֹשׁ” read through the Zohar; a lexical unpacking of “kadosh” as muvdal u’mufrash with the beautiful geographical proof from Kadesh Barnea and Kadesh Naftali; a full historical excursus on the Kuzari, from Rihal's biography to the Khazar legend to the Solomon Schechter Geniza letters to Ibn Shaprut; the Kuzari's own reading of why Atah Kadosh must follow Gevuros; the “lo dor va-dor” vs “Atah Kadosh” nusach question with the striking Rambam-Orchos Chaim-Kolbo revelation that the Ashkenaz nusach is actually the older one; Rav Avli Posweller's chiddush on ha-Kel ha-Kadosh on leil Rosh Hashanah and its rejection; and finally the opening of Atah Chonen with the Nedarim 41a “דֵעָה”-line and the heartbreaking closing about the Rav's two grandfathers.

2.What Was Strongest

  • The Kadesh Barnea / Kadesh Naftali geographic observation. This is the shiur's small jewel. Everyone knows kadosh means muvdal; the Rav pushes it into the physical landscape of Tanach and shows that the place-names Kadesh are literally the border-towns between one region and another. Once heard it cannot be unheard, and it grounds the abstract lexical claim in something you can point to on a map. The Rav's own admission that he only learned this recently gives it the right note of freshness.
  • The Rambam-Orchos Chaim-Kolbo revelation on “lo dor va-dor.” The historical inversion is genuinely surprising. The nusach we now identify as Ashkenazi turns out to be the original nusach ha-mesorah, preserved in the Sefardi Rambam's Seder Tefillah; the current Sefardi “Atah Kadosh” is the later structural takkanah. The Rav frames it correctly and gives the structural reason — three mentions of kadosh to mirror the malachim when a yachid has no Kedushah — which turns a nice historical note into a coherent halachic explanation.
  • The Kuzari excursus is proportioned right for a Sunday-morning shiur. It is long, but it earns its length. The Rav gives us Rihal's biography, the story, the Ashkenazi-descent-from-Khazar question with the right hedge, the Rav Yitzchak ha-Sangari-is-otherwise-unattested note, the Cairo Geniza corroboration with Ibn Shaprut, and the Arabic title (“Book of Disputations... for the Despised Religion”) which is the kind of detail that makes a shiur memorable. The pivot from history-of-the-sefer back to the Kuzari's own reading of Shemoneh Esrei is clean; the reader does not feel the transition.
  • The Kuzari's reading of the seder is a genuine chiddush. Gevuros presents Hashem as the rofei cholim, matir asurim, mechayeh meisim — the intimate, immanent Ribbono shel Olam. If one stopped there, He becomes our mechutan. Atah Kadosh is Chazal's corrective: yes, close, but do not collapse the distance. This is a real dialectical structure inside Shemoneh Esrei, not just a nice vort.
  • The Rav Yonah of Vizhnitz anecdote is well-placed. Dropped inside the discussion of the Yid's koach for kedushah, it prevents the section from becoming self-congratulatory. “וְנַפְשִׁי כֶּעָפָר לַכֹּל תִּהְיֶה” and the fight-over-the-shlishi are precisely the gap the berachah is meant to close.
  • The closing about the two grandfathers. One grandfather sharp until COVID took him at ninety-four in a Queens hospital three days after the Rav's last visit; the other grandfather, the Bar-Ilan professor, spent his last six years being fed by hand. The Rav lets the contrast do the work and does not moralize. That is exactly the right voice for the closing of Atah Chonen and gives “וְאַל תַּשְׁלִיכֵנוּ לְעֵת זִקְנָה” a real face.

3.What Was Weaker or Worth Pushing Back On

The Rizhiner's reading of “talmud lomar” deserves the caveat that it is a drush, not pshat. The standard Rashi on “קְדוֹשִׁים תִּהְיוּ” is unambiguous: “yachol kamoni” gets shut down by “talmud lomar ‘ki kadosh ani’ — kedushasi le-ma’alah mi-kedushaschem.” The Rizhiner's inversion is beautiful but goes against the grain of the drasha. Presented in the shiur as if it were a plausible alternative reading of Rashi; better acknowledged as a chassidishe re-hearing of the words — both to protect the Rizhiner's honor and to protect the reader from thinking this is what Rashi is saying.

The Kuzari's reading of Atah Kadosh is stated but not textually located. The Rav says “the Kuzari says” in a way that suggests a specific ma’amar in the sefer; no citation is given. For a shiur whose whole architectural spine is “the Kuzari's reading,” the exact ma’amar and siman should be pinned before the sefer version. If the reading is actually the Rav's own synthesis inspired by the general hashkafah of the Kuzari, that is worth saying — and would not diminish the chiddush.

The Rav Avli Posweller chiddush is a fun lomdishe aside but sits awkwardly in a shiur on Atah Kadosh outside Aseres Yemei Teshuvah. The Rav notes this himself (“I know we’re not Rosh Hashanah time”). In a live shiur that self-awareness works; in the sefer version, it will read as a digression unless the editor either (a) frames it explicitly as a seasonal supplement, or (b) moves it to a companion piece for the pre-Yamim Nora’im section of the sefer.

The “Ashkenazim descend from Khazars” aside needs a firmer hedge. The genetic-history question is a live scholarly and communal one, and the way the Rav puts it (“for all we know, there are people sitting in this room whose ancestors came from Khazar”) is charming in the room but will be quoted out of context. Studies of Ashkenazi genetics have decisively narrowed the Khazar-origin theory (Elhaik notwithstanding); the mainstream position is that Ashkenazi Jews descend from a Middle Eastern founder population with substantial European admixture, not from converted Khazars. Worth a one-line footnote noting the current state of the question.

The Kuzari's own reading of the paradox and the “Yid has capacity for kedushah” section pull in opposite directions. The Kuzari's point is: do not collapse the distance — Kadosh means He is far. The next section pivots to: we have the koach for kedushah ourselves. Both are true and both are in the berachah, but the shiur transitions between them without acknowledging the tension — almost as if the second point flows from the first. It does not; it partially answers it. Worth naming.

4.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The Rama's pesak on the late arrival deserves its own footnote treatment. The chiddush that the amen after Ha-Kel ha-Kadosh is more important than any other amen in chazaras ha-shatz — important enough to interrupt Kriyas Shema for, alongside Kaddish and Kedushah — is genuinely not widely known. The shiur presents it correctly; the sefer version should note the siman (OC 109 for the late-arrival pesak; OC 66 for the Kriyas Shema interruption), and note the machlokes with the Beis Yosef.

The three-kedushos-count explanation of the nusach change is halachically load-bearing. If the reason for the shift from “lo dor va-dor” to “Atah Kadosh v'Shimcha Kadosh” is to insert three mentions of kadosh when the yachid has no Kedushah, then a yachid davening be-tzibbur who does have Kedushah should in principle not need the tripled Atah Kadosh. The halacha le-ma’aseh is that he says it anyway (the nusach once fixed stays fixed); worth noting that the explanation raises a question it does not fully resolve.

The Rav Avli Posweller chiddush and its rejection turn on the internal-consistency principle. The rejection (“tarti de-sasri” with the surrounding yom tov nusach) is elegant, but note that it depends on treating the tefillah as a single unit whose parts must cohere with each other. This is a substantive halachic position — the Sha’agas Aryeh and others have views on tefillah-internal-consistency that would strengthen or weaken the rejection. The Mishnah Berurah's objection is not the only game in town, and the sefer version could gain from noting that.

The claim that the third berachah ends the shevach unit and therefore requires returning to the start is standard. But the shiur passes over what would have been a natural follow-up: if Atah Chonen begins with “Atah” precisely to bridge shevach into bakashah — as the Rishonim the Rav quotes hold — then arguably the “unit” of shevach is not as sealed as the halachic rule suggests. The halachic and thematic architectures are not perfectly aligned; that misalignment is itself interesting.

5.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.Where in the Kuzari is the reading of Atah Kadosh as the corrective to Gevuros's immanence located? Is it a single ma’amar, a synthesis of scattered passages, or a reading transmitted in the Rav's name in a later sefer that draws on the Kuzari's hashkafah?
Q2.If the switch from “lo dor va-dor” to “Atah Kadosh v’Shimcha Kadosh” was made to guarantee three mentions of kadosh for the yachid without a Kedushah, why does nusach Sefarad retain “Atah Kadosh” even in chazaras ha-shatz when the Kedushah already supplies the three?
Q3.The Rizhiner's reading of “talmud lomar” as an imperative (“teach yourself to say”) — is this a specific ma’amar in a sefer of the Rizhiner-Sadigora dynasty, or is it a transmission in the name of the Rizhiner that was later collected? For the sefer version we would want the source pinned.
Q4.On the tension between the Kuzari's “do not collapse the distance” and the acharonim's “we have the koach to be kadosh like Him” — does the Rav read these as complementary (He is far; we ourselves have capacity to become far) or as a real dialectic (He is infinitely far; nonetheless, we have some finite share of that)? The framing changes how the berachah's kavvanah should be structured.
Q5.The Rav Avli Posweller chiddush and its rejection: does the Rav hold that the rejection is airtight (the tarti de-sasri argument settles it), or is there a residual position — perhaps for a yachid davening Ma’ariv alone at home before the tzibbur-Ma’ariv of Rosh Hashanah has begun — where the chiddush retains some traction?
Q6.The closing about the two grandfathers is powerful. Is this a bakashah the Rav means for us to daven in the plural (“וְאַל תַּשְׁלִיכֵנוּ”) for our parents and grandparents, or specifically in the first person for our own zikanah? The sefer version could give a line of direction here.

6.Bottom Line

A wide-arc shiur that starts with the structural puzzle of why Atah Kadosh follows Gevuros, walks the reader through the Kuzari as historical artifact and philosophical text, resolves the puzzle through the Kuzari's dialectic of immanence-vs-transcendence, and lands on the Yid's own koach for kedushah. The lexical work on kadosh-as-muvdal with the border-town proof is the shiur's small jewel; the Rambam-Orchos Chaim-Kolbo revelation on “lo dor va-dor” as the original nusach is the shiur's most sefer-quotable finding. The Kuzari reading itself is beautifully framed but wants a citation. The Rav Avli Posweller aside is a fine lomdishe excursion but sits outside the shiur's main track. And the closing on the two grandfathers is the kind of ending that will make readers put the sefer down and go daven Atah Chonen with a full heart.
