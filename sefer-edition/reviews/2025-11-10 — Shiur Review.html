10 November 2025 — תחנון, נפילת אפים, and the Origins of והוא רחום

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, the AI summary, and the finished write-up (no Sofer.ai document exists for this date) · 18 October 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

A morning shiur in the tefillah series, continuing from the previous session’s opening on tachanun. The lecturer’s own remarks (“last Sunday,” “I won’t be here Sunday morning”) point to a Sunday session, although the recording is dated Monday, 10 November 2025. After thanks to the sponsor (Reb Azriel Dovid, surname heard as “Langer”) and a dedication to the yahrzeit of “Rav Chaim,” the shiur separates the parts of tachanun: vidui and the Thirteen Attributes (a Zohar and Arizal institution, with no source in the Gemara), then nefilas apayim itself. The Thirteen Attributes lead to Rosh Hashanah 17b and the Maharsha on the “white” tallis of light, with a long humorous story of a mother-in-law and a borrowed, stained tallis. Nefilas apayim was originally reshus, accepted as chiyuv; the Baal Shem Tov’s acronym on ואת מעכה; the old text (Tehillim 25 from the Zohar, and its danger) against the later Tehillim 6; a tentative explanation of the custom at Minchah. Then the odd verse ויאמר דוד אל גד in tachanun (Shmuel II 24), its possible Sabbatean source in the Kitzur Shelah of Yechiel Michel Epstein, R. Yaakov Emden’s gematria-based detection (814), and the omission of the verse by the Gra and others; Nasan Nata Hanover and his Shaarei Tzion. The last third turns to Vehu Rachum on Monday and Thursday: the two opposed reasons for those days, the tale of the three ships, the king’s fire test, the Yerushalmi’s dream of the verse with two כי and three לא (Yeshayahu 43), the name עזריאל in the text, and the alternative tradition of Chizkiyahu’s prayer against Sancheriv. The lecturer stops with more to say, announcing that the next Sunday’s session will be shortened and that he will be away the week after.

2.Overall Fidelity — What the Write-Up Does Well

The write-up is the AI summary almost line for line, with a footnote apparatus and a composed summary (סיכום) added. For the middle of the shiur it is faithful and well proportioned: the three-part structure, the ships → fire → dream → letter to the communities narrative, the two opposed reasons for Monday and Thursday, and the Emden episode all keep the lecturer’s order and emphasis. Hebrew terms are rendered in Hebrew letters consistently, and the lecturer’s tentative suggestions (“I do not want to alarm you; do not stop saying it”) are kept. The pattern to note is that the write-up inherits several errors from the AI summary, which departs from the recording in places (the most serious being that the summary replaces the verse under discussion with a different verse). It also loses the opening third’s colour (the tallis story, the sponsor, the named audience interventions).

3.Content Flags — What Is Wrong, Misattributed, Omitted, or Added

A. Misattributions and overclaims

Flag 1 — The verse is ויאמר דוד אל גד, not ויהי נועם. Section VIII of the write-up ends with “the פסוק ויהי נועם ה' אלקינו עלינו that is recited before תחנון — specifically because it contains the words כי פלא פעל ה' — originates from that very ספר.” The recording says otherwise: the lecturer is still on the verse he introduced (“the origin of the פסוק Vayomer Dovid el Gad that should be said before תחנון, because it has the words nipla na b’yad Hashem, comes from that book”), the Gaon’s second reason is that the text speaks of the plague (“we don’t want to say that before תחנון”), and the thread links back to the “strange פסוק” of Section VII. Neither ויהי נועם nor כי פלא פעל ה' is spoken. The summary introduced the substitution and the write-up copied it, with a footnote (fn 12) built on it. [Sefaria check: the ערוך השלחן on אורח חיים קל"א notes that some say ויאמר דוד אל גד צר לי מאוד and adds ואנו אין נוהגין לאמרו.] Corrected in the HTML.

Flag 2 — “The שלחן ערוך writes that כלל ישראל accepted נפילת אפים as a חיוב” should be the ערוך השלחן. The recording has “the Aruch HaShulchan does write that at the end of the day klal Yisroel were mekabel nefilas apayim as a chiyuv” (rendered “Sararuch hashulchan”). [Sefaria check: the ערוך השלחן on או"ח קל"א cites the טור citing רב נטרונאי that נפילת אפים is רשות, then שווינהו עלייהו כחובה. The שלחן ערוך itself (131) frames the matter as custom and does not call it an obligation.] Corrected.

Flag 3 — The responsum cited for רשות and the invented dates. The write-up has “רמב"ש in his תשובות” and a footnote that identifies him as the רשב"ש (Algiers, 1400–1467), adds רמב"ם הלכות תפילה פ"ה ה"י"ג and the רא"ש as supporting sources, and says to “verify the סימן.” The recording has “the Revash in a teshuvah,” i.e. most likely the רי"בש; no siman was given. [Sefaria check: the רמב"ם at 5:13 describes the practice but does not call it רשות; the רא"ש reference was not found; the טור does cite רב נטרונאי: נפילת אפים בציבור על פניהם אחר התפלה רשות היא.] The responsum remains unlocated.

Flag 4 — “Rav Heschel” (the parallel of ערבית). The summary names “Rav Heschel.” The recording has a name indistinctly rendered (“Reb Cheskel is mentioning something very important”), apparently an audience member who raised the parallel of ערבית. The parallel itself is sound [Sefaria: Berachos 27b, Rabban Gamliel “obligatory,” R. Yehoshua “optional”; Abaye obligatory, Rava optional], but the attribution should be left open.

Flag 5 — The gematria 814 is stated for a phrase that does not sum to 814, and the author’s name varies. The write-up has “The numerical value of משיח האמיתי ימות המשיח is exactly 814” (and fn 11 calculates only שבתי צבי = 814). Checked: שבתי צבי = 300+2+400+10+90+2+10 = 814; the four words as printed total 1,643 (משיח האמיתי 824; ימות המשיח 819); the two words ימות משיח total 814. The lecturer says the value is exactly 814, so the write-up merely repeats him; the sefer should carry that as his report and not as a checked fact. The write-up also gives the author as “ר' ישראל מיכל עפשטיין” in one passage; the recording has Yechiel Michel at the first mention and again (“Chil Mechal”). Corrected to יחיאל מיכל.

Flag 6 — Who omits the verse, and the “Italian מקובל.” The write-up says “the הגאון מווילנא and the משנה ברורה write that one should not recite it” and that “the סדור בני יששכר, a famous ספר from an Italian מקובל, also omits it.” The recording has “the Vilna Gaon and Ma’isarav” (very likely מעשה רב, the book of the Gaon’s customs, rather than the משנה ברורה) and “the Mission Chasidim — also a very famous sefer from an Italian mekubal,” which most plausibly is משנת חסידים (R. Emanuel Chai Ricchi, an Italian kabbalist). The Bnei Yissaschar is a Galician Chassidic work and not Italian, so the summary’s identification looks wrong. Both readings are reviewer inferences from an indistinct recording and are recorded as open. [Sefaria: Mishnah Berurah 131 has nothing on the verse.]

Flag 7 — What the Sephardim do with תהלים כ"ה. The recording: “The Sephardim do say it, but without nefilas apayim … they just do tachnun and then say L’Dovid. They don’t fall.” Footnote 7 says they recite the קפיטל “seated.” The write-up’s body is acceptable; the note’s word “seated” is an addition, and so is the flourish that מסירת נפש without כוונה “turns into an open door.” [Sefaria: the ערוך השלחן records the Sephardic custom, cites the זוהר, and mentions the danger of premature death.] The זוהר locus in fn 1 (“חלק ג', פרשת בלק רכ"ב ע"א”) is not in the recording and is internally inconsistent; it is retained as unlocated.

Flag 8 — The Minchah section is stated with more certainty than the lecturer had. Section VI is headed “Why No תחנון at מנחה?” and states as fact that the קהל canceled it. The recording shows the lecturer working it out aloud: he asks the room whether anyone says it at מנחה; someone says they do (the nusach is indistinct); he replies that he has davened with that community many times and does not know; and he says “it’s still very interesting — we’ll talk about it when we get to the reason.” His tentative explanation is the danger of saying it at night and the worry that a late מנחה will run past שקיעה. [Sefaria: שלחן ערוך אורח חיים קל"א takes תחנון at מנחה for granted, and has “there is no falling on the face at night.”] The Sefer Edition must report the question as an open one.

Flag 9 — The מקצת בנטיעת term. The write-up has “The ראשונים use the term מקצת בנטיעת.” The recording (“mekates b’natiyas”) is קיצץ בנטיעות, said of אחר. [Sefaria: Chagigah 14b, “Acher chopped down the shoots.”] The lecturer himself says he does not know why the expression applies.

Flag 10 — Biography and dates added by the write-up. Fn 13 gives Hanover as “of Zaslaw, Volhynia,” d. 1683, “eventually murdered by Turkish soldiers near Ungvár,” and estimates of “20,000–100,000” murdered. None of this is in the recording (“somewhere in western Ukraine”; “between twenty thousand and fifty thousand”), and a secondary reference states that his birthplace and death are disputed (one source: Uherský Brod 1663; another: Piove di Sacco). Fn 9’s “Frankfurt 1683” conflicts with the lecturer’s “early sixteen hundreds” for the author, and fn 10 adds a publication place and a second book not in the recording. Removed or hedged.

Flag 11 — Details the summary composed for חזקיהו and עזריאל. For the חזקיהו acrostic the recording is garbled (“Ches Zion … Zorim Oyrim … Kuf … Yud Hei”); the summary and write-up fill in “חשוב ציון, זכור ה', קום ה'” as if audible. For עזריאל, the recording has the letters of עוזרנו “ayin zayin reish aleph lamed” and “the yud of yishenu,” and the write-up simplifies to “עוזרנו spells ע-ז-ר-נ-ו; the יוד of ישענו completes it,” which does not produce the name. The lecturer also hedges the number of soldiers (“a hundred and eighty thousand, whatever it was”); 185,000 is correct on Sefaria (Melachim II 19:35). Hedged in the HTML.

Flag 12 — The זוהר is credited with a parallel the גמרא itself gives; and the בבא קמא locus. For “when there is דין below there is דין above” the recording has a garbled attribution (“that’s what Taisa says …”); the summary says זוהר. [Sefaria: שבת קכ"ט ע"ב itself says the courts convene on Monday and Thursday, “as does the heavenly court,” and that bloodletting on a day of judgment is dangerous.] The lecturer cites Bava Kama 82 (heard “daf pe-beis”); the passage on the two days is on ע"א [Sefaria], and the list of Ezra’s ten ordinances continues on ע"ב.

Flag 13 — The Hebrew date. The write-up gives “י"א חשון תשפ"ו” with 10 November 2025. The summary produced this. 10 November 2025 corresponds to 19 Cheshvan 5786; 11 Cheshvan fell on 2 November. Corrected in the HTML; the day-of-week discrepancy (the lecturer seems to be speaking on a Sunday) is left to the Open Questions.

B. Omissions

  • The sponsor and the opening. The lecturer thanks the sponsor of the shiur and of the breakfast (heard as Reb Azriel Dovid, “Langer”), thanks an audience member, remarks on the “extra stuff” that day, and dedicates to “Rav Chaim” whose yahrzeit was “yesterday” (the write-up says “on his yahrzeit”) and to the souls of others (indistinct). The sponsor’s name also gives the lecturer his link to R. Azriel at the end. Omitted.
  • The tallis story. After the Maharsha, a long story: a mother-in-law is upset that her daughter’s husband, visiting from Eretz Yisrael, wears a filthy tallis; the wife offers to have it cleaned; he says he forgot his in Eretz Yisrael and borrowed his father-in-law’s. Moral: never judge. The lecturer adds that he has no licence to speak of the Almighty’s tallis. Omitted from the write-up, and the most human passage of the shiur.
  • Where nefilas apayim appears in the Gemara. “We spoke about the story of the Gemara in Bava Metzia nun-tes that Rebbe Eliezer fell [on his face] … and his brother-in-law was nifter” — the previous session’s discussion, referred to in order to show that it is a reshus. [Sefaria: Bava Metzia 59b.] Omitted.
  • The Tachash/Maacha reading of the end of Vayeira. The write-up lists the lecturer’s questions (why we read it on Rosh Hashanah) and then gives only the Baal Shem Tov. The recording first gives an answer from an unnamed authority (heard as “the Belt”; perhaps the Baal HaTurim): the last two names are acronyms, מעכה for the king ruling over all the world (meloch al kol ha’olam) and תחש for the blowing of the shofar (the words are garbled). The lecturer calls it “cute” and says the Baal Shem Tov says something else. [Sefaria: Bereishis 22:24 gives the names; the Baal HaTurim could not be retrieved.] Omitted.
  • Halachic detail on Vehu Rachum. The lecturer says it is to be said slowly and standing; the Shulchan Aruch requires standing; the Mishnah Berurah allows sitting; the pashtus of the Shulchan Aruch is that standing is better. [Sefaria: SA OC 134:1; the MB locus was not retrieved.] The write-up keeps only “stay an extra five minutes.” The remark that one may not interrupt (hefsek) it is kept.
  • Hanover and his teacher; the verse in Shaarei Tzion. “Rav Chaim HaKohen from Aleppo in Syria” (“a very interesting story” how he reached Italy) is kept in a clause only. The statement that the verse ויאמר דוד אל גד is one of the additions in Shaarei Tzion, which the lecturer suspects comes from the Kitzur Shelah (“which was a little before him”), is turned into “some come instead from the Kitzur Shelah.”
  • Jokes and asides. The “first Abraham Accord in Portugal” and “maybe the Inquisition lit the fires because of these stories” jokes; the remark about searching Tanach today with a search engine (kept in the write-up); a joking exchange about which nusach someone follows; the fever; the announcement that next week the shiur will end five minutes early, that he will be in Eretz Yisrael, and that the next session will be in two Sundays. None of these belongs in a sefer.
  • The Rambam. Between the Minchah discussion and the verse, a passage (“someone asked the Rambam …”) is too garbled to use; it was properly dropped.

C. Added or editorial material not in the shiur
