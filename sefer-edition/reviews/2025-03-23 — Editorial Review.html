23 March 2025 — “הָאֵל הַגָּדוֹל הַגִּבֹּור וְהַנָּורָא,” the Two Bows, and בִּריַכַת גְּבוּרוֹת

Independent review · read fresh, no exposure to the writing process · 7 September 2026

1.Overall Read

A long, wide-ranging piece — ten numbered sections, walking the reader from the identity of the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah, through the Yoma sugya on “הַגִּבֹּור וְהַנָּורָא,” Rav Schwab on “Kel Elyon,” the two kri'os, the Chiddushei HaRim, the whole Birkas Gevuros including the Geshem/Goshem controversy with its historical excursus on Yitzchok Satnov, and finally Rav Aharon Kotler on “rav le-hoshia.” The transitions are handled well — each section closes with a clear davening-facing takeaway, and the reader always knows which berachah we are inside.

The document's structural strength is the Yoma spine: Yirmiyahu / Daniel / Anshei Knesses HaGedolah is a genuinely dramatic sugya, told with restraint and landing on a chiddush (“we are still here”) that the reader carries forward. The weakest structural stretch is the Satnov excursus (Section VI), which is fascinating but eats real-estate disproportionate to its role in the argument. Section IV (two bows) and Section IX (two dinim) are the two most argument-dense passages and deserve to be the anchor points of the sefer version. The lead paragraph is 100+ words and reads as a table of contents rather than a hook.

2.What Works

  • Section II (the Yoma sugya) is the piece's dramatic center of gravity. The four movements — Moshe coins, Yirmiyahu subtracts, Daniel subtracts further, Anshei Knesses HaGedolah restore — are paced properly, and the “we are still here” resolution earns its punch. This is the moment the reader will remember.
  • The two-kri'os section (IV) is elegantly structured: “zeh Keili / E-lokei avi” from Shemos ט״ו:ב gives the two modes a pshat anchor, Rav Schwab maps them onto the two bows, and the Chiddushei HaRim's “kol Yehudi hu Magen Avraham” lands the whole structure. The yahrzeit-note (that this was delivered on the Chiddushei HaRim's yahrzeit) is a nice touch of context.
  • The Kel Elyon section (III) lets the theological weight of Rav Schwab's “do not explain the Shoah” land without over-explanation. The restraint is the right move.
  • The Satnov narrative (Section VI) is enormously readable prose — even if it runs long, it will keep readers turning pages. The parenthetical about the 2020 Satanów remains is dark but well-placed and adds moral weight.
  • Section IX (Rav Aharon Kotler on two dinim) is the piece's most theologically satisfying stretch. The two-bullet structure of the two dinim is crisp, and the closing paragraph on “rav le-hoshia” as prayer for complete vindication is one of the strongest sentences in the document.
  • The mareh mekomos apparatus is thorough and well-formatted. Twenty numbered notes, most with expanded background paragraphs; the academic-flag treatment on fn11 (Satnov / Vayaatar Yitzchok), fn16 (Rav Aharon Kotler oral mesorah), and fn20 (Rav Fisher / Rav Shlomo Zalman) correctly signals to the reader where the citation cannot be pinned to a specific published source.

3.Where It's Weak or Could Be Sharper

The lead paragraph is a table of contents, not a lead. It runs 100+ words and lists every section that follows. A one-sentence hook — e.g., “A shiur that begins with a Gemara in Yoma and ends with Rav Aharon Kotler: what does it mean to say that klal Yisrael's survival is itself the gevurah?” — followed by a short bulleted arc of the three or four major stops would serve the reader far better.

Section VI (Satnov) is disproportionate. Six paragraphs on Satnov's biography and forgeries, three paragraphs on the actual pesak that follows. In a shiur transcript the ratio is understandable (the story is what people remember). In a printed sefer, the biographical material should compress by half and let the pesak discussion breathe.

Section IX (two dinim) is the argumentative peak and gets the fewest words. The Rav Aharon Kotler chiddush deserves more source-anchoring than a single citation of Rosh Hashanah ט״ז in the footnote. Consider adding: how the Ramban (in Sha'ar HaGemul) treats the eschatological din, or how the Rambam in hilchos teshuvah handles the same question. Even a paragraph of surrounding rishonim would elevate the section from insight to sustained argument.

The 2020 Satanów massacre parenthetical (Section VI) is powerful but structurally awkward — it sits inside a paragraph that is otherwise about a printer's biography, then vanishes. Consider either promoting it to a proper aside (its own indented note) or trimming to a short one-line sentence. As currently placed, it competes with the immediately following material about Mendelssohn's printing house, and the tonal whiplash is severe.

The closing (Section X) is thin. Two sentences after a section as heavy as the two-dinim discussion feels perfunctory. The closing paragraph could tie “rav le-hoshia” back to Section II's “we are still here” — if survival is the manifest content of the first berachah's gevurah, then “rav le-hoshia” is the prayer that the ledger of that survival, when finally opened, will vindicate us.

4.Factual / Citation Concerns

Section I, the arithmetic to 120. The derivation — 71 (Sanhedrin Gedolah) + 23 + 23 (two Sanhedrei Ketanos) + 3 nevi'im = 120 — is presented as if standard. It is a reckoning that circulates in acharonim and is worth citing, but this is not the peshat of the Gemara and the primary sources treat the number 120 with more variety than the section suggests. The footnote (fn1) says “the arithmetic rests on this mishnah” — that is true only in an interpretive sense; a specific acharon should be named as the source of this derivation.

Section VI, Yitzchok Satnov's dates and biography. The body gives “died in 1805 or 1806”; the footnote gives “c. 1732–1804/5”. These do not match. The standard bibliographic dates for Isaac Satanow are 1732–1804. Pin this down before print. Also: Satanów is not simply “near Lviv” — it is closer to Kamianets-Podilskyi in Khmelnytskyi Oblast, roughly 200 km east of Lviv. If the geographic locator matters, get it right.

Section VI, forged haskamos of the Pnei Yehoshua. The claim that the Pnei Yehoshua “initially cited him; only later did he discover the deception” should be double-checked. The Pnei Yehoshua (Rav Yaakov Yehoshua Falk) died in 1756, before Satanow's printing career took off. The forgery-of-haskamos tradition around Satanow is real but the dramatis personae need verification — a Pnei Yehoshua in his last decade citing Satanow and then “discovering” the fraud is chronologically tight and may be a shiur-room compression.

Section VI, Satnov as “the first to print the כִּתְבֵי הָאַריִ״ז״ל.” The summary refers to “כִּתְבֵי רַבּי חַיִּים וויטַ״ל” specifically; the write-up says “כִּתְבֵי הָאַריִ״ז״ל.” These are related but not identical (Rav Chaim Vital transmitted the AriZa"l's teachings, and much of what circulates as Kisvei HaAri is in fact Kisvei Rav Chaim Vital). The claim of being “the first to print” needs precision — there are earlier printed kabbalah works (e.g., Zohar Mantua/Cremona 1558; Pardes Rimonim 1592), so the claim is specifically about the AriZa"l/Rav Chaim Vital corpus. Sharpen.

Section VI, Rav Krause's kuntres. fn11 says the kuntres was published “roughly twenty-five years before the shiur” (~2000) and gives no author details beyond “Rav Krause.” For a source that is doing meaningful halachic work in the section (it is what caused Rav Shlomo Zalman to change his nusach), a proper bibliographic entry — author's full name, title, year, publisher — should be pinned down before print.

Section IX, the two-dinim attribution. fn16 correctly flags this as oral mesorah and notes the precise published source was not given. Since the chiddush is doing heavy lifting in the section, a serious effort to locate it in Mishnas Rabbi Aharon — volume, siman, page — is warranted. If it truly cannot be found in print, the body text should say “transmitted in his name by his talmidim” rather than presenting it as a Rav Aharon chiddush full stop.

Section III, The Jewish Observer. fn17 gives the correct dates (1963–2008) and identifies the publication properly. The description in the body (“the Agudah's English-language publication carrying genuine hashkafas gedolei Yisrael”) is warm but editorial; consider trimming the editorial gloss for sefer register or moving it to the footnote.

5.Small Polish Suggestions

  • Section I, the Satmar Rav anecdote. Charming, but it sits inside a callout about the Knesset, breaks the flow of the arithmetic discussion, and then the reader has to re-engage the 71+23+23+3 count. Move the anecdote to a footnote or a proper sidebar, or cut. As currently placed, the callout tries to do three things (Knesset origins, Warhaftig, Satmar) and does none of them cleanly.
  • The section-numbering scheme is Roman (I–X) but the berachah-numbering is spelled out. Standardize; either the sefer uses Roman throughout or Arabic throughout. Ten sections is a lot; consider consolidating VII (geshem inside techiyas ha-meisim) into VIII (techiyas ha-meisim as daily reality), and IX (Rav Aharon) into a proper closing arc.
  • Header typography. The main title uses two golden Hebrew phrases inline with English connectors (“הָאֵל הַגָּדוֹל..., the Two כְּריעוֹת, and בִּריַכַת גְּבוּרוֹת”). Attractive in browser but likely to render awkwardly on print — consider an English-only title with a Hebrew subtitle beneath.
  • Section II, blockquote for “הַגִּבֹּור וְהַנָּורָא...” shows only the Hebrew, no English gloss. For the sefer's stated audience of English readers, add a translation line under every Hebrew blockquote, as the sefer template does elsewhere.
  • The Rebbe/Rav/Rabbi Ephrati naming. Section III calls him “the Rebbe.” The rest of the piece and the header call him “Rabbi Ephrati.” Standardize across the sefer — and across every entry in the series.
  • Yiddishisms and colloquialisms. “chutzpadik,” “קרֶַדִיט” (for “credit”) in Section VI, and “frummer Polish yid” read as shiur-room register; in a printed sefer either italicize with a gloss or replace with register-neutral English.
  • “Since since שַׁבְּתַי צְבִי” in Section VI is a doubled word — typo, delete one “since.”
  • Sponsorship block. The blue-tinted lead paragraph carrying the sponsorship (“Rabbi Yossi Selig לעילוי נשמת משה זעליג בן [his father]”) has a placeholder “[his father]”. Either fill in the father's name before print or drop the placeholder entirely and give the niftar's name without it. The bracket is unprofessional in a printed edition.

6.Bottom Line

A wide-ranging, well-transitioned piece whose strongest sections are the Yoma spine (II), the two kri'os with the Chiddushei HaRim capstone (IV), and the Rav Aharon Kotler two-dinim chiddush (IX); the Satnov narrative (VI) needs the biographical dates and geography verified (1804 vs. 1805/6, Satanów's actual location, the Pnei Yehoshua chronology) and should shed some of its length; the lead paragraph should become a hook, not a table of contents; the Rav Aharon chiddush needs a genuine effort to pin the published source and deserves more space; the “[his father]” placeholder in the sponsorship line must be fixed before print — land those and this is a strong entry.
