9 March 2025 — פורים Falling on ערב שבת קודש

Independent review · read fresh, no exposure to the writing process · 30 August 2026

1.Overall Read

This is a practical, sure-footed piece of halachic writing that knows what it is: a briefing document for the unwalled cities on a rare calendar collision, laying out two valid derachim and then giving the mechanics of the more dramatic one in enough detail that a reader could actually execute it on Friday afternoon. The structure moves cleanly — underlying issur, the timing of seudas Purim, the collision, Derech 1 (Rema), Derech 2 (Maharil/Meiri), mechanics, bentshen, live testimonies, kabbalistic caveat, practical notes, simpler alternative, closing — and the numbered eight-step mechanics section (VIII) is the operational heart of the document.

The piece is strongest in its historical scaffolding of the Maharil/Meiri mesorah (Sections VI–VII) and in the two closing mesorah anecdotes (Section X), which together do the persuasive work of moving poreis mapah u'mekadeish from curiosity into lechatchila. Weaknesses are structural (redundancy between Sections V and XIII, mis-placed "Practical Notes" in Section XII) and one significant factual error in the Maharil's dating that needs correction. There is also a real transliteration question — "Rav Yitzchak Shor" vs. "Rav Yitzchak Isaac Sher" — that should be resolved before this goes into the sefer.

2.What Works

  • The two דרכים framing. Laying out the simpler Rema route and the older Meiri/Maharil route as parallel valid paths — rather than presenting poreis mapah as an exotic exception — is the right editorial move for a document that wants to leave the reader with an actual decision to make. The lead paragraph names both up front and the body earns the framing.
  • Section VIII — the numbered mechanics. Eight steps, times built in, alternatives flagged in parentheses. This is exactly the register the document promises. The specific note that bores pri hagafen and hamotzi are skipped — and that this is unrelated to lechem mishneh (which is a separate chiyuv) — is the kind of clarification a reader actually needs at the table, and would easily get muddled without.
  • The two מעשה רב testimonies in Section X. The Rav Zalman Nechemiah Goldberg letter (via his rebbe, via Rav Yitzchak [Isaac Sher/Shor]) and the Imrei Emes' tisch in Gur are placed with real editorial force. The tripled emphasis "lechatchila mamash mamash lechatchila" is repeated deliberately as the lead's bracket and as the Section X payoff, and it earns the repetition.
  • The dual-layer מראה מקומות. Short pin first, expanded biographical/contextual note second, with the academic-flag box (fn10, fn11, fn15) used correctly for sources that are oral mesorah or diffuse (Kabbalistic literature, the Gur oral tradition, the R' Z.N. Goldberg letter). This system is working well across the series and this piece uses it competently.
  • The kabbalistic caveat (Section XI). Brief, honest, and correctly non-dispositive — flagging the reordering of the standard ascent (Minchah → Kabbalas Shabbos → Maariv → Berachah Me'ein Sheva → Kiddush) without letting it override the halachic heter. Exactly the right length for a note of this kind.
  • The רבינו שמחה מויטרי "ליל שיכורים" citation (Section III). A small ornament that quietly establishes historical depth — Purim's kedushah stretches before and after the day proper — and grounds the mainstream late-afternoon minhag as something the Rishonim already knew.

3.Where It's Weak or Could Be Sharper

Section XIII (“The Simpler Alternative”) is a redundant restatement of Section V. Section V already lays out the Rema's route: eat before chatzos, mention of basar v'yayin, the Shelah's general preference. Section XIII restates the same route as a five-point recap. In a shiur this repetition serves a rhetorical bracketing function; in a written document it reads as an editor forgetting what he already said. Either cut Section XIII entirely, or merge its five-point summary into the closing of Section V so both derachim receive a matched practical checklist before the mechanics of Derech 2 begin.

Section XII (“Practical Notes”) is orphaned. The three subsections — "Before bentshen," "Latecomers," "Women," "Mishloach Manos" — are practical mechanics that belong inside Section VIII with the numbered steps. As currently placed, after the kabbalistic aside, they feel like items the author forgot and added at the end. Fold them into Section VIII as sub-notes at the relevant step: women go under step 3 (shkiah), latecomers under step 5 (Kiddush), Mishloach Manos under a new sub-note before step 1, phone-and-muktzeh as a boxed callout before step 3.

Section IX's three approaches to רצה and על הניסים collapse #2 into #3 without noticing. Approach 2 is labeled "Avoid the contradiction" and attributed to the Mishnah Berurah, but the text under it is a single sentence saying only that the MB is uncomfortable with tarti d'sasrei. Approach 3 is then "The compromise" — but the compromise (say Retzei, drop Al HaNissim, add the HaRachaman) is the MB's actual position, not a separate view. As written, the reader is left wondering who holds approach 3. Rewrite as two approaches: (1) Meiri — say both; (2) Mishnah Berurah — drop Al HaNissim from its standard place, insert it in the HaRachaman. That is the actual machlokes.

The two lead paragraphs bury the lead under a local announcement. The first lead is excellent — the two derachim, poreis mapah u'mekadeish, the Imrei Emes, Rav Yitzchak. The second lead is a Dor L'Dor announcement thanking named individuals ("Reb Yaakov, Reb Penny, Reb Archie ... Reb Chaski Rubin ... Reb Andy Goldstein"). In the live shiur this is a warm opening. In a preserved sefer piece it is a dated local notice that will mean nothing to the reader ten years from now and is a strange thing to encounter on the second breath of a halachic document. Either move it to a small dated sidebar with an hakaras hatov note, or omit it entirely for the sefer edition — matching the footer's own statement that "personal asides have been omitted."

The Rebbe's צ'ולנט callout in Section II is charming but personal. Same question as the second lead: the Flatbush-to-Lawrence drive, "one bowl," the pedagogical ta'amah — delightful in a shiur, but this is exactly the sort of personal aside the footer disclaims. If it stays, the same standard should apply to the Dor L'Dor lead. Pick a rule and enforce it.

Local times are baked into the body text. "Lawrence this Friday, chatzos is at 12:04 p.m.," "shkiah is at 6:42," "Minchah Gedolah is at 1:34 p.m." are pinned to a specific date and city. For a sefer edition — particularly one meant to serve the next occurrence twenty years out — these need to be either abstracted ("approximately chatzos," "roughly an hour and a half after chatzos") or moved into a boxed sidebar clearly marked as the 5785/Lawrence example. The rest of the document is timeless; the timestamps are not.

Section II ends with a dangling forward reference. "From the first pesak two reasons emerge" — the reader has just been told a Gemara in Gittin and a halachah in siman 249; "the first pesak" is ambiguous. Either name it ("From the Shulchan Aruch in 249 two reasons emerge") or restructure so the two reasons introduce themselves.

4.Factual / Citation Concerns

The מהרי"ל is dated to the wrong century in the body text. Section VI describes the Maharil's era as "late twelfth into early thirteenth century in Germany." This is off by two hundred years. R' Yaakov Moellin lived c. 1365–1427 — late fourteenth into early fifteenth century. The footnote (fn6) gets it right ("c. 1365–1427"), but the body claim will read as authoritative to a lay reader and is simply wrong. This is a stop-the-presses correction. It also creates internal tension with the Meiri paragraph in Section VII, which correctly places him "a hundred years before the Maharil" — a claim that only works with the true dating (Meiri 1249–1315).

“Rav Yitzchak Shor” is very likely Rav Yitzchak Isaac Sher. The person described — chosson and talmid of the Alter of Slabodka, rosh yeshiva, niftar תשי"ב (1952) — is Rav Yitzchak Isaac Sher (שר), the Slabodka rosh yeshiva who moved the yeshiva to B'nei B'rak and died in 1952. The Hebrew spelling is שר, not שור. If this is indeed the same figure — and the biographical details all fit — the transliteration should be corrected to "Sher" in both the body text and fn10. If the Rebbe genuinely said "Shor" and meant a different figure, that is worth confirming with him; otherwise this is a transliteration slip that will look sloppy in print.

Two משנה ברורה citations are unpinned. Fn5 is given as "משנה ברורה סימן תרצ"ה" without a se'if katan. Fn12 hedges with "in the relevant simanim in Hilchos Shabbos." Fn14 does the same ("in the relevant simanim on Birkas HaMazon, Purim, and poreis mapah"). For a mareh mekomos apparatus these need actual se'if katan numbers — MB 695:9–10 for the chatzos ruling, MB 271 for poreis mapah, MB 263 or 271 for the walking-to-shul question. Either pin them or explicitly flag them as "generally, in the discussion of…" using the academic-note box, so the reader knows the citation is directional rather than exact.

“יד אפרים on the תניא.” A modern reader hearing "Tanya" will think of the Chabad Tanya of the Baal HaTanya. Here the reference is to Tanya Rabbasi, the early Ashkenaz halachic compendium by R' Yechiel b. Yekusiel of Rome (13th c.), on which R' Ephraim Zalman Margaliot wrote his Yad Ephraim glosses. Worth a one-line clarification in fn5 so no one is misled.

“Buried near the חוות יאיר.” The Maharil (d. 1427) is described as buried in Worms "near the Chavas Yair." The Chavas Yair (R' Yair Chaim Bacharach) lived 1639–1702 — two and a half centuries later. The Maharil is not buried "near" him in any sequential sense; the Chavas Yair was later buried in the same Worms cemetery. Phrasing should be reversed: "in the Worms cemetery, where the Chavas Yair would later be buried as well." Small point, but the current phrasing has the temporal arrow backwards.

“The רמ"א cites the מהרי"ל more than any other single source.” This is a strong claim, plausible and often repeated, but worth attributing ("as is commonly noted") rather than asserting flatly — unless the writer has counted or has a source that has counted.

5.Small Polish Suggestions

  • The tripled "lechatchila mamash mamash lechatchila" in the lead and again in Section X is a deliberate bracket and works. Do not weaken it.
  • Section I opens with "This year, תשפ"ה…" — for a sefer edition, "In a year like תשפ"ה (2025)…" reads as retrospective rather than immediate and travels better through time.
  • Section VIII step 3 gives two shkiah times ("6:42 p.m. (Some hold 7:00 for bein hashmashos; shkiah proper is 6:42)"). The parenthetical is muddled — the 7:00 figure isn't a different shkiah, it's an approximate bein hashmashos or tzeis reference. Rewrite as: "shkiah is at 6:42; some are machmir to complete matters by 7:00 for bein hashmashos."
  • "Reb Penny" — a family name given as if a first name; will read strangely to a reader outside the community. If the second lead stays, at minimum this should be "Reb [Firstname] Penny."
  • The footer says "Hebrew terms preserved in the Hebrew alphabet; personal asides have been omitted." As noted above, several personal asides survive (Dor L'Dor thanks, the Rebbe's tzchulent story, the Worms trip). Either the footer needs to be adjusted or the asides need to go.
  • Section IV's one-sentence framing ("Combine the two halachos… What does one do?") is punchy and good. Keep.
  • "בנטשן" is used throughout for benchen/bentshen. This is authentic yeshivish orthography and consistent within the document; no change needed — but confirm it matches the house style being used across the sefer.
  • Section XIV ("Final Word") is one sentence plus a freilichen Purim. If Section XIII is cut per the recommendation above, Section XIV can absorb its function as a short summary sign-off. If XIII stays, XIV as currently written adds nothing.

6.Bottom Line

A capable, executable halachic briefing whose operational spine (Section VIII) and closing mesorah anecdotes (Section X) do exactly what they need to do; the Maharil's date is off by two centuries in the body text and must be fixed, the "Rav Yitzchak Shor" transliteration is almost certainly Rav Yitzchak Isaac Sher and should be corrected, Section XIII duplicates Section V and should be cut or merged, Section XII belongs inside Section VIII, and Section IX's second and third "approaches" collapse into one and should be re-labeled — fix those five and the local time-stamps, and this is a strong sefer-quality piece.
