27 October 2025 — The Depth of כוונה in שמונה עשרה

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, the AI summary, and the finished write-up (no Sofer.ai document exists for this date) · 18 October 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

A shiur on the close of the Amidah, picking up the previous week’s treatment of Yihyu Leratzon and Elokai Netzor. It opens with dedications and a short review: R. Moshe ben Machir’s Seder HaYom on the ten words and ten yuds of Yihyu Leratzon; the history of Elokai Netzor (absent among the Rishonim, a hint in Rav Amram Gaon, the Avudraham, the early printers); the personal prayers of the amoraim on Berachos 16b–17a and the lecturer’s plea that each person use his own words and language; a story of a late rav who skipped Elokai Netzor rather than keep the congregation waiting. He then asks why the printers chose Mar son of Ravina’s prayer, offering two readings (lashon hara; lips that spoke without the heart), and turns to v’nafshi ke-afar: the plain reading, the Vilna Gaon (the reward of dust is children like dust), the Maharsha (the dust finally treads on us), the story of R. Chaim of Volozhin and the land, the Novardok and Slabodka approaches to humility, R. Bunim of Peshischa’s two pockets, and the Kli Yakar on dust and stars. A personal kavanah (the ge’ulah and the Yiddish “until the inn, a drink”) leads to the four lema’an phrases, the Beis Yosef’s report of their reward, and the Chasam Sofer’s reading of keshti nasati ba-anan as the acronym קשת״י piercing the cloud of Eichah. The second half is the three steps back: the Gemara in Yoma 53 (the High Priest, the ma’amados, R. Elazar and R. Yochanan, Rava and Rav Yosef), the lecturer’s own Brisk anecdote, Nebuchadnezzar’s steps, the Beis Yosef citing R. Yitzchak Abuhav (a short biography included) for the three mil from Sinai, the lecturer’s difficulty from Shabbos 88b (twelve mil), and a question from the room about the first blessing. The shiur ends abruptly, to be continued with Tachanun.

2.Overall Fidelity — What the Write-Up Does Well

The write-up follows the AI summary closely and, for the sections it keeps, is faithful in order and in tone: the ten-yud remark, the two readings of Mar’s prayer, the two pockets, the four phrases and the Chasam Sofer’s acronym, the retreat of Rava with the blood on the threshold, the Abuhav digression and the lecturer’s unresolved difficulty about three versus twelve mil are all preserved, and the lecturer’s candour (“I do not understand for the life of me”) is kept. Hebrew terms are in Hebrew letters throughout, and the footnote apparatus is numbered 1–20 in body order with matching back-links.

The problems are of three kinds. First, a cluster of errors inherited from the AI summary (the truncated verse, Rabbah for Rava, a Heavenly “quotation” in the Nebuchadnezzar note, “midrash” for haggadah, the “lifted” expulsion decree). Second, reworded or embellished notes (the Peshischa/Kotzk gloss, Ein Zeitim, “led a delegation”, the invented Hebrew quotation in the Shabbos note). Third, omissions: the “use your own language” passage, the Solomon-and-Givon question, the first-blessing exchange, and several biographical details.

3.Content Flags — What Is Wrong, Misattributed, Omitted, or Added

A. Misattributions and overclaims

Flag 1 — The Hebrew date and weekday are wrong. The header and footer give כ״ו תשרי תשפ״ו and a “Sunday” shiur for 27 October 2025. That date was a Monday and corresponds to ה׳ חשון תשפ״ו (checked against a calendar converter); כ״ו תשרי was the Shabbos before (Parashas Bereishis). The recording is dated 27 October; the speaker refers to “this past week’s parashah, Noach,” which fits 27 October. The weekday the lecturer actually gave was not stated; the Sefer Edition should say “Morning shiur” and use ה׳ חשון.

Flag 2 — The verse is quoted short but counted as ten words. The body quotes יהיו לרצון אמרי פי והגיון לבי לפניך (seven words) and says it contains ten words, begins and ends with a יו״ד, and has ten. The count is true only of the whole verse, …לפניך ה׳ צורי וגואלי (Tehillim 19:15): ten words (counting the hyphenated pairs separately), first and last letters י, and ten י’s if the Divine Name is counted as spelled out (nine as ה׳). The recording says only “it has ten words… ten times the letter yud in that pasuk”. [Checked.]

Flag 3 — The Yigdal claim and the biography of R. Moshe ben Machir. The body says he “is, incidentally, the author of the famous Yigdal Elokim Chai, which was composed only in the 1500s.” The recording at this point is garbled (“the author of the ‘every it says where does that come from?’… It comes from the safer that was written in the fifteen hundreds… no other source”); it is the AI summary that supplies Yigdal. Yigdal is conventionally attributed to an earlier author, so the identification should not be stated. Note 1 also adds that he was a rav in עין זיתים, a first printing in Venice 1599, and a link to the ten ספירות; none is in the shiur.

Flag 4 — The Avudraham and the closing blessing. The write-up has the Avudraham say the prayer ended with ברוך אתה ה׳ עושה השלום and that Elokai Netzor was “reserved for יחידים”. The recording gives “Baruch Ata Hashem… Meisrael Hashalom”, apparently המברך את עמו ישראל בשלום, and says only that the Rishonim finished there and took three steps back. The “reserved for individuals” gloss belongs to the earlier remark about Rav Amram.

Flag 5 — “His first yahrtzeit is approaching” has no referent. The callout begins with a pronoun from the live shiur. In the recording the lecturer is speaking of a late rav of the community (“Wow, what a special rav we had”; the summary renders the name “Rabbi Kinsler”), whose first yahrtzeit was coming and who used to skip Elokai Netzor when the tzibur was waiting. The name is an ASR rendering and unconfirmed.

Flag 6 — “Fifteen amoraim”, and the wording of Mar’s prayer. The lecturer said “let’s say fifteen”; Berachos 16b–17a records about eleven (R. Elazar, R. Yochanan, R. Zeira, R. Chiya, Rav, Rebbi, Rav Safra; R. Alexandri, Rava, Mar son of Ravina, Rav Sheshes). The section heading “שפתי מדבר שקר” misquotes the prayer, which reads ושפתי מדבר מרמה (“deceit”); the lecturer’s paraphrase (“falsehood”) is faithful to the sense. The phrase ונפשי כעפר לכל תהיה comes partway through the prayer in the Gemara, and the prayer ends ה׳ צורי וגואלי; “we end the prayer with” refers to our siddur. [Checked.]

Flag 7 — The Vilna Gaon “brings it from the Zohar”. The write-up states it as fact; the lecturer said “I think he brings it from the…” and the transcript breaks off. The note adds that the teaching “draws on the Zohar’s teaching”. Keep as the lecturer’s uncertain report.

Flag 8 — “Reb Yitzchak, Reb Moshe” in the story of R. Chaim of Volozhin. The recording has R. Chaim address one party (the transcript “Rabbi Yisai”, the summary “Reb Yitzchak”); no second name is given. “Reb Moshe” is an addition. The story is told without a source.

Flag 9 — The Maharsha, Kli Yakar and Peshischa notes outrun the shiur or the texts. (a) The Maharsha is cited at Berachos 17a; the comment there on ונפשי כעפר refers to Tosafos and reads the phrase as continuing the preceding petition (his opponents should regard him as dust and not curse him). The reading the lecturer reports (the dust finally treads on us) was not found there. (b) The Kli Yakar on Bereishis 28:14 does speak of dust and of Avraham’s humility, but the pairing of dust and stars as a single balance was not found there; the lecturer himself said “I think in Vayetze”. (c) Note 7 calls R. Bunim “founder of Peshischa and rebbe of Kotzk”; he led the Peshischa school and taught the Kotzker, who was his disciple.

Flag 10 — The four phrases: “midrash”, R. Akiva, and the verses. The Beis Yosef (OC 122) cites a הגדה, not a מדרש, for the reward of קבלת פני שכינה, in connection with the additions after the prayer; R. Akiva and R. Eliezer are not in that paragraph (the lecturer says “it is brought down in the Rishonim”). The verse list in note 9 is not from the shiur and was not verified (only עשה למען שמך, Yirmeyahu 14:7, was). Also, the phrases are given in the order שמך, ימינך, תורתך, קדושתך, which is the order the lecturer gave; the Chasam Sofer’s acronym reorders them.

Flag 11 — The logic of “the gates of tears” is garbled. The body asks “how do we know the gates of tears are still open?” and answers with Eichah 3:44, which proves the opposite (the cloud blocks prayer). The Gemara (Berachos 32b) proves that the gates of prayer are locked from Eichah 3:8, and that the gates of tears are open from Tehillim 39:13; the Hebrew reads שערי דמעה (singular). Note 13 compounds this by calling 3:44 “the רש״י-cited proof-text”.

Flag 12 — Yoma 53: the student is Rava, not Rabbah; the blessing is misquoted; the three steps are in Yoma. The write-up has “רבה” throughout the story and in the Abaye dispute (note 16 itself says the standard mnemonic is אביי ורבא). The Gemara reads רבא. Rav Yosef’s blessing is יהא רעוא דתרום רישך אכולה כרכא (“may you lift your head over the whole city”, Yoma 53b), not “head of all Israel, head of מחוזא”; the lecturer’s identification of the city is his gloss. The High Priest’s exit is described in the Mishnah as retreating the way he entered, derived from Solomon; it does not give a number. The כהנים, לויים, ישראלים left sideways. Finally, the closing sentence of the section, “Yoma does not mention the number three”, is wrong: Yoma 53b states that one who finishes praying takes three steps back and says שלום, and that Abaye and Rava took the three steps in one bow. The lecturer’s remark is faithfully reported; the text contradicts it. [Checked.]

Flag 13 — Nebuchadnezzar: four steps, running after the messenger. Sanhedrin 96a has him run four steps after the letter until Gabriel stopped him; the reward is for those steps. The body has him “taking three steps backward in honor of the Shechinah”, which is the lecturer’s version (he said “the Gemara says four” and suggested the Rishonim had a reading of three). Note 17 prints a Divine “quotation” (“you ran three steps after My honor… three kings”) that is not in the passage. The link to the destruction of the Temple is the lecturer’s, and the link of the story to the three steps is attributed in the shiur to a Rabbi Kalish, whom the write-up omits. [Checked.]

Flag 14 — Abuhav and the three mil. (a) “The expulsion order was lifted in 1493” is the lecturer’s slip (the transcript has “It was lifted in fourteen ninety three”); it is not historically supportable and should not be repeated. (b) Note 18’s “led a delegation to Portugal” is not in the shiur, which says only that he went to Portugal, met the king, and tried to persuade him. (c) The Beis Yosef (OC 123) calls him רבינו הגדול מהרי״א and gives the three-mil reason in his name, citing a work abbreviated א״ח; the passage as retrieved does not spell out “Abuhav”. (d) Note 19 prints a Hebrew “quotation” of Shabbos 88b (חזרה נשמתן בטל) that is not in the text; the Gemara says twelve mil at each utterance, souls leaving, and a reviving dew. (e) The body twice writes שבת ח״ח for פ״ח, and the heading says “Hr Sinai”. (f) The summary’s “Mahariṭ Abohav” and the work “Menorat HaMaor” do not occur in the recording, which says Maharia and names no work; the write-up’s מהרי״א is the right reading.

Flag 15 — “The halachah is in the Shulchan Aruch and is based on the Magen Avraham.” The recording has the lecturer say the practice is “not a Gemara, it’s a Magen Avraham”. A commentary cannot be the basis of the code it comments on. Also, “one does not turn one’s back on the Shulchan Aruch” is a transcription of something else (most likely the Aron Kodesh). The Shulchan Aruch’s own text on the three steps (OC 123:1) says nothing about looking to the sides, which the write-up attributes to it.

B. Omissions

  • “Use your own words.” A central passage is missing: that the amoraim’ personal prayers show that each person may add his own request, in his own language (Hebrew, Yiddish, English), with the example of a person about to close a deal at ten o’clock; that Chazal fixed the Shemoneh Esrei for everyone and then added, “but you have your own mouth”; that the Mishnah Berurah and the acharonim say this is the time; and the practical reason for saying Yihyu Leratzon first (to be free to respond to Kedushah if the personal prayer is long).
  • The first blessing. A guest’s question leads to the point that the first blessing is the main one and that, if one had no kavanah there, the lecturer says “don’t go back”, because one would not have kavanah the second time and a repeat can be a hefsek; the Shulchan Aruch (OC 101:1) rules that one repeats, with a gloss that nowadays one does not.
  • Solomon and Givon. The Gemara in Yoma 53 derives the retreat from Solomon’s departure from Givon; a questioner asks why the verse does not say “ויפן”, and the lecturer is unsure (“that’s a good question”). Omitted; it should be recorded as an open question.
  • The Brisk story’s end. The lecturer did not know whether the three steps helped, “but I did get a call the next day that I was accepted.”
  • The Maharsha’s coda (“may we merit length of days and years”).
  • Biographical colour on Abuhav. That he seems to have been poor, in contrast to the wealthy Abarbanel (whom the lecturer describes as a treasury minister); that the Torah scroll of the Safed synagogue is not used today and is kept in a safe; that King João II was a figure famous for his conversations with Jews and rabbis; that the Beis Yosef calls him רבינו הגדול and takes many rulings from him; that the lecturer would “pay money” to anyone who could resolve the three-mil difficulty.
  • Practical details. That the Rishonim’ omission of Elokai Netzor is mentioned by the Tur and Shulchan Aruch (the summary: “the Tur and Shulchan Aruch discuss it”); the Yiddish “inn” story, in which Chassidim say “from this inn to that inn” (partly kept); and the announcement that Tachanun comes next week.
  • Asides (sponsor names, the visitors, jokes about the Wild West, “fixing Wikipedia”, the Portuguese spelling): none belongs in the body of a sefer.

C. Added or editorial material not in the shiur

The closing סיכום is composed. The lecturer ended abruptly (“It’s late; we’ll continue with Hashem next week and we’ll start Tachanun”). The final paragraph (“May we be זוכה to daven…”) is an editorial prayer and should be marked as such or dropped. The footer’s production note is also not part of the discourse.
