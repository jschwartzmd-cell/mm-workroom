Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

18 August 2024 — מִזְמוֹר לְתוֹדָה, Tzitzis, Yehi Ch'vod, Ashrei

Content-based review · reviewer read the finished write-up in full · 23 August 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

This shiur ties off Mizmor L'Todah, opens the surprisingly contested minhag of holding the front tzitzis at Baruch She'amar, and then moves through Yehi Ch'vod and Ashrei. The Mechaber's striking citation of the midrash that all korbanos will be nullified except todah anchors the first section, with Rashi's siddur-tradition attributing the mizmor to Moshe Rabbeinu adding a second layer. The tzitzis section is where the shiur becomes genuinely surprising: the Aderes' open dissent, the Vilna Gaon's hefsek concern, the Rimzei Elul author canvassing his generation's gedolim and coming up empty, and a beautiful pshat-origin in the Mishnah's mi'sheyakir bein techeles l'lavan. Yehi Ch'vod's Levush-counting kashya and Ashrei's Sefer HaEshkol memorization chiddush round out the halachic and hashkafic content — and the Eshkol then pivots the shiur into a serious digression on the Halberstadt forgery and the spurious Yerushalmi on Kodshim. The Rav uses the seam of the siddur to teach a lesson in mesorah-critical vetting.

2.What Was Strongest

  • The Aderes' dissent, presented without softening. A less honest maggid shiur would have quoted the Magen Avraham and the Kitzur and left the audience with a warm, unified minhag. The Rav names a first-tier Litvishe gadol who openly refused the practice — and shows that the Vilna Gaon's abstention was not eccentricity but a coherent hefsek-based halachic position within the Baruch She'amar – Yishtabach brachah-frame. This is the shiur's most valuable intellectual move: it teaches that a universal minhag in the room is not the same as a settled halachic question.
  • The mi'sheyakir pshat for the tzitzis-holding. The reconstruction — that in the pre-clock era one physically checked the front tzitzis for the techeles/lavan distinction at the moment of Baruch She'amar, and that our minhag is a fossilized living memory of that check — is genuinely satisfying. It explains the front two (not four), it explains the timing (why Baruch She'amar specifically, before Shema), and it does so without collapsing into pure Kabbalah. Even for a reader who accepts the Arizal's framing, this pshat is a valuable structural anchor.
  • The Halberstadt Eshkol digression. Structurally a detour, but methodologically the payoff of the shiur. Naming Rav Tzvi Binyamin Auerbach, the buried-manuscript story, Prof. Albeck's textual demonstration — and then linking it to the Yerushalmi on Kodshim and the Maharsham/Rogatchover exposures — models a mature stance: mesorah is not credulity, it is vetted transmission. The line "even a forger must possess deep talmid chacham capacity to convince other talmidei chachamim" is quotable.
  • The Levush's 21-Names kashya. The concrete arithmetic — only 19 explicit, with Kah as #20 and the embedded Sheim Havayah in "shamayim v'tageil" as #21 — is the kind of specificity that changes how one says Yehi Ch'vod the next morning. This is what a good tefillah-shiur does.

3.What Was Weaker or Worth Pushing Back On

The Magen Avraham's kesavim-citation is treated as sufficient, but the hefsek question is never really answered. The Gaon says the tzitzis-handling is hefsek. The Magen Avraham says do it. The shiur essentially concludes "most people follow the Magen Avraham" — but how does the Magen Avraham answer the hefsek concern? Presumably tzorech ha'brachah (handling tzitzis at a brachah that celebrates the King who wraps Himself in light is l'tzorech), or the general kavvanah-enhancement heter that permits kissing tefillin at Shema. The shiur owes at least a sentence of engagement rather than leaving the two positions in unresolved tension.

The Aderes biographical section is disproportionate to the halachic point. The material is rich — twin brother, Rav Kook, the apartment opposite Har HaBayis, 120 sefarim, weeping over the Shechinah — but it consumes a large fraction of the tzitzis discussion and pulls the shiur off the halachic thread. A tighter version would sacrifice the Rav Kook family tree for a fuller treatment of which Rishonim/Acharonim lined up on which side of the hefsek question.

The Rashi-siddur attribution of Mizmor L'Todah to Moshe Rabbeinu is dropped in and left standing. This is a strong claim — that a kapitel universally attributed to David actually predates him by four centuries. There is a real machloikes on which mizmorim are pre-Davidic (the Ibn Ezra, the Radak, the Meiri weigh in), and Mizmor L'Todah is not an obvious candidate for pre-Davidic attribution — David composed it as the song accompanying the korban todah he instituted for the Mishkan David. The Rashi-siddur's reading deserves either a defense or a counter-source.

The Ashrei section under-delivers on the Gemara's promise. The Berachos 4b guarantee of olam ha'ba to one who says Ashrei three times daily is the most theologically charged statement in the shiur, and the Rav resolves it purely structurally (acrostic + Posei'ach es yadecha). But why these two features earn olam ha'ba? The Maharsha, the Iyun Yaakov, the Nefesh HaChaim all have serious things to say about Ashrei's unique standing — the shiur names none of them.

The Eshkol-forgery discussion, powerful as it is, undermines the earlier chiddush without accounting for the damage. The Rav uses the Eshkol's memorization chiddush as the centerpiece of the Ashrei section — then immediately tells us that "substantial portions of the Eshkol as we have it — including passages quoted by the Mishnah Berurah — are likely later forgeries." Is the memorization chiddush in the authentic core or in the Halberstadt expansion? The audience is left hanging on precisely the question the shiur has just taught them to ask.

4.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The nun-omission in Ashrei is not mentioned. A shiur on Ashrei's acrostic structure that skips the missing nun is missing its most famous sugya. Berachos 4b itself brings the nefilah-based reason (naflah lo tosif kum betulas Yisrael) and Dovid's ruach hakodesh counter-move (somech Hashem l'chol ha'noflim). This is the classical answer to why Ashrei and it interlocks directly with the memorization chiddush the shiur does bring: the acrostic is not just a mnemonic, it is a theological device with a deliberate gap.

The hefsek question in Pesukei d'Zimra deserves the Chayei Adam/Mishnah Berurah framework. The Gaon's hefsek concern is not sui generis; it plugs into a whole halachic architecture. The Mishnah Berurah in siman 51 explicitly discusses which tzorech ha'tefillah interruptions are permitted between Baruch She'amar and Yishtabach (answering amen, kaddish, kedushah, barchu, and the tzitzis-holding among them). The tzitzis question is really: does the minhag qualify as tzorech ha'brachah (Gra: no) or as tzorech ha'tefillah (Magen Avraham: yes). Framed that way, the two positions are internally coherent expressions of a single lomdishe spectrum.

The 21-Names / 21-pesukim parallel invites a Sheim Havayah question the shiur doesn't ask. If the count only works by including the embedded Havayah in shamayim v'tageil, then Yehi Ch'vod is teaching us that shemos ha'kadosh can be present without being visible — a claim with real implications for hazkaras Sheim Shamayim, for reading Havayos in Tanach, and for Kabbalistic Shemos-shel-tzeirufim. The shiur touches the mechanic without naming the principle.

The korban todah's eternity has a lomdishe partner in the Rambam's Hilchos Melachim. The Rambam (Melachim 11:1, and the parallel in Vayikra Rabbah) is famously read by some Acharonim as saying that all korbanos return in the messianic era — complicating the midrash the Mechaber cites. The tension between korbanos-return and korbanos-nullification is one of the classical machloikos in hilchos beis ha'bechirah, and the shiur's brisk resolution (chatas/asham only) papers over a much wider debate.

The Rimzei Elul phenomenon is itself halachically interesting. A 19th-century rav canvasses his generation and no one can supply a reason for a universally practiced minhag. What is the halachic status of a minhag whose rationale is opaque to its own practitioners? The Chasam Sofer and the Divrei Chaim both wrote on minhagim whose reasons were lost; the shiur has a live case study in front of it and doesn't step into the meta-halachic question.

5.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.Where in the Eshkol does the memorization chiddush sit — in the authentic core cited by the Beis Yosef and Tur, or in the Halberstadt expansion that Albeck showed to be unreliable? If the latter, does the chiddush still carry weight as an anonymous Ashkenazic insight, or should it be attributed differently?
Q2.How does the Magen Avraham answer the Gaon's hefsek concern? Is his position that tzitzis-handling is tzorech ha'brachah, or that it falls under the general tzorech ha'tefillah permission for chavivus ha'mitzvah practices like kissing tefillin at Shema?
Q3.The Aderes and the Vilna Gaon both abstained. Are there contemporary poskim — among the talmidei ha'Gra, in Yerushalmi communities, in the Brisker orbit — who still refrain today? If a baal teshuvah from a Litvishe background asked whether to adopt the minhag, what would the Rav's psak be?
Q4.The missing nun in Ashrei — how does the Rav understand its relationship to the memorization chiddush? Does a deliberate alef-beis gap sit comfortably with a mnemonic purpose, or is there a tension there worth developing?
Q5.The Rashi-siddur attribution of Mizmor L'Todah to Moshe Rabbeinu — is this a widely accepted mesorah or a solo shittah? What do the Ibn Ezra, the Radak, and the Meiri hold on the authorship? And if it is Moshe's composition, how does that reshape the way we say the mizmor daily?
Q6.The midrash that all korbanos will be nullified except todah stands in real tension with the Rambam's hilchos beis ha'bechirah and hilchos melachim, which envision the return of the full korban order. How does the Rav reconcile the two? And does the Mechaber's decision to cite this particular midrash in Orach Chaim reflect a halachic position on the messianic avodah, or just a homiletic flourish?

6.Bottom Line

A quietly excellent shiur whose center of gravity is not where the title suggests. The Mizmor L'Todah material is workmanlike; the Yehi Ch'vod/Ashrei sections carry solid chiddushim; but the shiur's real subject is vetted mesorah — and both the tzitzis section (where the Aderes and the Gaon refuse a universal minhag) and the Eshkol digression (where a beloved Rishon turns out to be partially forged) are variations on that single theme. The shiur is at its best when the Rav trusts the audience to sit with the tension. It is at its weakest when biography crowds out lomdus, or when a resonant chiddush (the Eshkol on memorization) is followed within minutes by the discovery that the source may not be reliable. A talmid chochom leaves this shiur understanding tefillah better, but also with the more important lesson: our mesorah is a critical tradition, not a credulous one.
