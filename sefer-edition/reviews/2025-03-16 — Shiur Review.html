Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

16 March 2025 — מָגֵן אַבְרָהָם — The Soul of the First Berachah

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · 30 August 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

This Purim Katan shiur takes the puzzle of “מָגֵן אַבְרָהָם” alone — not “Yitzchak v'Yaakov” — from the Gemara in Pesachim 117 and layers three mehalchim onto it. The Kotzker (with an antecedent in Megaleh Amukos) reads “וָבְך חוֹתמִין” as identifying the final generation as the dor ha-chesed — a claim the Rav grounds in a striking observation about the scale of Purim tzedakah. The Tiferes Banim from Munkacs, riding on a face-value Radak in Yechezkel 46, argues that in the third Beis HaMikdash there is only a tamid shel shachar, therefore only shacharis — the tefillah of Avraham alone. The Maharam Shik, the difficult one, reads “מָגֵן אַבְרָהָם” as embracing the tikkun of the entire zera Avraham — the descendants of Yishmael and Eisav included — and threads it through the uncensored Rambam at the end of Hilchos Melachim that casts Yishu and Muhammad as unwitting preparation for messianic consciousness. The shiur closes by pushing all three layers back into the words of the berachah as objects of kavvanah.

2.What Was Strongest

  • The Tiferes Banim's chiddush. The move is genuinely elegant: face-value Radak on the missing tamid shel bein ha-arbayim in Yechezkel 46, combined with “tefillos ke-neged temidin tiknum,” yielding only shacharis in the future. The confirmation from the grammatical tenses of the three parshiyos — “עָמַד” as continuous present against the vav-hahipuch narrative pasts of “וַיֵצֵא” and “וַיִפְגַע” — is the kind of textual verification that turns a suggestive homily into something like a proof.
  • The handling of “lo tishtaneh ha-Torah.” The Rav does not leave the chiddush hanging against the Rambam's Thirteen Principles. He offers two independent reconciliations — the Rambam's counting of korban tamid as a single mitzvah, and the Ramban's parallel on the pig developing to satisfy the simanei kashrus. Bringing the Ramban's chazir in as structural precedent is the kind of cross-textual reflex that separates a serious shiur from a nice vort.
  • The uncensored Rambam in Hilchos Melachim. Foregrounding this passage — and naming the Frankel edition as the reason we can read it — gives the third mehalach a first-tier Rishon to stand on. Without the Rambam, the Maharam Shik's reading would be beautiful but easy to dismiss. With it, the whole architecture is anchored in mainstream halachic literature. The Yaavetz corroboration is a valuable pointer to how the Rambam has been received.
  • The dor ha-chesed callout with the Purim tzedakah observation. This is the kind of concrete, empirical detail that turns the Kotzker's vort from familiar homily into felt reality. The observation that the sheer volume of chesed in our generation is unprecedented in Jewish history — and that this is exactly the matbe'a of the final geulah promised to Avraham — is the shiur's most quotable moment.
  • The R' Boruch Ber wagon-driver story, well placed. Dropped inside the discussion of shituf and the Radbaz, it functions as a real argument, not decoration: even a false theology, taken seriously, retains a moral floor that pure unbelief does not. It softens the ground for the Maharam Shik's hardest claim — that zera Yishmael and zera Eisav carry something worth including in the chasima.

3.What Was Weaker or Worth Pushing Back On

The three mehalchim are stacked, not synthesized. The closing Synthesis section lists them — chesed, shacharis, universal tikkun — but does not attempt the harder question: are these three faces of one thing, or three unrelated drashos that happen to land on the same phrase? A unifying observation is close at hand: all three read “וָבְך חוֹתמִין” as pointing outward and forward — Avraham's middah as the universal-chesed engine, his tefillah as the eternal-present shacharis, his fatherhood as the umbrella under which every branch eventually returns. Stating this explicitly would have made the shiur cohere rather than accumulate.

The Maharam Shik's reading is presented but never defended against the natural objection. The Rav flags that the chiddush is difficult and asks for patience — fair. But the obvious objection is left standing: “magen Avraham” in the siddur follows immediately after “Elokei Avraham, Elokei Yitzchak, Elokei Yaakov,” where the three Avos are named specifically to distinguish them from the wider progeny. Reading the chasima as universally inclusive requires an argument for why Avraham in the chasima refers to “av hamon goyim” while Avraham two words earlier refers to the specific Av of the Yaakov mateh sheleimah. That linguistic pivot deserves a defense.

The gilgul material and the etymology of “Yeshu” from “Eisav” are asserted without engagement. The claim that Yod-Shin-Vav shares its letters with Ayin-Shin-Vav (with a substitution) is presented as if self-evidently significant. It is not self-evident, and the audience will sense the hand-wave. Either this is a substantive kabbalistic tradition worth grounding, or it is a mnemonic device better left out. The same holds for Muhammad-as-gilgul-of-Yishmael: attributed to R' Chaim Vital, but the theological work the claim is doing in the shiur is heavier than the passing citation can bear.

The Mankud Matav citation is opaque. The name is unfamiliar and the footnote in the write-up itself concedes that the attribution and edition were not specified. For a source that is being invoked for the actionable kavvanah in Shemoneh Esrei, this is thin. Either the attribution should be nailed down before publication in the sefer, or the kavvanah should be offered on the Rav's own authority as a synthesis of the three mehalchim.

4.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

The Rambam's chad-mitzvah read of tamid is doing real halachic work. If korban tamid is one mitzvah discharged twice a day, then the Tiferes Banim's chiddush does not violate “lo tishtaneh ha-Torah” — the same one mitzvah is discharged once. But this framing raises its own question: does “תְפִלוֹת כְנֶגֶד תְמִידִין תִקְנוּם” also collapse into one tefillah in the future, or do Minchah and Maariv retain some residual halachic status even without their korban anchor? The shiur assumes the former; it is not obvious.

The Ramban-on-chazir parallel is stronger than the Rambam-chad-mitzvah parallel. The Ramban's move is: the halachic category is stable, the physical world develops to fit it. Applied here, this yields: the halachos of tefillah (three per day, corresponding to three korbanos) remain intact, but the physical world — specifically, the cycle of light and dark — changes such that bein ha-arbayim no longer exists as a zeman. This is a cleaner fit than the chad-mitzvah framing and deserved to be the primary reconciliation rather than the secondary one.

The Radbaz on shituf is invoked but its halachic scope is understated. Shituf-for-a-non-Jew being permitted (or at least not a full issur) is a live machlokes — the Rema, the Noda BiYehudah, the Chasam Sofer all weigh in. The shiur treats the Radbaz's position as if it were consensus. Whether the Maharam Shik's reading — that Christianity retains something of Avraham's emunah — can rest on the Radbaz alone, or requires the broader Ashkenazic tradition, is a real question.

The Zohar-based kavvanah claim needs a halachic anchor. The Mankud Matav is invoked for a specific kavvanah during the chasima of Magen Avraham. But kavvanah in the first berachah is already halachically loaded — the Shulchan Aruch (siman 101) requires it, and b'dieved one repeats. Layering an additional kabbalistic kavvanah onto the required halachic one is not neutral; it raises the question of what happens when one is achievable and the other is not. The shiur presents it as pure enhancement, but the halachic geometry is more crowded than that.

5.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.Do the three mehalchim converge on a single underlying reading of “וָבְך חוֹתמִין” — something like “Avraham as the universal, eternal, and future-directed face of Klal Yisrael” — or should the sefer present them as three distinct drashos without claiming a synthesis?
Q2.On the Maharam Shik's reading, how do we account for the immediate context in Shemoneh Esrei? “Elokei Avraham, Elokei Yitzchak, Elokei Yaakov” names the three Avos specifically. What linguistic or theological shift lets “Avraham” two words later refer to “av hamon goyim” rather than to the specific Av?
Q3.The Tiferes Banim's chiddush depends on Radak's minority reading of Yechezkel 46. Are there other Rishonim who read the pasuk this way, or does the whole edifice rest on Radak alone? If it is Radak alone, does that weaken the halachic status of the corollary about Minchah and Maariv?
Q4.On the Rambam's long-arc framing of Yishu and Muhammad: is the messianic-preparation function meant to describe the current state of the world, such that we can already see the fruit, or is it strictly retrospective — something we will only appreciate lasid lavo? The Maharam Shik's kavvanah in “magen Avraham” requires the former; the Rambam's language allows either.
Q5.What is the halachic status of the added kavvanah from Mankud Matav? Is it a lechatchilah for anyone who knows it, or is it a kabbalistic overlay that a ba'al nefesh may adopt but that has no bearing on the halachic requirement of kavvanah in the first berachah?
Q6.The Kotzker's dor ha-chesed reading and the Tiferes Banim's shacharis reading are both fundamentally optimistic — they identify our era with the messianic threshold. Given the state of Klal Yisrael in 2025, is the Rav prepared to say we are living in the dor “ubcha chosmin,” or are these readings offered as aspirational rather than descriptive?

6.Bottom Line

A shiur of unusual ambition — it takes a single word, “מָגֵן אַבְרָהָם,” and pulls three distinct mehalchim through it, each anchored in serious Rishonim and Acharonim. The Tiferes Banim section is the shiur's high point, both for the beauty of the chiddush and for the disciplined way the Rav handles the objection from “lo tishtaneh ha-Torah.” The Maharam Shik section takes real courage to teach and is essential for anyone thinking honestly about the Rambam's uncensored Hilchos Melachim, but it leaves the linguistic problem in the siddur undefended and rests some of its weight on unattributed gilgul traditions. The three mehalchim are stacked rather than synthesized — a unifying reading is available and would elevate the sefer's version. Worth the hour; worth returning to before ever davening Shemoneh Esrei casually again.
