23 November 2025 — סיום תחנון and Opening the ארון קודש

Independent review · read fresh, from the finished HTML only · 25 October 2026

1.Overall Read

An eleven-section writeup (roughly 4300 words of body plus a twenty-eight-note Mareh Mekomos apparatus of about 2,900 words) on the close of Tachanun and the opening of the Aron. The spine is easy to follow: Shomer Yisrael (I), the close of Tachanun (II), VaAnachnu Lo Neda (III), Al Tizkor (IV), Chatzi Kaddish (V), the open Aron (VI), Kel Erech Apayim (VII–VIII), VaYehi BiNsoa (IX), Brich Shmei (X), and a brief close (XI). The strongest material is the three-reasons analysis of Kel Erech Apayim and the VaYehi BiNsoa kavanah. The hard editorial issues are six: (a) the voice of oral delivery and of editorial correction is mixed into the body; (b) at least five places where a footnote contradicts the sentence it supports; (c) Section IV's long business digression, which buries the link to the verse; (d) the order of VI, VII, VIII and XI, which scatters the Aron material and states the practical conclusion on Kel Erech Apayim before the reasons; (e) an apparatus that still carries working notes ("Verify exact ...", "an earlier draft ... was corrected") and cites Sefaria as an authority; (f) spelling and naming that vary within the document.

2.What Works

  • Section VII as a piece of argument. The two printed versions, the question "why," and then three separately labeled reasons (the latecomers; the days of din and the Machzor Vitry; the apology before the reading), each with a stated practical consequence (only on Tachanun days; Monday and Thursday regardless; every taking-out), is how a halachic puzzle should be laid out for a general reader.
  • Section IX. The move from Rashi's three days' journey to "the Torah is always ahead of us; we are always falling behind" to the kavanah is short, earned, and portable.
  • Section V. The question (why half?), the answer from Rabbeinu Manoach (a Kaddish for the one who must leave, but not a full one), and the closing sentence about the departing Jew's partial send-off are clean and complete in two paragraphs.
  • The treatment of the parentheses in Brich Shmei (X) is humane and gives the reader two readings (the unmarried; the older person with children and grandchildren) without forcing a choice.
  • Footnote hygiene. The twenty-eight references run in body order from 1 to 28 with matching back-links, and the short/expanded format is consistent. The note-academic blocks are a good device, and several (fn 13, fn 14, fn 19) are honest about what could not be located.
  • The three postures (III) is a good example of a single idea (we stood, we sat, we fell) carried across three sentences without a wasted word.

3.Where It’s Weak or Could Be Sharper

The voice is the voice of a live shiur, and two other voices are mixed in. Oral delivery: "I do not know," "I have never seen an Ashkenaz סידור that includes it," "I emphasize this because I need to hear it as much as anyone," "בלי נדר, starting tomorrow I will say both," "Personally I started davening for good children when I was still a בחור," "I cringe every time," "Two Sundays ago," "last week's פרשה," "we will discuss it more next week," "Next week בעזרת השם." Editorial correction in the body: "the lecturer said 'the 1200s,' but the סידור רש״י ... dates to about 1100," "(the recording also gives the name ר' שעיה'לע — see the note)," "the lecturer said in Turkey ... the first edition was in fact at Mantua c. 1558 (see the note)." The body thus alternates between "I," "we," "the lecturer," and "the recording." For a sefer edition the body should be third-person and uniform; corrections belong in the notes.

Five notes contradict the sentence they support. (1) Section IV prints כי אם ראש חודש ושבת as the Ach Pri Tevuah's allusion and asks, "Do you recognize those words? They appear at the very beginning of ש״ס"; fn 9 says the phrase "does not appear in this passage." (2) Section X says the Magen Avraham "writes that one should not say it on Monday and Thursday"; fn 26 says the reasoning and the Monday and Thursday exclusion "were not located" and rest on the lecturer's report. (3) Section VIII says "The אבודרהם states explicitly that it is an error to say only one"; fn 22 says that was not located. (4) Section VI says the Chida "states bluntly"; fn 14 says the work and siman were not named and the lecturer "does not know the exact halachic limits." (5) Section V says the Rambam writes that Kaddish is said before and after every Kri'as Shema; fn 11 describes Kaddish before the blessings of Kri'as Shema and after Shemoneh Esrei, which is not the same claim. Each should be hedged in the body or the note should be brought into line.

Section IV carries a digression that hides its point. The section sets out to explain Al Tizkor; the reader then gets Dovid HaMelech's alarm clock, the business of the court, a mitzvah to buy from Jews with a price caveat, the advice to "take from the פלשתים," and only then the two first questions. The Philistines line is presented as Dovid's controversial advice with no note and no hedge; a reader will want to know whether it is a Gemara or a gloss. Consider a short summary sentence at the top ("the verse is read as a plea that poverty excuse the two first failures") and moving the business-ethics matter to a callout.

The Aron material is scattered. Section VI introduces the open Aron and the call for silence; the Baal Shem Tov on Brich Shmei sits inside it, three sections before Brich Shmei itself (X); and the call for silence returns in XI. Section VI even names קל ארך אפים and בריך שמיה before either has been introduced. Fold VI's silence material into XI, or open VI with a sentence saying what is coming.

Section VII states its conclusion before its reasons, and VIII asks the question again. "The Right Practice — Say Both" (VII) tells the reader to say both versions, with the promise "Let me explain"; the explanation arrives only in VIII (the Avudraham's fifty words). VIII then opens "Why two נוסחאות?" as if VII had not asked it. Moving "say both" to the end of VIII, and merging the two sections' openings, would remove the double start.

The aside on נוסח בני רומי is longer than its role. Three paragraphs, including a description of a Jerusalem museum, interrupt the first reason, and the aside ends by saying the Roman rite does not have the prayer at all. The point (the Gra's attribution to Italy is puzzling) can be made in four sentences; the rest could go to a note.

A reader cannot see the three reasons at once. A three-row table (reason; source; when the prayer is said) at the end of VII would give the practical result in one glance; the text currently leaves it to be assembled.

Naming and unsourced claims in the body. R' Ram Shor (a living rav) is named for an anecdote told in the first person; the ArtScroll "later printing" claim has no note; "the אחרונים explain" the parentheses in Brich Shmei has no note; the parenthetical on שבתאות and the Yekkes is a delicate claim that is unsourced; "a testimony that R' Nosson Adler added it" has no source.

4.Factual / Citation Concerns

  • The calendar. The header gives 23 November 2025 (ג׳ כסלו תשפ״ו). That is the Sunday after Toldos; the verse of the veil (Genesis 24:65) is from Chayei Sarah, read the week before that. "Last week's פרשה" is therefore loose; name the parshah and drop "last week."
  • The chronology in fn 12. The body says Rabbeinu Manoach "is brought once in תוספות in מסכת חולין; it is presumably the same ראשון"; fn 12 describes him as of Narbonne, late thirteenth century. A reader will ask how Tosafos cite a commentator of that date. Either hedge or drop the equation.
  • The Gra and Italy (fn 17). The note cites Biur HaGra to Orach Chaim 134; the body says only that the Gra "attributes" the takanah to the sages of Italy. If the passage cannot be found, say "as reported."
  • fn 5 and fn 24 quote the Mishnah Berurah and Rashi with verse and comment numbers; each should be checked against the printed text (see Part 3), and fn 23 should place the Tanchuma citation on the right words of the verse.
  • fn 25's historical claims (the Zohar's first printing; Shaarei Zion, Prague 1662, as the vehicle of the practice; "within decades") are the editor's; the second and third have no source in the note.
  • Sefaria as an authority in the notes. "Date and place ... are as given on Sefaria" (fn 1), "verified on Sefaria" (fn 5, 9, 19, 22, 24, 26, 27) and "(Sefaria dates it c. 1055–1105)" (fn 19) cite a website, not an edition. For a sefer, name the edition and siman; keep the status flag in the Open Questions.
  • Drafting history in the notes. "An earlier draft of this shiur wrote זכרנו and was corrected here" (fn 5), "the Aramaic wording that an earlier draft printed here ... has been removed" (fn 13), "the further details earlier printed here ... have been removed" (fn 14), "An earlier draft of this note added ..." (fn 23), "The earlier citation to מגן אברהם סימן קל״ד was corrected" (fn 26). Readers of a printed edition do not need the history of the draft.

5.Small Polish Suggestions

  • Spelling and naming that vary. אל תזכור (body, heading) against אל תזכר (fn 6); ויהי בנסוע (heading, body) against ויהי בנסע (fn 23, 24); יסמח משה (body) against ישמח משה (fn 7); סידור and סדור (both in the body and in the notes); עונות and עוונות; ליסקער רבי and ליסקער רב; הגר״א in the body and גר״א in fn 3; ארון קודש and ארון הקדש. Pick one of each.
  • Hebrew density. The body runs English and Hebrew together at a rate that makes the English spine hard to follow, with bare Hebrew words (מנהג, דין, גבורה, ממתיק הדינים) and unglossed Yiddish (שמועסן, שטיקעלע, גוישע, יידן, hefker zone). For a sefer edition, adopt one convention: transliterated term first, Hebrew in parentheses, and a one-time gloss for Yiddish.
  • Heading style. The h3s mix capitalization ("The Three Postures of davening"; "The Halacha of Sitting and Standing"; "First Reason — For the Latecomers") and language ("The אך פרי תבואה"). Standardize.
  • Header, lead and footer. The lead says the piece ends "with the open ארון קודש and the תפילה of בריך שמיה"; it ends with a section on the proper manner and a "next week" line. The header says "Reworked from the original שיעור" and the footer says "Reworked from a שיעור delivered by Rabbi Ephrati"; the footer's "Hebrew terminology preserved in Hebrew script throughout" is not information a reader needs.
  • The "next week" close. The final paragraph announces next week's topic. Cut it, or move it to an editorial coda.
  • Footnote anchors are placed variously before and after punctuation and dashes (compare fn 6, fn 8 and fn 13). Standardize.
  • Duplicate citations. fn 13 and fn 25 give the same Zohar locus; fn 26 and fn 27 both cite Mishnah Berurah 134:13. Cross-refer instead.

6.Bottom Line

A good core: the three reasons for Kel Erech Apayim, the VaYehi BiNsoa kavanah, and the clear one-idea passages (three postures, why half) are strong. Before this goes into a sefer: (a) recast the oral-delivery voice and move the editorial corrections ("the lecturer said ... but") out of the body and into the notes; (b) reconcile the five places where a note contradicts the body (כי אם ראש חודש ושבת, the Magen Avraham, the Avudraham, the Chida, the Rambam) by hedging the body; (c) restructure IV (lead with the point; move the business matter aside), gather the Aron material (VI and XI), and put "say both" after the Avudraham; (d) tighten the Roman-rite aside and add a three-row table; (e) clear the apparatus of working notes, drafting history and "verified on Sefaria" language; (f) fix the spelling and naming variants. Fix these and this is a strong treatment of the close of Tachanun and the opening of the Aron.
