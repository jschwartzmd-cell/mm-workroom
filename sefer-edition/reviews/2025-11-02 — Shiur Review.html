2 November 2025 — תחנון: the Confession, the Fall on the Face, and the Argument over Skipping

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, the AI summary, the enhanced transcript text, and the finished write-up (no Sofer.ai document exists for this date) · 18 October 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

A Sunday-morning shiur on tefillah, opening with a dedication le’iluy nishmas Danny Kriegsman (David ben Moshe Shalom), the father-in-law of Reb Meir Weissman and the father of Mrs. Weissman, whose yahrzeit fell on Shemini Atzeres (the same day as the bris of a grandson, Yoel Rubin, in Baltimore), with a brief account of his life (a NASA contractor; thousands of shomrei Shabbos hired; a heter to hear the Megillah by telephone from mission control on Purim). A question from Reb Zeriel David (to whom is ve-imru amen at the end of Oseh Shalom addressed?) is answered: to the angels. The lecturer then begins tachanun. Part one is the vidui (Ashamnu, the Thirteen Attributes) that precedes nefilas apayim: not original, an addition of the Arizal from the Zohar, to pre-empt the mekatrigim who pounce on our prayers when we finish; why we confess sins we did not commit (we are one body); the Chida’s story of the sage who would not say Al Cheit and the Rambam’s reply (every sin is committed daily: anger is avodah zarah; one must also confess the refusal to confess); bagadnu as infidelity; and the midrash of the Yesod VeShoresh HaAvodah (R. Alexander Ziskind of Horodna, who nearly became a disciple of the Maggid of Mezritch) that God shows the angels how Israel says ve-atah tzaddik. Part two is nefilas apayim itself: the sources in Eikev and Korach; the lack of an explicit Gemara; and the long narrative of the oven of Achnai, the ban on R. Eliezer, Imma Shalom’s daily vigil, the day she erred and Rabban Gamliel died; from which the Rishonim (the Ritva) derive that it follows Shemoneh Esrei immediately and is a reshus, with a digression on when to disobey a wife (the Chofetz Chaim). Part three is the practice: the Chassidic custom of skipping on a yahrzeit (the dangers of tachanun without kavanah; the hilula), the Minchas Elazar’s fire-and-brimstone critique, the Divrei Chaim of Sanz and his stick, the story of the Radoshitzer and the 5,000 tachanunim; the days on which it is omitted; the fast on a yahrzeit; Rav Moshe Feinstein (do not fight; say it quietly on one’s own); and the story of the Chazon Ish, who said tachanun alone after a baal tefillah skipped it in his own minyan and then thanked him (yasher koach). The shiur closes with a remark that the matter of nefilas apayim will be taken up the following Sunday, and a few announcements.

2.Overall Fidelity — What the Write-Up Does Well

The write-up follows the AI summary closely and, for that reason, inherits both its good order and its smoothing. It is accurate on the architecture of the shiur: the opening question, the vidui and its Zohar rationale, the collective-responsibility answer, the Rambam story, the Yesod VeShoresh HaAvodah midrash, the Imma Shalom narrative, the Ritva and the reshus, the Chassidic section in the order the lecturer gave it (Besht’s disciples, Minchas Elazar, Sanz, Radoshitz), the list of days, Rav Moshe and the Chazon Ish. The Imma Shalom narrative is the best-preserved passage: every beat (the ban, the sister, the daily distraction, the miscalculated Rosh Chodesh, the shofar, the second version with the poor man, “we want to be like Imma Shalom”) is there. The lecturer’s halachic caution (“do not learn from that heter”; “please do not use this against people you dislike”; “be careful with shalom bayis”) is faithfully kept, and the closing moral (say it quietly, on your own) is exactly the lecturer’s.

Its weaknesses are: (i) several Gemara and halachic claims are firmer, or different, in the write-up than in the recording (the source of “כל הכועס”, the Korach verse, the oven, the calendar error, R. Akiva’s role, the Ritva/Beis Yosef chain); (ii) the footnote apparatus supplies loci, biographies and details the lecturer never gave, some of which do not check against Sefaria; and (iii) real material is missing: the posture of nefilas apayim in the past and today, the Besht’s disciples’ hilula rationale, the lecturer’s disclaimers about not mocking Chassidim, and the announcement that the topic continues.

3.Content Flags — What Is Wrong, Misattributed, Omitted, or Added

A. Misattributions and overclaims

Flag 1 — “First space mission” versus “first spacewalk.” The write-up has the lecturer say that Neil Armstrong “would not have survived the first space mission.” The recording says “the first spacewalk” (the AI summary agrees), and the lecturer relays it from a text message he had just received, with the hedge “do you know this fascinating story?” The year is not his: he says “I think nineteen seventy, when did Apollo,” and a listener supplies “69.” Neither the mission nor the year should be stated as established; the Sefer Edition should report the claim as relayed from Mrs. Weissman’s message.

Flag 2 — Who fell on their faces in Korach. The lecturer says “Klal Yisrael said vayiplu al penehem… they all fell,” and the write-up repeats it. The verse (Bamidbar 16:22; again 16:45) has Moshe and Aharon fall and speak. [Sefaria check: Bamidbar 16:22, “But they fell on their faces and said, ‘O God, Source of the breath of all flesh…’”; the subject of the verb is Moshe and Aharon.] The inference that they were forgiven through the falling is the lecturer’s own (“it seems that…”).

Flag 3 — “The Gemara says כל הכועס כאילו עובד עבודה זרה.” The lecturer says “the Gemara says,” and the write-up cites Shabbos 105b (“R. Avin”) and a Zohar. [Sefaria check: the wording is not in Shabbos 105b, which has R. Avin on Tehillim 81:10 (the strange god within a man is the evil inclination) and a baraisa that whoever rends garments, breaks vessels or scatters money in anger should be like an idolater in your eyes; nor in Nedarim 22b, which has other statements about anger. The exact wording is the Rambam’s, Hilchos De’os 2:3: “the early Sages said: anyone who becomes angry is like one who worships idols.”] The Zohar reference (I 27b) is the write-up’s.

Flag 4 — The oven of Achnai and the dispute. The lecturer says the oven “was broken down and reconnected again with cement” and that Rabbi Eliezer held that reconnecting does not make it a keli. [Sefaria check: Mishnah Kelim 5:10 has the oven cut into rings with sand between them; Rabbi Eliezer declares it clean, the Sages unclean. Bava Metzia 59b: the carob tree is uprooted a hundred cubits (some say four hundred); the walls lean and, out of respect for both sages, neither fall nor straighten.] The lecturer’s “walls fell… almost fell” and the “cement” are loose retellings.

Flag 5 — R. Yochanan ben Zakkai’s place in the story; R. Akiva’s role. The lecturer sets the oven dispute in R. Yochanan ben Zakkai’s time, as a public statement by him that the authority of Chazal was intact, and calls the quarrel “pivotal… between Rebbe Yochanan ben Zakkai, Gamliel, Rebbe Yehoshua and Rebbe Eliezer.” The Gemara (BM 59b) names no R. Yochanan ben Zakkai: the ban is imposed by Rabban Gamliel and the Sages at Yavne. His founding of Yavne at the time of the siege is in Gittin 56b (verified), so the background is sound; the framing of the oven episode as his statement is the lecturer’s. And “R. Akiva… also joined all the Chachamim” understates the Gemara, where R. Akiva is the one who volunteers to bring R. Eliezer the news, in black, sitting four cubits away.

Flag 6 — The calendar error is stated as fact. The write-up says she thought it was Rosh Chodesh “but it was the thirtieth day, not the thirty-first.” In the recording the lecturer is unsure (“it was lamed, not lamed-aleph… she wasn’t even sure… she missed by one day”). [Sefaria check: BM 59b says she confused a full month with a deficient one.] The Sefer Edition should say that she was mistaken by a day about the month.

Flag 7 — “We are sorry, but we had to do it.” The lecturer says the Sages came to R. Eliezer on the day of his passing and said it. [Sefaria check: Sanhedrin 68a has R. Akiva and the Sages visit him in his illness, sitting at a distance of four cubits because he was in niduy; on his death R. Yehoshua declares the ban lifted. The words of apology are not in the text.] Present as the lecturer’s paraphrase.

Flag 8 — The Ritva and the Beis Yosef. The write-up has the Ritva derive both that nefilas apayim follows Shemoneh Esrei and that it is a reshus, and says the Ritva is “cited by the Beis Yosef ad loc.” In the recording the Ritva asks whether Imma Shalom could have stood by him all day, and answers that she needed only a few minutes right after Shemoneh Esrei. The reshus point is attributed to “the Rishonim” without a name. And the Beis Yosef’s “raya” is raised by the lecturer as something the Acharonim question. [Sefaria check: the Beis Yosef on OC 131 cites תלמידי הרשב"א on BM 59b and concludes that one may not speak between tefillah and tachanun; it does not name the Ritva. A Ritva on Bava Metzia could not be located on Sefaria.]

Flag 9 — The Chofetz Chaim and the wife. The lecturer says only that the Chofetz Chaim writes in his sefarim, and that “we learned this from other Rishonim,” that one does not listen to a wife whose request goes against God’s will. The write-up’s note adds Shmiras HaLashon and cites Rashi on Bereishis 21:12 and BM 59a as the source of the principle. [Sefaria check: Rashi on 21:12 says Avraham was inferior to Sarah in prophecy; BM 59a has R. Chelbo on honouring one’s wife and the proverb “if your wife is short, bend down and whisper.” Neither is a source for the principle.]

Flag 10 — Brisk and Vilna. In the recording the source for the bris-and-Mincha question is “the Bar Heitam in kuf-lamed-aleph,” and the passage as transcribed is self-contradictory (“even if the bris was before Mincha… you don’t say”). The write-up (like the summary) turns it into “the Beis Yosef in siman 131.” [Sefaria check: the Beis Yosef on 131 does not mention Brisk or Vilna; Rema (SA OC 131:4) rules that at Mincha tachanun is said even in the baby’s presence; the “Bar Heitam” may be the Ba’er Heitev, which could not be retrieved.] The Brisk/Vilna dispute is the lecturer’s report, not located.

Flag 11 — Why only at Shacharis. The write-up (“Why Only at Shacharis?”) gives, as the Zohar’s answer, that the mekatrig acts most actively after Shacharis. In the recording the lecturer says “good question… according to our minhag, we’ll get to that in a moment,” then adds “I don’t know what’s going on up there, Natan. Maybe you are privy to this information. It seems like right after the end of davening…” The explanation is his tentative inference, and the question of Mincha and Maariv is deferred.

Flag 12 — “Three or four hundred years.” The lecturer says the Arizal instituted it “not more than three hundred, four hundred years ago,” then “for over four hundred years Klal Yisrael has been adhering to….” The Arizal lived 1534–1572, so the figures are loose. The write-up’s note adds “17th century” and says the Tur in OC 131 notes the custom of some communities; the lecturer said that the Tur mentions some kehillos that were noheg. [Sefaria check: the Tur’s siman 131 as retrieved describes nefilas apayim (the Rambam, Rav Natronai, Rav Hai Gaon’s text) but not a vidui before it; SA 131 mentions vidui only for a bris on a public fast day (131:5). Left UNCERTAIN.]

Flag 13 — The Yesod VeShoresh HaAvodah. The lecturer says the sefer brings the midrash at the point where we say ואתה צדיק; he is unsure of the ArtScroll translation (“I think”). The note says the midrash appears in the author’s “treatment of nefilas apayim” and gives the author’s dates as 1730–1794; neither is from the lecturer. [Sefaria check: Yesod VeShoresh HaAvodah is on Sefaria; the passage could not be searched; the verse is Nechemiah 9:33.] The Maggid’s reply to R. Alexander (“the Litvaks need you”) is the lecturer’s report.

Flag 14 — The Zohar loci and the Arizal’s works. The note gives Zohar III “Balak 222a” and I 58b, and cites Shaar HaKavanos, Drushei Nefilas Apayim. The lecturer named no passage. The pair of loci is internally doubtful (222a does not lie within Balak in the standard pagination, as far as I know) and the attempt to open them on Sefaria failed; they cannot be confirmed.

Flag 15 — The Sanzer’s one yahrzeit. The write-up has the Divrei Chaim omit tachanun only on כ"א אדר, the yahrzeit of the “נורא עלימה מלך.” The recording has “Nurey Ameli Melech,” garbled; the date (21 Adar) and the sound point to the Noam Elimelech (R. Elimelech of Lizhensk), as the note itself suggests. The identification is an inference. The phrase “besides for like Boimer” (before it) is unrecoverable.

Flag 16 — “Rav Zalman Meltzer” and the yahrzeit fast. The write-up has “the Melamdei Zalman would fast on a yahrzeit; his grandson, the Heilige Rav Zalman Meltzer, would fast and host a seudah.” The recording is: “Rabbi Zalman brought a [seudah?] and he fasted. That you don’t know, that’s his grandfather, the Heilige Zalman Meltzer.” The lecturer is pointing, it seems, at someone in the room (he had introduced a guest, “Rabbi Melzer… eynikl… in Eretz Yisrael,” at the start). “Melamdei Zalman” and the “grandson” have no basis. The fast itself is verified: SA YD 402:12 (main text, not the Rema): “It is a religious duty to fast on the day one’s father or mother died.”

Flag 17 — Identifications supplied by the write-up. The Radoshitzer is not identified in the recording (“the Radashitzah… his name was…”); the note adds R. Yissachar Ber (1765–1843), a disciple of the Chozeh of Lublin, and a printed source. The “Rav Moshe” teshuvah is not located by the lecturer (note: “Igros Moshe OC”); the recording has only “the Remoisha Feinstein writes….” The Chazon Ish story’s printed sources (Pe’er HaDor, Maaseh Ish) are the write-up’s suggestions.

B. Omissions

  • The posture of nefilas apayim. The lecturer says that in the past they literally fell on the floor in every shul; that today “we cheat,” some doing it standing; and that it should be done sitting “according to kabbalah.” [Sefaria check: SA OC 131:2 itself says it is done sitting, not standing; the Rema, only where there is a Torah ark.] Omitted from the write-up entirely.
  • The Besht’s disciples’ second rationale. Besides the danger of saying tachanun without kavanah, the lecturer says they instituted that the yahrzeit of a tzaddik is a hilula, like Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai’s, so tachanun is omitted. The write-up has only the danger.
  • The lecturer’s disclaimers. “I don’t know what the minyan in this shul is… I want to know who decides… I don’t want to make fun of Chassidim; it’s not my place to degrade and criticize them.” Omitted; they are the frame of the whole Chassidic section.
  • The lecturer’s own experience. He says he davened at Kerestir with the Rebbe’s Chassidic yeshiva and that he did not say tachanun there very often (“I couldn’t do it every day… it wasn’t that bad, but anyway. You are supposed to say it”). The write-up has the Kerestir handbook but not the personal remark. The recording is partly garbled.
  • The unnamed sage of the Chida’s story. “I don’t know who he is. It sounds like the Chida knew, but he doesn’t want to say.” Omitted.
  • The extension of the Rambam story. After “you must also say Al Cheit for not wanting to say it,” the lecturer adds that even one who did not steal that day “perhaps did something that was a shtikel gezeilah, and therefore you have to say that.” Omitted.
  • Remarks on the Yesod VeShoresh HaAvodah. That many of the Besht’s circle’s visitors stayed and that is how Chassidus flourished; that the author was the only one of that era so steeped in kabbalah and what could be called chassidus in the general sense, “not chassidus as in the movement”; and the Litvish thanks that “we held on to this last one.”
  • R. Eliezer’s plight. That he could not go to minyan and that nobody spoke to him; “nobody can learn tahor for me.” Also the aside that ten rabbis today have wives who do not speak to each other’s wives (a joke).
  • The aside on Chavah. After the Chofetz Chaim, a remark on a controversial comment of the Baal HaTurim that Chavah struck Adam with a branch to make him eat from the tree (“some say it was a forgery”), with a joke about bringing out the sticks. The AI summary listed it in the sources (“Baal HaTurim on Chavah compelling Adam”). The write-up drops it. [Sefaria check: no comment of the Baal HaTurim on Bereishis 3:6 is available on Sefaria; not located.]
  • The introduction to the unit. That tachanun is “a whole journey” to understand its importance, with a programme (why one says it; its parts and their sources in Chazal; then the Chassidic approach) and the quip that “the world” (the Litvaks) say Chassidim never say tachanun.
  • The guest. A guest, Rabbi Meltzer, introduced at the start as a grandson (eynikl) of the Rosh Yeshiva in Eretz Yisrael (the recording is partly garbled).
  • Closing. A remark about a Friday, Europe, and “no tachanun” (garbled); the statement that the laws of nefilas apayim will be taken up “from next Sunday”; and an announcement by Roy Silver. None belongs in the body, but the continuation should be noted.
