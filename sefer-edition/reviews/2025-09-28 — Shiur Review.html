Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

28 September 2025 — The Cholera Epidemic and רבי ישראל סלנטר’s Decision on יום כיפור

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · 12 October 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

A pre-Yom Kippur departure from the regular tefillah series, delivered as a historical-halachic story rather than a shiur in the usual sense. The lecturer introduces Rav Yisrael Salanter as a gaon before he was a baal mussar (the turn to the masses under the influence of Rav Yosef Zundel of Salant, the social upheaval in Vilna, Brisk as the one yeshivah that still holds the original opposition, and the carpentry prescribed after his stroke). He then sets out the sources for the Yom Kippur cholera incident — The Making of a Gadol (a long discussion at p. 1136), Mekor Baruch of Rav Baruch Epstein — and tells the incident in the Mekor Baruch version: the 1848 epidemic in Vilna, the physicians’ unanimous warning against fasting, the young Rav Yisrael (“thirty-six, thirty-seven”) rising in the main shul without consulting the senior dayanim, the frozen congregation of “a thousand or fifteen hundred,” the kiddush on the bimah, the rebuke of the elderly dayan, the Koidenover chassidim’ claim about the later stroke, the summons to beis din, and the kashya on gittin that silenced the court. Two halachic questions are then raised (why not pachos mikeshiur, and why kiddush at all), followed by the eyewitness version of Shimon Strashun (individual feeding in small portions, no kiddush), the defence reported from the Chofetz Chaim through Rav David Leibowitz and Rav Yaakov Kamenetsky (a rav who must publicize in advance that pikuach nefesh is not asked about; the chasid shoteh), the Shavel incident, the Brisker joke, the Chasam Sofer in Pressburg, Rav Yaakov’s bein adam lachaveiro explanation, and the later reception (Rav Baruch Ber of Kamenetz; the Rishon LeTzion in Jerusalem). It closes abruptly with “it is only a breach of the fast if one eats more than a shiur.”

2.Overall Fidelity

The write-up is a faithful and well-organised reworking. It restores Hebrew spelling for what the transcription mangled (Salanter, Chasam Sofer, Mekor Baruch), turns a rambling oral telling into fourteen numbered sections, keeps the principal anecdotes (the cakes in the pocket, the kiddush club, the Brisker Rav’s remark on embellishment, the Shavel man who collapsed, the Brisker joke) and builds a serviceable mareh mekomos. The problems are of three kinds: (a) identifications that came from the summary rather than from the recording (the elderly dayan, the Ketzos); (b) a Hebrew year and a surname that the recording does not support; and (c) editorial research in the notes that is presented as part of the lecture. None is large, but the first two would be embarrassing in print.

3.Content Flags — Misattributions and Overclaims

Flag 1 — The elderly dayan is split into two people and named wrongly. The transcript calls him “Rebbe Tzalal Hakoyin” (Section V of the write-up), then “Reb Itzal Hakohen” (the summons), and treats them as one man: “the chief dayan of Vilna,” eighty years old, author of a sefer the transcript renders “B’Kurim.” The write-up gives him as רבי יחזקאל לנדא הכהן in Section V and as רבי איצל הכהן מווילנא in Section VII, as if two figures, and the summary (not the recording) makes him the Ketzos HaChoshen. Neither identification can be right: the Noda BiYehudah and the Ketzos both lived and died decades before 1848. The recording supports only a single figure, called by the name that sounds like רבי איצל הכהן (a kohen, a dayan of eighty), whose full name and work the lecturer did not give. Applied in the original HTML: Section V now reads רבי איצל הכהן, with the work and identity marked unresolved.

Flag 2 — The eyewitness’s surname is not שטרויסון. The transcript has “Shimon Shpashun,” “not the Roshashun; the Roshashun was Shmuel Shpashun,” and later “this strashun witness.” That is שטראשון (Strashun), and the famous Vilna figure is the Rashash, שמואל שטראשון. The write-up’s שטרויסון (and “ראש שטרויסון”) is a transcription error, repeated six times, including note 6. Corrected in the original HTML.

Flag 3 — The Hebrew year תר״ח is not in the recording and is wrong. The lecturer says only “1848.” The summary supplied “תר״ח (1848).” Hebrew year תר״ח (5608) began in autumn 1847; the Tishrei of autumn 1848 falls in 5609, תר״ט. Seven occurrences corrected (subtitle, lead, Section III heading and text, Section IV, Section IX, note 12). The lecturer himself says in passing that the date is uncertain across the versions (“I am not really bothered about the details of the date”).

Flag 4 — The kiddush position is credited to the Ketzos HaChoshen; the recording says something else. Both times the transcript reads “the kiveh eger holds that when Yom Kippur falls on Shabbos and you are a choleh, you have to make kiddush” — much closer to Rav Akiva Eger than to the Ketzos. The summary replaced it with the Ketzos HaChoshen, and the write-up (Section VIII and note 5) followed. Sefaria verifies the opposite general rule (Magen Avraham 618:10 and Mishnah Berurah 618:29 say a sick person who eats on Yom Kippur makes no kiddush, a berachah levatalah), but neither attributes the Shabbos exception to anyone. The note is now marked unverified and records the possible reading of the recording.

Flag 5 — Note 8 invents authority for the Chofetz Chaim and the Mishnah Berurah. The lecturer says only that Shalom Almanori brings the eyewitness version in his book on those who died on Yom Kippur, and that “the Mishnah Berurah and Biur Halachah quote the story in passing” (“ein sham”). The write-up’s note 8 adds that the Chofetz Chaim’s strong language on a rav’s duty to rule for pikuach nefesh is shaped by this case and that his rulings carry “firsthand weight.” Nothing of the sort is in the recording. On Sefaria the Mishnah Berurah on siman 618 (se’ifei katan 21 and 29) does not mention Rav Yisrael or cholera; the Biur Halachah was not retrievable. The unsupported sentences are removed; the lecturer’s report is kept as a report.

Flag 6 — Sefaria disagrees with several details in the mareh mekomos. (a) Note 3: the Rambam (Hil. Rotzeach 11:4) grounds the duty to guard life in Devarim 4:9, not 4:15 (4:15 is cited at Berachos 32b). (b) Note 4: Shulchan Aruch 618:7 gives the measure as about two-thirds of a medium egg with a pause of the time to eat four eggs, not כותבת; the seifim were misassigned (seif 1 is patient against physician, 2–5 disagreement and doubt, 7 the measures). (c) Note 9: the Yerushalmi (Yoma 8:5) reads הזריז משובח והנשאל מגונה והשואל שופך דמים. (d) Note 10: the Rambam (Shabbos 2:3) forbids delay but does not use השואל / שופך דמים, which are in Shulchan Aruch OC 328:2. All corrected in the original HTML.

Flag 7 — Numbers and ages. The recording gives the congregation as “a thousand or fifteen hundred” (the write-up has “fifteen hundred” twice, stated flatly). It gives the Chofetz Chaim as “ten or twenty years old” (from the 94-versus-104 age dispute); the write-up drops the range and says only “a young boy.” It gives The Making of a Gadol as published “about fifteen years ago”; note 1 says 2002 and gives the author’s death year, neither of which is from the shiur (the death year is removed in the corrected HTML). The lecturer’s figure for Rav Yisrael’s age in 1848 (“thirty-six, thirty-seven”) is also not checked here and sits uneasily with the birth year usually given for him (c. 1809/10); the write-up reports it as the lecturer’s.

4.Omissions and Added Material

  • The Koidenover sub-story is cut short. The transcript explains that Vilna had some chassidim, that the Koidenover chassidim heard of the incident and told their Rebbe, who spoke against Rav Yisrael; the chassidim then blamed the later stroke on the Rebbe’s kefeidah. The write-up gives only the last sentence in Section VII, where the Koidenover Rebbe appears for the first time. Restored in the Sefer Edition (Section V).
  • Other versions are mentioned and lost. The lecturer says “Reb Moshe Steinbach has a different version of the story” (unclear name) and points to p. 1137 of The Making of a Gadol as well as 1136, and says the dating of the event is disputed. The write-up has only p. 1136.
  • Biography of Rav Baruch Epstein and his book. The recording adds that he came to America for three years in the 1920s, that the English My Uncle the Netziv (1988) was edited down after complaints and is “not as geshmak” as the Hebrew, and that the Netziv later became a relative by marriage. The write-up keeps only the book list.
  • Practical halachah trimmed. The lecturer says that the Shulchan Aruch speaks of “only three things” one does not set aside for pikuach nefesh; that fasting where forbidden is like lighting a fire on Yom Kippur; that using both cheeks is easier; that the question of a pregnant woman should be settled with a doctor before Yom Kippur. These are absent or compressed.
  • The Brisk detail. The recording says that in Brisk they eat on the minor fasts even for a small headache and apparently that Yom Kippur and Tisha B’Av are the exceptions (the audio is garbled at this point); the write-up has only “on regular fasts in Brisk they ate.” The Shavel rabbi’s name is also garbled in the recording and appropriately omitted.
  • The COVID Purim story (a rabbi leading dancing hand-in-hand in the early weeks of the pandemic, followed by deaths among the congregation) is cut; the write-up keeps only the brief “we remember COVID-19.” A personal aside, appropriately cut, but it is the lecturer’s illustration of why the physicians’ fear was real.
  • Material added by the write-up. The scholarly caveat in note 2 (that Etkes questions Epstein’s narrative), the fuller biographical dates in notes 1, 2, 8, 12 and 13, and the assertion in note 8 are editorial additions. The Etkes sentence is unverifiable and is hedged in the corrected HTML; Sections VI and XIV are partly a synthesis in the editor’s voice (“walked the very narrow line”) and not in the recording.
  • First-person framing. “I am thirty-eight years old,” “I always tell people,” and “where I learned” are reproduced in the lecturer’s voice, and the personal psak on the nine-minute interval is delivered as advice. Fine for a write-up of the shiur; for the sefer it must be depersonalised, and the practical guidance should carry a note to consult a rav.

5.Halachic and Lomdish Observations; Questions for the Rav

The two versions are not reconcilable, and the write-up should not try. The Mekor Baruch version has a blanket permission and a public kiddush; the Strashun version has no kiddush, only the sick, only less than a shiur, and no eating by Rav Yisrael. The lecturer presents them side by side and does not choose. The 1836 Chasam Sofer precedent, the Shulchan Aruch and the closing line (“Rav Yisrael was one hundred percent right”) all rest on the second version. The write-up’s conclusion (Section XIV) is faithful, but should say that it holds for the Strashun version.

The “less than a shiur is not a breach” line needs a qualifier. The lecturer closes: “it is only breaking the fast if you eat more than a shiur.” As a statement about what a choleh or one in safek sakanah is permitted, that is the Shulchan Aruch’s whole point; as a general statement it is imprecise, since for a healthy person eating less than a shiur is still forbidden at the rabbinic level (reviewer’s recollection, to be verified). The write-up repeats the line without that setting.

The pregnant-woman analogy is the lecturer’s, not necessarily the Chasam Sofer’s. The recording introduces it as “the halachah is this” and draws a kal vachomer; the write-up’s Section XI says “The reasoning is straightforward,” which reads as the Chasam Sofer’s own. The “one hundred percent” sentence is also unclear in the recording (“because shiur is at one hundred percent is not a shailah at all”) and is rendered in the write-up as a certainty of safek pikuach nefesh, which is a contradiction in terms.

The “Rabbeinu Baruch” of Section VIII. The recording has “Reb Bernie asks the halachic question,” with no introduction; the write-up turns it into רבינו ברוך and a section title without identifying him. It may be Rav Baruch Epstein in Mekor Baruch, or Rav Baruch Ber (who appears later), or another; the shiur does not say.

Q1.Who exactly is the elderly dayan (רבי איצל הכהן?), and what is his sefer called? Is the “Bikurim” a title or a surname?
Q2.Who holds that a sick person on a Shabbos Yom Kippur makes kiddush — the Ketzos, Rav Akiva Eger, or another — and where?
Q3.Who is “Reb Bernie” who asks the questions of Section VIII? And is the kashya on gittin known from any source?
Q4.Which teshuvah of the Chasam Sofer deals with the 1836 Pressburg cholera, and is the pregnant-woman reasoning his own?
Q5.The full titles and authors of Gedolim fun Unzer Zeit and the book by Shalom Almanori (“Alecha Shimcha Yisrael”?), and the passage in the Mishnah Berurah / Biur Halachah that tells the story.
Q6.Whose is the “different version” (“Reb Moshe Steinbach”) mentioned at p. 1137, and does it change the facts?
Q7.Dating: Rav Yisrael’s age and the year of the stroke, and when exactly the cholera reached Jerusalem relative to Rishon LeTzion Rav Chaim David Chazan’s term (the lecturer says “a couple of years later”).

6.Bottom Line

A faithful, well-ordered treatment of a story told in two irreconcilable versions, with the lecturer’s own scepticism intact. The errors that should not reach a sefer are the following, and are corrected in the original HTML: the invented identity of the elderly dayan (split into two men, one of them anachronistic), the surname שטרויסון (it is Strashun), the Hebrew year תר״ח (the date is 5609), the four citation details in notes 3, 4, 9 and 10, and the unsupported claims in note 8. The Ketzos attribution is open and flagged. Restore, for the Sefer Edition: the Koidenover episode, the range “a thousand or fifteen hundred,” the Chofetz Chaim’s “ten or twenty,” the second page of The Making of a Gadol, and the fuller biography of Rav Baruch Epstein, and take out the first-person halachic advice.
