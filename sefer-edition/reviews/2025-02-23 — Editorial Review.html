23 February 2025 — The Three-Part Structure of שמונה עשרה and ברכת אבות

Independent review · read fresh, no exposure to the writing process · 30 August 2026

1.Overall Read

This is a well-constructed, ambitious piece that succeeds at what it sets out to do: take a familiar structural question about Shemoneh Esrei and layer it with halachic sources, biographical color, and a genuinely dramatic Aggadic passage that lands with force at the end. Twelve numbered sections plus sixteen footnotes; the architecture is clean, and the reader can feel the shiur's original momentum — from the abstract structural rule, through the two tirutzim, through the strange figure of Nachum HaMadi, into the ma'aseh with Eliyahu and the Avos, and out to the closing image of Chevron. The dual-layer footnote apparatus is used consistently and adds real value.

The piece is strongest in Sections IV (Nachum HaMadi) and X–XI (Bava Metzia / Chevron), which are the sections where narrative and idea reinforce each other. It is weakest in one significant biographical error in Section III (the Terumas HaDeshen attribution), in one place where the argument gets ahead of the sources (Section VII on the alternate tirutz), and in the somewhat crowded overlay of Rebbe-asides in the first half. These are correctable, and none of them touches the spine of the piece.

2.What Works

  • The Nachum HaMadi arc (Section IV) is the most satisfying set-piece in the document. The remarkable lashon of תשתכח דברי מעיה, Bar Minyomi's complaint to Abaye, the parallel to R' Eliezer ben Hurkanos, and the possible identification with Nachum Ish Gamzu are all woven together economically. The pivot — "the one halacha we pasken like him is the very structural rule we've been discussing" — is a genuine payoff. It gives the abstract rule a human face and a memorable proof-text.
  • The Bava Metzia passage (Section X) is presented as narrative rather than as a block of Aramaic-plus-translation, and the choice pays off. The escalating rhythm — wind fells trees at "משיב הרוח," graves shake at "מחיה מתים," the malachim intervene, the pulsa d'nura — is genuinely gripping. Placing it just before the synthesis is the right structural choice.
  • The Mabit / Terumas HaDeshen pairing (Section III) works as a diptych. Two answers, one revealing the interior mechanism of praise (the mispallel needs it, not Hashem), the other its external mechanism (bypassing the mekatregim). Framing them as "complementary rather than competing" is exactly right and avoids a fake machlokes.
  • The Chevron section (XI) is a graceful landing. The Sadigurer Rebbe's בערדיטשעב parallel to "חברון" and to "אלקי אברהם" tightens three ideas into one image and gives the piece a proper closing chord.
  • The dual-layer footnote apparatus is used with discipline throughout. The academic-flag note on the Ketzos/Terumas HaDeshen biographical background (fn5) and on the Sadigurer Rebbe (fn16) are correctly cordoned off from the standard citation notes. This is exactly the pattern established in the earlier review and it should stay.

3.Where It's Weak or Could Be Sharper

Section VII (the alternate tirutz on מלכות in Birkas Avos) is too thin. The subsection headed "An Alternate Tirutz — Dovid HaMelech" is barely three sentences and does not attribute the position to a specific rishon. It reads as if a placeholder was left in. Either name the source ("Rashba," "the Rosh," etc.) with a footnote, or drop the subsection and fold the observation about Dovid's wish to be mentioned in Birkas Avos into the Tosafos discussion. As written, it invites the sophisticated reader to distrust the paragraph.

The first-half Rebbe-asides accumulate. Section I closes with the "davened so quickly" anecdote; Section III has two long callout boxes on the Mabit and on the Terumas HaDeshen brothers; Section III's second callout ends with a Baal Shem Tov story; Section V has a "standing puzzle" of the Rebbe's; Section X ends with the "טאטי, I don't want to daven in the same minyan as you" quip. Individually each is charming. Together they slow the first half's momentum. Consider promoting the strongest one or two to the callout treatment they already have and demoting the rest into footnote asides, or cutting the "davened so quickly" opening entirely — it competes with the far more effective Baal Shem Tov story that arrives two sections later on the same theme (empty tefillah).

Section II asks "Why not אלקי אברהם יצחק ויעקב?" implicitly and answers it fully only in Sections IX and X. That is a long delay for a question the structural framing has already raised. A single connective sentence at the end of Section II ("— and why each Av is named separately is a question we will return to at length below") would keep the reader oriented across the intervening halachic material.

Section VI (Rav Hai Gaon on זכרנו לחיים) sits oddly. The kushya is real and important, but it interrupts the arc from "Nachum HaMadi's one accepted halacha" (Section IV) directly into Birkas Avos (Section VII). Consider either moving Section VI to a footnote or repositioning it as a "digression" callout, so the through-line from the structural rule to Birkas Avos is not broken by a seasonal exception.

4.Factual / Citation Concerns

The Terumas HaDeshen identification in Section III is wrong. The callout box on the Terumas HaDeshen states that "the Terumas HaDeshen here is R' Yehudah Leib Heller's elder brother — author of the Ketzos HaChoshen's companion volume of the same name." This conflates two entirely different works. There are two Terumas HaDeshen: (i) the medieval one by R' Yisrael Isserlein (1390–1460), which is a foundational sefer of she'eilos u'teshuvos and whose hakdamah is quoted in the classical halachic-kabbalistic literature on tefillah; and (ii) the eighteenth-century Avnei Miluim-adjacent Terumas HaDeshen by R' Yehudah Kahana Heller (brother of the Ketzos, R' Aryeh Leib Heller), which is a work of lomdus, not of Aggadah / tefillah-mechanics. The hakdamah discussion of mekatregim blocking tefillah is from R' Yisrael Isserlein, not from the Heller brother. The callout must be rewritten: identify the correct Terumas HaDeshen (R' Yisrael Isserlein, Austria/Wiener Neustadt, fifteenth century, also the source teacher of the Rema's mesorah), and either cut the Ketzos/Vilna Gaon anecdote entirely (it belongs to a different sefer) or move it to a footnote clearly labeled as being about a different Terumas HaDeshen. Footnote 5, which currently notes the distinction as a "source-type note," should be promoted into the body correction rather than left as a fine-print aside.

The Mabit biography in Section III is mostly correct but overstates one point. The Mabit did serve as dayan in Tzefas for many decades, and the gematria of דיין = 64 (ס״ד), not 54 (נ״ד). The claim in the callout that "his matzeivah in Tzefas records exactly that: נ״ד years, the gematria of דיין itself" is arithmetically wrong: ד=4, י=10, י=10, ן=50 — sums to 74 with final nun, or 14 with the standard nun. Neither reading yields נ״ד. The gematria claim needs to be verified against the actual matzeivah inscription and either corrected or dropped. The safe move is to say "he served as dayan for over fifty years" and cut the gematria flourish.

Nachum HaMadi's provenance in Section IV is described as "מודי (a meluchah near the border of Turkey and Iraq)." "Madi" refers to Media (ancient Medea), located roughly in northwestern Iran, not on the Turkey-Iraq border. The phrasing also inconsistently spells the place as "מודי" in the parenthetical but "מדי" (correctly) in Nachum's title. Standardize to מדי = Media, and adjust the geography ("in what is today northwestern Iran, historically Media").

Bar Minyomi's home is likely also Media, not "also from Madi" as if it were a personal identifier. Verify the Gemara's lashon in Bava Metzia 33b and adjust: the point of the anecdote is that Bar Minyomi is standing up for his fellow Medean, which the current phrasing partially conveys but could be sharpened.

Footnote 7 cites "Bava Metzia 33b and parallels" for the Nachum HaMadi material. The classical location for תשתכח דברי מעיה is Shabbos 63b or Bava Basra 153b (depending on the specific citation); verify the exact daf before publication. The Bar Minyomi / Abaye anecdote is at Bava Metzia 33b in most editions but the "תשתכח" formulation appears elsewhere.

Rav Hai Gaon's dates (Section VI): "born 938, lived to age 100." The standard dating is 939–1038, which is ninety-nine years, not one hundred. "Lived nearly to age 100" is safer.

The "הפני מזרח מאירין עד חברון" reading in Section XI has a grammatical problem. The Mishnah's lashon is "האיר פני מזרח עד שבחברון" (Yoma 3:1). The rendering in the body text should be corrected to the received Mishnah lashon.

5.Small Polish Suggestions

  • Section III uses the word "install" twice in close proximity ("install in you the recognition"; "install in the mispallel the recognition"). Vary the second instance — "instill," "produce," "cultivate."
  • The colloquial Yiddish "pasken" (in "we pasken like him") appears twice in Section IV. It works in a shiur register but is slightly informal for the sefer register the rest of the piece is aiming at. Consider "the halacha follows him" or "we rule with him."
  • Section V's callout about "Yiddishe kinderlach" being the most important bakashah a father or grandfather has is a lovely detail, but the Yiddish-transliterated plural jars against the surrounding Hebrew transliteration. Standardize: either "Jewish children" or the fully Hebrew "זרע יהודי."
  • Section VIII uses "becher" transliterated as "בכר" in the sentence "a beautiful בכר without wine is hollow." This is a Yiddish word rendered in Hebrew letters — the effect is jarring. Either transliterate ("becher") or use the Hebrew "גביע."
  • Section X's ma'aseh is told in short quoted exchanges that break at every line. This works well but the attribution tags ("Rebbi asked," "Eliyahu answered," "Rebbi") are inconsistent between the two spellings. Standardize to one.
  • The section headers alternate between English and Hebrew phrasing (e.g., "Is This Not חנופה?" vs. "The Power of Their Combined תפילה"). This is fine, but consider whether a couple of Hebrew-only sub-headers (e.g., Section IV's "נחום איש גמזו?") would benefit from an English gloss for the less-Hebrew-facing reader who might be the future sefer's audience.
  • Footnote 4 is labeled "Terumas HaDeshen, הקדמה (general reference)." If the correction above is adopted (identifying the correct Terumas HaDeshen as R' Yisrael Isserlein), pin the reference more precisely: R' Yisrael Isserlein, Terumas HaDeshen, hakdamah, with any known siman or paragraph.

6.Bottom Line

A well-shaped piece with a genuinely dramatic center (the Bava Metzia ma'aseh) and a good structural spine. The Terumas HaDeshen misidentification in Section III is the one factual mistake that must be corrected before the document goes anywhere, the Mabit gematria and Rav Hai Gaon's dates should be verified, and the alternate tirutz in Section VII needs either a proper source or removal. Consolidate the first-half Rebbe-asides so the piece doesn't slow down before it accelerates — make those four fixes and this is a strong sefer-ready essay on the interior architecture of שמונה עשרה.
