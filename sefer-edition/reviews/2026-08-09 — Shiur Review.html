Mareh Mekamos · Shiur Review

2026-08-09 — Entering Shabbos Shacharis (מזמור לתודה, תפילין, and the Seventh Wing)

Content-based review · reviewer read the transcript, summary, and finished write-up · 9 August 2026

1.The Shiur in One Paragraph

This week Rabbi Ephrati opened a new chapter of the Tefillah series — שחרית של שבת — by surveying, in one sitting, the four or five things that are visibly different on Shabbos morning: the extra kapitlach in פסוקי דזמרה, the omission (or not) of מזמור לתודה, the absence of תפילין, the later davening time, and the different voice of the tefillah (בקול ובניגון instead of בלחש). The arc was less a single lomdish thread than a mapping exercise — he wanted to set the terrain before drilling in future weeks. The unifying move was to frame each difference as an expression of Shabbos as an אות — a day of presence that makes symbols redundant (תפילין), that gives us the leisure to be מאריך (nine o'clock, בניגון), and that promotes Klal Yisrael into the שירה-role of the מלאכים (the seventh wing). The closing story — Dovid's madness before Achish, and the personal excursus about the לשכת גיוס — was more homiletic-biographical than halachic.

2.What Was Strongest

  • The framing of Shabbos-as-אות with the Maharsha/Smag's שני עדים. The move — that מילה + תפילין are the weekday עדות, and Shabbos itself replaces תפילין as the second עד — is the tightest lomdish moment of the shiur. It ties the halachic ruling (no תפילין) to a Torah-level principle (שני עדים יקום דבר) and makes the reason for the prohibition follow naturally rather than feel like a gzeirah.
  • The Rav Hai Gaon → Mordechai → Radvaz arc on davening time. This was the shiur's best sustained sugya work. He built up the mesorah (Rav Hai's remez from בבקר בבקר vs. וביום השבת), then honestly named the textual problem (וביום השבת is about מוסף), then brought the Radvaz's beautiful move — defending Rav Hai as דברי קבלה while personally davening ותיקין. That last twist is what gives the whole passage its bite.
  • The Zoharic "seventh wing" as the answer to why these kapitlach. It's an elegant single-answer explanation for the content of the additions, not just their length — the added מזמורים are literally the מלאכים's שירה that we take over. Good closer.
  • Naming the מזמור לתודה dispute as a real regional-halachic phenomenon. Framing פרובנס as the machloikes-battlefield between France and Sfarad gave the sugya a historical texture that a bare Rashi-vs.-Tur presentation wouldn't have.

3.What Was Weaker or Worth Pushing Back On

The shiur is a survey, not a chakirah. Five topics in forty-five minutes means nothing gets fully developed. The מזמור לתודה machloikes, the אות sugya, the זמן שחרית question, and the seventh-wing Zohar each deserve their own shiur; here each got a compressed treatment that left the lomdish thread half-drawn.

The Franz Joseph / Rav Shimon Sofer story is charming but doesn't carry the halachic weight the Rav hangs on it. He himself admits it's "a shtickel kinderish" — but he then leans on it as the hashkafic explanation of why תפילין on Shabbos is a זלזול. A mashal from a 19th-century vort about a portrait isn't really the same category as the ספרים הקדושים argument that a third עד is a diminishment. He conflated a nice story with the underlying svara.

The excursus about the לשכת גיוס and Rav Avraham Yehoshua Soloveitchik was genuinely interesting but was, halachically, a detour. Its supposed link to דוד המלך's שינה את טעמו is thin — Rav Soloveitchik's point was about שקר, not about pikuach nefesh, so it doesn't really parallel Dovid's case (which is precisely a pikuach nefesh היתר for something that would otherwise be problematic). If anything the two stories cut against each other.

He never actually explained the connection of לדוד בשנותו את טעמו to Shabbos. He told the beautiful ma'aseh, but the closing line — "when did the story happen? On Shabbos. That's why we say this mizmor on Shabbos" — was asserted, not sourced. Where does that mesorah come from? Which meforshim on תהלים or on the siddur say the Achish episode happened on Shabbos? The talmid is left hanging.

On the 9:00 minyan question he essentially punted. After the whole build-up — Rav Belsky's pet peeve, the Ramo, the Mordechai, the Radvaz's chas v'shalom to daven late — the practical conclusion was "we'll continue davening at nine, just don't miss זמן קריאת שמע." Given how forcefully the sources pushed the other way, that's an unearned landing. Either he holds the מנהג is defensible (in which case he should say against the Radvaz why), or he doesn't (in which case he should say so). The soft landing didn't match the sharp build.

4.Halachic / Lomdish Observations

On the מזמור לתודה sugya: He gave Rashi and the Tur, but the position of the Vilna Gaon — who was מקפיד on it every day it's said and gave it structural weight in his siddur — would have sharpened the Ashkenazi picture. The Gra's whole approach to פסוקי דזמרה (that it's a fixed unit, not open to accretion) is directly relevant.

The Tur/B"Y machloikes has more sides than presented. The Rambam does not include מזמור לתודה in his סדר התפילה at all — worth noting, since it changes the picture from "Rashi vs. Tur" to "which of three different Rishonic frameworks are we in."

On the "four psukim = four types of לחמי תודה" remez — the לחמי תודה are actually forty loaves, in four categories (חלות מצה, רקיקין, חלות רבוכה, and חמץ). The Rav gestured at this ("forty לחמי תודה, four types") but it went by so fast that the remez lost its force. Worth slowing down.

On שני עדים / אות: the Rav didn't distinguish between the two דרשות for no-תפילין on Shabbos — the דרשה from ושמרתם את החקה הזאת (Menachos 36b, the Ba'al HaMaor / Rashba dispute over whether it's a דאורייתא or דרבנן) is arguably the primary sugya, and the שני עדים framing is more of a hashkafic overlay from the Smag/Maharsha. Collapsing them makes the picture cleaner but obscures the real Rishonic machloikes.

On the "voice of tefillah" distinction (בלחש vs. בקול/בניגון): He attributes this to the Ariza"l via the Mishnah Berurah, but the Ariza"l's own position on kavana in פסוקי דזמרה is more nuanced — the לחש is tied to specific yichudim, and the shift on Shabbos is not simply "louder because we have time" but connected to the דעת שבת השבתות. Worth flagging.

On the מרדכי's tradition from רב האי גאון: the אחרונים don't only defend or reject it — the Beis Yosef on Siman רפ״א brings a completely different reading: the delay is not for sleep, and not for שנים מקרא, but for ציבור-forming (so latecomers can make it). That's a third שיטה the shiur missed.

5.Follow-up Questions Worth Asking the Rav

Q1.What is the actual source that the Achish/דוד ha-madness episode happened on Shabbos? Is it in חז״ל, in a midrash, or is it a later meforshic reading that got attached to the placement of תהלים ל״ד in the siddur?
Q2.How does the Rav learn the sugya in Menachos 36b about תפילין בשבת — is the איסור דאורייתא (like the Ba'al HaMaor) or דרבנן? And does that shape whether "זלזול בקדושת שבת" is a real halachic category or a hashkafic overlay?
Q3.Given the Radvaz's sharp language ("חס ושלום, לא מנהג יפה, לא מנהג הגון"), on what basis does the Rav feel comfortable defending the 9:00 minyan as a legitimate נמשך to the Ramo rather than as a stretched application?
Q4.Does the Rav hold the seventh-wing Zohar as a hashkafic explanation for the added kapitlach, or does he see it as generating actual halachic weight — e.g., could one say the extra kapitlach are more מעכב on Shabbos than the Mishnah Berurah's "not מעכב" ruling implies?
Q5.Where does the Rambam fit in the מזמור לתודה picture — since he doesn't include it at all in his סדר התפילה, is that a third position, or does it collapse into Rashi?
Q6.On the מהרי"ל's claim that כהנים slept later on Shabbos morning in the בית המקדש — is there any actual source for this in חז״ל, or is it a svara the מהרי"ל built to defend the מרדכי?

6.Bottom Line

This was a transition shiur — the Rav consciously closing the מעריב/קריאת שמע שעל המטה chapter and opening the שחרית של שבת chapter, and doing it by laying out the whole landscape of Shabbos-morning differences at once. As such, it did what transition shiurim do: broad rather than deep, more taxonomy than chiddush, with a few genuinely lovely moments (the שני עדים framing, the Radvaz's defense-then-repudiation, the seventh wing) surrounded by material that will presumably get its proper treatment in coming weeks. Within the arc of the Tefillah series it functions well as a table of contents; judged on its own it feels a little thin — five sugyos means none of them fully lands. The Dovid HaMelech / לשכת גיוס closing was memorable but hashkafically loose, and the practical p'sak on davening time was softer than the sources demanded. If the coming weeks return to any of these five topics with real depth, this shiur will read in retrospect as a good scaffold; if they don't, it will feel like a set of teasers.
